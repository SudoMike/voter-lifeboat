## Background

Dave Larson, 68, of Federal Way, is a retired trial judge who presided over the Federal Way Municipal Court, which he joined in 2008; he was elected to that court in 2009, 2013, 2017, and 2021 [S8]. Sources differ on his retirement year: the primary-cycle record cites 2025, while the Spokesman-Review's September profile lists his service as 2008–2026 [S2][S8]. He reports 41 years of legal experience: 18 years as a trial judge and 23 years as a trial lawyer, plus work as a mediator and arbitrator from 1990 to 2008 [S1]. He owned a law firm (1991–2001) and a mediation company (1998–2001) [S8]. He holds a BA from the University of Puget Sound and a JD from Seattle University School of Law [S1][S8]. He was president of the Federal Way School Board [S1][S8].

He has run for the Supreme Court before; the Spokesman-Review lists unsuccessful runs in 2000, 2016, 2020, and 2024, losing the 2024 race by about 20,000 votes statewide [S8]. (The primary-cycle record listed 2016, 2020, and 2024 [S2].)

## Primary result

In the August 4, 2026 primary, Larson placed second of four with 559,629 votes (32.24%), behind Theo Angelis (623,396; 35.92%) and ahead of Sharonda Amamilo (371,059; 21.38%) and Greg Miller (181,542; 10.46%), per Secretary of State data as published by the Spokesman-Review (last updated August 21) [S16].

## Positions

Judicial philosophy and approach:

- Campaign site: "Apply the law as written, not as you wish it had been," and the role of a justice "is not to impose ideology" but to ensure "that the law is applied as written, consistently, and without political pressure from any direction" [S3][S4].
- Pamphlet: pledges to apply "the law without fear or favor," restore trust in the courts "by rising above partisan interests," and restore judicial independence he says is "currently under threat" [S1].
- Says the Supreme Court needs a justice with trial-judge experience; the pamphlet says "our Supreme Court's decisions have weakened" trial courts' response to crime and addiction [S1].
- Primary-cycle reporting recorded him calling the court's State v. Blake drug-possession decision "motivated by ideology" and describing the court as "a ship lost at sea" [S2].

Criminal justice (documented statements and record only):

- States he collaborated with legislative Democrats and Republicans on trial-court approaches to crime and addiction "with compassion and accountability," and that his "criminal justice reforms have been recognized nationwide" [S1].
- Served on the District and Municipal Court Judges Association (DMCJA) Therapeutic Courts Committee, 2019–2025 [S6].
- Primary-cycle reporting recorded his opposition to proposed bail rules he said would "cripple" trial courts and his view that public-defender caseload standards treat symptoms rather than causes [S2].

Judicial selection and court administration:

- Proposes a bipartisan Judicial Elections Commission that would evaluate candidates and send agreed-upon names to the governor, rather than leaving appointments solely to the governor; he frames the current process as creating "the appearance … that it's cronyism" [S8][S9][S10].
- Supports raising juror pay from the $10 a day set in 1959 [S9].

## Record

- Presiding judge of Federal Way Municipal Court; the campaign says he managed over 270,000 case filings [S3][S8].
- DMCJA Therapeutic Courts Committee (2019–2025) and other DMCJA committees on legislation, judicial independence, and public trust and confidence [S6].
- DMCJA President's Award (2018), which his pamphlet describes as recognizing protection of judicial independence; Washington Judges' Foundation Judge William Nevins Award (2014) for civics education [S1][S6].
- Trial lawyer in civil litigation for 23 years [S1][S3].

## Ratings

- Pamphlet claim: "Rated AV Preeminent as a trial lawyer and Exceptionally Well Qualified (Judge and Supreme Court candidate)" [S1]. The campaign site repeats that he "has been rated 'Exceptionally Well Qualified' by multiple bar associations" without naming them [S4].
- Located 2026 ratings: the Tacoma-Pierce County Bar Association rated him "Qualified" for the Washington Supreme Court in its August 8, 2026 release (listed as "Dave Larsen") [S11]. The King County Bar Association lists him as "N/A," not rated within the last three years [S12]. Washington Women Lawyers' and the Joint Asian Judicial Evaluations Committee's 2026 Supreme Court lists do not include him [S13][S14].
- No 2026 source naming an organization that rated him "Exceptionally Well Qualified" was found. The Cardozo Society's ratings page could not be loaded.

## Endorsements

- Party organizations: recommended by the Washington State Republican Party, King County Republican Party, and Mainstream Republicans of Washington; endorsed by the Forward Party and Cascade PAC [S5][S8].
- Business: National Federation of Independent Business Washington, Washington State Farm Bureau, and the AGC of Washington BUILD PAC (announced Oct. 5, 2026) [S5][S15].
- Law enforcement and labor: Washington Council of Police & Sheriffs (WACOPS), ten county sheriffs, police guilds and deputy associations, and IAFF Local 31 (Tacoma firefighters) [S5].
- Individuals: Sheley Secrest, president of the NAACP Alaska Oregon Washington State Area Conference [S5][S8].

## Campaign finance

- PDC contributions data show $405,098.67 in reported contributions (817 records, cash and in-kind) through filings dated October 1, 2026, and $263,228.09 in reported expenditures through August 31, 2026 [S7]. The Spokesman-Review reported about $390,900 raised as of October 3 [S8].
- Reported donors include the NFIB Washington PAC, the Council of Police Political Support, the Skagit County GOP, and the AGC BUILD PAC [S8].
- Independent spending: before the primary, Concerned Taxpayers of Washington State spent about $50,000 supporting Larson, and Beware the Attack on Our Courts spent about $16,000 opposing him [S10].
