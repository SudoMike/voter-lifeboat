## Background

Theo Angelis (Theodore J. Angelis), 54, of Seattle, is a sitting Washington State Supreme Court justice. Governor Bob Ferguson appointed him to succeed retiring Justice Barbara Madsen, and he began serving on April 4, 2026 [S3][S8]. Position 5 is on the ballot for the 2-year unexpired remainder of that term [S1]. He describes himself as the first justice of Middle Eastern descent on the court, citing Turkish and Greek ancestry [S1].

Before his appointment he spent 26 years at the Seattle firm K&L Gates, where he led the appellate practice and served as global leader of the intellectual property practice [S1][S3]. After law school he clerked for Judge Stephen F. Williams of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit [S3][S8]. His pro bono work included litigation over children appearing without lawyers in immigration court, which he says ended with the federal government agreeing to pay for lawyers, and representation of an Army veteran detained by ICE for seven months [S1]. Awards include the ACLU Humanitarian Award (2017), the Kids in Need of Defense Founders Award (2018), the Northwest Immigrant Rights Project Amicus Award (2011), and King County Bar Association Mentor of the Year (2012) [S1][S3].

He holds a BA from Claremont McKenna College, master's degrees from Oxford and the London School of Economics, and a JD from Yale Law School [S1][S3]. Bar service includes King County Bar Association trustee, president of the Middle Eastern Legal Association of Washington, and president of the M. Margaret McKeown Bar Association [S1][S3].

The Spokesman-Review reports that Angelis worked at the same law firm as Ferguson for about a year and a half, did work on Ferguson's first King County Council campaign, and received baby gear from Ferguson after the birth of his twins [S8]. The Washington State Standard notes he worked with Ferguson at K&L Gates more than 20 years ago [S17].

## Primary result

In the August 4, 2026 primary, Angelis placed first of four with 623,396 votes (35.92%), ahead of Dave Larson (559,629; 32.24%), Sharonda Amamilo (371,059; 21.38%), and Greg Miller (181,542; 10.46%), per Secretary of State data as published by the Spokesman-Review (last updated August 21) [S16].

## Positions

Judicial philosophy and approach (as described by the candidate):

- Says he has "fought for the rule of law, the rights of all individuals," and for courts that "remain fair, independent, and accessible" [S1].
- Campaign site: "The Court's foremost responsibility is to faithfully interpret and apply the Constitution and laws of Washington State" [S5].
- To the Washington State Bar News: "When we hear cases, we put aside all policy and political preferences. Our role is to apply the law fairly, independently, neutrally, and consistently to the facts as presented to us" [S10].
- Says that in his short time on the court he has "already had to rule in ways I thought were incorrect … but that's what the law requires" [S9].
- Describes a case process in which he writes out a physical list of his potential biases for every case and consults it while working on the case [S8][S9][S10].
- Disputes that a justice's rulings can be predicted from who appointed them: "I don't think that you can point to any single justice and say, 'Oh, I'm sure this is how she or he is going to rule'" [S8].
- The Stranger's July 2 endorsement characterized him as holding that courts should protect individual rights rather than defer to legislators; this is the editorial board's characterization, not a direct quote [S12].

Access to justice and court administration:

- Calls the inability to afford legal representation "the biggest challenge the public faces," saying representation is "a financial impossibility" for most middle- and working-class Washingtonians [S10].
- Supports raising juror pay, which has been $10 a day since 1959 [S9].

## Record

- Serving on the Supreme Court since April 4, 2026 [S3].
- Authored opinions: none found. A review of every Supreme Court opinion information sheet filed from April through October 1, 2026 on courts.wa.gov found no opinion (majority, concurrence, or dissent) authored by Angelis [S4]. In most of those cases, which were argued before he joined the court, he is listed as "Did Not Participate," including Climate Solutions v. State (No. 104,240-0, filed Sept. 17, 2026) [S4].
- Joined opinions: he is listed as signing the unanimous majority in State v. Perrin (No. 104,816-5, filed July 30, 2026; author Justice González), In re Recall of O'Neil (No. 105,253-7, filed Aug. 20, 2026; author Justice Gordon McCloud), and Earl v. Campbell (No. 104,495-0, filed Sept. 3, 2026; author Justice Johnson) [S4].
- As an attorney, sued the federal government over children appearing without lawyers in immigration court; per his statement, the federal government eventually agreed to pay for lawyers ("has done") [S1].

## Scoring notes

- Sitting justice by appointment, about six months on the bench; no authored opinions found through October 1, 2026, so there is no written judicial record to place on the `judicial` axis [S3][S4].
- His interpretive statements ("faithfully interpret and apply the Constitution and laws," "put aside all policy and political preferences," ruling as "the law requires" against his own view) are largely standard judicial language; the clearest rights-protective framing comes from The Stranger's characterization rather than his own words [S5][S9][S10][S12].
- `safety`: no prosecution or public-defense career and no documented bail, diversion, or therapeutic-court positions; his formation is civil appellate/IP litigation plus pro bono immigration work [S1][S3].
- `experience`: runs as the incumbent with institutional backing (all sitting colleagues, current and former governors) but a short tenure; the opponent frames the appointment process itself as a problem [S1][S8][S9].
- Placed first in the primary (35.92%) and leads the general field narrowly in reported contributions [S7][S16].
