## What it does

Initiative Measure No. IL26-001 is an initiative to the Legislature on the November 3, 2026 statewide ballot. Its ballot title reads: "This measure would repeal amendments to a statute listing certain rights of parents and guardians of public-school children and re-enact the law as it was originally enacted by Initiative to the Legislature 2081." [S1] The Legislature did not act on it during its 2026 regular session, so it goes to the voters [S6]. If approved, it takes effect 30 days after the election [S6]; OFM gives the effective date as December 3, 2026 [S3].

The measure amends one statute, RCW 28A.605.005, the "parents' bill of rights" [S5][S9]. Section 1 amends "RCW 28A.605.005 and 2025 c 369 s 501": it strikes the version the Legislature wrote in 2025 and puts back the list of rights enacted by Initiative 2081 [S5][S6]. The other sections say the act is to be "liberally construed," add a severability clause, and name it the "Washington Parents' Bill of Rights" [S5]. The act's title lists only "amending RCW 28A.605.005; and creating new sections" [S5].

The text's own intent subsection says it reinstates "Initiative 2081's laws guaranteeing parents' rights, including reviewing their children's instructional materials, accessing their school records, being notified when medical services are provided, and opting-out-of certain classes," and that "Voter approval of this initiative provides two years of constitutional protection from legislative interference" [S5].

## The laws involved: I-2081 (2024) and ESHB 1296 (2025)

- **I-2081.** Initiative 2081 was an initiative to the Legislature that lawmakers passed themselves in 2024: House 82-15 and Senate 49-0 on March 4, 2024, Chapter 4, Laws of 2024, effective June 6, 2024 [S8]. It listed rights of parents and legal guardians of public school children under 18 to examine instructional materials, get student records within 10 business days, receive specified notifications, and opt their child out of certain activities [S6].
- **ESHB 1296.** The 2025 amendments came in Engrossed Substitute House Bill 1296, Chapter 369, Laws of 2025: passed by the Senate 30-19 on April 11, 2025 and the House 59-39 on April 24, 2025, approved by Gov. Bob Ferguson May 20, 2025, effective May 20, 2025 [S7]. Section 501 of that act rewrote RCW 28A.605.005 [S7][S9]. This is the amendment IL26-001 repeals [S5][S6].
- **What ESHB 1296 changed in the list** (per the House Office of Program Research [S6]):
  - Added rights: a nondiscriminatory school setting, filing harassment/intimidation/bullying complaints, enrollment rights (military transfer, no legal residence), and language access notice.
  - Modified rights: classroom observation was added and access to materials made subject to local policies and procedures; records access aligned with the federal FERPA 45-day standard, with "education records" replacing "public school records"; the immediate notice of a child taken off campus became a right not to have the child removed without authorization, "subject to school campus removal laws"; criminal-action notice changed to "upon an allegation" and narrowed to school-related incidents or abuse, sexual misconduct or assault by staff; the opt-out right narrowed to surveys, analyses or evaluations covered by the federal Protection of Pupil Rights Amendment.
  - Eliminated rights: prior notice of medical services offered (except emergencies), notice of medical services or medications that could affect the family's insurance or copays, and notice of school-arranged treatment needing follow-up care after hours.
  - It also said nothing in the list creates a private right of action, and it broadened the records-withholding rule for parents under abuse or neglect investigation to health care, social work, counseling and disciplinary records and to criminal proceedings where the student is the named victim [S6].
- **Parts of ESHB 1296 IL26-001 does not touch.** ESHB 1296 also amended RCW 28A.642.010 (school nondiscrimination, including gender identity), added a student safety and privacy policy section to chapter 28A.320 RCW, added a statement of student rights (Part Two), a complaint and compliance process (Part Three) and employee retaliation protections (sec. 401), and amended RCW 28A.320.160 to require districts to "immediately notify" parents after a report that a student was the victim of abuse, sexual misconduct or assault by a school employee or contractor [S7 Parts One to Four, sec. 601]. IL26-001 amends only RCW 28A.605.005, so those sections stay in force [S5][S7].

## What the measure restores, provision by provision

Compared with current law, per the Attorney General's explanatory statement [S2] and the measure's text [S5]:

- **Materials.** Parents may "examine the textbooks, curriculum, and supplemental material used in their child's classroom" (sec. 1(3)(a)); the qualifier that access follows school policies and procedures is removed [S5][S2].
- **Records.** Copies within 10 business days of a written request instead of up to 45 days; no in-person requirement, without the current exception where the school cannot verify identity; no charge for electronic copies [S5 sec. 1(3)(b)][S2]. "Public school records" include medical or health records, records of any mental health counseling, vocational counseling, discipline, attendance, learning-challenge screening, IEP and section 504 plans, and "any other student-specific files, documents, or other materials that are maintained by the public school" [S5]. The exceptions for the record-maker's sole-possession notes and the statement that health-care disclosure law (chapter 70.02 RCW) controls health information are removed [S2].
- **Medical notification.** Three rights eliminated in 2025 return: prior notification when medical services are offered (emergencies excepted, with notice as soon as practicable), notification of services or medications that could affect health insurance payments or copays, and notification when the school arranges treatment that results in follow-up care after school hours [S5 sec. 1(3)(c)-(e)][S2][S6].
- **Crime and law enforcement.** "Immediate notification if a criminal action is deemed to have been committed against their child or by their child," without the current school-property and school-day limits; immediate notification if law enforcement questions the child, except where the parent is accused of abuse or neglect [S5 sec. 1(3)(f)-(g)][S2]. The listed right to immediate notice of a report of abuse or misconduct by a school employee or contractor is removed from this statute [S2]; the same duty remains in RCW 28A.320.160 as amended by ESHB 1296 sec. 601 [S7].
- **Removal from campus.** "Immediate notification if their child is taken or removed from the public school campus without parental permission, including to stay at a youth shelter or 'host home'" [S5 sec. 1(3)(h)][S2].
- **Nondiscrimination.** The current listed right to a school setting free of discrimination on the bases listed in chapters 28A.640 and 28A.642 RCW (including sexual orientation, gender expression and gender identity) is removed from this list and replaced by a right "to receive assurance their child's public school will not discriminate against their child based upon the sincerely held religious beliefs of the child's family" [S5 sec. 1(3)(i)][S2].
- **Opt-outs.** Written notice and opt-out of "any surveys, assignments, questionnaires, role-playing activities, recordings of their child, or other student engagements" that ask about the child's sexual experiences or attractions; family beliefs, morality, religion or political affiliations; mental health or psychological problems of the child or a family member; and all areas covered by the federal Protection of Pupil Rights Amendment [S5 sec. 1(3)(j)][S2]. The sex education opt-out is rewritten as notice and opt-out of "instruction on topics associated with sexual activity" under RCW 28A.300.475 [S5 sec. 1(3)(k)][S2].
- **Records during abuse investigations.** A school "shall not be required to release" a student's medical or health records or mental health counseling records to a parent who is the target of a pending child abuse or neglect investigation, unless the parent has a court order [S5 sec. 1(4)]. The current version covers health care, social work, counseling and disciplinary records and also applies to a parent who is the defendant in a criminal case where the student is the named victim [S7 sec. 501][S6].
- **Removed from the list.** The harassment/bullying complaint right, the enrollment, language access and special education rights, and the statement that the statute creates no private right of action [S2]. The Attorney General notes that the 2025 list "again restated many rights that already existed elsewhere in state and federal law" [S2].
- **Unchanged.** The calendar, required fees and dress code provisions [S2].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, written by Travis Reep (public school elementary teacher, elected union representative), Alesha Perkins (parent and education advocate), Peter Rosenkranz (Superintendent, La Center School District), Tony Harris (public school science/STEM teacher, elected union representative), Amy Sousa (women's rights advocate) and Jess Grant (Member, Democrats for an Informed Approach to Gender) [S4]:

- The restored rights include access to instructional materials and records, notice of medical services, opt-outs, and immediate notification when law enforcement questions a child or the child is removed from campus [S4].
- "School staff remain mandatory reporters of suspected child abuse or neglect," and "safeguards designed for children at risk should not become a default policy of withholding information from all parents" [S4].
- "Current policies allow schools to withhold critical information when a child expresses a new gender identity at school," and these policies "may require staff to conceal this information from parents" [S4]. (This is the statement's argument; the measure's text does not mention gender identity [S5].)
- Rebuttal: the measure "expressly states that schools 'shall not be required to release' mental health counseling records when a parent is the target of a child abuse or neglect investigation" and requires "immediate notification" if a criminal action is deemed committed against or by the child [S4].
- Let's Go Washington's response to the opposition: "Current law requires notification when the school receives a report alleging a crime against the child on school property, during the school day, or during a school-sponsored activity. I-001 removes those location and time restrictions and extends the provision to criminal acts committed either against or by the child." [S17]

## Arguments against

From the statement against, written by John Lovick (State Senator, Democrat, former Snohomish County Sheriff), Roshelle Cleland (Washington Coalition Against Domestic Violence and Sexual Assault), Justin Gill (President, Washington State Nurses Association), Karen Crowley (President, League of Women Voters of Washington), Courtney Normand (Washington State Director, Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates) and Jaime Fineman-McKinney (school counselor, Fife) [S4]:

- The measure would require "schools to hand over sensitive information to parents under criminal investigation for raping, abusing or otherwise harming their own child" [S4]. (Its text keeps a court-order requirement for medical, health and mental health counseling records while the parent is the target of an abuse or neglect investigation; it drops the current coverage of social work and disciplinary records and of criminal proceedings [S5][S7].)
- "An unintended consequence of IL26-001 is ending Washington's legal requirement that schools immediately notify parents when the crime is reported" [S4]. (For reports of abuse or misconduct by school employees, RCW 28A.320.160 keeps that duty [S7 sec. 601]; for other crimes, current RCW 28A.605.005 gives notice "upon receipt of a report" of an alleged crime on school property, which the measure replaces with immediate notice when a criminal action "is deemed to have been committed" [S9][S5].)
- It "discourages students facing domestic violence, sexual abuse, or bullying from seeking help from trusted teachers and school counselors, who would be required to release students' confidential records," and "forces schools to knowingly put LGBTQ+ kids in increased danger ... by outing them to potentially abusive families" [S4].
- Rebuttal: the measure is "bankrolled by ultra-conservative interests" and "would eliminate key existing parental rights" [S4].
- Gov. Bob Ferguson: "This initiative, no matter how hard the other side tries to spin it, actually weakens existing laws by removing parents' rights." [S17]

## Axis notes

- `parental-rights`: the measure's whole subject is the statutory list of parent rights in public schools. A yes vote restores broader access to curricula and records (10 business days, all student-specific files including counseling and health records), prior notice of school-offered medical services, and wider opt-outs from surveys, assignments and activities [S5][S2][S6]. This is the rubric's pole A ("Parental notification and opt-out ... broad opt-out rights"). The 2025 version it replaces kept more room for school policy, student health-record confidentiality under chapter 70.02 RCW, and records withholding in abuse cases, which is pole B ("student privacy and confidentiality in some health and counseling matters; narrower opt-outs") [S7][S9][S6].
- `social`: the text does not mention gender identity, sexual orientation, sports or reproductive policy except in one place. Section 1 strikes the 2025 subsection (2)(g), which listed a parent's right to a school setting where discrimination on bases including "sexual orientation, gender expression, gender identity" is prohibited "under chapters 28A.640 and 28A.642 RCW," and substitutes a right to assurance against discrimination based on the family's religious beliefs [S5][S2]. The prohibition itself is in RCW 28A.642.010, which ESHB 1296 sec. 103 amended and IL26-001 does not touch [S7][S5]. Gender identity enters the campaign through the arguments (the yes statement on disclosure of a new gender identity, the no statement on "outing" LGBTQ+ students) [S4], not through a provision on gender policy. Under the rubric split, this is not evidence on `social`.
- `local-control`: the 2025 version made classroom-access and materials rights subject to locally adopted policies and procedures; the measure removes that qualifier [S6][S2]. This is a narrow wording change inside a statewide rights list, not a shift of authority between state and district.
- No argument addresses taxes or spending; OFM finds no state fiscal impact [S3][S4].
