## What it does

Initiative Measure No. IL26-638 is an initiative to the Legislature on the November 3, 2026 statewide ballot. Its ballot title reads: "This measure would prohibit students it defines as 'biologically male' from competing in certain school athletic activities intended for female students only. It would require verification of biological sex by students' healthcare providers." [S1] The Legislature did not act on it during its 2026 regular session, so it goes to the voters [S6]. A supporting website says it was certified January 22, 2026 after supporters submitted 444,434 signatures [S18]. If approved, it takes effect 30 days after the election [S6]; OFM gives the effective date as December 3, 2026 [S3]. The text calls itself the "defending equity in interscholastic sports act" [S5].

**The rule (new section in chapter 28A.600 RCW, sec. 3(1)).** "Policies, procedures, rules, and other requirements adopted in accordance with RCW 28A.600.200 by a school district board of directors or a voluntary nonprofit entity must prohibit biologically male students from competing with and against female students in athletic activities with separate classifications for male and female students if the athletic activity is: (a) Intended for female students only; and (b) An individual or team competition activity." [S5]

**The verification (sec. 3(2)), verbatim.** "A student who elects to participate in individual or team competition activities intended for female students only governed under subsection (1) of this section shall provide, as part of the required routine sports physical examination, a health examination and consent form or other statement signed by the student's personal health care provider that verifies the student's biological sex, relying only on one or more of the following: The student's reproductive anatomy, genetic makeup, or normal endogenously produced testosterone levels." [S5]

- **Who must verify.** Every student who elects to compete in a female-only competition, not only students whose sex is questioned [S5][S2]. No comparable statement is required for boys' or mixed competition [S5][S13].
- **Undefined terms.** The text does not define "biological sex" or "biologically male"; it does define "knowingly" for the penalty rules in RCW 28A.600.200 [S5][S13].
- **How often.** The statement is part of "the required routine sports physical examination" [S5]. Under current WIAA rules that physical is required before the first practice and "shall be valid for 24 consecutive months" unless local rules are stricter [S7 Rule 18.4.0, 18.4.6].
- **Which schools.** The rule binds policies adopted under RCW 28A.600.200, the statute under which school district boards control interschool athletics and may delegate that control to the WIAA or another voluntary nonprofit entity [S5][S6]. The Attorney General says it would "affect public K-12 schools and also private schools that participate in the WIAA or similar nonprofit organizations" [S2]. The text sets no grade floor [S5].
- **Which sports.** Interschool individual or team competitions that have separate male and female classifications and are intended for female students only [S5][S6]. The Attorney General: "This would generally prevent transgender girls from participating on girls' sports teams." [S2] It says nothing about transgender boys on boys' teams [S5][S16].
- **Statutory changes.** Section 2 rewrites RCW 28A.600.200 into numbered subsections (governance, nondiscrimination, written rules, penalties, appeals) and adds that district and WIAA rules "must conform with section 3 of this act" [S5]. Section 4 amends RCW 28A.640.020 so athletics "shall be offered to all students without regard to sex, except as provided in section 3 of this act" [S5][S6]. The text does not amend chapter 28A.642 RCW or the Washington Law Against Discrimination [S5].
- **Enforcement.** The text creates no new penalty or enforcement office. Compliance runs through the district and WIAA policies it requires [S5]. Under existing law, OSPI enforces sex-discrimination rules in school athletics and may take corrective action, withhold state apportionment or impose other sanctions [S6].
- **Findings.** The text's findings say existing law and district policies already require a physical "which includes documentation of the student's sex assigned at birth" and that this "provides a reliable and medically verified basis to determine a student's biological sex" [S5]. The Washington State PTA says the WIAA forms contradict this: the clinician's examination form has no field for sex, and sex appears only on a history form the family fills in, which is not sent to the school [S13].

## Current policy it would override

- **WIAA Rule 18.16.0 (2026-27 Handbook).** "All students have the opportunity to participate in WIAA athletics and/or activities in a manner that is consistent with their gender identity. ... Athletes will participate in programs consistent with their gender identity or the gender most consistently expressed. School personnel responsible for student eligibility will work collaboratively with the student-athlete to determine eligibility." Once granted, eligibility lasts for the student's participation and need not be renewed each season [S7]. Rules 19.3.3.1 and 19.6.2.1 set an appeal process when a student is declared ineligible on the basis of gender identity [S7].
- **OSPI guidance.** Since 2012, OSPI guidelines have encouraged districts to let students take part in athletics according to their gender identity and refer to WIAA eligibility rules for sex-segregated competition [S6].
- **State law.** State education law and the WLAD prohibit school districts from discriminating based on sexual orientation, gender expression or gender identity [S6]. The Attorney General: OSPI's regulations "prohibit discrimination based on sex and gender identity" in school sports [S2].
- **WIAA's own vote.** In April 2026 a member amendment introduced by the Lynden School District, limiting girls' sports to students "whose biological sex is female," failed the 60% threshold for a second year [S8]. The WIAA said that even if passed it could not have been implemented because it conflicts with state law, and that it "will maintain its current policies unless state law changes" [S8].
- **Numbers.** The WSPTA says about ten transgender athletes are known among more than 200,000 students who compete statewide [S13]. The opposition campaign cites an estimate of 5 to 10, attributed to Superintendent Chris Reykdal and WIAA assistant executive director Justin Kesterson [S15]. The pamphlet rebuttal for the measure says "Washington competition results document at least 269 placements by male athletes competing in girls' interscholastic categories, including 74 firsts and 156 top-three finishes" [S4]. These figures come from advocates and were not checked against WIAA results.

## Legal context

- **Federal.** On June 30, 2026 the U.S. Supreme Court held, in West Virginia v. B. P. J. and Little v. Hecox, that "the States may maintain women's and girls' sports for biological females. They may determine eligibility for women's and girls' sports based on biological sex." [S9 slip op. p. 28] The holding permits such laws; it does not require them [S9]. The House Office of Program Research also summarizes Executive Order 14201 (February 5, 2025), which directs federal agencies to oppose male participation in women's sports and to review funding for programs that do not comply [S6].
- **State.** Washington's Equal Rights Amendment bars denial of rights "on account of sex" and does not define "sex"; the WLAD defines "sex" as "gender" [S6]. The opposition campaign says "lawyers say it discriminates against girls, making it unconstitutional in Washington," and that it "likely violates" the state ERA [S15]. No lawsuit challenging IL26-638 itself was found as of October 8, 2026; any challenge would come after enactment.
- **Pamphlet dispute.** The yes statement says verification would happen "during routine, medically necessary primary care recommended by the American Academy of Pediatrics" [S4]. The Seattle Times reported that the AAP asked Let's Go Washington to remove the reference because neither the national group nor its local chapter has taken a position, and that the campaign declined [S16].

## Fiscal impact (OFM)

- "The Office of the Superintendent of Public Instruction would incur minor costs to update current guidance. Local school districts may also incur costs to revise existing policies, procedures, rules, and other requirements, but this fiscal impact is indeterminate." [S3]
- OSPI: "a one-time cost of approximately $21,000 in SFY 2026 to update existing guidelines" [S3]. (OFM's own assumptions give an effective date of December 3, 2026, which falls in SFY 2027 under OFM's July-June definition; the statement's "SFY 2026" is recorded as printed.)
- "The initiative would not impact state or local revenue." [S3]
- OFM does not estimate costs to families. The WSPTA argues that genetic and hormone tests are unlikely to be covered by insurance for this purpose, so a reproductive-anatomy exam would be the most accessible option for most families, and that the personal-provider requirement may push families off school-arranged group physicals [S13].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, written by Alesha Perkins (parent and education advocate), Dori Whitford (high school track and cross country coach), Carol Brown (Olympic bronze medalist, rowing; board member, ICONS), Erica Li, MD (board-certified pediatrician), Ahnaleigh Wilson (student athlete) and Jess Grant (Member, Democrats for an Informed Approach to Gender) [S4]:

- "Vote yes to protect fairness in girls' sports"; it would affirm "the hard-fought rights established through Title IX in 1972" [S4].
- "Washington currently allows males to compete against girls"; the measure would align the state with federal law and the policies of the Women's Tennis Association, World Athletics and the International Olympic Committee [S4].
- Verification would be done by "a licensed medical professional ... based on the student's birth records, medical history and/or a standard, non-invasive physical examination" [S4]. (The text says the provider relies "only on one or more of" reproductive anatomy, genetic makeup or testosterone levels; it does not mention birth records [S5].)
- Rebuttal: the measure "doesn't require invasive or expensive exams"; "The initiative doesn't ban anyone from sports. It simply requires participation consistent with one's sex." [S4]
- Let's Go Washington spokesperson Hallie Herzberg: "We're talking about adding a checkbox back onto a form that doctors already fill out." [S16]

## Arguments against

From the statement against, written by Justin Gill (President, WA State Nurses Association), Courtney Normand (WA State Director, Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates), Karen Crowley (President, League of Women Voters of WA), Grace Shih, M.D. (family physician), Katie Johnson (retired school nurse) and Paul Charbonneau (high school girls' wrestling coach) [S4]:

- It "would require girls as young as 8 to undergo intrusive medical sex certification as a new part of sports physicals" and "bans parents from using birth certificates" [S4]. (The text lists only the three bases above [S5].)
- "Section 2 says doctors must subject girls to invasive examinations of their 'reproductive organs,' or expensive genetic or hormone tests. These requirements apply to girls only, boys are exempt." [S4] (The verification language is in section 3(2) of the text and says "reproductive anatomy" [S5].)
- "Many of Washington's 147,500 girls without a regular family doctor would be subject to genital examinations by total strangers." [S4]
- It is "the most extreme proposal of its kind in the U.S." and a "statewide blanket ban to kick 5 - 10 trans girls off school teams" that leaves "no room for case by case decisions" [S4].
- Gov. Bob Ferguson: "I-638 won't create opportunities for girls but it will force them into invasive sex verification exams in order to participate in sports." [S16]

## Axis notes

- `social`: the measure sets sex-based eligibility for girls' interscholastic sports and requires medical verification of biological sex for every girl entering a female-only competition [S1][S5]. The rubric names "Sex-based eligibility for girls' and women's sports" as pole A of `social` and "Trans participation in sports by gender identity" as pole B; current WIAA Rule 18.16.0 is a pole B policy [S7]. A yes vote is a direct choice of pole A.
- `local-control`: the opposition says the measure "takes decision-making power away from parents, schools and athletic associations" [S15], and the text does require district and WIAA rules to conform to it [S5]. But districts and the WIAA already operate under a statewide rule: the WIAA says it cannot adopt a sex-based eligibility rule because state law bars gender-identity discrimination, and OSPI guidance has steered districts to gender-identity participation since 2012 [S8][S6]. The measure replaces one statewide standard with another; it does not move the decision between local and state levels.
- `parental-rights`: the verification is a provider statement tied to the sports physical, not a parental notice or opt-out right [S5]. No evidence here for that axis.
- No argument addresses taxes; OFM's costs are a one-time $21,000 for OSPI plus indeterminate district costs [S3].
