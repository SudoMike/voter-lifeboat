## Background

Todd A. Bloom is a Tacoma tax and business attorney, CPA, financial planner and Navy veteran [S3][S8][S10]. His pamphlet statement reports more than two decades providing legal and professional services to companies, executives, business owners and families, and more than a decade as a tax attorney and senior manager at major accounting firms [S1]. The Spokesman-Review identifies those firms as "big four" firms including Deloitte and PwC [S8]. He served as a FINRA arbitrator from 1999 to 2020 [S1]. He holds CPA, CFA and CFP designations [S1].

He was in the Naval Reserve from 1997 to 2002 and was an active-duty Navy officer from 2002 to 2013, recalled after 9/11, and earned his commission from the enlisted ranks [S1][S8]. His campaign site says he retired as a Lieutenant Commander in 2018 and that his Navy roles included enforcing standards, overseeing disciplinary matters and serving as an investigating officer [S3]. Education: LL.M. in taxation from the University of Washington; J.D. and M.B.A. from Tulane; M.A. from the Naval War College; B.A. in economics from Simpson College; College of Naval Command & Staff [S1][S8].

He is admitted in Washington and Louisiana and before the U.S. District Courts for the Western District of Washington and the Eastern District of Louisiana and the U.S. Tax Court [S3]. He served on the Washington State Bar Association Board of Governors (District 6) for 2023–2026 and was re-elected to a second term running through September 2029 [S3][S11]. Other listed service: a Nonprofit Finance Council, adjunct faculty at UW and Tulane, and blood donation [S1].

**Prior campaigns:** He ran for the U.S. House in Washington's 6th Congressional District in 2016 and 2022, both times as a Republican [S8]. In the 2024 Supreme Court Position 2 primary he received 286,298 votes (16.3%) and did not advance [S13].

**Primary result (Aug 4, 2026):** Bloom finished second of four with 468,347 votes (27.20%), behind Debra Stephens (938,584; 54.50%), and advanced to the general election, per The Spokesman-Review's results table [S8]. News reports give his share as 27.1% to 27.5% [S7][S9]. The Secretary of State results site could not be loaded for this refresh, so the certified figures were not checked directly.

**Campaign website:** The general-election pamphlet lists only a phone number and a Gmail address [S1]. The campaign does run a website at electtoddbloom.com [S3].

## Positions

Judicial philosophy (stated):

- Says he is running because "judges should faithfully apply the law as written, uphold the Constitution," and give everyone a fair and impartial hearing [S3][S10].
- Describes his philosophy as deciding cases on "the Constitution, state statutes, and precedent," and says judges "best serve the public by exercising judicial restraint, respecting the separation of powers, and applying the law consistently and impartially" [S10].
- Says the role of judges "is not to make the law," but to defend the Constitution and see that laws are applied within constitutional limits [S4].
- On precedent: calls it "one of the cornerstones of our legal system," says it "is not absolute," and says any departure should have "a clear legal basis" [S10].
- Says the court should start from "What does that constitution actually say, and what do the court cases actually say?" rather than working back from values to an outcome [S14].
- Also says judges must consider how a ruling "is going to be received": "to think that the rulings you make are done in a vacuum is just a silly academic approach that is not in touch with reality" [S7].
- Says the court should "find a way to respect the will of the voters, at least in a greater degree than we're seeing today" [S7].
- Names former Chief Justice Gerry Alexander as a justice he admires, for his "commitment to the rule of law" and judicial temperament [S10].
- Pamphlet: offers "meaningful new perspective, experience, and common sense which will bring balance to the court," and promises to "uphold and apply the law impartially and fairly" [S1].

Comments on the court's decisions:

- **I-2066 (2026):** says Stephens's majority opinion striking the initiative is an example of the court finding ways "to uphold things that have been enacted or are favored by one political party" and "shut down" the other, which "gives us kind of a two-tier justice appearance." He said he was "sympathetic" to Justice Mungia's dissent but would "reserve judgment" because single-subject cases may return to the court [S7].
- **McCleary (2012):** questions whether the added school funding improved student achievement ("money is not the answer to every problem"). He says holding the Legislature in contempt and fining it $100,000 a day was "wrong for the court to overstep the boundaries of the separation of powers" [S7].
- **Quinn (capital gains tax):** says it was "wrongly decided" and that he "would have sided with the dissent" by Justice Gordon McCloud [S4]. The Seattle Times reported that both challengers it interviewed criticized Quinn and said the court tilts too far left and lacks judicial restraint [S11].
- **Blake (drug possession):** criticized the decision in the primary-season Q&A [S2]. He also said some court decisions "seem to undermine or undercut law enforcement" [S4].

Court administration and access:

- Names public confidence in the courts and timely access to justice as the system's largest challenges, and says delays burden "individuals, families, victims, businesses, and communities" [S10].

Criminal-justice formation: his career is in tax, business and securities law and FINRA arbitration. His Navy service included disciplinary matters and investigating-officer duties [S1][S3][S4]. No prosecution or criminal-defense practice is documented, and no positions on bail, diversion or therapeutic courts were found.

## Scoring notes

- **Experience:** a challenger with no judicial service. He offers FINRA arbitration, Navy disciplinary and investigative duties, and bar governance in place of time on the bench, and frames the race as a choice between the current justices and "someone new" [S1][S4][S7].
- **Judicial:** states a restraint and separation-of-powers philosophy ("apply the law as written," "judicial restraint"). He criticizes the McCleary contempt remedy, the Quinn majority and the I-2066 ruling. He also says judges should consider how rulings will be received and should give more weight to voter-approved measures [S4][S7][S10].
- **Safety:** no prosecution or defense formation and no documented bail, diversion or therapeutic-court positions. His criticism of Blake and comments about law enforcement concern how cases were decided and are not evidence on this axis under the judicial rules [S2][S4].
- **Resources and support:** carries the state Republican Party recommendation and has about one-fifth of his opponent's reported contributions. He has no endorsements from judges or newspapers on record [S5][S6][S11].
