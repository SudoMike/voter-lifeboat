## Background

Ian S. Birk, 47, of Seattle, is a judge on Division I of the Washington State Court of Appeals [S11][S20]. Governor Jay Inslee appointed him to the court in April 2022, and he ran unopposed to retain the seat in November 2022 [S6][S12]. He announced his Supreme Court candidacy on February 5, 2026, for the seat held by Justice Charles W. Johnson, who must retire under the state's age-75 limit [S11][S20].

Before joining the bench he spent about 20 years (2002-22) as a civil litigator in Seattle, including as a partner at Keller Rohrback, with a practice focused on insurance and consumer protection law [S6][S11]. He describes that practice as representing individuals, families, and small businesses "often against powerful corporate interests" in insurance, consumer protection, workplace discrimination, and complex civil cases [S1][S3]. He began his career as a law clerk to Washington Supreme Court Justice Susan Owens (2001-02) [S6][S11].

He volunteered for more than 10 years with the King County Bar Association Neighborhood Legal Clinics, providing free legal help to people who could not afford an attorney [S1][S6]. He is a board member of the Washington Courts Historical Society [S6]. He holds a BA and JD from the University of Washington [S1][S6].

Identity note: every source used here concerns the Court of Appeals judge (Keller Rohrback, Division I, appointed 2022). No source about the former Seattle police officer of the same name was used.

## Positions

Judicial philosophy and approach (as described by the candidate):

- Says he decides "cases based on the law and the facts—never politics, pressure, or popularity," with "careful attention to the record, respect for legal precedent, and thoughtful reasoning" [S5].
- Describes his method for novel legal questions (such as AI and digital privacy) as starting with "careful attention to statutory text, constitutional principles, and precedent," while identifying where "new interpretations must be developed carefully" [S7].
- Told the Spokesman-Review the state constitution is "more important now than it ever has" been, and that opinions should be written accessibly because "The law belongs to the people" [S11].
- Says he sees judging as "an inherently non-political thing" [S13].
- Campaign frames the goal as courts that are "independent, fair, and grounded in the real lives of the people we serve" [S2].
- Says judges should "speak honestly about the ways the system has fallen short, particularly its disproportionate impact on communities of color and people of limited means" [S7].

Criminal-justice formation:

- His professional background is civil litigation and appellate judging; no prosecution or public-defense employment appears in his official bio or campaign materials [S3][S6].
- Reports attending a National Judicial College seminar on anti-racist courtroom practices and "reducing disparity through nontraditional diversion" and says he intends to bring that to his work [S7]. No sourced record of bail or diversion rulings was found.

Court administration and access to justice:

- Says he would have the Supreme Court "slow down its rulemaking process" and work with the Legislature on implementing the court's caseload standards [S13].
- Says AI is "not close to" generating legal work product and favors treating AI as several distinct issues rather than one [S11][S13].
- Emphasizes equal access to justice and "fair outcomes" across his career as a lawyer, pro bono volunteer, and judge [S1][S5][S20].

## Record

- Has authored more than 200 appellate opinions across civil, criminal, family, administrative, and constitutional law, and has sat in all three divisions of the Court of Appeals (per campaign and official bio) [S3][S6].
- Authored the published Division One opinion in No. 87005-0-I (March 2026) holding that the Attorney General's Office has statutory authority under the charitable trust act to subpoena records from the Archdiocese of Seattle concerning clergy sexual abuse. The opinion held the subpoena enforceable for evidence of criminal or tortious acts but not to examine whether expenditures served the church's religious purposes, citing federal and state religion clauses, and remanded for the superior court to draw the line [S8][S9]. The court reversed a 2024 superior-court ruling that had quashed the subpoena [S9].
- Lists among his opinions: SEIU Healthcare 1199NW v. Snohomish County Public Hospital District No. 1 (union enforcement of benefit plans outside arbitration), Summers v. Sea Mar Community Health Centers (consumer class actions), Flying T Ranch v. Stillaguamish Tribe (tribal sovereign immunity), Skinner v. InCity Properties Holdings (tenant rights after an unlawful eviction), and State v. Wagner (hate-crime offense) [S7]. These are the candidate's own descriptions; only the archdiocese opinion was read directly for this dossier [S8].
- In Flying T Ranch, the Washington Supreme Court (Oct. 9, 2025) affirmed the Court of Appeals' holding that tribal sovereign immunity has no immovable-property exception absent a clear waiver by Congress or the tribe [S10]. The Supreme Court opinion does not name the Court of Appeals author [S10].

## Campaign finance and conduct

- PDC contributions data (receipts through Oct. 1, 2026) show about $500,900 in net contributions across 657 records; reported expenditures through Aug. 31, 2026 total about $92,000 [S19]. The Spokesman-Review candidate profile reports about $500,900 raised as of Oct. 3 [S12]. Reported donors include attorneys from personal-injury firms, including 16 contributors from Pfau Cochran Vertetis Amala [S11].
- In September 2026 his campaign sent fundraising emails describing O'Donnell as "a Republican-affiliated judge" and asked him to accept or disavow the state Republican Party's recommendation [S14][S15]. Sixty-two current and former judges signed a letter calling the allegations false [S14]. His campaign said voters "deserve transparency" about political support [S14]. The Seattle Times editorial board argued the emails conflicted with Code of Judicial Conduct Canon 4 [S15].

## Scoring notes

- Experience: the only candidate with appellate judicial experience (four years on the Court of Appeals, 200+ authored opinions); his pamphlet frames this as "statewide judicial experience" [S1][S6]. He came to the bench by gubernatorial appointment, then retained the seat unopposed [S6].
- Judicial philosophy: describes a text-, precedent- and record-focused method, and also states the state constitution is "more important now than it ever has" been and that courts should acknowledge disparate impacts [S5][S7][S11]. The archdiocese opinion applied both statutory text and state and federal religion-clause limits [S8]. The evidence does not place him clearly at either pole.
- Safety: formation is civil plaintiff-side litigation, not prosecution or defense; there is no documented bail or diversion record [S3][S6].
- Endorsement profile: most sitting justices, labor, and Democratic organizations; the race has been shaped by his campaign's emails about his opponent's Republican recommendation [S1][S4][S14][S15].
