## What the office does

The Washington State Supreme Court is the state's highest court. Its nine justices are elected statewide in nonpartisan races to six-year terms [S1][S2]. Position 4 is a full six-year term [S1]. Five of the nine seats are on the 2026 ballot, which the Spokesman-Review calls unprecedented in state history [S2]. Two new justices will join the court after this election [S6].

## Why the seat is open

Justice Charles W. Johnson has held Position 4 since 1991. He must retire under the state's mandatory retirement age of 75, which makes this an open seat [S2][S3][S13]. Neither candidate is an incumbent on the Supreme Court.

## Primary

Only two candidates filed for Position 4, so the race was not on the August 4, 2026 primary ballot and both candidates advance directly to the November general [S2][S3][S4]. VoteWA lists both as finalists for the general election [S5].

## Race dynamics

- Two sitting judges at different levels are running. Ian Birk, 47, has been a Division I Court of Appeals judge since an April 2022 appointment by Gov. Jay Inslee, after 20 years as a civil litigator [S2][S11]. Sean O'Donnell, 54, has been a King County Superior Court judge since 2013, after 12 years as a King County prosecutor [S2][S12]. The Spokesman-Review frames the race as trial judge versus appellate judge [S2].
- Fundraising is close. PDC contributions data show net receipts of about $500,900 for Birk (through Oct. 1) and about $422,500 for O'Donnell (through Oct. 3, plus a $5,000 candidate loan). Reported spending through Aug. 31 was about $92,000 for Birk and about $65,400 for O'Donnell [S10]. Birk's reported donors include plaintiff-side law firms; O'Donnell's include business PACs such as AGC and NFIB [S2].
- Endorsements split along recognizable lines. Birk lists most sitting justices, the Washington State Labor Council, and Democratic organizations [S1][S2]. O'Donnell lists former Gov. Gregoire, U.S. Reps. Adam Smith and Marilyn Strickland, the state Republican Party, 15 county prosecutors, police and sheriffs, Farm Bureau, and the Seattle Times [S1][S2]. The Stranger endorsed Birk [S4].
- The partisan-label dispute: in September, Birk's campaign emails called O'Donnell "a Republican-affiliated judge" and demanded he accept or disavow the state GOP recommendation [S7][S8]. Sixty-two current and former judges signed a letter calling the allegations false [S7]. The Seattle Times editorial board argued the emails conflicted with the Code of Judicial Conduct [S8]. O'Donnell declined to disavow, citing his candidate obligations and support from both parties [S7].
- A televised debate was scheduled for Oct. 9 on TVW [S6].

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

- **Type of judicial experience (`experience`).** Birk has four years and 200+ authored opinions as an appellate judge [S11]. O'Donnell has about 13 years as a trial judge with administrative leadership (chief criminal and family judge, Superior Court Judges' Association president) and pro tem Supreme Court service [S1][S12]. Neither is a sitting justice. Birk reached his current bench by appointment and O'Donnell by election [S11][S12].
- **Professional formation (`safety`).** O'Donnell is a former prosecutor (Green River Task Force, the state's first human trafficking case) who emphasizes victims' rights and firearm surrender in protection-order cases [S1][S12]. Birk's background is plaintiff-side civil litigation and pro bono legal clinics; his official bio and pamphlet list no prosecution or criminal-defense practice [S1][S11]. Neither candidate has documented bail or diversion rulings in the sources reviewed.
- **Judicial philosophy (`judicial`).** Birk calls judging "an inherently non-political thing" [S6] and emphasizes the state constitution's growing importance and accessible opinions [S2]; O'Donnell describes balancing the legal framework with "the spirit of the law... how this affects people" [S2]. The stated differences are modest; much of both candidates' language is boilerplate.
- **Bar evaluations.** The King County Bar Association rated O'Donnell "Exceptionally Well Qualified" and lists Birk as "N/A" [S9]. Washington Women Lawyers rated Birk "Exceptionally Well Qualified" [S14]. Birk's pamphlet statement also claims a Cardozo Society "Exceptionally Well Qualified" rating, while O'Donnell's cites "Exceptionally Well Qualified" ratings from "multiple bar associations" without naming them [S1].
- Party endorsements are recorded as facts only. Per the rules for this nonpartisan office, they are not evidence of judicial philosophy.
