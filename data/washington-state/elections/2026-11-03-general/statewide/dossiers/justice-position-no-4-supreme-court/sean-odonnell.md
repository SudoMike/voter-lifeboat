## Background

Sean P. O'Donnell, 54, of Seattle, has been a King County Superior Court judge since 2013; the court's official bio says he was "Elected to the King County Superior Court bench in 2013" [S6][S7]. He has served on the court's family law, civil, and criminal dockets and has been Chief Criminal Judge and Chief Unified Family Court Judge [S6]. His court contact listing shows courtroom ITA 1, and his campaign describes him as lead judge of the King County Involuntary Treatment Act Court at Harborview [S3][S6]; the pamphlet calls this role "mental health court lead judge" [S1]. He has also served as the county's Special Inquiry Judge and as a Supreme Court justice pro tem in select cases [S1][S3][S7]. He announced his Supreme Court candidacy on February 6, 2026 [S17]. The Spokesman-Review describes this as his first electoral race [S7][S8].

From 2001 to 2013 he was a senior deputy prosecutor in the King County Prosecuting Attorney's Office [S6][S7]. During that time he served on the Green River Task Force that prosecuted Gary L. Ridgway and tried the state's first human trafficking case [S1][S3][S6]. He was also a Special Assistant U.S. Attorney in the Western District of Washington [S1][S3]. Before law school he worked as a legislative aide on Capitol Hill [S7].

His peers elected him president of the Superior Court Judges' Association; the campaign cites a 2017 SCJA presidency [S1][S3][S5]. He chairs the Board for Judicial Administration Courthouse Security Committee and serves on WSBA and Supreme Court AI task forces [S3]. He was named the King County Bar Association's 2004 Outstanding Lawyer of the Year and the Washington chapter of the American Board of Trial Advocates' 2018 Judge of the Year [S3][S17]. He holds a BA from Georgetown University and a JD (cum laude) from Seattle University School of Law, where he is adjunct faculty [S1][S3].

## Positions

Judicial philosophy and approach (as described by the candidate):

- Says his decisions begin with "the Constitution, the laws enacted by the Legislature, and the legal precedent," and that interpretation must consider "its effect on people, families, and communities" [S4].
- Told the Spokesman-Review that alongside the legal framework, "The spirit of the law is the other side...how this affects people" [S7].
- Said in a radio interview: "My job is to serve in this job, not to infuse it with the worldview" [S11].
- Lists campaign values including "Upholding our State Constitution which guarantees our civil rights, individual freedoms, and our fundamental democratic rights" [S2].
- Says voters should weigh "My experience, my impartiality, and my ability to have a vision and work with others" [S9].

Criminal-justice formation (documented):

- Twelve years as a prosecutor, including the Green River Task Force and the state's first human trafficking prosecution [S1][S6].
- Says judges can "honor victims' rights while vigorously protecting defendants' constitutional guarantees," and that victims' perspectives are often missing from reform discussions [S4]. His pamphlet says he has "centered crime victims' voices" [S1].
- Pretrial release: campaign says he co-chaired a statewide task force on pretrial release reform, advocated for "improved pre-trial release decisions," and obtained funding for a text-reminder program to reduce failures to appear [S3][S4]. No specific bail rulings were found in news coverage.
- Therapeutic courts: his ITA court role concerns involuntary civil commitment [S3][S6]. No sourced evidence was found of him presiding over a diversion or therapeutic court such as drug court.
- As Chief Family Law Judge he helped start a civil protection order court; his pamphlet says it led to "hundreds of firearms being surrendered" by people who committed domestic violence [S1][S3]. His campaign says his ruling in the Montesi case upheld the constitutionality of the state's weapons-surrender statute [S2]. This dossier did not confirm his role in Montesi from an independent source.

Court administration and access to justice:

- Calls for firm AI guardrails, including rules barring judges from using AI to decide cases [S7][S9].
- Supports reviving the limited license legal technician program, discontinued in 2023, to address civil legal affordability [S7].
- Says the Supreme Court cut public-defender caseloads "without a plan how to make it work" [S9].
- Chairs statewide court-security work and helped draft rules for remote jury trials during COVID-19 [S3][S5].

## Record

- King County Superior Court judge since 2013 (Chief Criminal Judge; Chief Unified Family Court Judge; Special Inquiry Judge; ITA court) [S3][S6].
- Supreme Court justice pro tem in select cases [S1][S7]. No specific pro tem opinions were identified.
- Campaign credits him with the Montesi ruling upholding the weapons-surrender statute and with establishing King County's civil protection order court [S1][S2].

## Scoring notes

- Experience: a trial-court judge for nearly 14 years with leadership roles (chief criminal and family judge, SCJA president), versus an opponent with four years of appellate experience. He frames this as operational and consensus-building experience [S1][S6][S7].
- Safety: prosecution formation (12 years, Green River Task Force); emphasizes victims' rights and firearm surrender in protection orders; campaign-reported pretrial-release work centers on reducing failures to appear [S1][S3][S4]. No documented diversion or therapeutic-court rulings were found.
- Judicial philosophy: statements combine a text/statute/precedent starting point with attention to "how this affects people," plus a disclaimer of infusing a "worldview." This is largely boilerplate and does not place him clearly at either pole [S4][S7][S11].
- Endorsement profile: cross-partisan (Gregoire, Smith, Strickland, the state Republican Party, law enforcement, business and agriculture groups) and the Seattle Times, versus labor and most sitting justices for Birk [S1][S7]. Per nonpartisan-race rules, party endorsements are listed as facts only.
