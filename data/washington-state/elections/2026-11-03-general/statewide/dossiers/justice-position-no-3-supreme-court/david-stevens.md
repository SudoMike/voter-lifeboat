## Background

David Stevens (PDC filer name David Stevens y Perez), 64, of Shelton, is a Mason County Superior Court judge [S1][S5][S8]. He was elected to that court in November 2023, defeating an incumbent appointed by Governor Jay Inslee, and was re-elected in 2024 [S4][S8]. He served in the U.S. Navy from 1983 to 1989, then attended the University of Washington on veterans' benefits (BA; JD from the UW School of Law) and holds a State Department certificate in Islamic jurisprudence [S1][S8]. Before law school he was a federal civil rights investigator with the Fair Housing Enforcement Center in Seattle (HUD) [S1][S4][S8]. His legal career spans prosecution and defense: Spokane County deputy prosecutor (2001-2010, the office's repeat-offender prosecutor for fraud and property cases), federal prosecutor in Sacramento, U.S. State Department justice adviser in Bamiyan, Afghanistan, international (EU) prosecutor in Pristina, Kosovo, federal public defender, and chief public defender for the Colville Confederated Tribes, before returning to prosecution in Okanogan and Mason counties [S1][S8]. He ran unsuccessfully for Spokane County prosecutor around 2010 [S8]. As a defense attorney he represented Gail Gerlach, who was acquitted of manslaughter by a Spokane jury in 2014 [S8]. This is his first run for the Supreme Court [S8].

## Positions

**Judicial philosophy and approach**
- Describes himself as a textualist and originalist [S8]. His site says "The Washington State Constitution should be interpreted based on its text and original meaning and not on evolving societal standards" and that "The Supreme Court is a constitutional court, not a policy-making body" [S3].
- "I believe that the courts are inherently conservative, and I don't mean that in a political sense. I mean that it's not for the courts to lead the way. That's what the Legislature and the governor do. We just weigh things against the Constitution" [S8]. After the primary he said, "If the constitution says you can't do something, then you just can't do it" [S10].
- Pamphlet: "Public policy is the province of the political branches and not the courts" [S1].
- Says constitutional change must come through formal amendment rather than judicial reinterpretation, and that the Supreme Court uses bar regulation to pursue policy objectives that belong to the Legislature [S3].
- Pointed to the court's 2021 Blake decision (striking down the drug-possession statute) as an example of precedent being overturned poorly, while saying the statute may not have been constitutional [S9].

**Appointment vs. election**
- Argues that gubernatorial appointment of most judges departs from the state constitution's design of electing judges and "leads to ideological and political decisions"; says his election "will bring common sense and balance" to the court [S1][S4].
- Said the current justices are "fundamentally changing the criminal justice system in Washington state, and I think this is because they're ideologically driven," citing proposed bail and juvenile-case changes [S9].

**Criminal procedure and court rules**
- Criticizes Supreme Court rule changes restricting juvenile warrants and tightening in-custody arraignment deadlines, and the court's jury-selection rules addressing racial bias, which he says require courts "to treat jurors differently based on their race" [S3][S10].
- Supports trial judges' ability to issue warrants for defendants who fail to appear [S3].
- His main stated issue is court rulemaking and criminal rulings; he says the mental health sentencing alternative allows defendants who qualify to be routed to treatment instead of serving firearm-enhancement prison time, undercutting "hard time for armed crime" [S8].
- KUOW reported he also cited the 2023 Quinn decision upholding the capital gains tax among the court actions that prompted his run [S10].

## Record

- Has tried more than 200 jury trials as a lawyer and judge [S1][S3][S8].
- Sitting Mason County Superior Court judge since his 2023 election; no published rulings by Judge Stevens were identified in the sources reviewed [S4][S8].

## Endorsements

- Washington Council of Police and Sheriffs (WACOPS), the only endorsement listed on his campaign site, and the Council of Police Political Support (contributor) [S8].
- Recommended by the Washington State Republican Party and the Spokane County Republican Party [S8]; the primary dossier also recorded King County and Snohomish County Republican party recommendations [S2].
- Pamphlet cites support from "tribes, judges, public defenders, prosecutors, attorneys, and law enforcement" without naming them [S1].
- Contributors named in news coverage include George Petrie (Goodman Real Estate), former state Sen. Mike Padden, and the Mason County Republican Women's Club [S8].
- Editorial positions: in the primary, the Seattle Times editorial board endorsed Mike Diaz, describing Stevens as backed by Republicans [S11]. No general-election newspaper editorial endorsement for this race could be retrieved from the outlet's own site.
- Bar ratings: as of 2026-10-08 the King County Bar Association ratings page lists him as "N/A" ("not been rated by the KCBA Judicial Candidate Evaluation Committee within the last three years") [S12].

## Campaign finance and primary result

- PDC campaign finance summary (record updated 2026-10-05): $74,433.50 in contributions, $40,445.81 in expenditures, no debts; $49,719.30 in independent expenditures supporting him and $16,000 against [S5]. The Spokesman-Review reported about $62,200 raised as of Oct. 3, including about $1,400 he loaned his campaign [S8]. KUOW reported about $50,000 in outside spending for him from Concerned Taxpayers of Washington State as of early August [S10].
- Aug. 4 primary (certified Aug. 21, 2026): Stevens finished first with 612,267 votes (35.29%), ahead of Jaime Hawk (33.37%) and Mike Diaz (31.34%) [S6][S7]. He topped the vote in Spokane County [S8]. Coverage noted that the Democratic-backed vote was split between Hawk and Diaz in the primary [S8].

## Scoring notes

- Judicial (`judicial`): the race's explicit textualist/originalist, with repeated statements that courts should not "lead the way" or make policy and that the constitution should not be read by "evolving societal standards"; Hawk describes a text-then-intent method and a cautious but open standard for overturning precedent [S3][S8][S9].
- Safety (`safety`): formation includes roughly a decade as a state prosecutor plus federal and international prosecution, alongside public-defender work; on the record he criticizes the court's juvenile-warrant, arraignment-deadline, and bail-related rule changes and the mental health sentencing alternative as applied to firearm enhancements, and supports failure-to-appear warrants. Hawk's formation is public defense and the ACLU [S1][S3][S8][S9][S10].
- Experience (`experience`): reached the bench by defeating an appointed incumbent and makes opposition to the appointment pipeline central to his campaign, against Hawk's appointment-then-election path and her endorsements from the sitting governor and most current justices [S1][S4][S8][S9].
- Placed first in the primary but trails Hawk about four to one in campaign-committee fundraising, partly offset by independent expenditures [S5][S7][S8].
- Party organizations endorse on both sides of this nonpartisan race (Republican parties for Stevens; Democratic organizations for Hawk); these endorsements are listed as facts only [S8].
