## Background

Jaime Michelle Hawk, 49, of Federal Way, is a King County Superior Court judge; the court's judge page lists her in Department 10, courtroom 3H, at the Kent courthouse with a Unified Family Court assignment [S5][S9]. Governor Jay Inslee appointed her to the King County Superior Court in July 2022, and she was subsequently elected [S9][S11]. She earned her undergraduate degree (1999) and law degree (2004) at Gonzaga University [S9]. Her career before the bench: attorney fellow on Senator Ted Kennedy's U.S. Senate Judiciary Committee staff; juvenile public defender in Grant County; federal public defender in Spokane for Eastern Washington and Idaho; clerk for a federal judge in Yakima; civil-rights class-action litigation in Seattle; and Legal Strategy Director for the ACLU of Washington's Smart Justice campaign (2015) [S3][S9]. She describes herself as the first ACLU of Washington attorney to become a judge [S1][S2]. She has taught as an adjunct professor at Gonzaga University School of Law and serves on the Washington Supreme Court's Minority and Justice Commission [S1][S9]. Raised by a single mother, she was the first in her family to attend college, and before law school she worked supporting domestic violence survivors [S1]. Her longtime partner is state Treasurer Mike Pellicciotti [S9]. This is her first run for the Supreme Court [S9].

## Positions

**Judicial philosophy and approach**
- Says her philosophy is "grounded in strengthening public trust and confidence in our courts and faithfully applying the rule of law, so judges decide cases based on our Constitution, our statutes … precedent or case law … not personal views or policy preferences" [S9].
- Says her approach starts from the text, then looks to legislative intent where the text is ambiguous, with an emphasis on clarity in how opinions explain the law [S9].
- At fall forums she said "overturning precedent should be approached very cautiously and carefully, and has to meet those thresholds of incorrect and or wrongfully decided and harmful," and pointed to recent decisions overturning "harmful and incorrect" precedents in the wake of the racial justice movement as examples [S10].
- In its primary endorsement of Mike Diaz, the Seattle Times editorial board wrote that "Hawk's vague answers to the board and endorsement list suggests she's more likely to veer out of the judicial branch and into the lane of legislative-like policymaking than her opponents" (the board's characterization, from the primary) [S12].

**Court administration and access to justice**
- Supports court funding, higher juror pay (most Washington jurors are paid $10 a day, a rate she says has not changed since 1959 and which she says is a barrier to representative juries), and representation for family-law litigants, noting that roughly half of litigants lack a lawyer [S9][S10].
- Supports expanding traveling sessions of the Supreme Court to make it more visible and accessible statewide [S9].
- Campaign emphasizes court reform to eliminate bias and improve accessibility for working families [S2].
- Says she will bring the perspective of women and working families to the court [S1].

**Criminal-justice formation**
- Pre-bench career was on the defense and civil-liberties side: state and federal public defender handling trials and appeals for indigent clients, then ACLU of Washington [S1][S3][S9]. No documented statements on bail, diversion, or therapeutic courts were found in the sources reviewed [S2][S3][S9][S10].

## Record

- Has presided over hundreds of criminal, civil, and family law cases, as well as appeals from lower courts; her pamphlet states she has never had a trial overturned for legal error [S1][S3].
- Senators Murray and Cantwell appointed her to their federal judicial selection committee; the American Bar Association selected her to observe Guantanamo Bay military hearings and later to assist families separated at the border [S1][S9].
- No published rulings by Judge Hawk were identified in the sources reviewed.

## Scoring notes

- Judicial (`judicial`): states a text-first, then legislative-intent method and a cautious-but-available standard for overturning precedent ("incorrect and or wrongfully decided and harmful"), citing recent precedent reversals tied to racial-justice concerns as legitimate examples; this contrasts with Stevens' self-described textualist/originalist approach and his view that "it's not for the courts to lead the way" [S9][S10]. Much of her other framing ("rule of law," "not personal views") is boilerplate [S9].
- Safety (`safety`): professional formation is defense and civil-liberties (public defender, ACLU); no documented bail, diversion, or therapeutic-court positions found. Stevens has both prosecution and defense experience and has publicly criticized recent court rule changes on criminal procedure [S3][S9][S10][S11].
- Experience (`experience`): trial judge since a 2022 gubernatorial appointment, then elected; endorsed by the sitting governor and most of the current Supreme Court. Stevens runs explicitly against the appointment pipeline [S4][S9][S10].
- Leads in fundraising roughly four to one over Stevens' campaign committee, though Stevens has more independent-expenditure support [S6][S9].
- Party organizations endorse on both sides of this nonpartisan race (Democratic organizations for Hawk; Republican parties for Stevens); these endorsements are listed as facts only [S4][S9].
