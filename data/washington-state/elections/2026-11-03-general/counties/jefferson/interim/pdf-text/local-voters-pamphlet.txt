13 Journey of your Ballot Your ballot is removed from the envelope and security sleeve by trained sta ff . This is where your votes are separated from your identity. The ballot is then visually inspected to make sure it is able to be scanned. Your ballot is scanned and an image is stored on a secure and closed system until 8 p.m. on Election Night. Our mail sorting machine scans your ballot envelope and sends an image of your signature to be verified. Right after 8 p.m. on Election Night, we tabulate results and post them on our website for all to see. *Results are not official until the election is certified. Signature Verified The signature on your voter r egistration record is compared to the image of the signature from your ballot envelope. If the signatures match, your ballot moves on to be opened. If your signature is missing o r doesn’t match your registration record, the ballot will be challenged. We will contact you to resolve the issue before the ballot is opened. You can return your ballot to an official ballot drop box, 24/7, until Election Day at 8 p.m. sharp. If returning your ballot by mail, USPS recommends mailing it at least one week before Election Day. Jefferson County Elections mails ballots to all registered voters around 20 days prior to Election Day. 14 Bonds To Rebuild Forks Middle School The Board of Directors of Quillayute Valley School District No. 402 adopted Resolution No. 10-25/26, concerning bonds to provide safe, modern facilities to enhance student learning. This proposition would authorize the District to: construct a new middle school to replace Forks Middle School (including modern, flexible learning spaces; secure, single- point entrance; efficient parking and pick-up/drop-off area to improve student and pedestrian safety and traffic flow; full-size gym and cafeteria for student and community use); issue $34,000,000 of general obligation bonds maturing within 25 years; and levy annual excess property taxes to repay the bonds, all as provided in resolution No. 10-25/26. Should this proposition be: □ Approved □ Rejected Explanatory Statement: Proposition 1 authorizes school construction bonds to rebuild Forks Middle School. Proposition 1 will replace aging and educationally outdated school facilities, improve student, staff and community safety and security, and improve and expand educational opportunities and programs. Proposition 1 allows Quillayute Valley School District to construct a new middle school to replace three existing buildings at the Forks Middle School site, which may include: (1) modern, flexible learning spaces; (2) a secure, single-point access main entrance with entry/ access controls; (3) efficient parking and pick-up/drop-off area to improve student and pedestrian safety and traffic flow; (4) full-size gymnasium and cafeteria for student and community use, including dedicated access to maintain secure separation from student learning areas; (5) outdoor play spaces designed to meet the needs of middle school students; and (6) other facility and infrastructure improvements to the middle school site. Further information at: www.qvschools.org/ page/facilityprojects. The $34,000,000 of general obligation bonds will be repaid from annual excess property taxes sufficient to pay bond debt service over a maximum term of 25 years. Exemptions from taxes may be available, contact your county assessor. Statement written by: Lee Marchisio Bond Council for School District “For” Statement: No committee was formed. “Against” Statement: No committee was formed. 15 Local Measure - For and Against Statements Clallam County Fire Protection District No. 1 | Proposition No. 1 Resolution Providing For The Submission Of Levy The Board of Clallam County Fire Protection District No. 1 adopted Resolution No. 26-001 concerning a proposition to maintain and adequately fund District operations. This proposition would fund fire protection and emergency medical services by establishing the District’s existing regular property tax levy in 2026 at $1.00 per $1,000.00 of assessed valuation and authorize annual adjustments for the nine succeeding years based on the greater of one percent or the 12-month West Region CPI-U reported in June of each levy year. The maximum allowable levy in 2035 shall serve as the base for subsequent levy limitations. Should this proposition be: □ Approved □ Rejected Explanatory Statement: Clallam County Fire Protection District No. 1 provides emergency fire protection and medical services to its citizens. Fire protection districts are authorized by law to levy at the rate of $1.50 per thousand of assessed value. If approved by the voters, this proposition will authorize the District to establish its existing regular property tax levy rate at $1.00 per thousand of assessed valuation for collection in 2027 and will allow the District to keep pace with inflation by establishing a consumer price index based annual growth factor each year for the following nine years, subject to statutory limits. At the end of the ten-year period, absent further voter approval, the fire department’s tax levy will be subject to statutory one percent limit. The Board of Commissioners has determined that the tax levy and the revenue it will produce is necessary to maintain the current level of fire protection, fire suppression and emergency medical services while providing for the safety of our firefighters. The levy is subject to the property tax exemptions provided for qualifying seniors, veterans and others under chapter 84.36 RCW. Statement prepared by: Brian K.
