## Background

- Chimacum farmer and business owner; moved to Jefferson County in 1996 and founded Dig It landscape excavating [S4][S6].
- Co-founder and operations director of Finnriver Farm & Cidery and founder of Chimacum Valley Grainery (since 2020), which grows, mills, malts, bakes and brews organic grain [S1][S4][S6].
- Grew up on a dryland wheat farm in Eastern Washington; environmental science degree from the University of Colorado Boulder; field science instructor in Yosemite; wind and solar design certificate; EMT training with East Jefferson Fire Rescue [S1][S4][S6].
- Community service: Port of Port Townsend's Short's Farm steering committee; WSU Bread Lab variety-trial volunteer [S1].
- No prior elected office [S1].
- Raised $10,325.90 and spent $4,820.37 as of the PDC report updated Oct. 1, 2026 [S8].

## Positions

### Rates and affordability

- Priorities: keep rates "stable and transparent," strengthen the grid and ratepayers' resiliency, and extend broadband to rural and under-served areas [S3].
- Named affordability one of his three top priorities, covering monthly bills and connection costs for affordable housing; noted the PUD's reduced capacity charge for qualifying affordable housing and its low-income rate program, which he wants to make more accessible [S7].
- Pamphlet: "strategic infrastructure investments, innovative policies, clean energy technologies, and careful budgeting" [S1].

### Power supply and climate

- Compared the county's reliance on the Bonneville Power Administration to a "250-mile extension cord from the Columbia River to our homes" and supports developing local sources, wind, solar and battery storage [S7].
- Cited a PUD grant to study a hydro generator in the Lords Lake-to-Port Townsend water line, and wants conservation and efficiency, from weatherization to smart appliances that cut peak demand [S7].
- On data centers: the Olympic Peninsula may not suit them (cost of power, seismic risk), "But I don't want to just push the problem into somebody else's backyard" [S7].

### Water

- Emphasized conservation; noted the PUD owns Peterson Lake as a possible backup water source; would analyze well capacities before deepening wells [S7].

### Growth

- Said density in urban growth areas is more efficient and affordable for the PUD than sprawl, needing fewer miles of wire [S7].

## Endorsements

- Organizations shown on his campaign site: Jefferson County Democrats and Washington Conservation Action [S3][S5].
- Individual endorsers include County Commissioner Heidi Eisenhour and a retired IBEW lineman [S5].
