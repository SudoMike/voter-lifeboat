## What it does

Proposition No. 1 is a multi-year levy lid lift for Clallam County Fire Protection District No. 1, the Forks-area fire district [S1][S2][S4]. It would set the district's regular property tax levy at $1.00 per $1,000 of assessed value for collection in 2027, and for each of the next nine years let the levy grow by the greater of 1% or the 12-month West Region CPI-U instead of the usual 1% limit; the maximum allowable 2035 levy becomes the base for later limits [S1][S2].

- Fire districts may levy up to $1.50 per $1,000 [S1].
- After the ten-year period, absent another vote, the levy returns to the statutory 1% growth limit [S1].
- Exemptions for qualifying seniors, veterans and others under chapter 84.36 RCW apply [S1].
- **A small part of the district is in Jefferson County.** The pamphlet notes it [S1], and the county's Elections page calls it "Clallam/Jefferson County Fire District No. 1" [S3]. The Jefferson part is a small area south of Forks that includes Dowans Creek Road; DOR's tax data number it Jefferson "Fire Dist #9" [S5][S6].

## Cost

- **Current rate:** for taxes due in 2025 the district levied $0.47059 per $1,000 in both counties. The Jefferson part has $5,659,457 of assessed value and paid $2,663; the Clallam part, $629,687,997 and $296,325 [S5].
- **Proposed rate:** $1.00 per $1,000 for 2027 collection [S1].
- **Computed for this dossier from [S1] and [S5]:** on a $300,000 property the district tax would go from about $141 a year to $300 in 2027; at 2025 values the Jefferson part would pay about $5,660 a year instead of $2,663. The CPI factor then lets the levy grow faster than 1% a year through 2035.

## Background

- The district says it is funded mainly by property taxes, that inflation has exceeded 1% for years, and that costs have risen faster than revenue [S4].
- The commissioners say the levy is needed "to maintain the current level of fire protection, fire suppression and emergency medical services while providing for the safety of our firefighters" [S1].

## Arguments for

- No one filed a statement for the measure; the pamphlet says no committee was formed [S1][S2].
- The district's case: keep current fire and EMS service and firefighter safety, and keep up with inflation after years of 1% limits [S1][S4].

## Arguments against

- No one filed a statement against the measure; the pamphlet says no committee was formed [S1][S2].
- No organized opposition was found [S4][S7].

## Endorsements and campaign

- No PDC committee for or against the measure was found [S7].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote more than doubles the district's regular levy rate, from about $0.47 to $1.00 per $1,000, and swaps the 1% growth limit for a CPI-based limit for nine years [S1][S5]. It stays within the $1.50 statutory cap, the case for +1; the size of the increase and the escalator put it at +2.
- `spending`: the district frames the money as maintaining current service; no mapping.
