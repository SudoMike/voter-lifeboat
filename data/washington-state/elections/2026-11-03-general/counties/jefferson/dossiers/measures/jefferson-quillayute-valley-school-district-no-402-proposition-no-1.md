## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would let Quillayute Valley School District No. 402 (Forks) issue $34,000,000 in general obligation bonds maturing within 25 years, repaid by annual excess property taxes, to build a new middle school replacing Forks Middle School [S1][S2].

- The new school would replace three existing buildings on the site and may include flexible learning spaces, a single secure main entrance, a reworked parking and drop-off area, a full-size gym and cafeteria for student and community use, and outdoor play space [S1][S2].
- News coverage describes students walking outdoors between four separate buildings [S3][S4][S5]; the ballot materials say three buildings would be replaced [S1].
- The school was built in 1952; the district says it does not meet current safety, seismic and energy codes and that renovating would trigger code upgrades throughout, so replacement is more cost-effective [S3][S4][S5].
- **The district reaches into Jefferson County.** Its Jefferson part is in the West End (for example along Dowans Creek Road and the Upper Hoh Road near Forks), so those Jefferson voters vote on it along with the Clallam County part [S2][S9].
- School bonds need at least 60% yes votes and a minimum turnout to pass [S8].

## Cost

- The ballot materials give no tax rate [S1][S2].
- **District estimate:** $1.94 per $1,000 of assessed value, almost $680 a year (about $56 a month) on a $350,000 home [S3][S4].
- **Timing:** the new bond would phase in during 2028 as the Forks High School bond is paid off at the end of 2028 [S3][S4].
- **Current bond rate:** for taxes due in 2025 the district's bond levy was $1.34127 per $1,000, the same rate in both counties; on the Jefferson side it raised $36,676 on $27,344,159 of assessed value, against $1,012,999 on $755,252,878 in Clallam [S6]. The Jefferson part is about 3.5% of the district's assessed value (computed from [S6]).
- **"Will not increase the tax level."** In March, board chair Bill Rohde said the bond "would be a replacement for the outgoing Forks High School Bond and will not increase the tax level" [S5]. The district's later $1.94 estimate is above the outgoing bond's 2025 rate of $1.34; no source reviewed explains the difference (comparison computed from [S3], [S4] and [S6]).
- **State money and timber revenue:** passing in November 2026 qualifies the district for about $21 million in state construction assistance, dropping to about $17 million if delayed to 2027; the district must have a voter-approved bond to collect timber sales revenue, about $9 million from 2008 to 2025 [S4]. In March the figure was given as about $22 million [S5].

## Background

- The board voted unanimously on July 28, 2026 to place the bond on the ballot [S4]. A predesign committee of parents, staff, community members and business owners began meeting in September 2025 and surveyed the community in spring 2026 [S4][S5].
- Problems the committee listed include multiple campus entrances, drop-off traffic on Spartan Avenue, roofing and drainage at the end of their useful life, limited classroom electrical capacity, and an electric heating system more than 37 years old [S5].

## Arguments for

- No one filed a statement for the measure; the pamphlet says no committee was formed [S1][S2].
- The district's case: safety under one roof with a single secure entrance, code compliance, lower long-run cost than remodeling, and capturing state matching money and timber revenue [S3][S4][S5].
