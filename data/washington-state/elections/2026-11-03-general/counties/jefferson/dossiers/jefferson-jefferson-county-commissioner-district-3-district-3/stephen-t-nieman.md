## Background

- Port Ludlow resident since building his home there in 2005; originally from Wisconsin, moved to the Northwest in 1987; he and his wife raised four children [S3][S8].
- Construction manager and consultant for about 30 years; now works mainly on construction disputes and litigation [S1][S8]. Lists contractor registrations in Washington and California, a Hawaii RME license, forensic roof inspection, a Washington private investigator license (armed), drone pilot and FEMA contractor credentials [S1].
- Community service: neighborhood emergency-management block captain, 2016-2025 [S1][S8].
- The only Republican in the field; says the Jefferson County Republican Party asked him to run in 2022 (he declined) and again in spring 2026 [S3][S8]. No prior elected office [S1].
- Finished second in the District-3-only primary with 1,624 votes (32.0%) [S11].
- Raised $6,772.34 and spent $2,506.75 as of the PDC report updated Sept. 21, 2026 [S12].

## Positions

### Taxes and budget

- Pamphlet: "The biggest challenge facing Jefferson County is balancing the budget without further tax increases" [S1].
- Says federal COVID-era and state money "helped mask serious deficiencies in the Jefferson County budgeting process" and that the county "must face reality and make hard budget decisions which may mean cuts in some services or making them more efficient" [S1].
- Wants programs and departments that keep "producing the same ineffective results" to face "measurable change, stronger oversight, and a renewed focus on what works"; cites the county's new waste-recycling program as one to review on results [S1].
- His infrastructure plan rests on "Growth Pays For Growth": "Existing taxpayers should not bear the cost of infrastructure required by new development" [S4].
- Criticizes "a growing tendency toward expanding government oversight, increasing reliance on grants and public programs, and building a larger administrative structure to manage that dependence" [S3].

### Housing and growth

- Wants "a thorough review of permitting requirements" to lower the cost of new housing [S1].
- Proposes "Workforce Townhome Villages" of 40-75 homes for households at 80-140% of area median income, with a developer equity stake (for example $70,000 of a $350,000 home) that stays attached to the home on resale, and community infrastructure districts financed by connection charges, assessments, latecomer agreements, developers and state grants [S4][S5].
- Says "Developers Must Benefit" and calls the program "a market-oriented public-private partnership model" [S4].
- Cited Habitat for Humanity's Mason Street neighborhood in Port Hadlock as a model at the Sept. 30 forum [S10].

### Land use and the Critical Areas Ordinance

- Describes "a religious-like zeal to an unusually restrictive Critical Areas Ordinance" as one reason he is running [S3].
- His CAO page argues the county ordinance goes beyond Growth Management Act requirements and proposes more buffer flexibility, thresholds below which special reports are not required, shared burden of proof, and "compliance or approved mitigation plan" in place of "full compliance" [S7].

### Economy

- Top priority after the primary: a stronger forest economy, plus opportunities for working families, small-scale specialty agriculture and the Port of Port Townsend's maritime trades [S9].
- Argues the county is "increasingly dependent on retirees, transfer payments, external wealth, tourism spending, and a shrinking working-age population" and produces too few family-wage jobs [S6].
- Wants to replace what he calls a "protectionist mindset" with "this attitude of expansion" and to involve more local businesses in the county fair [S10].

### Transportation

- Proposes mobility hubs, shared-ride feeder service and on-demand transit for seniors and people with disabilities, instead of people walking narrow road shoulders to bus stops; "Now this is not a prototypical conservative position, but I just don't care" [S3][S5][S8].
- "The ultimate goal ... is to get cars off the road" [S10].

### Water and data centers

- Asked about the county's water supply, said he would need more information before answering and did not want "hypothetical ideas and fear" to drive decisions [S10].
- Not enthusiastic about a local data center but said he might consider one that came with an offer of county infrastructure support [S10].

### Climate

- In explaining why he is running, he describes growing up amid "apocalyptic expectations" in which "climate fears filled the news," and the toll he says such narratives took on young people [S3].

### Immigration enforcement

- Opposed an ICE detention center in the county ("I don't like that stuff"); said immigration policy must be set by legislation, that he proposed legislation to the GOP to protect immigrant labor in Washington, and that businesses profiting from immigrant labor "need to pony up"; also voiced concern about cartels [S10].

### Parents and schools

- No position on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs was found.

## Endorsements

- No endorsements were found beyond the Jefferson County Republican Party's request that he run [S3][S8].

## Scoring notes

- Puts "no further tax increases" and possible service cuts at the center of his platform, with stronger oversight of programs that do not deliver results [S1].
- Market-oriented housing plan with developer equity stakes and "growth pays for growth" infrastructure finance [S4].
- Seeks to change what he calls an entrenched county governing philosophy, including the Critical Areas Ordinance [S3][S7].
- First run for office; construction management background [S1][S8].
- Breaks with some party positions on transit and immigrant labor [S8][S10].
