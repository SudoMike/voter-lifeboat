## Background

- Third-generation Jefferson County resident who grew up on Eaglemount and lives in Port Ludlow; runs as a Democrat [S1][S3][S7].
- 4-H coordinator for the Washington State University Extension office in Jefferson County. She was earlier the office's finance administrator, which she says taught her how county budgets, financial software and grant systems work [S3][S8].
- Former executive director of the Sequim-Dungeness Valley Chamber of Commerce, during the COVID-19 pandemic [S1][S7]. Managed the City of Port Townsend's Mountain View Pool for 20 years and began as a preschool teacher [S1][S8].
- Environmental science degree from the University of Hawaii; post-graduate work in public administration and organizational development [S1][S8].
- Elected experience: Precinct Committee Officer [S1]. Serves on the Jefferson County Fairgrounds Association board (vice chair of the fair board, per [S10]) and lists more than 20 years in salmon restoration [S1][S10].
- Her father, Al Scalf, worked on the county roads crew and later directed the county's Department of Community Development [S8].
- Finished first in the four-way, District-3-only primary: 1,853 votes (36.5%) to Stephen T. Nieman's 1,624 (32.0%) [S11].
- Raised $21,855 and spent $17,359.67 as of the PDC report updated Oct. 7, 2026 [S12].

## Positions

### Budget and county finances

- Calls the lack of a long-range financial plan for the county its central problem and says building one would be her top priority: "live within our means while we grow the tax base" [S3][S4][S9].
- Proposes requiring every county department to report how it spends its money and what that delivers, extending the impact reports she writes for 4-H [S6].
- Says the county should stretch every dollar through efficiency and partnership before asking residents to pay more (campaign account of the June 25 Port Ludlow forum) [S6].
- Pamphlet: "spending wisely, planning for rainy days, and fighting for our fair share will be critical in the coming years" [S1].

### Housing, permitting and growth

- Wants permitting simple enough for small businesses and homeowners to "walk in, get it, and walk out" [S3][S4].
- At the Sept. 30 forum said the county could adopt stock building plans to reduce costs, and favored reducing car dependence through "village-like communities" with co-located services [S10].
- Lists improving housing and infrastructure among her goals [S1].

### Natural resources, water and data centers

- Pledges to protect public lands, working waterfronts, small farms, waters and wildlife [S3][S4].
- Called the system carrying water to Port Townsend "incredibly fragile," wants it modernized, and supports contingency plans such as reservoirs and working with large users like the Port Townsend Paper Company on efficiency [S10].
- On a local data center: "My knee-jerk reaction is hell no, and I'll stand by that" [S10].

### Public safety, emergency preparedness and health care

- Concerned about wildfire prevention and one-road-in, one-road-out access in rural areas; wants an emergency-preparedness plan for Port Ludlow [S4][S6].
- Wants better access to health care in the rural parts of the county [S4][S8].

### Immigration enforcement

- Opposed a hypothetical ICE detention center in the county and said she would use her "privilege" to protect immigrant neighbors; described people who no longer join public activities "because of fear" [S10].

### Governance

- Emphasizes collaborative leadership, listening and civic engagement; criticized the notice given for a proposed North Bay cell tower as the legal minimum rather than real engagement [S3][S4][S6].
- Wants county decision-making to move "away from a personality based advocacy system to one that relies on systems and documents, institutional memory" [S6].

### Parents and schools

- No position on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs was found.

## Endorsements

- Outgoing District 3 Commissioner Greg Brotherton; former District 3 commissioners Kathleen Kler and John Austin; former District 2 commissioners David Sullivan and Glen Huntingford; former Auditor Rose Ann Carroll; former Clerk Ruth Gordon; Port of Port Townsend Commissioner Carol Hasse [S1][S5][S6].
- Organizations shown on her endorsement page: 24th LD Democrats, Washington Education Association PAC, National Women's Political Caucus [S5].
- At the Sept. 30 forum she said she has endorsements from five current and former county commissioners [S10].

## Scoring notes

- Frames the race around long-range financial planning and department-level spending transparency, while also calling for investment in families, housing, health care access and infrastructure [S1][S3][S6].
- First run for countywide office, backed by the outgoing commissioner and three predecessors; stresses continuity and institutional knowledge [S5][S6].
- Favors simpler permitting and stock plans to reduce housing costs [S3][S10].
- Stronger than her opponent in opposing a local data center and an ICE detention facility [S10].
