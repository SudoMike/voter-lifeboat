## What this office does

The District 3 commissioner is one of three members of the Board of County Commissioners, which oversees county government, adopts the annual budget, sets land-use policy, approves major infrastructure projects and contracts, and helps direct public health, parks and other county services; the term is four years [S2]. District 3 covers Discovery Bay, Port Ludlow, Quilcene, Brinnon and the West End [S3]. The candidates are nominated by District 3 voters in the primary and elected by the whole county in November [S4][S5][S6].

## Race dynamics

- Open seat: Commissioner Greg Brotherton is not seeking re-election [S2].
- In the four-way primary (District 3 voters only), Democrat Anji Scalf led with 1,853 votes (36.5%), Republican Stephen T. Nieman had 1,624 (32.0%), Democrat Jean Ball 1,219 (24.0%) and Democrat Christopher Hannon 376 [S4].
- Both finalists live in Port Ludlow. Scalf is a WSU Extension 4-H coordinator and former Sequim chamber director backed by the outgoing commissioner and three predecessors; Nieman is a construction manager and consultant making his first run, at the Jefferson County Republican Party's invitation [S1][S3].
- Both call county finances the central issue. Scalf proposes a long-range financial plan and department-level spending reports; Nieman wants to balance the budget "without further tax increases," possibly with service cuts [S1].
- At the Sept. 30 League of Women Voters forum they differed most on a local data center (Scalf flatly opposed, Nieman open with infrastructure support) and on water supply planning; both favored less car dependence and opposed an ICE detention center [S7].

## Differentiating issue axes

- `taxes` and `spending`: Nieman's explicit no-new-taxes and oversight platform versus Scalf's planning-and-transparency approach that also calls for investment.
- `reform`: Nieman seeks to change an entrenched county governing philosophy and the Critical Areas Ordinance; Scalf stresses continuity and institutional knowledge.
- `experience`: neither has held county office; Scalf has the backing of past commissioners, Nieman runs as an outsider.
- `housing`: both favor simpler permitting; Nieman's plan is market-oriented with developer equity stakes.
