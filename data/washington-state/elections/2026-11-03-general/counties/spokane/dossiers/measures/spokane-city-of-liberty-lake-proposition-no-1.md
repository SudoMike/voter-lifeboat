## What it does

Proposition 1 asks City of Liberty Lake voters whether the city should impose a sales and use tax of one-tenth of one percent "for the purpose of funding public safety and criminal justice services pursuant to RCW 82.14.450" [S1]. The explanatory statement says the tax would let the city "continue providing public safety and criminal justice services consistent with the current level of service as the city continues growing faster than most cities in Washington" [S1]. The ballot title sets no expiration date [S1].

The Spokesman-Review reports that the city council voted 6-0 on March 17, 2026 to send the measure to voters [S3]. Mayor Cris Kaminskas told the paper the money "is strictly dedicated to public safety" and would fund "things directly related to the Liberty Lake Police Department, whether it's people, equipment, anything with regards to the public safety department" [S3].

The city's question-and-answer page says the money would be directed toward [S2]:
- "15 more uniformed officers to maintain 2 officers per 1,000 population" at a projected population of 23,000;
- "$8.7 million in facility improvements to accommodate more officers and evidence handling," citing the city's Facility Master Plan;
- "Advanced emergency management capacity to handle ever-increasing wildfire threats."

Councilman Dan Dunne told the Spokesman-Review that the $750,000 "would reasonably cover at least one and possibly two additional officers" [S3].

**Statute.** RCW 82.14.450 lets a city impose up to 0.1% with majority voter approval, says one-third of the money "must be used solely for criminal justice purposes, fire protection purposes, or both," and requires a city to send 15% of its proceeds to the county and keep 85% [S5]. Neither the pamphlet nor the city page says whether the $750,000 estimate is before or after the county's 15% share [S1][S2].

## Cost

- The rate is one-tenth of one percent, "10 cents on a $100 purchase" [S1].
- The city projects "an estimated $750,000 in annual revenue" [S1][S2].
- The city says approval "would increase the sales tax rate in the city of Liberty Lake from 9.0% to 9.1%" [S2]. The Department of Revenue's Q4 2026 table lists Liberty Lake (location code 3212) at a combined 9.0% (local 2.5% plus state 6.5%) [S4].
- The city says the tax "excludes motor vehicles and most food items" [S2]. RCW 82.14.450 exempts motor vehicle sales and leases of up to 36 months [S5].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This is a new tax, not a renewal. The pamphlet asks whether the city should "impose" it, and no existing Liberty Lake tax under RCW 82.14.450 is mentioned in the pamphlet, the city page or the news coverage [S1][S2][S3].

The city's case is about growth and costs, not a funding gap from an expiring source:
- Police service calls rose from 4,900 to 14,800 since 2020 [S2].
- The city says public safety costs "have increased 2 times faster than all other operating expenses" [S2].
- It says its population grew 20% since 2020, "a higher rate than 267 of Washington's 281 cities" [S2].
- Dunne said "Public safety is well over 50% of the city's budget" [S3].
- Councilwoman Linda Ball said city growth, "not an uptick in crime," is the main reason for the request [S3].
- Kaminskas said police salaries fell behind after officers left a Teamsters contract about four years ago and formed their own guild, and the city has spent four years catching up [S3].

**Related statewide context.** The Spokesman-Review reports that a 2025 state law lets a city council adopt a further 0.1% public safety sales tax without a public vote only if voters had recently approved a public safety sales tax, as Spokane voters did in 2024, and that Spokane County officials are considering the same tax [S6]. No source found says Liberty Lake plans such a step.

## Arguments for

The statement in favor (no individual author is printed; the "For" committee contact is Kathryn Lynn Atkins) says [S1]:
- "Liberty Lake is growing, and public safety must grow with it."
- "Public safety operating costs have increased significantly over the past four years," and relying on the general fund alone "is not a sustainable long-term solution."
- "Everyone who makes taxable purchases in Liberty Lake contributes—including visitors and shoppers—not just our homeowners."
- Dedicated funding "helps ensure officers have the personnel, equipment, and technology needed."

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed: "After repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement against the ballot measure" [S1]. No organized opposition was found in news coverage [S3].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote creates a new 0.1% sales and use tax with no stated expiration, raising the combined rate from 9.0% to 9.1% and an estimated $750,000 a year [S1][S2][S4]. The King precedent for new public safety sales taxes (Milton, SeaTac) is +2.
- `spending`: the city describes keeping the current level of service as the city grows, with a longer-term goal of 15 more officers and $8.7 million in facilities [S1][S2]. The money is new revenue for expanded staffing, but no source ties the measure to a specific spending plan, so no spending mapping is made.
- `safety`: the measure funds police staffing, but the rubric does not apply the `safety` axis to measures; no mapping.
