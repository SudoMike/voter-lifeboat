## What it does

Proposition No. 2 would replace Cheney School District's capital levy, which expires in 2027, with a three-year capital levy collected in 2028-2030 [S1]. The board adopted Resolution No. 28-25-26 [S1]. The money would pay for three kinds of work [S1]:

1. Acquiring, installing and modernizing instructional technology equipment, infrastructure and systems.
2. Security cameras, entry and access controls, door locks and other safety systems.
3. Other safety infrastructure, "including bleachers, locker rooms, heating and cooling systems and equipment, electrical, plumbing, roofing, lighting and fencing."

The district's levy page says it "replaces an expiring levy to continue funding for" technology upgrades, security improvements, facility infrastructure, furniture and equipment, and implementation and ongoing support [S2]. Superintendent Ben Ferney said the money goes to technology such as security cameras and replacement Chromebooks, and to smaller capital expenses such as a new roof, carpeting or parts of HVAC systems [S4]. Most of the 2024 bond went to the new Skyway Elementary in Airway Heights. Ferney said there is "always a component of capital levy that's needed to maintain buildings" [S3]. The board voted unanimously to send it to the ballot [S3]. Passage requires a simple majority [S3].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

**The expiring levy.** In November 2023 the board sent a capital levy to the February 2024 ballot that would "renew to 9 cents per $1,000," about $2.3 million over three years [S5]. Cheney's levies passed in February 2024 [S6]. No source found gives the rate actually collected.

**How the new levy compares.**
- The new levy is larger than the expiring one's 2023 estimate: about $4.1 million versus $2.3 million over three years, and $0.10-$0.20 versus $0.09 per $1,000. That comparison is computed for this dossier [S1][S3][S5].
- In dollars per $1,000 the change is small. The district projects the combined school tax rate, with both 2026 levies and the 2024 bond, to fall from $3.93 to $3.90 per $1,000 [S3][S4].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, by the Committee for Great Schools (Meridth Lemelin, Nicole Williams, Amy Pruett) [S1]:

- "This is a replacement levy, not a new tax" [S1].
- It funds technology "such as digital whiteboards," plus code compliance, roof repairs, HVAC, fire alarm systems, security cameras, access controls and door locks [S1].
- "Failure to pass Proposition 2 would result in deferred maintenance and would only cost more money down the road" [S1].
