## What it does

Proposition No. 2 would authorize Spokane County Fire Protection District No. 9 to issue "$70,000,000 of general obligation bonds maturing within a maximum of 20 years" and to levy annual excess property taxes to repay them [S1]. The money would "renovate, modernize and construct fire stations and facilities and replace a fire station, construct a training/classroom building, and make capital improvements" [S1].

- The board adopted Resolution No. 26-9 [S1]. The first reading was June 23, 2026 [S3], and the board adopted it unanimously on July 14, 2026 (Commissioner Anderson moved, Commissioner Bennett seconded) [S4].
- Section 2 of the resolution lists the projects [S2]:
  - Renovate and improve four fire stations.
  - Build a fire station to replace "an inadequate and outdated fire station."
  - Build a training and classroom building with public meeting space.
  - Renovate, expand and improve the maintenance and logistics building.
  - Other capital improvements in the district's 2026 Capital Facilities Plan.
- The resolution says total project costs "are estimated to be more than $70,000,000" and will be paid from bond proceeds, other district funds and other sources [S2]. It bars using bond proceeds for replacing equipment [S2]. If projects become impractical, the board may move proceeds to other capital projects or to paying the bonds, after a public hearing [S2].
- The June 23 minutes say the bonds are intended for "replacing Station 99, building a training classroom facility and renovating/adding to the existing fleet and facilities building" [S3].
- Chief Matthew Vinci told the Spokesman-Review that relocating Station 99, the district's busiest station, is the bond's "No. 1 focus." He said Station 99 was a house bought about 20 years ago and retrofitted as a temporary station. The other four career stations would get new HVAC, electrical and alerting systems, fitness space, roof repairs, and single bedrooms in place of bunk rooms. The goal is to finish the projects within six years [S6]. The Spokesman-Review describes both measures as covering part of the district's "more-than $100 million to-do list" [S6].
- The district's newsletter says its facilities have not seen "significant improvements" in more than 30 years [S5].
- Bonds need 60% approval [S6]. The pamphlet does not state the threshold [S1].

## Cost

- The pamphlet estimates the excess levy at "$0.39/$1,000 of assessed valuation (approximately $196/year, or $16.34/month, for a $500,000 residential property)" [S1][S6].
- Bonds of up to $70,000,000, repaid over up to 20 years from issuance [S1]. Total interest cost is not stated in the pamphlet or resolution [S1][S2].
- This is in addition to Proposition 1 (levy lid lift), which the district says would add $423 a year on a $500,000 property in 2027 [S6]. The district's newsletter instead describes $423 as the "combined additional cost of Propositions 1 and 2" [S5]; the pamphlet figures imply about $619 for both together (see the Proposition 1 dossier).

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This is a new bond levy. No source found describes an existing district bond levy that it would replace [S1][S2][S6]. The district's other property taxes are its regular fire levy (about $0.65 per $1,000) and a voter-approved maintenance and operations levy of about $1.49 per $1,000 for 2027 [S6][S7].

## Arguments for

No statement for was filed: "After repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement for the ballot measure" [S1]. The district's case, from its materials:
- Stations "are no longer adequate to meet the evolving public safety needs of the community" [S1].
- "Our team spends 48 hours and sometimes 72 hours in these stations... It's their home away from home" (Vinci) [S6].
- "After more than three decades without significant facility improvements, it is time to address these needs" [S5].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed [S1]. Vinci acknowledged the two propositions are a "big ask" [S6].
