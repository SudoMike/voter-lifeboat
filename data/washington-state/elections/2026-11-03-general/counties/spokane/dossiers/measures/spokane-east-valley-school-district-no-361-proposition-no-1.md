## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would replace East Valley School District's capital levy, which the explanatory statement says expires at the end of 2026, with a two-year capital levy collected in 2027 and 2028 [S1]. The board adopted Resolution No. 2848 [S1]. The money would pay for district-wide work in four areas [S1]:

1. Safety, security and communication systems.
2. Parking and traffic improvements.
3. Plumbing, HVAC, roof and electrical replacements.
4. Educational technology, cybersecurity and online safety.

The board found these improvements "required due to safety and security concerns, educationally outdated school facilities, deteriorating infrastructure," and the condition of technology equipment [S1].

Superintendent Mandi Rehn said a roof repair and replacement at one school was estimated at about $6 million, most of the first year's collection [S2]. "We didn't get to that 60% threshold on either attempt, so we need to keep the buildings functional while we work on the next steps," she said [S2]. Passage requires a simple majority [S2].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

**The expiring levy.** The ballot title calls this a replacement of an expiring levy that ends in 2026 [S1]. In January and April 2026 the Spokesman-Review reported that "an existing 74 cents per thousand tax levy from the school is set to expire" [S3][S4]. If that is the levy being replaced, the rate rises from about $0.74 to an estimated $0.99 per $1,000, an increase computed for this dossier.

**Conflicting report.** An August 2026 Spokesman-Review article says East Valley's first capital levy passed in 2018 and "expired four years ago" [S2]. That conflicts with the ballot title's statement that a capital levy expires at the end of 2026 [S1]. The sources found do not settle which levy the 74 cents refers to or what was collected under it. The ballot title is treated as authoritative on the replacement point.

**Background: failed bonds.**
- The district's $220 million bond to replace its 64-year-old high school and 57-year-old middle school failed twice in 2026: 54.6% yes in February and about 49.7% on election night in April. Bonds need 60% [S5].
- East Valley's last successful bond passed in February 1996 [S5]. Since then the district has relied on capital levies for repairs and small projects [S4][S5].
- Board member Carolyn Petersen said a November capital levy would pay for "Band-Aid" and "duct tape" repairs [S5].

## Arguments for

No statement for the measure was submitted: "After repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement for the ballot measure" [S1].

In news coverage, Rehn cited aging buildings, roofs "in bad shape" and HVAC needs, and said "security is always our No. 1 concern" [S2].

## Arguments against

No statement against was submitted [S1].

Spokane County Republican Party chair Rob Linebarger said the party stands by a resolution not to support any new taxes for five years. He said he sympathizes with the district's need [S2]. The party had endorsed the 2026 bond [S3].

## Endorsements and campaign

The Spokane County Democrats' 2024 platform supports majority votes for education bonds and levies. The party did not comment on this measure [S2]. No committee specific to this levy was identified.

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote replaces an expiring levy, according to the ballot title [S1]. Reporting puts the expiring school levy at 74 cents, so the estimated rate rises about 25 cents, to $0.99 [S1][S3]. It is a replacement levy with a rate increase, comparable in size to other replacement levies scored +1.
- `spending`: deferred maintenance and safety repairs to existing buildings [S1][S2]. No program expansion.
