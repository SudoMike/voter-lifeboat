## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would renew Spokane County Fire Protection District No. 2's regular emergency medical services (EMS) property tax levy of "$.50 per $1,000.00 of assessed valuation for a period of six consecutive years to be collected beginning in 2027" [S1]. The board adopted Resolution 2026-4 [S1]. The explanatory statement calls it a "replacement measure" that "will allow the District to maintain the level of EMS services it is currently providing to its residents" [S1].

The district covers 144 square miles around Fairfield and is all-volunteer [S2]. Fire Chief Eric Olson told the Spokesman-Review: "It is a renewal. We've been running this same levy for years and years now" [S2]. The district runs its own ambulance; Olson said "We were the first fire district-based transport in Spokane County" [S2]. He said levy money pays for medical equipment and supplies and, with diesel prices rising, "Definitely part of that EMS levy will be used for diesel" [S2]. If approved, the levy runs through the end of 2032 [S2].

## Cost

- $0.50 per $1,000 of assessed value a year for six years, collected 2027 through 2032 [S1][S2]. On a $300,000 home that is $150 a year (computed for this dossier from the stated rate).
- The current EMS levy also collects 50 cents per $1,000 [S2].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- The expiring levy was approved in the August 2020 primary: $0.50 per $1,000 "for six consecutive years beginning in 2020 for collection in 2021," for Basic Life Support services, CPR training, education, support services, training and equipment [S3]. It passed with 74.55% (123 yes, 42 no) [S4].
- The current levy expires at the end of 2026 [S2].
- Under RCW 84.52.069, a district may levy up to 50 cents per $1,000 for EMS for six or ten years or permanently. The first imposition of a six-year levy needs a three-fifths supermajority with a turnout minimum, but "subsequent approval" of a six-year levy needs only a simple majority [S5]. Because this measure continues an existing six-year levy, the simple-majority rule appears to apply; the pamphlet does not state the threshold [S1][S5].

## Arguments for

The statement in favor was submitted by the "Renew EMS Levy" committee (Steven Braun, David DeGon, Stephen Primmer, Connie Reinhardt) [S1]. It says:
- "The levy is a continuation of the current funding sources rather than the creation of a new tax."
- The money "is dedicated exclusively to EMS" and helps "maintain the district's current local ambulance operations, support emergency medical personnel, replace and maintain medical equipment."
- Without approval, "the district loses this dedicated source of EMS funding" and would need to decide how to cover the shortfall [S1].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed: "After repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement against the ballot measure" [S1].

## Endorsements and campaign

The pamphlet names the "Renew EMS Levy" committee [S1]. No endorsements were found.
