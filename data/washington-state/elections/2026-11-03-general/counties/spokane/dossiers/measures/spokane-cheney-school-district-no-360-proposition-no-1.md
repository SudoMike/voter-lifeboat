## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would replace Cheney School District's educational programs and operation (EP&O) levy, which expires in 2027, with a three-year levy collected in 2028-2030 [S1]. The board adopted Resolution No. 27-25-26 [S1]. The explanatory statement lists what the levy pays for, all expenses the state does not fund [S1]:

- Athletics, extracurricular activities and electives
- Class size reduction, counselors and mental health supports
- Staffing above the state's prototypical model, and substitutes
- Safety and security, instructional technology and transportation
- Nutrition services and special education costs above state and federal funding
- Nursing above funded levels, and early learning

The district's page lists athletics and activities, staffing above the prototypical model, counselors, paraeducators, nurses, custodians, special education and YES officers [S2]. Superintendent Ben Ferney said the levy is nearly 20% of an annual budget of about $105 million [S4]. An earlier report said it made up 16.5% of operating revenue the previous year [S3].

The board voted unanimously to send it to the November ballot [S3]. The district moved its levies from February to November hoping for higher turnout [S4]. Passage requires a simple majority [S3].

|---|---|---|
| 2028 | $2.10 | $18,450,000 |
| 2029 | $2.10 | $19,000,000 |
| 2030 | $2.10 | $19,550,000 |

- The total is about $57 million over three years [S3].
- Ferney noted the $2.10 estimate is "well below" the $2.50 state maximum [S4].
- The voters' guide gives no household example [S1].
- A July report said collection would begin in 2027 [S3]. The ballot title's first collection year is 2028 [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

**The expiring levy.** In November 2023 the board sent a three-year EP&O levy of more than $51 million to the February 2024 ballot. The district estimated it at $1.97 per $1,000, up from $1.30 in 2023, as federal pandemic funding ended [S5]. Cheney's levies passed in February 2024 [S6]. No source found gives the rate actually collected under that levy.

**How the new levy compares.**
- The amount rises from about $51 million to about $57 million over three years, roughly 12%. The estimated rate goes from $1.97 to $2.10. Both comparisons are computed for this dossier [S1][S5].
- With both 2026 levies and the 2024 bond, the district projects a combined school tax rate of $3.90 per $1,000, compared with $3.93 now [S3][S4]. Ferney said the board set a goal of staying under $4 [S4].
- Ferney: "It's not new taxes. It's to continue the programs we have now" [S4].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, by the Committee for Great Schools (Meridth Lemelin, Nicole Williams, Amy Pruett) [S1]:

- The levy "allows CSD to maintain the exceptional educational and extra-curricular programs currently offered" [S1].
- "Failure to pass this measure would result in drastic cuts to educational programs, student support, and services" [S1].
- "This levy will replace the current levy ... This is not a new tax" [S1].

## Arguments against

No statement against was submitted: "After repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement against the ballot measure" [S1]. No organized opposition was found.
