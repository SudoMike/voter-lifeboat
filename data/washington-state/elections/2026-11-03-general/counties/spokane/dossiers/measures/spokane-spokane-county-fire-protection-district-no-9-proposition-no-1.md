## What it does

Proposition No. 1 is a six-year levy lid lift for Spokane County Fire Protection District No. 9's regular fire levy [S1]. It would "restore its regular property tax levy to $1.50 per $1,000.00 of assessed valuation" for collection in 2027, then allow "annual levy revenue increases of up to 6% per year for the next five years (but not to exceed the $1.50 rate limit)" [S1]. The 2032 levy amount would become the base for later limits under chapter 84.55 RCW [S1].

- The board adopted Resolution No. 26-8 [S1]. The first reading was June 23, 2026 [S3], and the board approved it unanimously on July 14, 2026 (Commissioner Bennett moved, Commissioner Anderson seconded) [S4].
- The resolution says the current regular levy is "approximately $.65 per $1,000.00" as limited by the 101% limit, and that "the District has no record of ever previously levying to a rate of $1.50 per thousand" [S2].
- The district serves 125 square miles north of Spokane and Spokane Valley [S1][S7]. The district says calls have "nearly doubled since 2013" [S1][S5]; Chief Matthew Vinci told the Spokesman-Review the district had nearly 4,100 calls in 2014 and almost 9,000 last year [S7].
- The district says it "has not requested a voter-approved increase to its general fire levy in over 45 years" [S1][S5].
- Passage needs a simple majority [S2][S7]. RCW 84.55.050 allows a multi-year lid lift of up to 10 years with a stated limit factor for the later years [S12].

**What the money would pay for, per the district.** The flyer lists replacing aging apparatus and equipment, personnel, training and day-to-day operations, and planned replacements [S5]. Vinci told the Spokesman-Review that half the levy would go to fire vehicles: four engines, four brush trucks, three tenders and a ladder truck, with an engine costing $1 million and a ladder truck $2.5 million [S7]. He said the levy would also fund three more firefighters and replacement firefighting equipment [S7].

## Cost

- The rate would rise from about $0.65 to $1.50 per $1,000 in 2027 [S1][S2].
- The district's figure for a $500,000 property: "$423 per year, or $35.25 per month" [S1][S5]. At the stated rates the difference is $0.85 per $1,000, or $425 on $500,000 (computed for this dossier); the district's figure is slightly lower, probably because the current rate is approximate.
- In later years, levy revenue may grow up to 6% a year, still capped at $1.50 per $1,000 [S1].
- **Promised offset.** The district "commits to reducing its maintenance and operations levy by $0.20 per $1,000," which it says would cut the net increase to $323 a year ($26.92 a month) on a $500,000 property [S1]. The flyer says this applies "Beginning in 2028" and "If the District's $0.20 per $1,000 M&O levy reduction takes effect" [S5]. The newsletter explains that the M&O levy comes up for renewal in 2027, and that the board is "committed to reducing the M&O levy by $0.20" if Propositions 1 and 2 pass [S6]. This commitment is not part of the ballot title [S1].
- **A conflicting figure.** The commissioner's newsletter column says "the combined additional cost of Propositions 1 and 2 for a home assessed at $500,000 would be approximately $423 in 2027" [S6]. The pamphlet attributes the $423 to Proposition 1 alone and gives a separate $196 a year for the Proposition 2 bonds [S1]. Using the pamphlet figures, both measures together would add about $619 in 2027 on a $500,000 property (computed for this dossier).

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This is an increase, not a renewal. The district's regular levy has been about $0.65 per $1,000: the April 2024 M&O levy pamphlet said the district "is currently collecting $0.65 per $1,000" in regular property taxes [S10], and the 2026 resolution gives the same figure [S2]. Proposition 1 would more than double that rate [S1].

**The district's other levy.** The district also has a voter-approved maintenance and operations (M&O) excess levy [S6][S8]:
- In April 2024 a replacement M&O levy received 59.11% yes [S10][S11].
- In the August 2024 primary, voters approved a replacement M&O levy with 66.64%: $13,650,000 for 2025 (about $1.47 per $1,000), $14,200,000 for 2026 (about $1.48) and $14,800,000 for 2027 (about $1.49) [S8][S9].
- That levy expires after 2027 collections and will go back to voters in 2027 [S6].

On the pamphlet's numbers, the district's 2027 rates would be about $1.50 (regular, if Prop. 1 passes) + about $1.49 (M&O) + about $0.39 (bonds, if Prop. 2 passes), or about $3.38 per $1,000 (computed for this dossier from [S1], [S8] and the $0.39 bond rate reported in [S7]). This does not include any district EMS levy; this research did not check whether the district has one.

**Why now, per the district.**
- A 2026-2031 strategic plan found operating costs of about $408 per resident against an average of about $422 among comparable agencies, but "efficiency alone cannot overcome the financial challenges" because property tax revenue growth is generally limited to 1% a year [S6].
- Vinci said the fleet "is in tough shape" and that the district took out loans to replace breathing apparatus and protective equipment: "I don't want to use tax dollars to pay interest" [S7].
- The district had a severe 2026 fire season, including the Upriver fire and the Spokane Complex fires [S6][S7].
- The district started its own ambulance service on July 1, 2026; Vinci said it completed 966 transports by early October and that district residents "will never see a bill" [S7].
- The district recently received a $1.7 million FEMA SAFER grant for six firefighter positions and a roughly $200,000 Assistance to Firefighters grant [S7].

## Arguments for

No statement for was printed: "A statement for the measure was not received by the submission deadline" [S1]. The pamphlet lists a "Committee 'For' SCFD9 Levy Lid Lift" with contact Caroline Kusher [S1]. The district's case, from its own materials:
- "These aren't nice-to-haves... These are must-haves for us to deliver this service" (Vinci) [S7].
- Calls have nearly doubled and the district has not asked for a regular levy increase in over 45 years [S1][S5].
- "Maintaining today's level of service while preparing for tomorrow's needs requires a sustainable long-term funding plan" (Commissioner Brian Mather) [S6].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed: "After repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement against the ballot measure" [S1]. Vinci acknowledged the two propositions are a "big ask" [S7].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote more than doubles the district's regular levy rate, from about $0.65 to $1.50 per $1,000, then lets revenue grow up to 6% a year for five years [S1][S2]. The district says it is the first regular-levy increase request in more than 45 years [S1]. The promised $0.20 M&O reduction is a board commitment tied to a 2027 vote, not part of this measure [S1][S6]. Larger tax: +2.
- `spending`: the district frames the money as replacing aging vehicles and equipment and maintaining service, plus three firefighters [S5][S7]; no mapping.
