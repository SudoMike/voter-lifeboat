## What it does

Proposition No. 1 asks whether Fire District 11 may levy "a regular property tax in the sum of $.35 per one thousand dollars of assessed valuation each year for six years beginning in 2026 to be collected in 2027 for the provision of emergency medical services" [S1]. The explanatory statement calls it a "replacement measure" that would "restore its regular real property tax EMS levy rate to $.35 per $1,000 of assessed valuation," noting that "the current levy is approximately $.2676 per $1,000" [S1]. The 2027 amount becomes the levy base, and from 2028 the levy is subject to the 1% annual limit unless voters approve more [S1].

The district covers about 70 square miles around the town of Rockford and has about two dozen volunteer responders [S2]. Fire Chief Gary Hill told the Spokesman-Review that about 80% of calls are medical and that the EMS levy buys the medical supplies and equipment for them [S2]. He said the rate "was kept low on purpose, particularly as other taxes are rising": "We don't want to put members in our district in financial distress" [S2]. If the levy were not renewed, "we'd have to pull money away from fire expenditures," he said [S2].

## Cost

- $0.35 per $1,000 of assessed value for 2027 collection, up from about $0.2676 now [S1]. On a $300,000 home that is $105 a year, about $25 more than the current rate would produce (computed for this dossier from the stated rates).
- After 2027, levy revenue may grow no more than 1% a year without another vote, so the rate will fall as values rise [S1].
- The legal maximum for an EMS levy is 50 cents per $1,000 [S2][S5].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This renews a six-year EMS levy that expires this year [S2]. It returns the rate to the level voters approved last time.

- In November 2021, voters approved the expiring levy at $0.35 per $1,000 for six years from 2022 collection, with 69.44% yes (125 to 55) [S3][S4]. That pamphlet said the levy had then fallen to about $0.3043 [S3].
- The rate has since dropped to about $0.2676 [S1]. This happens because the 1% revenue limit holds collections down while assessed values rise.
- Hill said voters "have approved the renewal of the EMS levy every six years for quite some time" [S2].
- RCW 84.52.069 requires only a simple majority for the "subsequent approval" of a six-year EMS levy [S5]. The pamphlet does not state the threshold [S1].

## Arguments for

No statement for was filed: "After repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement for the ballot measure" [S1]. The district's case is the explanatory statement: the levy is needed "to maintain an effective level of EMS services, supplies and equipment due to the ever rising costs of all" [S1][S2].
