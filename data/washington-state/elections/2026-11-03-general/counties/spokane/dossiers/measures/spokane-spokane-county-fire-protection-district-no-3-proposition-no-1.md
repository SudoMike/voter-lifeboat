## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would authorize Spokane County Fire Protection District No. 3 to impose "a regular property tax levy of $.50 or less per $1,000.00 of assessed valuation for a period of six consecutive years to be collected beginning in 2027" for emergency medical services [S1]. The board adopted Resolution #26-5 [S1]. The explanatory statement says the levy would "allow the District to improve the level of emergency medical services" and that the revenue would be used "exclusively for emergency medical services" [S1].

The district's FAQ answers "Is this a new levy, a renewal, or a replacement?" with: "This is a proposed new six-year EMS levy. It would be in addition to the district's regular fire levy" [S3].

**The district.** The FAQ describes about 30,000 residents across about 570 square miles, 11 stations, about 30 full-time personnel and 120-140 volunteer firefighters and EMTs [S3]. It calls itself "the largest volunteer fire organization in Washington state" [S2][S3]. About 75% of its emergency calls are medical [S2][S3].

**What the money would pay for.** The district says the levy "would provide dedicated funding to hire 14 full-time firefighter/EMTs" (12 line and 2 officer positions) [S2][S3]:
- Twelve positions would staff two more stations around the clock. The preferred locations are Station 31 in Cheney and Station 36 in Spangle; today only Station 33 (Four Lakes) and Station 311 (Medical Lake) are staffed 24 hours [S2][S3].
- More than 900 emergency calls came from the areas the two new staffed stations would serve in 2025 [S2][S3].
- The district estimates about 21 months to build a fully trained workforce, and says it will pursue grants to offset costs [S2].

## Cost

- Up to $0.50 per $1,000 of assessed value a year for six years, starting with 2027 collections [S1].
- The district's examples: $50 a year ($4.17 a month) per $100,000 of assessed value; $250 a year ($20.83 a month) on a $500,000 property [S3]. The statement in favor uses the same "$4.17 per month for every $100,000" figure [S1].
- Estimated annual revenue: "Approximately $2,785,967" [S3].
- The district's current regular fire levy rate is $1.48 per $1,000; with this levy the estimated combined district rate would be $1.98 [S2][S3][S4].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This is a new tax; it does not replace an expiring levy [S3].

**Why now, per the district:**
- Call volume grows "approximately 4–5% each year" [S2][S3].
- The district says it "carries zero debt" but has "already required the District to eliminate two positions" as costs rose [S3].
- Health insurance premiums rose 35% in two years, and fuel costs rose about 45% since March 2026 [S3].
- "Based on the District's six-year financial forecast, current revenues will not sustain current staffing and services long term" [S3].
- State law generally limits growth in property tax revenue from existing property to 1% a year [S3].
- Deputy Chief Aaron Bollar told the Spokesman-Review that "the number of calls is outpacing the workforce's ability to keep up," and that without the levy further staff cuts would be needed and service would likely suffer [S4].

**Voting history.**
- The district asked for the same kind of EMS levy in the August 2022 primary: $0.50 or less per $1,000 for six years from 2023 [S5]. It received 53.48% yes (3,960 to 3,444) [S6]. RCW 84.52.069 requires a three-fifths majority, with a turnout minimum, for the first imposition of a six-year EMS levy [S9], so 53.48% was not enough. The district's 2026 materials call this levy new, which is consistent with the 2022 levy not taking effect [S3].
- In the August 2023 primary, voters approved restoring the district's regular fire levy to $1.50 per $1,000 with 65.59% [S7][S8]. That pamphlet said the levy was then about $1.22 and that voters had last approved $1.50 in 2021 [S7].
- The same three-fifths threshold applies to this measure because it is a first imposition [S9]. The 2026 pamphlet does not state the threshold [S1].

## Arguments for

The statement in favor (committee contact Dale Fulfs) says [S1]:
- "Rising operating costs have already forced the district to leave two positions unfilled."
- The levy "would provide the staffing needed to place Firefighters/EMTs in two additional stations 24 hours a day," at Station 31 in Cheney and Station 36 in Spangle.
- "Every taxpayer benefits, even if they do not live near one of these stations," because neighboring crews would be pulled away less often.
- "The department has managed taxpayer resources responsibly."

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed: "After repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement against the ballot measure" [S1]. The Spokesman-Review reports that the district "has gotten pushback, like with most levies, from people on social media who do not want to pay more in taxes" [S4].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote adds a new EMS levy of up to $0.50 per $1,000 on top of the $1.48 fire levy, raising the district's estimated combined rate to $1.98 and about $2.79 million a year [S2][S3]. The district itself calls it new [S3]. New tax: +2.
- `spending`: the levy funds 14 new positions and two new 24-hour staffed stations, which is an expansion of service [S2][S3]. The district also frames it as stabilizing current service [S3]. The convention maps spending only with explicit evidence, and the expansion here is explicit, but the axis is about government-wide investment versus audit, so no mapping is made for a single district's staffing levy.
