## What it does

Proposition No. 2 would authorize a new six-year capital levy, collected 2027-2032 [S1]. The board adopted Resolution No. 12-26 [S1]. The money would replace the failing roof and condemned portable classrooms at Lakeside High School and modernize "aged security systems, fire systems, facilities, and infrastructure District-wide" [S1]. Unlike the district's other measures, the ballot title does not call it a replacement levy [S1].

Superintendent Jeff Baerwald said the focus is replacing the Lakeside High School roof [S2]:
- The roof was replaced with asphalt shingles after the original tile roof failed in 2008. It began deteriorating after 11 years because the fasteners were too short, and has been patched since.
- A two-classroom portable installed in 1996 without a concrete slab developed dry rot and has been condemned.

The pro statement says leftover funds would go to HVAC controls [S1]. Passage requires a simple majority [S3].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

**A new levy that takes over an expiring bond's rate.** The Spokesman-Review calls the capital levy "a new tax." It would be offset by the end of a 20-year construction bond paid off at the end of 2026, which taxpayers pay at 38 cents per $1,000 [S2]. The pro statement says the levy replaces "a Bond, which expires January 1, 2027 ... at the same rate as the expiring Bond of $0.38" [S1]. A yes vote keeps the district's school tax rate from dropping by about 38 cents. A no vote lets it drop.

**Combined rate.** With the operations levy (Proposition 1) also passing, the district estimates $2.48 per $1,000 from 2028, compared with $2.65 in 2026 [S2].

**The April 2026 levy.** In April 2026 voters rejected a five-year, $6.2 million capital levy at an estimated 58 cents per $1,000, with 45.2% support on election night [S3]. It would have paid for the roof, track resurfacing, two boilers, HVAC controls, cameras, a replacement portable and flooring [S4]. With it, the district's rate would have risen to an estimated $2.90 in 2027 [S3][S4]. The November levy is smaller: 38 cents and fewer projects. The district is seeking a grant for the failed boiler [S2]. Baerwald said April turnout was low, "We had 24% of parents vote" [S2].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, by Future Focused For Nine Mile (Melanie Reynolds, Devin Bauer, Candace Haggard) [S1]:

- It "will maintain the current local tax rate by replacing a Bond ... with a new six-year Capital Levy at the same rate" [S1].
- "After listening to community concerns about rising taxes, the School Board prioritized only the district's most critical facility needs and removed several previously proposed projects" [S1].
- It would update the high school fire alarm and video surveillance systems "ensuring compliance with the required safety laws" [S1].
- Rebuttal: "OSPI data shows NMFSD revenue per student is 8.2% below statewide average" [S1].

## Arguments against

From the statement against, by "NO Means NO! Voters Said NO Just 90 Days Ago!" (David Joplin) [S1]:

- "The Voters said No only 90 days before this Proposal was submitted!" [S1]
- The proposal is "far more vague about 'projects'" than the April version and permits "carte blanche" spending [S1].
- "NMFSD is 50th in income, but 235th in dollars/student" [S1].
- The proposal "budgets for recovering these sunk costs" of the April election [S1].
- Rebuttal: "NMFSD has operated without condemned portables for over two years; therefore, not 'essential'" [S1].

The con statement's figures were not checked.

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: legally a new levy, but a yes vote holds the district's rate where it is by continuing the 38 cents of an expiring bond [S1][S2]. In effect on tax bills, that is the same as a same-rate replacement, so it maps like one. Reasonable reviewers could treat "new levy" as +2.
- `spending`: repairs to existing facilities. No evidence of expansion.
- `parental-rights` / `social`: not addressed.
