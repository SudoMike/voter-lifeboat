## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would replace Spokane Public Schools' educational programs and operation (EP&O) levy, which expires at the end of 2027, with a three-year levy collected in 2028-2030 [S1][S2]. The board adopted Resolution No. 2026-11 [S1]. The levy pays for expenses the state does not fully fund, "including class size, advanced courses, special education, nurses, counselors, technology support, safety staff, music, athletics, and extracurricular activities" [S1].

The levy is about 15% of the district's operating budget [S2][S3]. The pro statement says about 14% [S1]. The board voted unanimously on June 17, 2026 to send it to the November ballot [S3]. The district historically runs levies in February. Superintendent Adam Swinyard said it chose November for higher participation [S2][S4]. Chief financial officer Cindy Coleman said a February vote would not leave time for a second attempt before layoff notices if it failed [S4]. The district held town halls on Sept. 29 and Oct. 6 [S5].

## Cost

Ballot title figures [S1]:

| Collection year | Estimated rate per $1,000 | Levy amount |
|---|---|---|
| 2028 | $2.50 | $107,000,000 |
| 2029 | $2.50 | $111,000,000 |
| 2030 | $2.50 | $115,000,000 |

- The total is $333 million over three years [S3].
- The rate cannot exceed $2.50 per $1,000 under current state law, and collections cannot exceed the voter-approved amounts [S1].
- Spokane County Assessor Tom Konis put the average home value in the district at $333,700, for about $830 a year in levy taxes [S2].
- The con statement describes "a continued $1,000 tax per year on a typical home" [S1]. Its home value basis is not stated.

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

**The expiring levy.** Voters approved the current levy in February 2024. That ballot authorized $297 million over three years at an estimated $2.50 per $1,000 [S6][S7]. The same election's bond failed, the district's first failed bond since 1967 [S7]. The current levy collects at $2.50 per $1,000 [S4]. It covers $93.3 million of the district's $627.5 million 2026-27 budget [S2].

**How the new levy compares.**
- The rate stays the same, $2.50 [S3][S4].
- The amount rises from $297 million to $333 million over three years, about 12%. That comparison is computed for this dossier [S3][S6].
- The district's total school tax rate, counting previously approved bonds, is estimated to rise from $3.96 in 2027 to $4.15 in 2028-2029 and $4.17 in 2030 [S3][S4]. The levy rate itself is unchanged, so the sources imply the increase comes from other voter-approved debt rather than this levy.

**No expansion.** Swinyard: "This won't be an expansion of services; it won't be increasing staffing levels" [S3]. The district cut about 200 positions in response to inflation and expects more cuts even if the levy passes [S2]. He has said a failed levy would mean cutting 400-500 positions [S3][S4]. The district's rebuttal says about 700 [S1].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, by the "For" Statement Committee (Carol Wendle) [S1]:

- The district "has earned exemplary ratings on annual independent financial audits" and "has reduced staffing by more than 200 positions to maintain a balanced budget" [S1].
- It cites early-literacy gains, record graduation, more Advanced Placement completion and a new Trades High School with Spokane Community College [S1].
- The levy "supports smaller class sizes, special education, school safety, mental health services, nurses, counselors, technology, career and technical education, advanced learning, arts, athletics, and extracurricular activities" [S1].
- Rebuttal: "Without the replacement levy, SPS would face cuts to another 700 positions," and "Over 70% of SPS students were proficient in literacy (ELA) on state assessments last spring" [S1].

## Arguments against

From the statement against, by the "Against" Statement Committee (Russell Neff) [S1]:

- "This levy requests the maximum rate allowed by law, $2.50 per $1,000 of assessed value, for three straight years," on top of rising household costs [S1].
- "Roughly 97% of last year's levy went to extra salaries and benefits not covered by state funding"; the state constitution makes basic education the state's responsibility [S1].
- "40% of Spokane students don't meet math standards, 32% fall short in English, and nearly one in three are chronically absent" [S1].
- "Students will still be educated if this levy fails" [S1].
- Rebuttal: "Cutting 200+ positions proves the district can work with less" [S1].

Neff said the district asked him to lead the con committee. He ran for the school board in 2024. He said he is "not against the district, just against levies" [S2].

## Endorsements and campaign

None found beyond the committees named in the voters' guide. The Spokane Education Association supported the 2024 levy; its 2026 position was not found [S6].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote replaces the expiring EP&O levy at the same $2.50 rate, with higher dollar amounts [S1][S3][S4][S6]. It is a replacement at the same rate, not a new tax.
- `spending`: the district says the levy funds existing services with continued cuts, not expansion [S2][S3]. The con side argues the state, not local levies, should fund staff [S1]. Neither is grounds for a spending mapping.
- `parental-rights` / `social`: not addressed.
