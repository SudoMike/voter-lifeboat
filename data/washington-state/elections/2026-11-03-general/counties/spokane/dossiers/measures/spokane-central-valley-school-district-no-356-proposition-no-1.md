## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would replace Central Valley School District's educational programs and operations (EP&O) levy, which expires at the end of 2027, with a three-year levy collected in 2028, 2029 and 2030 [S1]. The board adopted Resolution No. 26-13 [S1]. The levy pays for educational expenses the state does not fund or fully fund, "including, but not limited to, teacher and classified staff compensation, nurses, counselors, safety staff, music, athletics, advanced courses, extracurricular activities" [S1]. Passage requires a simple majority [S7].

The district says levy revenue is 16.2% of district revenue in the 2026-27 general fund budget [S2][S5]. Its levy page lists what the levy covers, including 100% of extracurricular activities and athletics ($5.8 million), 95% of substitute costs, 35% of facilities, utilities and insurance, 20% of support staff costs, and 14% of teacher, nurse and certificated staff costs [S2].

The school board voted unanimously in late July 2026 to send the levy to the November ballot [S4]. Central Valley usually runs its levies in February. Superintendent John Parker said the district chose November for the turnout and so that "if we have to run it again, we're way ahead of schedule" [S5].

## Cost

Ballot title figures [S1]:

| Collection year | Estimated rate per $1,000 | Levy amount |
|---|---|---|
| 2028 | $2.50 | $48,900,000 |
| 2029 | $2.50 | $50,550,000 |
| 2030 | $2.50 | $52,400,000 |

- The three-year total is about $151.9 million [S4].
- The district estimates $1,045 a year ($87 a month) for a median assessed home value of $418,000 [S2]. Work-session documents reported by the Spokesman-Review put it at about $1,025 a year on a $410,000 average home [S5].
- $2.50 per $1,000 is the most state law allows for this kind of levy, and the district cannot collect more than the voter-approved amounts [S2][S4].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

**The expiring levy.** Voters approved the current EP&O levy in February 2024 with 52.75% yes [S7]. The board had sent a $137 million, three-year levy to that ballot at an estimated $2.40 per $1,000 [S6]. That was larger than the $93 million three-year levy voters approved in the 2021 cycle [S6]. Assessed values grew less than projected, so the current levy collected about $2.45 per $1,000 in 2025 and nearly $2.50 in 2026 [S4]. The district's levy page puts the current rate at $2.50 per $1,000 [S2].

**How the new levy compares.**
- The rate stays at $2.50, "the same rate they paid in 2026" [S4].
- The dollar amounts rise about 3.5% a year [S4]. Superintendent Parker: "We're not necessarily asking for anything new, although the levy is for more money" [S5]. Executive financial officer Shannon Johnston said the district expects to keep cutting costs even if it passes [S5].
- The district and the pro committee project the total local school tax rate (EP&O levy, 2018 bond and capital levy) to stay at $3.92 per $1,000 through 2030 [S3][S4].

**Rate cap resolution.** Board member Pam Orebaugh brought a resolution pledging the levy rate would not exceed $2.50 per $1,000 even if the Legislature raised the levy lid [S4][S5].

**State support.** The district projects state Local Effort Assistance falling from about $5.7 million in 2026-27 to $1.3 million in 2028-29 [S2].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, by the Central Valley Citizens for Education "For" Committee (Lindsay Miller, Cris Kaminskas, Buffy Nustad) [S1]:

- "Replace the levy. Keep the rate." [S1]
- It funds what state funding does not fully cover, including "advanced coursework, career and technical education, arts, athletics, extracurricular activities," plus paraeducators, nurses, safety and security staff, counselors, technology and custodial staff [S1].
- "The district will not exceed $2.50 per thousand of assessed property value, which is the current rate that taxpayers are already paying" [S1].

The committee's website says total local school taxes stay at $3.92 per $1,000 and that the levy brings in "roughly one dollar in six" of what it costs to run Central Valley schools [S3].

## Arguments against

No statement against was received by the deadline [S1]. The voters' guide lists Dennis Reed as the "Against" statement committee [S1].

Board members raised concerns while voting to send the measure to the ballot [S4]:
- Vice President Anniece Barker voted yes. The newspaper described the roughly 3.5% annual increase as unpalatable to her, and she said: "I do not support continuing to raise taxes on our community."
- Pam Orebaugh asked for assurance the rate would not exceed $2.50.

Spokane County Republican Party chair Rob Linebarger said the party has resolved to support no new taxes for five years and that districts should "lean it up and make it work" [S5].

## Endorsements and campaign

The pro committee's website quotes supporters including former Spokane Valley Chamber board chairs Brooke Baker Spink, Matt Albright and Greg Repetti [S3]. School Board President Stephanie Jerdon said she will vote for the levy [S5]. No organized opposition campaign was found.

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote continues an expiring EP&O levy at the same $2.50 rate it collects in 2026 [S1][S2][S4]. The dollar amounts are higher than the expiring levy's: about $50 million a year versus $137 million over three years [S1][S6]. This is a replacement at the same rate, not a new tax.
- `spending`: district officials describe the levy as maintaining current programs amid inflation, with cuts continuing [S5]. That is not evidence of expansion.
- `parental-rights` / `social`: not addressed by the measure or the statements.
