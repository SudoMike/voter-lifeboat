## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would let the Town of Rockford levy a property tax in 2027 "at an estimated rate of $0.51 per $1,000 of assessed value to raise $40,934.57" for 2027 fire protection services [S1]. The ballot title says "This levy replaces an expiring levy previously collected by the Town" [S1]. The town's fire department "provides emergency fire protection and medical services to its residents," and the council says the levy is needed "to maintain an effective level of services, fire fighter staffing, equipment and facilities considering rising costs" [S1].

The council approved Resolution 26-05, "the 2027 Fire Levy Ballot Measure," on July 15, 2026 [S2]. At the public hearing that evening, a resident asked whether it was "just a replacement levy," and Mayor Carrie Roecks confirmed that it was [S2].

**A wording inconsistency in the pamphlet.** The ballot title calls it "an excess property tax," while the explanatory statement says approval "will authorize the Town to retain its existing regular real property tax levy rate of $.51" [S1]. The same mismatch appears in the town's 2025 pamphlet entry [S3]. The town's own minutes call it a "fire excess levy" [S2][S5].

## Cost

- About $0.51 per $1,000 of assessed value in 2027, raising $40,934.57 [S1]. On a $300,000 home that is about $153 for the year (computed for this dossier from the stated rate).
- The levy is for one year (2027) only [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

Rockford asks voters every year to renew this one-year fire levy. The VoteWA guide shows the same $0.51 rate for 2025 and 2026; the dollar amount rises as property values grow [S1][S3][S4]:

| Levy for | Rate per $1,000 | Amount | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | $0.51 | $35,380.67 | Approved, 69.93% [S7] |
| 2026 | $0.51 | $36,075.08 | Approved, 72.93% [S6] |
| 2027 (this measure) | $0.51 | $40,934.57 | |

- The 2027 amount is about 13% more than the 2026 amount at the same estimated rate (computed for this dossier from [S1] and [S3]).
- Voters also approved the town's fire levy proposition in November 2023 with 66.15% [S8]. At the July 2023 hearing on "replacing the fire levy," the mayor said "the levy would be the same percentage of property tax" [S5].

## Arguments for

No statement for was filed: "After repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement for the ballot measure" [S1]. The town's case is the explanatory statement: the levy is needed to maintain fire protection "considering rising costs" [S1].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed [S1]. No public comment against the levy is recorded in the July 15, 2026 hearing minutes [S2].
