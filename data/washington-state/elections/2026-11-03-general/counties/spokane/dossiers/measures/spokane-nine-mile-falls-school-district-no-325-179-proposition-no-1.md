## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would replace Nine Mile Falls School District's educational programs and operations (EP&O) levy, which expires in 2027, with a three-year levy collected in 2028-2030 [S1]. The board adopted Resolution No. 11-26 [S1]. The explanatory statement lists what the levy pays for, all expenses the state does not fund [S1]:

- Staffing and supports for safety, music and fine arts, nurses and counselors
- Class size reduction, and certificated and classified staffing and salaries
- Athletics, extracurricular, enrichment and summer activities
- Transportation, maintenance and operations, and technology
- Nutrition services and special education

The district crosses into Stevens County [S1]. Superintendent Jeff Baerwald gave the district's breakdown of each levy dollar: 45 cents for teachers, support staff and other supplemental positions; 42 cents for extracurricular and enrichment activities; 10 cents for maintenance and operations; and 3 cents for technology. He said the levy is 17% of the district's budget and called it the district's "lifeblood" [S2].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

**The expiring levy.** Voters approved the current replacement EP&O levy in February 2024 with 53.2% yes [S3]. Baerwald said the current levy collects $2.27 per $1,000 in 2026. The new levy's estimated $2.10 for 2028 is lower [S2]. No source found gives the expiring levy's dollar amounts.

**Combined rate.** If both of the district's November propositions pass, the district estimates a combined rate of $2.48 per $1,000 starting in 2028, compared with the $2.65 taxpayers pay in 2026 [S2]. Baerwald: "We're actually looking to lower the rate" [S2].

**The April 2026 vote.** In April 2026, voters rejected a separate $6.2 million, five-year capital levy at 45.2% support on election night [S4]. The con statement's "90 days" refers to that vote. This proposition is the operations levy, not a rerun of that capital levy [S1][S2][S4].

No statement in favor was received by the deadline [S1]. The voters' guide lists "Supporting Nine Mile Students" (Ann Fackenthall) as the "For" committee [S1].

## Arguments against

From the statement against, by the committee "NO Means NO! Voters Said NO Just 90 Days Ago!" (David Joplin) [S1]:

- "The Voters said No only 90 days before this Proposal was submitted!" It says the proposal was "written last year as a contingency," and calls its timing deliberate [S1].
- The levy wording "permits NMFSD to spend on anything 'not funded by the State;' an end-around on voters' mandate," including items like the carpeting and track repairs voters rejected [S1].
- "Out of 294 Washington School Districts, NMFSD is 50th in income, but 235th in dollars/student! If not to students, or infrastructure, where has the money gone?" [S1]
- It says the district committed "$40,000 taxpayer dollars" to the failed April measure [S1].

No rebuttal to the con statement appears in the voters' guide record [S1]. The con statement's figures (income ranking, $40,000) are its own and were not checked.
