## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would replace Riverside School District's educational programs and operations (EP&O) levy, which expires at the end of 2027, with a three-year levy collected in 2028-2030 [S1]. The board adopted Resolution No. 6-26-03 [S1]. The levy pays for programs, services and staff the state does not fund, "including elective and advanced courses, vocational education, nurses, counselors, technology support, safety, security, performing arts, athletics, and extracurricular activities" [S1]. The district spans Spokane and Pend Oreille counties [S1].

Superintendent Ken Russell said the levy is 13% of the annual budget. It pays for extra teachers to reduce class sizes, Advanced Placement teachers, counselors, nurses and paraeducators, and "one hundred percent of athletics" [S2]. It is the district's only levy [S2]. The district moved the vote from the usual February ballot to November for turnout [S2].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

**The expiring levy.** Russell said taxpayers currently pay $1.52 per $1,000 for the levy. The first year of the new levy is estimated at $1.58, about 6 cents more [S2]. "We've been running the same levy every three years for decades," he said [S2]. Riverside's levy passed in the February 2024 election [S4]. No source found gives the expiring levy's dollar amounts.

**Failed capital levy.** In November 2025, voters rejected a five-year, $18 million capital levy for security, technology and HVAC, of which $16 million was for HVAC [S3]. The district has no capital levy now. Russell said the board decided not to seek one: "We've heard from our voters that they just can't afford it" [S2]. The district is using grants and set-aside funds for HVAC repairs [S2][S3].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, by Community for the Riverside Replacement Levy (Jeni Vineyard, Kevin Koesel, Jennifer Gaffaney) [S1]:

- "Proposition 1 replaces the current EPO levy when it expires; it does not create a second levy" [S1].
- It pays for electives and advanced courses, vocational education, nurses, counselors, technology support, safety, performing arts and athletics. "These are not 'extras' to the students who depend on them" [S1].
