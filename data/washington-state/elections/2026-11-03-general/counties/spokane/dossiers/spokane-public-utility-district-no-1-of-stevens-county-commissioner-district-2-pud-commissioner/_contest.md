## What the office does

Public Utility District No. 1 of Stevens County (Stevens PUD) is a water and wastewater utility. It sold its electric system to Washington Water Power (now Avista) in 1955 and began building and rehabilitating water systems in the early 1980s [S3]. It serves about 5,000 water and sewer customers in Stevens County and parts of Spokane County. A three-member board of elected commissioners governs it, and an appointed general manager runs it [S3]. The PUD's site has listed 18 or 19 water systems and 8 wastewater systems [S3][S4]. The About page describes the board as "elected by the voters of Stevens County," although its own system pages place some systems in Spokane County [S3][S5]. Commissioners serve six-year terms [S2][S14].

## Who votes: the Spokane County portion

- **The PUD reaches into Spokane County.** The PUD's own site says it "provides water and wastewater services in Stevens County and portions of Spokane County" [S3]. Its archived system pages name four water systems as "in Spokane County": Spokane Lake Park (about 95 connections), Riverside (about 54), Westshore (about 164) and Halfmoon Ranchos (about 72) [S5][S6][S7][S8]. Its water-system list also includes "Riverside/Chattaroy Springs West," "Lake Spokane (Suncrest & Long Lake)" and others [S4]. Those four pages put at least about 385 connections in Spokane County. The county of the other systems was not confirmed from these pages.
- **Countywide in the PUD, not just District 2.** Under RCW 54.12.010, only voters of a commissioner district vote in its primary, but "voters of the entire public utility district may vote at a general election to elect a person as a commissioner of the commissioner district" [S2]. So every PUD voter, including those in Spokane County, votes on the District 2 seat in November. Only voters living in District 2 may run for it [S2]. VoteWA lists the race on Spokane and Stevens county ballots [S1].
- **Small Spokane County electorate.** In 2020, when Kubik ran unopposed for the same seat, Spokane County's portion of the race cast 354 votes (350 for Kubik), against 19,399 Kubik votes in Stevens County [S9][S10]. The PDC lists the jurisdiction at 35,850 voters [S12].
- **Not found.** Stevens PUD's live site (stevenspud.org) returns HTTP 403 to automated requests, and WebFetch was refused too. No current (2025-2026) official statement of its Spokane County service area or customer counts was retrieved. The figures above come from Internet Archive captures from 2023-2024 [S3][S4][S5][S6][S7][S8]. ## Race dynamics

- **Incumbent vs. challenger.** Howie Kubik says he has been a PUD commissioner for 17 years [S1]. State results show him winning the District 2 seat unopposed in 2014 and 2020 [S9][S11]. He is a retired 37-year Avista lineman and foreman [S1]. Andrea Morgan is a registered nurse of nearly 40 years and a 33-year Stevens County property owner, running for her first office [S1].
- **No primary.** Only two candidates filed, and the PDC lists both as not on the primary ballot and "Qualified for general" [S12][S13][S14].
- **Little campaign activity.** Neither candidate lists a website [S1]. Both registered with the PDC under the "mini" reporting option, and neither had filed reports [S12][S13].
- **Messages.** Kubik stresses reliable, safe and cost-effective service and his relationships with employees and customers [S1]. Morgan stresses preventive maintenance to keep rates predictable, balancing infrastructure with "natural resources and private property rights," and transparency to ratepayers [S1].

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

- **Record vs. renewal.** A 17-year incumbent with a utility-trade background faces a first-time candidate with a health-care background [S1]. The `experience` axis does not apply to PublicUtility contests in this rubric, so this is shown descriptively only.
- **Stated priorities.** Kubik: reliability and cost-effectiveness [S1]. Morgan: preventive maintenance, rate predictability, transparency and private property rights [S1].
