## What the office does

Spokane County District Court judges hear misdemeanor and gross-misdemeanor criminal cases, traffic infractions, small claims, civil cases and protection orders [S10][S11]. They are elected countywide to four-year terms [S12]. In practice a judge's discretion centers on arraignments and pretrial matters, plea and sentencing decisions on misdemeanors, protection orders and therapeutic-court supervision [S8][S10].

## Race dynamics

- **Open seat.** Judge Patti Connolly Walker won Position 4 unopposed in 2022 [S3] and is not on the 2026 ballot [S1]. Ulrich's campaign site quotes her: "When I decided to retire after 25 years on the bench, I immediately thought of Nick Ulrich to take my place" [S4]. Her retirement was not confirmed in an official court source; Spokane County's court pages return HTTP 403 to automated requests.
- **No primary.** Two candidates filed, so the race goes straight to the November ballot [S1][S12].
- **Candidates.** Nicholas Ulrich is a section chief in the Torts Division of the Washington Attorney General's Office and has been a pro tem judge in Spokane County courts for more than five years [S1]. Michael Addams is the City of Cheney's prosecutor, a pro tem judge and commissioner in Spokane County courts, and a 24-year Air Force and Air National Guard veteran [S1].
- **Bar ratings.** The Spokane County Bar Association's evaluation panel rated Ulrich "Exceptionally Well Qualified" and Addams "Qualified." In the 2026 bar poll (about 17% of members responding, 4-point scale), Ulrich averaged 2.88 to 3.03 across the five criteria and Addams 2.82 to 3.14. Addams scored higher on judicial temperament (3.10 vs. 2.96) and integrity (3.14 vs. 2.97); Ulrich scored higher on legal ability, relevant experience and suitability [S2].
- **Endorsements.** Ulrich lists Walker, most sitting Spokane County Superior and District Court judges, several Court of Appeals Division III judges, Prosecutor Preston McCollam, County Commissioners Amber Waldref and Chris Jordan, the Spokane Police Guild and the Spokane Regional Labor Council [S4]. Addams lists Rep. Suzanne Schmidt, Mike Padden (retired state legislator and retired District Court presiding judge), three sitting District Court judges, and many elected and deputy prosecutors around the state [S7]. Three District Court judges (Johnson, Maurer, Smith) and Superior Court Judge Dean Chuang appear on both lists, as does Deanna Crull, a candidate for Position 2 [S4][S7].
- **Money (PDC).** Ulrich: $46,970 raised, $30,030 spent (updated Oct. 2). Addams: $44,490 raised, $23,059 spent (updated Oct. 6) [S5][S6].
- **News coverage.** No Spokesman-Review, Inlander or RANGE Media story on this race was found as of 2026-10-08.

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

- **Professional formation (`safety`).** Addams's career centers on prosecution: municipal prosecutor for Cheney and Eastern Washington University, former director of the Washington Association of Prosecuting Attorneys' child-support program, and a prosecutor-heavy endorsement list [S7][S10]. He pairs "accountability that supports safer outcomes" with support for therapeutic courts [S8]. Ulrich's career is mainly civil litigation defending the state, after a clerkship and a short stint as a public defender [S11]. Neither has a documented bail position.
- **Judicial approach (`judicial`).** Ulrich says a judge must "follow the law wherever it leads and not be swayed by personal preferences, beliefs, or opinions" [S9]. Addams's stated approach is general: fairness, consistency, applying the law equally [S1][S8].
- **Experience (`experience`).** Neither is an incumbent. Ulrich offers a Court of Appeals clerkship, appellate and Supreme Court advocacy and pro tem service since 2021 [S11]. Addams offers municipal prosecution, pro tem service (Superior Court commissioner pro tem since 2021, District Court judge pro tem since 2025) and military leadership [S10].

## Not differentiating

- Both promise accountability, fairness and efficiency [S1].
- Both serve as pro tem judicial officers in Spokane County courts [S1].
