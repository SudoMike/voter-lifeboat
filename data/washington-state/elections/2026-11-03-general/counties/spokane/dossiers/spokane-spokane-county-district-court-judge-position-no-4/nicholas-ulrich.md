## Background

Nicholas Ulrich is a Spokane native who attended Ferris High School [S1][S3]. He holds B.S. degrees in physics and mathematics from Washington State University and a J.D. from the University of Washington School of Law, and he served in AmeriCorps between college and law school [S1][S3]. He began his legal career as a law clerk to Judge Kevin Korsmo of the Washington Court of Appeals, Division III, then worked as a public defender and in private practice at Winston & Cashatt before joining the Washington Attorney General's Office [S3]. At the AGO he rose from line attorney to team leader to section chief; his site says he leads the Litigation Support Section of the Torts Division, and his statement calls it "Section Chief – Torts Division" [S1][S3]. He has been a pro tem judge or commissioner since 2021 in Airway Heights Municipal Court, since 2022 in Spokane County District Court and since 2024 in Spokane County Superior Court [S3]. He serves on the WSBA Judicial Recommendation Committee and the Eastern District of Washington Rules Committee [S1].

## Positions

- **Judicial philosophy.** His site says "A judge's responsibility is to follow the law wherever it leads and not be swayed by personal preferences, beliefs, or opinions," and that a judge must hold people responsible for their actions and require follow-through on court obligations [S2]. In a campaign video he says "My goal is that you won't be able to tell my partisan preferences from my decision making" [S5].
- **Court administration.** Says he will "balance ensuring procedural justice with efforts to modernize the court and find efficiencies in court practices" [S1].
- **Temperament.** Campaign theme: "Empathy, Integrity, Accountability: Every Day, Every Case" [S1][S2].
- No statement on bail, diversion or therapeutic courts was found.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

## Record

- As an assistant attorney general he has defended the state and its agencies in litigation and argued before the Washington Supreme Court; he serves on the AGO Opinions Committee and is the Attorney General's designee to investigate whistleblower complaints against the State Auditor's Office (self-reported) [S3].
- Pro tem service has covered preliminary criminal hearings, civil protection orders and family-law motions (self-reported) [S3].
- No published rulings were found.

## Ratings

- Spokane County Bar Association Judicial Candidates Evaluation Panel (2026): "Exceptionally Well Qualified." He was the only Position 4 candidate given that rating [S2][S6].
- SCBA bar poll (2026, about 17% of members responding, 1-4 scale): legal ability 3.03, judicial temperament 2.96, integrity 2.97, relevant legal experience 2.88, suitability 2.89 [S6].

## Endorsements

His site lists "retiring District Court Judge Patti Connolly Walker", who is quoted saying she "immediately thought of Nick Ulrich to take my place" [S4]. It also lists seven Court of Appeals Division III judges and commissioners (some retired) and most sitting Spokane County Superior Court judges. Other endorsers: District Court judges Johnson, Biviano, Zappone, Fassbender, Smith and Maurer; Spokane County Prosecuting Attorney Preston McCollam; County Commissioners Amber Waldref and Chris Jordan; Assessor Tom Konis; Rep. Timm Ormsby; the Spokane Police Guild; the Spokane Regional Labor Council; the Spokane County Deputy Sheriffs Association; the Spokane County Lieutenants and Captains Association; AFSCME Council 2 Local 492; IUPAT DC5; and many attorneys [S4].

## Scoring notes

- Formation is mainly civil litigation for the state, after an appellate clerkship and a short stint as a public defender, with five years of part-time pro tem service. No documented bail or diversion posture [S3].
- States a restraint-oriented philosophy: follow the law "wherever it leads," not personal preference [S2][S5].
- Open seat: not an incumbent, but endorsed by the retiring incumbent and most of the local bench [S4].
