## Background
Julia Payne is the Democratic candidate for the open Position 2 seat in the 6th Legislative District and a first-time candidate [S1]. She moved to Spokane in 1989 as the daughter of Air Force veterans, graduated from Medical Lake High School and has lived in Airway Heights and Indian Trail [S1]. She has worked as an office administrator, including in law offices [S1]. She describes herself as disabled and mobility-impaired, a mother and a foster mother [S1][S6]. She has volunteered with Spokane Pride for more than a decade, including a year as board vice president, and with the Spokane AIDS Network [S1]; she also lists Planned Parenthood among organizations she has volunteered with [S5].

In the August 4 primary she placed second with 25.6% in the election-night count, behind Republican Jonathan Bingle (51.2%) and narrowly ahead of independent Aaron Croft (22.9%) [S2]. In the August 7 update she led Croft by 368 votes, 25.1% to 23.6% [S3]; her campaign later announced she had advanced to the general election [S8].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** supports the Millionaire's Tax passed by the Legislature, wants to "fix our broken and regressive tax code," and would "push for more creative revenue options" [S6]. In the RANGE questionnaire called for a "more graduated tax responsibility so that those who have the means pay taxes commiserate with their assets," with revenue directed to programs such as child care [S5]. Lists the House Finance Committee as a preferred assignment to restructure the "regressive tax code" [S5].
- **Data centers and technology:** supports "a ban on their construction unless they meet strict requirements to be self-sufficient and sustainable" and comprehensive legislation on data-center construction, operation and resource use; says a data center proposed for the district should wait until oversight catches up [S6][S5].
- **Healthcare:** says "a universal health care approach" could raise compensation for providers and ease the shortage of mental-health providers; supports expanded virtual care [S5].
- **Housing:** would work with groups from Habitat for Humanity to local builders on multi-family options and home ownership for low-income families, saying more homes must be built "in the right price range" [S5].
- **Homelessness:** says some people need treatment-first approaches and some need housing-first approaches, and that programs built on self-advocacy work best [S5][S4].
- **Public safety:** says the Legislature must give jurisdictions the tools to maintain public safety and that law enforcement should be "held to a higher standard of accountability"; says not all cities need the same laws or enforcement [S5]. Calls the prosecution of three Spokane residents charged after a June 11, 2025 protest ("the Spokane 3") "unjust" and says the protesters were exercising constitutional rights [S6].
- **Wildfire:** wants the Department of Natural Resources to lead prevention, comprehensive risk evaluations and properly funded and compensated firefighters; also calls for policies on development in the wildland-urban interface [S5][S6].
- **Accessibility and anti-discrimination:** would push for legislation strengthening protections against discrimination in housing, employment and public spaces, and fund model ADA legislation for local governments [S6].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no policy statement of her own found. Long-time Spokane Pride volunteer [S1]; endorsed by Washington State Stonewall Democrats [S7] and Planned Parenthood Advocates Greater Washington and North Idaho [S9].
- **Parents and schools:** no position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Representation:** campaign slogan "Approachable to everyone, Accountable to you"; says current representatives are not available to constituents who travel to Olympia [S1].
