## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House, for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets: K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the top two finishers from the August primary.

## Race dynamics
The seat is open. Rep. Jenny Graham, a four-term Republican first elected in 2018, announced in February that she would not seek re-election [S3]; she voted against ESSB 6346, the 2026 tax on income over $1 million, on House final passage [S5]. The district includes part of Jonathan Bingle's former northeast Spokane council district, Airway Heights, Medical Lake, Fairchild Air Force Base and much of unincorporated Spokane County [S2].

Republican Jonathan Bingle, a Spokane City Councilmember from 2022 until his narrow re-election loss in November 2025, entered after Graham's announcement and is endorsed by Graham, Rep. Mike Volz and Sen. Jeff Holy [S2][S1]. He led the primary with 51.2%; Democrat Julia Payne, a first-time candidate, took second with 25.6%, narrowly ahead of independent Aaron Croft (22.9%) [S4]. Bingle reports $179.2k raised to Payne's $8.7k (jonathan-bingle.md S11; julia-payne.md S11).

Bingle opposes all forms of income tax, proposes zero-based budgeting, would replace Housing First with treatment-first models, supports rolling back the Climate Commitment Act and runs on a detailed parental-rights platform and limits on gender-affirming care for minors (jonathan-bingle.md S9). Payne supports the Millionaire's Tax and more progressive revenue, a conditional ban on data-center construction and a universal health care approach (julia-payne.md S5, S6). Neither candidate has stated a position on the three statewide initiatives.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Bingle opposes all income taxes; Payne supports the 2026 tax on income over $1 million and a more graduated tax code.
2. **Spending:** zero-based budgeting and cuts to non-core programs (Bingle) vs. new revenue directed to child care and other programs (Payne).
3. **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** Bingle's council amendment and platform to restrict gender-affirming care for minors and a reported 13-week abortion limit vs. Payne's long Spokane Pride volunteer work and Stonewall and Planned Parenthood endorsements.
4. **Parents and schools:** Bingle's notification and curriculum-transparency planks; Payne has no stated position.
5. **Public safety and homelessness:** treatment-first, enforcement-oriented council record vs. Payne's mix of treatment-first and housing-first and her call for police accountability.
6. **Tech:** Payne supports a conditional ban on data-center construction; no Bingle position found.
7. **Record vs. renewal:** one council term vs. a first-time candidate.
