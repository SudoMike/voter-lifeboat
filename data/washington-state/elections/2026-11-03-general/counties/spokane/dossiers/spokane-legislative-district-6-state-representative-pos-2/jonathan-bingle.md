## Background
Jonathan Bingle is the Republican candidate for the open Position 2 seat in the 6th Legislative District [S1]. He served on the Spokane City Council from 2022 through 2025, representing northeast Spokane (District 1) [S1][S2]. He has owned small businesses for 15 years, including the Bent Trivia and Bent Events companies and a painting company, and has been a pastor for 19 years [S1]. He attended Rogers High School and Spokane Falls Community College [S1]. He lost a 2019 race for Spokane mayor and received about 4% in the 2024 primary for the 5th Congressional District [S2]. In November 2025 he lost re-election to the council to Sarah Dixit by a little more than one percentage point [S2].

He entered the race in February 2026 after Rep. Jenny Graham, a four-term Republican, announced she would not seek re-election; Rep. Mike Volz had already said he would not run, so both district seats are open [S2][S3]. In the August 4 primary he led with 51.2% in the election-night count; Democrat Julia Payne (25.6%) edged independent Aaron Croft (22.9%) for the second general-election spot [S4], a lead Payne kept in the August 7 update [S5].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** pamphlet promises to "reduce the tax burden, and protect your paycheck" [S1]. Campaign site: "Oppose all forms of income tax, including capital gains expansions," oppose new taxes "disguised as 'fees'" and oppose payroll-tax expansions [S9][S6]. After leaving the council he publicly opposed tax proposals from the Legislature [S2].
- **Spending:** proposes zero-based budgeting and strong performance audits, prioritizing "core functions, not ideological programs" [S9][S6]. At a Republican forum said he would cut anything that does not relate to infrastructure or keeping people physically safe [S8]. Says state homelessness spending "clearly and obviously has not been working" and that "that level of charity belongs in the private area, not in the public" [S7].
- **Public safety:** pamphlet says he "fought for public safety" on the council and will support law enforcement [S1]. Campaign site calls for stronger penalties for repeat and violent offenders and support for police recruitment and retention [S9].
- **Homelessness and addiction:** would replace Housing First mandates with treatment-first models, end open drug-use zones, require behavioral standards in publicly funded housing and audit statewide homelessness spending [S9][S6].
- **Housing:** says government regulation, impact fees and delays made housing expensive; supports reducing impact fees, streamlining permits, reforming SEPA and incentivizing entry-level homes [S9].
- **Climate and energy:** supports repealing or rolling back the Climate Commitment Act and carbon auctions, and opposes carbon policies that raise fuel and fertilizer costs [S9].
- **Labor costs:** supports freezing or reforming automatic minimum-wage escalators [S9].
- **Local government and agencies:** would stop unfunded mandates on cities and counties, limit agency rulemaking that bypasses the Legislature, rein in emergency powers and "eliminate ideological DEI agencies" [S9].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** supports banning puberty blockers, cross-sex hormones and surgeries for minors, and protecting "girls' sports and private spaces" [S9]. Wants to restrict abortion in Washington to 13 weeks, per RANGE [S7]. See Record for his council amendment on these subjects [S2].
- **Parents and schools:** says parents, not bureaucrats, "are the primary authority in a child's life"; supports full curriculum transparency for parents, restoring "parental notification and authority in all school matters," repealing laws that let shelters "hide minors from parents," and expanding charter schools, open enrollment and school choice [S9]. Sends his son to a private Christian school and contrasted it with public schools at a Republican forum [S8].
- **Transportation:** supports finishing the North-South Corridor and shifting spending from "mega-transit schemes" to road improvements [S9].
- Did not respond to the RANGE Media candidate questionnaire [S12][S6].

- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Related: campaign planks on curriculum transparency and parental notification in all school matters [S9].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Related: campaign plank to "protect girls' sports and private spaces" [S9].

## Record
- Spokane City Council, 2022-2025: censured by the council less than a month into his term for refusing to comply with the mask mandate [S2][S7]. Part of successful efforts to criminalize open drug use, make being in city parks after hours an arrestable offense and toughen city homelessness laws [S2].
- Opposed a 2025 city ordinance with LGBT+ protections, including city health-plan coverage of gender-affirming care for employees, and proposed an amendment restricting transgender people's bathroom access and participation in women's sports, and access to gender-affirming treatment under 18 [S2].
- Crossed party lines several times to support lowering regulatory barriers to development, including eliminating parking requirements for new housing near transit and eliminating height restrictions on new downtown buildings, and sought, with mixed results, to keep development fees low [S2][S7].
- Former council liaison to the Spokane Park Board and former member of the Spokane Employees' Retirement System board [S1].
- Admitted to RANGE that he had misstated who helped draft an ethics complaint he filed against former Council President Ben Stuckart [S7].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $179,179.89 in contributions and $74,398.93 in expenditures, no loans [S11]. Contributors through July included Puget Sound Energy, the Washington Association of Realtors and the Washington Council of Police and Sheriffs [S6].
