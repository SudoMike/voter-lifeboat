## Background
Rob Tupper, of Spokane Valley, is a Democrat running for office for the first time [S1][S7]. He holds a bachelor's in mathematics from the University of Alaska and a master's in secondary education from Whitworth University [S1]. His website lists work as a commercial fisherman, Alaska oil-industry technician, college instructor, high-school STEM and math teacher (including four years at Mead High School), robotics engineer and real-estate investor; he retired in 2024 [S2][S7]. He lists volunteer work with World Relief, an Episcopal church board and a community garden [S1]. He says he filed on the last day of filing week after attending Rep. Chase's town hall on HB 2085 and learning the incumbent was unopposed [S2][S5].

In the August 4 primary he placed second with 16,778 votes (40.66%) to Chase's 17,757 (43.03%) [S7]. The Spokesman-Review notes the district has not elected a Democrat since 1992 [S6].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** said that if elected he will focus on maintaining the new state tax on income over $1 million [S6]. His website argues Washington's reliance on sales tax falls hardest on lower-income Eastern Washington families and that Eastern Washington receives about $1.30 in state services per $1.00 paid [S3]. Says he will study how taxes are spent and challenge unfair distributions [S5].
- **Spending:** statement and website say "responsible spending balanced against revenue" [S1][S3]; wants the state to "aggressively" pursue lower childcare costs [S5].
- **Healthcare:** website pledges to "fight for universal healthcare coverage" and create incentives for employers to offer public or non-profit insurance plans [S3]; would be interested in partnering with Whole Washington, a universal-healthcare advocacy group [S6].
- **Public safety:** statement calls for "community-oriented police service, coordinated with social services" to address homelessness, gangs and drugs [S1]; website frames property crime as rooted in poverty, trauma, addiction and homelessness and cites the county's police-clinician co-deployment unit [S3].
- **Climate and energy:** says increasing fossil-fuel use contributes to climate change; favors preserving forests, reviving nuclear research, agricultural research and using carbon-credit funds to cut building energy use [S5][S6].
- **Technology and data centers:** opposes data centers; his website's first issue is "No Data Centers," citing power, water, land and tax breaks [S3][S6].
- **Housing:** lists "reasonable housing" and workforce housing as priorities, without a mechanism [S1][S3].
- **State structure:** opposes Chase's autonomous-regions bill as "fiscally ridiculous" and impractical [S3][S5][S6].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** says it is not the government's business to interfere in private matters like abortion, citing his Christian faith [S5].
- **Parents and schools:** names quality education as a priority and wants civics taught starting in eighth grade [S1][S6]. No position on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs found.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- **I-1 (parental rights):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
