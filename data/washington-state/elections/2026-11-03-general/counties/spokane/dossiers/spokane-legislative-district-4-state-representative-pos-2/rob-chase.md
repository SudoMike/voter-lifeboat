## Background
Rob Chase, of Liberty Lake, is the incumbent Republican State Representative for the 4th Legislative District, Position 2 [S3]. He served 2021-2022, lost re-election in 2022 to now-Sen. Leonard Christian, and returned after defeating Democrat Ted Cummings in 2024 [S1][S11][S13]. He was Spokane County Treasurer 2011-2018 and has been a Republican precinct committee officer since 2008 [S1]. He ran unsuccessfully for state Senate (2000), U.S. House (2002), county commissioner (2012, 2018) and Spokane Valley City Council (2023), and was Eastern Washington coordinator for Ron Paul's 2008 and 2012 presidential campaigns [S11]. He holds a BA in business administration from Eastern Washington University and served in the Army Reserve 1972-1978 [S1][S11]. His voter-guide community service list includes Let's Go Washington Initiatives and the Citizens Alliance for Property Rights [S1].

In the August 4 primary he placed first with 17,757 votes (43.03%) to Democrat Rob Tupper's 16,778 (40.66%); Republican Bob Curtis had 6,731 (16.31%) [S11].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** opposed the 2026 tax on income over $1 million and says he would focus on "lowering taxes for everyone" and attracting businesses [S10]; called the Climate Commitment Act "the most damaging tax we've had" [S10]. His website describes him as a "taxpayer advocate" for property-tax partial-payment and late-penalty legislation [S2].
- **State structure:** prime sponsor of HB 2085, creating two autonomous regions (Puget Sound and Columbia) with regional governors, judges and legislators while remaining one state for federal purposes; plans to seek a public hearing next session [S8][S9][S10]. Said a lower-tax, lower-regulation eastern region would attract business [S9].
- **Climate and energy:** says climate change is not affected by human activity and "I don't believe man-made global warming is a threat"; says the state should not focus on slowing climate change and that the Climate Commitment Act is "taxing the air we breathe" [S9][S10]. His statement cites a House-passed bill to "preserve both salmon and dams along the Snake River" [S1].
- **Technology and data centers:** supports AI data centers and said the state should not cut back on building them unless they significantly affect nearby residents [S10].
- **Environment:** prioritizes litter, plastic pollution and fish and wildlife; opposes grocery bag fees [S9][S10].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** believes life begins at conception and would allow abortion only for ectopic pregnancy [S9]. Supported the initiative to bar athletes assigned male at birth from girls' sports [S9]. Website cites a perfect score from the Family Policy Institute of Washington [S2].
- **Parents and schools:** supported the parental-rights initiative giving parents authority over their child's education and health care [S9]; voted for a bill requiring school districts to give parents a copy of a child's special-education evaluation report within 35 school days [S9]; voted against ESHB 1296 (see Record) [S5].
- **Property rights and government:** website frames his platform as "life, liberty, property" and a "return to America First principles" [S2].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted against ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S4], and says he opposed it [S10]; lists Let's Go Washington Initiatives in his community service [S1].
- **I-1 (parental rights):** supported the parental-rights initiative when it was before the Legislature [S9]. Record: voted against ESHB 1296 (2025), the amendments to the I-2081 parental-rights law that I-1 would undo [S5].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** supported the initiative when it was before the Legislature [S9].

## Record
- Voted nay on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million (2026-03-09, passed 51-46) [S4].
- Voted nay on House final passage of EHB 2081, business and occupation tax increases (2025-04-22, passed 50-48) [S6], and on ESSB 5814, excise taxes on select services (2025-04-23, passed 50-47) [S7].
- Voted nay on ESHB 1296 ("promoting a safe and supportive public education system"), on House passage (2025-03-12, 56-37) and on final passage as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24, 59-39) [S5].
- Prime sponsor of HB 2085 on autonomous regions (introduced 2025-04-24) [S8].
- Statement: sponsored three bills in the last session that passed unanimously [S1]. Website: has introduced 33 bills [S2].
- As county treasurer, pushed legislation allowing partial property-tax payments and eliminating late-payment penalties [S1][S2].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $20,285.00 raised and $11,686.43 spent [S12]; contributors reported include Delta Dental, Washington Beer and Wine Distributors, Kaiser Aluminum and Spokane Valley Councilmember Pam Haley [S11].

## Endorsements
Website cites a 100% rating from The Freedom Index, a Citizens Alliance for Property Rights award and a perfect score from the Family Policy Institute of Washington [S2]. No organizational general-election endorsement list found.
