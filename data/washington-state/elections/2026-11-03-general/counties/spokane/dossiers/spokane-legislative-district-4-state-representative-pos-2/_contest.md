## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the two candidates who advanced from the August primary.

## Race dynamics
Republican incumbent Rob Chase, a former two-term Spokane County treasurer who served in the House 2021-2022 and again since 2025, faces Democrat Rob Tupper, a retired teacher and engineer running for the first time [S1][S2]. The primary was close: Chase 43.03%, Tupper 40.66%, with Republican Bob Curtis's 16.31% eliminated (rob-chase.md S11). The district has not elected a Democrat since 1992 [S2].

The campaign centers on Chase's HB 2085, which would create two autonomous regions within Washington; Chase wants a hearing next session, and Tupper, who says he entered the race after Chase's town hall on the bill, calls it impractical and fiscally harmful to Eastern Washington [S2] (rob-tupper.md S2, S3). Chase voted against the 2026 tax on income over $1 million and the 2025 B&O and services tax bills and pledges lower taxes for everyone; Tupper says he would focus on maintaining the income tax (rob-chase.md S4, S6, S7; rob-tupper.md S6). They also split on climate (Chase rejects human-caused climate change; Tupper backs carbon-credit-funded efficiency), data centers (Chase supports; Tupper opposes), health care (Tupper supports universal coverage) and abortion (Chase opposes except for ectopic pregnancy; Tupper says government should not interfere) [S2] (rob-chase.md S9; rob-tupper.md S3, S5). Chase supported the I-1 and I-638 initiatives before the Legislature and voted against ESHB 1296; Tupper has no stated position on the initiatives (rob-chase.md S5, S9). Chase reports $20.3k raised to Tupper's $10.4k (rob-chase.md S12; rob-tupper.md S8).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Chase's recorded no votes on the 2026 income tax and 2025 tax bills vs. Tupper's pledge to maintain the income tax.
2. **Climate and energy:** Chase rejects human-caused climate change and calls the Climate Commitment Act the most damaging tax; Tupper backs climate action.
3. **Record vs. renewal:** two-term incumbent and former treasurer vs. a first-time candidate.
4. **Technology:** Chase supports AI data centers; Tupper's top website issue is "No Data Centers."
5. **Healthcare:** Tupper supports universal coverage; no Chase position found.
6. **Social and parents and schools:** Chase supports the girls'-sports and parental-rights initiatives and opposes abortion; Tupper opposes government involvement in abortion and has no position on parental rights.
