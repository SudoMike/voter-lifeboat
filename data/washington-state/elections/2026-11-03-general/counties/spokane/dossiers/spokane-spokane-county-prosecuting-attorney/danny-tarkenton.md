## Background

Danny Tarkenton, 36, is running for prosecutor with no party preference [S1][S9]. She grew up in Mead [S9]. She served six years in the Army National Guard, four of them activated, deployed to Iraq and Kuwait in 2016, and reached the rank of sergeant [S1][S9]. She holds a philosophy degree from Gonzaga, a psychology degree from Eastern Washington University and a 2024 Gonzaga law degree, and is finishing a master's in forensic psychology at Arizona State [S1][S6][S9].

She externed with the Attorney General's Office in 2022 and was a Rule 9 intern in the Spokane County Prosecutor's Office in 2024 [S6][S9]. She was hired as a deputy prosecutor in March 2025, was admitted to the bar in April 2025, and worked in the criminal division and then on involuntary treatment cases before leaving the office in late August 2025 [S9]. Her statement describes a 500-plus case docket [S1]. She has not held elected office [S6].

In the August 4 primary she "came within striking distance" of McCollam, who received 50.6%; with only two candidates, both advanced [S6][S8].

## Positions

### Charging, diversion and restorative justice
- Her statement: "Spokane's jail costs $53 million a year, overdose deaths broke records, and the cycle of crime grows." It says her psychology background "will reduce repeat offending" [S1].
- Her priorities say "Prosecuting without addressing what drives the behavior doesn't make Spokane safer" and back therapeutic courts and diversion [S3].
- Wants restorative justice and diversion expanded, including mental health, veterans and DUI therapeutic courts. "Having a retributive, 'prosecute everything' philosophy is so harmful. It's inefficient. It's expensive. It doesn't work" [S9]. She says restorative approaches do not fit every crime and can be dangerous in domestic violence cases [S9].
- Said jail capacity and safety problems can be addressed "by more carefully choosing which crimes to prosecute," and compared building a larger facility to "giving a hoarder a larger house" [S6].
- Supports resentencing review that weighs the age-crime curve, the person's rehabilitation and the victim's input [S9].
- On police: "when misconduct occurs, by anyone, the law applies" [S3].

### Taxes and office budget
- Declined to take a position on asking voters for a tax increase. She argued the office can save money by becoming more efficient [S8]. She said a sales tax increase would be better spent fixing the existing jail and outsourcing support services to the community than on a new facility [S6].
- Says office data-keeping is lacking, especially for tracking repeat misdemeanor defendants [S6].

### Transparency and independence
- "This office will publish its outcomes and answer for them" [S3].
- "As an Independent, Danny believes prosecutors serve their communities, not political parties" [S1]. Her website says "No party machines. No entitlements" [S2]. She told the Spokesman-Review she supports a number of progressive causes but filed as an independent to stay free of partisan influence [S6]. She also cited the federal-agent shootings of Renée Good and Alex Pretti in Minnesota as a reason she ran [S6].

### Experience
- Argues trial experience is not needed for the elected role, which she likens to setting policy for the office: "You don't need trial experience to do a good job in this role, and you are not expected to supervise" [S8][S6].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Endorsed by Pro-Choice Washington and Planned Parenthood Advocates of Greater Washington and North Idaho [S4]. Her statement lists Gonzaga Pride Law (vice president) and Odyssey Youth [S1].

## Scoring notes

- Safety: a diversion-, restorative-justice- and treatment-first approach that criticizes "prosecute everything." McCollam leads with enforcement.
- Experience: licensed in 2025 and argues an outsider is needed to change the office [S8]. McCollam is a 13-year prosecutor and the current officeholder.
- Reform: runs as an independent, "No party machines," while drawing endorsements mostly from Democratic officials and progressive groups.
