## Background

Preston McCollam, 38, a Republican, is the appointed Spokane County Prosecuting Attorney [S6][S8]. He served as interim prosecutor after Larry Haskell retired in July 2025. In September 2025 the county commissioners unanimously appointed him to finish Haskell's term. Under state law that replacement fell to the Spokane County Republican Party and the commissioners [S6][S8]. His statement says he has served as prosecutor since July 2025 [S1]. This November is his first time on the ballot [S7].

He earned a JD from Gonzaga in 2013 and a BA from Eastern Washington University in 2010 [S1][S6]. He was a deputy prosecutor in Benton County from 2013 to 2015, then returned to Spokane County as a deputy prosecutor in 2015 [S6][S8]. He became chief criminal deputy in 2023, overseeing about 50 attorneys, and was also interim civil chief [S1][S8]. He taught trial advocacy at Gonzaga Law from 2015 or 2016 to 2022, and has instructed narcotics, homicide and gang-investigator associations [S1][S6]. He was a U.S. Forest Service firefighter and a long-term volunteer at Lakeland Village [S1][S6]. He was the last president of the deputy prosecutors' union before members dissolved it [S8].

In the August 4 primary he received 50.6% against Danny Tarkenton; with only two candidates, both advanced [S6][S7].

## Positions

### Charging, enforcement and diversion
- His statement promises to work "alongside law enforcement," hold "offenders accountable," and deliver "principled, no-nonsense prosecution." It says he "consistently stands against policies that weaken public safety and silence crime victims" [S1].
- His website says "violent and repeat offenders must face meaningful consequences" and that fentanyl dealers and traffickers "must be aggressively investigated and prosecuted" [S2][S3].
- His priorities also include "expanding alternatives to incarceration that do not jeopardize public safety," including diversion programs and therapeutic courts, with strict accountability to program requirements [S3].
- He would advocate in Olympia to restore felony charges for some drug crimes. "One benefit of having certain drug crimes be a felony is that it provided a mandatory pause in somebody's addiction cycle... we've removed rock bottom" [S6].
- Said the office is on track to charge about 2,500 felonies in 2026, down from over 6,000 a year seven to nine years ago [S8].

### Jail, regional justice center and taxes
- Supports a regional justice center that mixes confinement with medical, behavioral health and drug treatment providers, and worked on the Safe and Healthy Spokane Task Force [S6].
- Would support asking voters for a sales tax increase if it is narrowly tailored to fund the public safety overhaul [S6][S7]. "Our system is struggling. It's broken. We're straining at the seams in terms of how we fund public safety" [S7].

### Office management and transparency
- Has pushed for higher deputy prosecutor pay and a larger management structure. He says the office should have 73 attorneys but has been held at its reduced level for 2027 [S8].
- Wants more transparency about the office and a simpler court process for defendants [S6].
- On resentencing, the office has made a few requests under the 2020 prosecutor-initiated resentencing law, and McCollam says he has worked on at least three [S8].

### Victims
- "Victims should never feel like an afterthought in the criminal justice system" [S3].

## Record

- Prosecuting Attorney since 2025 (interim from July, appointed September) [S6][S8]. Chief criminal deputy from 2023 [S6][S8].
- Partnered with County Community Services on "Step Forward," a felony diversion program that routes eligible defendants to therapeutic courts. His team is developing a misdemeanor diversion program [S8].

## Endorsements

His statement lists every Spokane County prosecutor since 1969, Sheriff John Nowels, former U.S. Rep. Cathy McMorris Rodgers and U.S. Rep. Michael Baumgartner [S1]. His website adds former Prosecutor Larry Haskell, former Sheriff Ozzie Knezovich, the Spokane Police Guild, the Spokane County Deputy Sheriff's Association, Commissioners Al French, Mary Brooks and Josh Kerns, Clerk Tim Fitzgerald, the Washington Farm Bureau, and dozens of current deputy prosecutors and law enforcement officers [S4]. Baumgartner urged voters to support McCollam after Tarkenton's retracted endorsement claim [S9].

## Campaign finance

PDC summary as of the October 6, 2026 update: $114,084.33 raised, $51,202.28 spent, $1,976.25 in debt [S5]. The Spokesman-Review listed contributors including businesses tied to developer Harley Douglass, the Spokane Police Guild PAC, Commissioner Mary Brooks and Avista Corp. [S6]

## Scoring notes

- Experience: more than a decade as a prosecutor, the office's criminal chief and now its appointed head. Tarkenton was admitted to the bar in 2025.
- Safety: an enforcement-led platform (felony drug charges, aggressive trafficking prosecution, law-enforcement endorsements) that also includes diversion and therapeutic courts.
- Taxes and spending: supports a narrowly tailored sales tax for a regional justice center with treatment services. Tarkenton declined to take a position on a tax increase.
