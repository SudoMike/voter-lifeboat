## What the office does
The prosecuting attorney brings civil and criminal cases for the state and county, advises the Board of County Commissioners and defends the county in lawsuits (S2). The prosecutor decides which cases are charged, which crimes get priority given limited staff, and whether defendants are offered diversion or other alternatives (S2). The office has about 140 full-time employees, including 68 attorneys in civil, criminal and family-law divisions (S2). The ballot covers both the short term (the rest of Larry Haskell's term) and the full four-year term (S1).

## Race dynamics
Larry Haskell, prosecutor since 2015, retired in July 2025. The commissioners appointed Republican Preston McCollam, his chief criminal deputy, to finish the term in September 2025 (S2, S3). McCollam is on the ballot for the first time. Danny Tarkenton, an Iraq War veteran admitted to the bar in 2025 who briefly worked as a deputy prosecutor, runs with no party preference (S2, S3). McCollam received 50.6% in the two-candidate primary (S3). McCollam has the sheriff, police unions, every past Spokane prosecutor since 1969 and many of the office's deputy prosecutors behind him (S3). Tarkenton has the Spokane mayor, Democratic legislators, labor and progressive groups (preston-mccollam.md S1, S4; danny-tarkenton.md S4). Both candidates say they want to reduce recidivism. They split on enforcement versus diversion, on experience, and on a sales tax for a new regional justice center, which McCollam supports and Tarkenton declined to back (S3). McCollam reports $114.1k raised to Tarkenton's $43.5k (preston-mccollam.md S5; danny-tarkenton.md S5). In September Tarkenton retracted a Ferguson endorsement her campaign said was faked (danny-tarkenton.md S7).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Safety:** McCollam's enforcement-led platform (restore felony drug charges, aggressive trafficking prosecution) alongside expanded diversion. Tarkenton's restorative justice, therapeutic courts and charging restraint ("prosecute everything" is "so harmful").
2. **Record vs. renewal:** a 13-year prosecutor and sitting appointee against an outsider licensed in 2025.
3. **Taxes and spending:** McCollam backs a narrowly tailored sales tax for a justice center with treatment services. Tarkenton cites efficiency savings and opposes a larger facility.
4. **Reform/independence:** Tarkenton runs as an independent against "party machines." McCollam says his plans are his own, not a party's.
