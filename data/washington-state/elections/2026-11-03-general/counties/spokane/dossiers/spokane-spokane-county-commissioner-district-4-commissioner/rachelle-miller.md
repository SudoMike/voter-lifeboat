## Background

Rachelle Miller is a licensed independent clinical social worker with an office in Spokane Valley and a former social worker for the state Department of Social and Health Services [S3][S5]. She holds a BA in social work from the University of Montana and an MSW from Eastern Washington University [S1][S5]. Her statement lists a small business, leading the accreditation of a regional adoption program, and multiple state awards for leadership and innovation [S1]. Her community service includes PTSA vice president, disability-rights advocacy and youth hockey volunteering [S1]. This is her first run for office [S1][S5]. She lists her party preference as Independent [S1] and told the Spokesman-Review "I consider myself an independent" [S6].

**How she reached the general ballot.** Miller did not file during May filing week. She ran a write-in campaign in the August 4 primary, where Suzanne Schmidt was the only name on the ballot [S5][S6]. A write-in candidate in Washington must win at least 1% of the primary vote to appear on the general ballot [S5][S6]. The two Spokesman-Review accounts give different counts. The August 23 column reports 1,083 votes, "slightly more than 3%" [S6]. The September 14 article reports 2,114 votes, "roughly 8% of the turnout," against Schmidt's 22,128 [S5]. RANGE describes her as "an independent who won enough write-in votes in the primary to qualify her for the midterm ballot" [S7]. The column says she filed write-in paperwork in late June [S6]. The VoteWA general-election candidate list records her filing date as 8/21/2026 [S9]. The general-election online voters' guide carries a statement from her [S1]. The Spokesman-Review column had reported that a write-in would not be eligible to submit one for the printed general pamphlet [S6].

She told the Spokesman-Review that Petra Hoy of the group "Be The Change 509 – Eastside Gladiators" recruited her after the filing deadline, and that she decided to run over data centers [S5][S6].

## Positions

### Data centers
- Her statement opens: "Spokane County is not for sale. AI data centers threaten our water, our land, and our power grid." It says the centers get "massive tax breaks" while residents pay higher utility bills [S1].
- She said she does not think any data center is a "good idea" in Spokane County and would support an outright ban [S5]. RANGE reports the same view [S7].
- Her platform calls the county's four-month moratorium too short and says it "suspiciously pushes the issue just beyond the election." It points to Kootenai County's six-month moratorium and its moves toward banning data centers near the aquifer [S2].
- Her campaign manager said Miller would not support tax breaks to attract data centers [S5].

### Taxes and the county budget
- Her platform says the county's nearly $30 million deficit comes from state mandates and the 1% annual cap on property tax growth, "not because residents are not paying enough." It says: "The answer is not more taxes on families who are already struggling. The answer is accountability" [S2].
- She told the Spokesman-Review she would consider raising property taxes to help close the deficit, particularly if that decides funding for public safety and other essential functions [S5].
- She said she would explore all revenue options for a new jail and public safety facility with wraparound services. She shares Schmidt's reluctance to ask voters for a sales tax increase [S5].
- She calls for "honest accounting of where county money goes and whether it is working" and making sure "every dollar already coming in goes where it is supposed to go" [S2].
- She said she did not know enough about the commission's tax-incentive tools to comment [S5].

### Housing, homelessness and the opioid crisis
- Her statement calls for affordable housing and "real, lasting solutions for the opioid and homelessness crises, not handouts for tech billionaires" [S1].
- Her platform says the commissioner controls zoning and oversees housing funds in unincorporated Spokane County, and that land-use decisions should be made "transparently and in the public interest" [S2].
- She backs the county's opioid-settlement programs. She cites the Regional Stabilization Center, which has served nearly 7,000 people, co-responder models "keeping people out of jails and emergency rooms," and the P.A.T.H. crisis and recovery center. "These programs work when they are funded" [S2].

### Public safety and the jail
- Her platform calls the jail "overcrowded and outdated" and the public defense system underfunded, and says the state pays only 4% of public defense costs [S2].
- Her statement lists wildfire protection as a priority [S1].

### Running as an independent
- "No party sent me. No donor owns me" [S1][S2]. She told the Spokesman-Review she is "really sick and tired of the extreme polarizations in our community" and wants a swing role like Brooks's [S5].

### Parents and schools
- She worked with Rep. Rob Chase on a bill expanding parental access to special education evaluation reports. Gov. Bob Ferguson signed it in March [S5]. Her site says she drafted HB 2557 so that "parents receive special education evaluation reports at least five school days before eligibility meetings" [S3]. The Legislature's record shows ESHB 2557, sponsored by Rep. Chase, as chapter 34, Laws of 2026, effective June 11, 2026 [S8].
