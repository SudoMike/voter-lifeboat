## What the office does
Spokane County has a five-member Board of County Commissioners. Each commissioner is elected by district to a four-year term. The board adopts the county budget, sets county property tax and land-use policy for unincorporated areas, and oversees the county jail and criminal justice funding (S2). The next board must close a projected $30 million deficit for 2027, decide how to replace the county jail with a public safety facility (likely needing voter-approved funding), and set rules for proposed large data centers (S2).

## Race dynamics
Commission Chair Mary Brooks, a Republican described as a swing vote on a board of three Republicans and two Democrats, is retiring (S2). District 4 covers Liberty Lake, the southern half of Spokane Valley and rural southeast Spokane County (S2). Republican State Rep. Suzanne Schmidt was the only candidate after May filing week and the only name on the primary ballot, where she received 22,128 votes (S2, S3). Independent Rachelle Miller, a licensed clinical social worker, ran a write-in campaign over data centers. She cleared the 1% write-in threshold to reach the November ballot; the Spokesman-Review's two accounts give her 1,083 and 2,114 votes (S3, S2). Miller would support a ban on data centers. Schmidt would accept one that does not raise utility rates or harm water and the grid (suzanne-schmidt.md S1, S2; rachelle-miller.md S5). Schmidt reported about $61.5k raised to Miller's $15.1k (suzanne-schmidt.md S3; rachelle-miller.md S4).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Schmidt voted against the 2026 income tax on income over $1 million and the 2025 capital-gains and B&O tax bills, and says property taxes are already too high. Miller says "not more taxes on families" but would consider a property tax increase to close the deficit.
2. **Record vs. renewal:** a two-term legislator with eight enacted bills against a first-time independent who says "No party sent me. No donor owns me."
3. **Safety and services:** Schmidt's platform leads with backing law enforcement. Miller's leads with co-responders, the stabilization center and stable funding for treatment.
4. **Data centers** split the race most sharply, but no rubric axis covers them. Neither candidate frames the issue as climate policy.
5. **Housing:** Schmidt favors streamlined permitting and development-friendly rules. Miller names affordable housing and transparent land use without a mechanism.
