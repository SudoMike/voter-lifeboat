## Background

Suzanne Schmidt is the Republican State Representative for the 4th Legislative District. She was first elected in November 2022 and re-elected in 2024 [S1][S4]. She is ranking member of the House Labor and Workplace Standards Committee and assistant ranking member on the Transportation Committee [S1]. She was president and CEO of the Associated Builders and Contractors Inland Pacific Chapter, its workers' compensation program manager before that, and an administrator for the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers at Libby Dam [S1][S4]. She holds associate degrees from Flathead Valley Community College and Spokane Community College [S1]. She lists board service with the Greater Spokane Valley Chamber, the SBA Regulatory Fairness Board and the Washington State Building Code Council [S1]. She is 63 [S4].

She is running for the District 4 seat that Commission Chair Mary Brooks is leaving at retirement [S4]. District 4 covers Liberty Lake, the southern half of Spokane Valley and rural southeast Spokane County, including Rockford, Fairfield and Latah [S4]. She was the only name on the August 4 primary ballot and received 22,128 votes [S4][S5].

## Positions

### Taxes and the county budget
- Her statement says she is "committed to protecting taxpayers" [S1]. Her website says she "will cut waste and keep taxes in check" and "protect taxpayer dollars" [S2].
- With the county facing a projected $30 million deficit before 2027, she told the Spokesman-Review that property taxes are already too much of a burden and that she would rather grow the tax base than raise property taxes [S4].
- She said short-term tax incentives for development, like those behind Kendall Yards and Mead Works, can be a good tool [S4].
- She agreed the county needs a new public safety facility to replace the jail, and the Spokesman-Review reported she was "chagrined" at the prospect of asking voters for a sales tax increase to fund it [S4].

### Economy, permitting and housing
- Says she will "cut red tape" and support small businesses and workforce growth [S1][S2].
- Told the Spokesman-Review that growing revenue means advocating at the state level for rules friendly to small businesses and housing development, and streamlining county permitting [S4].

### Data centers
- Her statement says data centers "will not be supported without full input from constituents and ensuring no utility rates or water resources are impacted" [S1].
- Her website says she will not support a project that threatens the water supply, raises utility rates, strains the energy grid or leaves taxpayers and ratepayers "footing the bill" [S2].
- She told the Spokesman-Review data centers can bring tax and economic benefits [S4], and told RANGE she is open to data centers if they do not harm ratepayers or the environment [S6].

### Public safety
- Her website says she "will back law enforcement and keep criminals off the streets" [S2]; her statement lists "support public safety" [S1].
- Said the jail "is really in poor condition; it's not safe for the employees or the inmates" and that she wants to be involved in replacing it [S4].

### Local government
- Told the Spokesman-Review that her time in the Legislature taught her that "communities really do want more local control, rather than making legislation at the state level" [S4]. (Not a scored axis for county offices.)

### Parents and schools
- Voted no on ESHB 1296 in the House, on final passage on 2025-03-12 and on final passage as amended by the Senate on 2025-04-24 (59-39) [S8]. ESHB 1296 amended the parental-rights list that I-2081 added to state law.

## Record

- Voted no on ESSB 6346 on House final passage, 2026-03-09 (51-46). On the same day she voted yes on a motion to postpone the bill indefinitely and offered floor amendment 2540, which failed 40-56 [S7].
- Voted no on ESSB 5813 (2025), which created a more progressive rate structure for the capital gains and estate taxes, on House final passage, 2025-04-23 (53-45) [S9].
- Voted no on ESHB 2081 (2025), which modified business and occupation tax surcharges and rates and created a temporary surcharge on large companies, on House final passage, 2025-04-22 (50-48) [S10].
- Voted no on ESSB 5167, the 2025-27 state operating budget, on House final passage as amended (2025-03-31) and on the conference report (2025-04-27, 52-45) [S11].
- Voted no on ESHB 1296 on House final passage (2025-03-12) and on the Senate-amended version (2025-04-24) [S8].
- Says all eight of her bills signed into law had a Democratic co-sponsor [S4].

## Scoring notes

- Taxes: no votes on three 2025-26 state tax bills (ESSB 6346, ESSB 5813, ESHB 2081) plus her own statements against raising property taxes and for keeping "taxes in check." Miller would consider raising property taxes.
- Experience: a two-term legislator with enacted bills, against a first-time candidate.
- Data centers: open to them with conditions on rates and water. Miller would support a ban. This splits the race most clearly, but no rubric axis covers it directly.
- Safety: her campaign leads with backing law enforcement. Miller's platform leads with treatment and co-responder programs.
