## Background
Luc Jasmin III, a Democrat, is running for the open Position 2 seat that Democrat Timm Ormsby has held since 2003 [S13]. Since 2022 he has been the Eastern Washington regional representative (liaison) for Governors Jay Inslee and Bob Ferguson, serving ten counties [S1][S8]. He has owned Parkview Early Learning Center since 2014 and co-owns the Jasmin Group accounting and bookkeeping firm (since 2020); he also lists co-founding Northeast Youth & Family Services and the Washington Childcare Centers Association and serving as tri-chair of the Washington State Chamber of Commerce Child Care Task Force [S1][S8]. He chairs the Police Ombudsman Commission and the Chief Garry Park Neighborhood Council and is 3rd vice president of the NAACP Spokane chapter [S1]. He holds a BA in elementary education from Eastern Nazarene College and is pursuing an MBA at Eastern Washington University [S1]. He ran unsuccessfully for Spokane School Board in 2019 and Spokane City Council in 2021 [S6]. In the August 4, 2026 primary he finished second with 13,581 votes (32.43%), 211 votes behind Republican Natalie Poulson; third-place Democrat Pam Kohlmeier then offered him her support [S8][S10].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** supports "a fairer, more progressive tax structure that asks those with the most resources to pay a reasonable share" while reducing the burden on small businesses and low- and middle-income households, including reviewing exemptions [S4]. RANGE reports he backs higher taxes for the wealthy and continued property-tax relief for seniors [S5]. In June 2026 the Spokesman-Review reported he "will not support further tax increases in his first term," quoting him: "I think we've done enough right now" [S6]. Would champion letting Spokane pilot a "Universal Basic Exemption" tax structure [S4].
- **Spending and budget:** says closing the budget gap "can't come through cuts to schools, behavioral health, or infrastructure" [S4]. Proposed increased investment in health care, education and union protections [S7], and investment "in education from early learning to college" [S6]. Would prioritize Apple Health in the budget and reduce administrative waste [S4].
- **Housing:** supports missing-middle and infill housing near transit, incentives for affordable rentals and starter homes, down-payment assistance, energy-efficiency upgrade funds, targeted property-tax relief for seniors and low-income homeowners, and "stronger protections against unreasonable rent hikes and displacement" [S4]. Website says he will push for "investment in construction, tenant protections" [S2]. Wants to make office-to-housing conversions downtown pencil out through tax incentives, financing tools or code flexibility, and says current regulations make it too hard and slow to build [S3].
- **Public safety and homelessness:** backs "housing-first approaches that have a proven track record" and behavioral-health crisis response "so police aren't the only option" [S3]. On post-Blake drug policy supports more treatment, harm reduction and housing "combined with clear expectations regarding public use," with law enforcement focused on trafficking and violent crime [S4].
- **Immigration and federal relations:** says Washington "must act as a firewall," including ensuring state agencies "do not become tools of punitive immigration crackdowns," and supports using funding, regulation and litigation to push back on harmful federal policies [S4]. Lists immigrants' rights among his priorities [S9].
- **Health care:** calls access to health care "a human right"; would protect Apple Health and expand preventive and behavioral-health services [S4]. Website pledges to "lower costs, expand coverage, and protect access to essential care" [S2].
- **Climate and energy:** opposes expanding blanket data-center tax breaks and would condition them on labor standards and renewable-energy and water commitments [S4]. Supports dedicated funds for energy-efficiency upgrades to homes [S4].
- **Technology and AI:** supports required impact assessments and independent audits for high-risk AI systems, a ban on algorithmic discrimination, human oversight of major decisions and strong personal-data protection [S4].
- **Government transparency:** opposes broad use of legislative privilege to exempt legislators' records from disclosure [S4].
- **Local control:** says "local leaders and residents know our economy better than Olympia does" in backing Spokane's tax pilot, and supports "protecting local governments from unfunded mandates" [S4].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** supports current state protections for gender-affirming care and strengthening shield laws, and would improve access and fight unfair insurance denials [S4]. Fully supports Washington's abortion protections and wants to expand access in underserved areas [S4]. Website pledges to protect access to reproductive health care [S2].
- **Parents and schools:** pledges to "expand early learning, make childcare more affordable, and invest in public schools" [S2]; names improving the education system his top priority [S6]. No position found on parental notification, record access or opt-outs.
