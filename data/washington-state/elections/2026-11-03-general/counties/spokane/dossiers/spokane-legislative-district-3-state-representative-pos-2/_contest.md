## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including the state tax structure, K-12 and child-care funding, landlord-tenant and housing law, behavioral-health funding and criminal-justice statutes.

## Race dynamics
The seat is open: Democrat Timm Ormsby, who has held it since 2003 and chairs the House Appropriations Committee, announced in April 2026 that he would not seek re-election (S2). Republican Natalie Poulson, a special-education teacher who lost to Ormsby in 2022, finished first in the August primary with 32.93%; Democrat Luc Jasmin III, the governors' Eastern Washington liaison and a child-care business owner, was second with 32.43%, edging fellow Democrat Pam Kohlmeier (30.23%) (S3). A Spokesman-Review column noted that Poulson's share matched the district's base Republican vote and that the 3rd has elected one Republican to the House since 1948 (S4).

Poulson runs on opposing any state income tax and new broad-based taxes, cutting the B&O tax, auditing spending, enforcement-first public safety and parental rights, and describes herself as pro-life (natalie-poulson.md S2, S4, S6). Jasmin runs on cost of living, child care, education and health care, supports a more progressive tax structure but said he would not back further tax increases in his first term, and supports housing-first approaches, tenant protections, gender-affirming care and abortion protections (luc-jasmin-iii.md S2, S3, S4, S6). Jasmin reports $131.6k raised to Poulson's $9.8k (luc-jasmin-iii.md S11; natalie-poulson.md S11). Neither has a stated position on I-645, I-1 or I-638.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Public safety:** Poulson: "Enforcement first," involuntary treatment, treatment-first over housing-first; Jasmin: housing first, crisis responders, harm reduction.
2. **Taxes and spending:** Poulson opposes any income tax and new broad taxes and wants audits; Jasmin favors progressive revenue and opposes cuts to schools and behavioral health, though no new tax increases in his first term.
3. **Social policy:** Poulson is pro-life and wants limits on gender-affirming care for minors; Jasmin supports current protections for both.
4. **Housing:** Poulson centers deregulation and market-rate supply; Jasmin combines supply measures with tenant protections against rent hikes.
5. **Immigration and federal relations:** Poulson opposes suing the federal government and sanctuary incentives; Jasmin wants the state to act as a "firewall."
6. **Parents and schools:** Poulson names parental rights as a plank; Jasmin focuses on early learning and school funding with no position on notification or opt-outs.
