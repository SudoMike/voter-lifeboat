## Background
Alan Nolan is the Republican candidate for the open Position 1 seat in the 6th Legislative District and a director (Position 2) and vice president of the Mead School Board [S1]. He is a retired U.S. Air Force lieutenant colonel with a 22-plus-year career flying helicopters and fighters, including command of a pilot training squadron [S1][S6]. He holds a bachelor's in economics from the U.S. Air Force Academy and a master's in international relations from Troy State University [S1]. After moving to Spokane in 2015 he and his wife started a design and construction firm [S6][S3]. He was elected to the Mead School Board in 2023, defeating a 25-year incumbent [S6][S3]. He has served on the Spokane County Planning Commission and the state Energy Code Technical Advisory Group (2018 residential code), and previously on the State Building Code Council [S1][S3].

He finished second of six in the August 4 primary with 26% in the election-night count, behind Democrat Michaela Kelso (29.7%) and ahead of Republican Isaiah Paine (19.1%) [S4]. The seat is open because Rep. Mike Volz chose to focus on his job as Spokane County treasurer [S2].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** does not support the 2026 tax on income over $1 million; says he expects the Legislature to lower its threshold, believes it is unconstitutional under the state's uniformity clause, and that "We do not need another tax" [S2]. Pamphlet cites "rising taxes" and "reckless spending" [S1]. Campaigned against Spokane Transit Authority Proposition 1, the renewal of a 0.2% sales tax, citing STA's cash reserves [S7][S3].
- **Spending:** names restoring fiscal responsibility "through reductions in spending" as his top goal and says he does not support "another government program to redistribute wealth" [S8]. Pledges to reduce unnecessary spending and demand measurable results from agencies [S5]. At a Republican forum, named cutting "50%" of the attorney general's budget as his first budget cut [S9]. Proposes that every bill state a goal and metric and require legislative reauthorization after five years [S7].
- **Housing:** would make housing cheaper by repealing recent energy-efficiency mandates and reverting to the 2018 building codes [S2][S8]; supports reforming the Growth Management Act to reduce restrictions on buildable land [S8]; supports second dwellings and accessory dwelling units on existing lots [S2]; wants lower fees and regulations on new homes [S3].
- **Climate and energy:** says state energy policy seeks "to limit energy consumption without regards to cost" [S2].
- **Data centers and technology:** supports a "robust" review process but says Eastern Washington has good reasons to host data centers, and that local governments should decide on locations: "I believe in local control" [S2].
- **Public safety:** says the Legislature has undercut law enforcement with recent laws [S3]; campaign site calls for consequences for criminal behavior and policies that address addiction "not enable it" [S5].
- **PFAS contamination:** proposes extending municipal water service to residents whose wells are affected [S8].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** supports Initiative 638 and says he worked with school board members statewide on proposals "protecting the opportunity for girls to have safe and fair competition in sports" [S7][S2].
- **Parents and schools:** says parents "are the rightful authority" in decisions about their children and that state education dollars should follow the student, including to charter and private schools [S7][S2]. Supports school choice and "education reforms that restore local control" [S5]. Wants Washington to opt into the federal Education Freedom Tax Credit for scholarship-granting organizations [S7][S9]. Says he "fought to remove ideology from classrooms" [S7][S2].
- **Public records:** says he favors transparency but that the Public Records Act is a heavy burden on small governments; did not state a position on legislative privilege [S8].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Related: opposes the tax I-645 would repeal [S2].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no public position on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. Related: campaigns on parental authority over children's education [S7].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** supports; his campaign site asks voters to vote yes [S7][S2].

## Record
- Mead School Board (elected 2023): says he helped end a fiscal emergency dating to 2019 and reach a balanced budget, and helped adopt new curriculum with a vetting process that gave district parents a role [S7].
- Says that when OSPI "attempted to impose policies" the board considered beyond state law and in conflict with Title IX, he and the board acted to stop them [S7].
- In December 2025 the Mead board, of which he is a member, unanimously approved a staff-expression policy barring classroom decor and teacher messaging that promotes personal political, social or religious beliefs [S10].
- Says he joined other nominees in a lawsuit after Gov. Inslee passed over his nomination to the State Building Code Council, and won [S7].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $33,535.83 in contributions, $25,208.18 in expenditures, $6,000 in loans [S11]. Contributors include Mead board member Michael Cannon, companies owned by developer Harley Douglass and Avista Corp. [S2].

## Endorsements
- Airway Heights Mayor Larry Bowman and former Rep. Bob McCaslin Jr. (4th LD), per his campaign site [S5].

## Scoring notes

- Supports I-638 and school choice with state funding following the student; his opponent opposes I-638 and I-1 [S2][S7].
- Housing plan centers deregulation: reverting the energy code, GMA reform, ADUs [S2][S8].
- Prefers local decisions on data centers; his opponent favors a statewide moratorium [S2].
- Holds a local elected office (school board since 2023); his opponent has not held public office [S1][S6].
