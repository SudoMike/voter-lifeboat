## Background
Michaela Kelso is the Democratic candidate for the open Position 1 seat in the 6th Legislative District [S1]. She immigrated to the United States from Germany in 1993, joined the U.S. Army in 1995 and retired in 2018 after 23.5 years, the last 15 in the Military Intelligence Corps as a linguist and interrogator, with deployments to Iraq, Afghanistan and Bosnia [S1]. She holds three associate degrees from Spokane Community College (arboriculture, landscape management, small farms production) [S1]. She has been a Democratic precinct committee officer and state committee member since 2020 [S1] and serves on the executive board of the Spokane County Democrats [S3]. She lives in Chattaroy [S7].

She ran unsuccessfully for the district's other House seat against Rep. Jenny Graham in 2022 and 2024 [S5][S3]. The seat she now seeks opened when Rep. Mike Volz chose to focus on his job as Spokane County treasurer [S2]. In the August 4 primary she placed first among six candidates with 29.7% in the election-night count, ahead of Republican Alan Nolan at 26% [S4]; the Spokesman-Review later reported the primary result as Kelso 30%, Nolan 26% [S2].

## Positions
- **Priorities:** names housing inventory and costs, education funding, healthcare costs and rural infrastructure, including safe drinking water on the West Plains [S1].
- **Taxes:** says she supports the 2026 tax on income over $1 million and that higher earners should be taxed at a higher rate: "They got rich off of us. Let them pay their fair share." [S2]
- **Housing:** says expecting every family to own a single-family home is "folly" and that the state should "build up, not out" [S2]; proposes limiting how much land in urban growth areas can be zoned single-family only [S6]. Wants incentives for developers to include low-income units within market-rate developments rather than separate low-income complexes [S2][S5]. Says she helped write a 2023 resolution supporting rent-control bills and called the state's rent-control bill "a good first start" [S5]. Says developers of large projects should pay for water, power and sewer hookups [S5].
- **Healthcare:** campaign site says "Healthcare is a Human Right!" and that single-payer systems in other countries are "vastly superior" to the U.S. system; at a minimum she wants regulation protecting patients from large bills and making medications affordable [S6]. Told the Spokesman-Review the state could start with incremental changes, beginning with insurance denials [S3]. Cites rural hospital access as a district priority [S5].
- **Education funding:** wants school funding that does not depend on repeated local levies and bonds [S6]; proposes lowering the vote threshold for school construction bonds from 60% to a simple majority [S3]. Says the state places too many requirements on school districts that are then funded through property taxes [S2].
- **Data centers and technology:** favors a temporary statewide moratorium on data centers so the Legislature can set rules, citing power-line costs and water use on the West Plains, where wells have PFAS contamination [S2].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** opposes Initiative 638, objecting to its requirement that girls verify their sex through a health-care provider [S2]. Endorsed by Planned Parenthood Advocates Greater Washington and North Idaho [S10].
- **Parents and schools:** opposes Initiative 1 (the "Parents' Bill of Rights"); says it seems to rest on a premise that teachers are "indoctrinating" students, which she called "a ridiculous thought," and that professionals should determine what students learn [S2].
- **Representation:** says a representative must serve all constituents, not only members of her party [S1].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Related: supports the tax I-645 would repeal [S2].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** opposes [S2].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** opposes [S2].

## Record
- No elected public office; party offices only (PCO and Democratic state committee member since 2020) [S1].
- Ran for the 6th District House in 2022 and 2024 and lost both races to Rep. Jenny Graham [S5].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $6,348 in contributions, $1,836.66 in expenditures, $957.51 in loans [S12]. She pledged to raise no more than $7,000 under the PDC's mini-reporting option but has chosen to report; contributors include the Spokane Fire Fighters Union PAC [S2].
