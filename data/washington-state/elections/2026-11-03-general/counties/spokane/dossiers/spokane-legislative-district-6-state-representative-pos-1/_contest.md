## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House, for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets: K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the top two finishers from the August primary.

## Race dynamics
The 6th District covers the West Plains, parts of Spokane and unincorporated Spokane County as far north as Chattaroy [S2]. The seat is open: Republican Rep. Mike Volz, who voted against the 2026 tax on income over $1 million (ESSB 6346) [S4], chose to focus on his job as Spokane County treasurer [S2]. Six candidates ran in the primary; Democrat Michaela Kelso led with 29.7% and Republican Alan Nolan, a Mead School Board director, was second with 26%, ahead of Republican Isaiah Paine (19.1%) and Democrat Nicolette Ocheltree (12.1%) in the election-night count [S3].

The candidates disagree on most issues the Spokesman-Review asked about [S2]. Kelso supports the tax on income over $1 million; Nolan opposes it and makes spending cuts his top goal (michaela-kelso.md S2; alan-nolan.md S2, S8). Kelso opposes Initiatives 1 and 638; Nolan asks voters to approve I-638 and campaigns for school choice with state dollars following the student (alan-nolan.md S7). On housing, Kelso favors density, inclusionary affordable units and has backed rent-control legislation, while Nolan would revert the state energy code to 2018 and reform the Growth Management Act (michaela-kelso.md S5, S6; alan-nolan.md S8). Kelso wants a temporary statewide data-center moratorium; Nolan wants location decisions left to local governments (alan-nolan.md S2). Nolan reports $33.5k raised to Kelso's $6.3k (alan-nolan.md S11; michaela-kelso.md S12).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Kelso supports the tax on income over $1 million; Nolan opposes it and campaigned against a transit sales-tax renewal.
2. **Parents and schools:** Kelso opposes I-1 and says educators should decide what students learn; Nolan centers parental authority, school choice and "removing ideology from classrooms."
3. **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** Kelso opposes I-638; Nolan supports it and worked on girls' sports proposals as a school director.
4. **Housing:** density, inclusionary units and rent control vs. energy-code rollback and GMA deregulation.
5. **Tech / local control:** statewide data-center moratorium vs. local siting decisions.
6. **Spending:** Kelso emphasizes education funding and lower bond thresholds; Nolan's top goal is spending reductions.
7. **Record vs. renewal:** a sitting school-board director vs. a candidate who has not held public office.
