## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including the state tax structure, K-12 and child-care funding, landlord-tenant and housing law, behavioral-health funding and criminal-justice statutes.

## Race dynamics
The 3rd Legislative District covers most of the city of Spokane and is the most Democratic-leaning district in Eastern Washington (S3). This is a 2024 rematch: Democrat Natasha Hill, first elected in 2024 and now Vice Chair of the House Housing Committee, faces Republican Tony Kiepe, a Medicare insurance agent and repeat candidate she beat by 17 points (natasha-hill.md S1; tony-kiepe.md S1; S3). In the August primary Hill took 64.50% to Kiepe's 30.27%, with independent John Kness at 5.23% (S2).

Hill campaigns on her role in the 2026 millionaires tax (ESSB 6346, which I-645 would repeal), condominium and land-bank legislation and immigrant and voting protections; she voted for the 2025 rent-increase limits and the ESHB 1296 parental-rights amendments, and is listed as opposing I-001 and I-638 (natasha-hill.md S1, S3, S4, S5, S10). Kiepe runs on a full state budget audit, cuts over tax increases, faster housing permits and more police and jail capacity (tony-kiepe.md S2, S3). Hill reports $44.6k raised; Kiepe is under the $7,000 mini-reporting pledge (natasha-hill.md S2; tony-kiepe.md S4). Kiepe has no public position on the three statewide initiatives.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Hill voted for and campaigns on the millionaires tax and taxing the wealthy; Kiepe says "the answer is not tax more."
2. **Spending:** Kiepe centers a complete government audit and program cuts; Hill opposed child-care and early-learning cuts and supports investment in treatment and housing.
3. **Public safety:** Kiepe wants more police and jail capacity; Hill says incarceration without care does not work and favors treatment investment.
4. **Housing:** Hill backs rent-increase limits plus supply measures; Kiepe focuses on permitting speed and cost.
5. **Record vs. renewal:** one-term incumbent vs. a candidate who has never held office.
6. **Parents and schools / social:** Hill voted for ESHB 1296 and opposes I-001 and I-638; Kiepe has no stated position.
