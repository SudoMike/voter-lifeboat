## Background
Natasha Hill is the incumbent Democratic State Representative for the 3rd Legislative District, Position 1, elected in November 2024; she is Vice Chair of the House Housing Committee and serves on the Early Learning & Human Services and Capital Budget committees [S1]. She lists 19 years as a civil litigation attorney, adjunct law teaching at Gonzaga Law School (2019-2023), work as a Spokane Realtor (2023-2025) and interim editor of The Black Lens (2024) [S1]. She grew up in Hillyard, attended Rogers High School and Spokane Community College, and holds a sociology BA from the University of Washington and a JD from Southwestern Law School [S1][S8]. She ran unsuccessfully for Congress in 2022 [S8]. In 2024 she defeated Republican Tony Kiepe by 17 points for the seat [S14]. In the August 4, 2026 primary she received 27,075 votes (64.50%) to Kiepe's 12,705 (30.27%) and independent John Kness's 2,197 (5.23%) [S8]. She completed her second legislative session in 2026 [S13].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** her 2026 statement says she "led on the historic millionaire tax" [S1]. Her campaign site says she believes in "a tax structure that requires the wealthy to pay an equitable share" [S7]. In 2024 she said the state could not afford to cut revenue and needed to keep the capital gains tax [S9].
- **Spending and budget:** co-wrote a February 2026 guest opinion urging the Legislature to reject proposed cuts to the Working Connections Child Care program and early learning, calling child care "essential infrastructure" [S11]. Her site supports investments in mental health, substance-abuse treatment, housing and job training [S7].
- **Housing:** in 2024 she called for renter protections "including rent stabilization across our state," more funding and revenue to build housing at every income level, and removing restrictions on building condominiums [S9]. Her 2026 statement cites removing barriers to building condominiums, establishing land banks and securing affordable-housing investments [S1].
- **Public safety:** says "incarceration without care is not effective" for recovery and supports investment in mental health, substance-abuse treatment, housing and job training to address the fentanyl crisis [S7].
- **Climate and energy:** supports "a just and equitable ... transition away from fossil fuels" and investment in clean-energy jobs, with costs starting "with the wealthy that have the greatest environmental impact" [S7]. Her statement also cites work with labor to preserve Spokane's Waste-to-Energy Facility [S1].
- **Immigration and civil liberties:** says she "supported critical protections for immigrants and voting rights" [S1]. Co-wrote a May 2026 guest opinion arguing that the federal conspiracy convictions of three Spokane protesters at the ICE facility erode First Amendment protections [S12].
- **Health:** cites legislation adding tribal representation to local health boards [S1].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** her site pledges to keep Washington's protections on reproductive health care access and to be a "champion" for safe access to abortion [S7]. Listed among elected officials opposing I-638 (and I-001) by the NO HATE in WA campaign [S10].
- **Parents and schools:** supports "equal access to good schools and well-compensated teachers," pre-K through post-secondary education and more affordable child care [S7]. Voted for ESHB 1296 (see Record) [S4]; listed as opposing I-001 [S10].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted for ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S3], and her 2026 statement says she "led on the historic millionaire tax" [S1].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** listed among elected officials opposing I-001 by the NO HATE in WA campaign [S10]. Record: voted for ESHB 1296 (2025) [S4], the Legislature's amendments to the I-2081 parental-rights law that I-1 would repeal [S15].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** listed among elected officials opposing I-638 by the NO HATE in WA campaign [S10].

## Record
- Voted yea on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million (2026-03-09, passed 51-46) [S3].
- Voted yea on House final passage of ESHB 1296 as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24, passed 59-39), which amended the I-2081 parental-rights law [S4][S15].
- Voted yea on House final passage of EHB 1217 as recommended by the conference committee (2025-04-27, passed 54-44), the law limiting rent and fee increases for residential and manufactured-home tenants [S5][S6].
- Lists Spokane-initiated legislation adding tribal representation to local health boards and establishing land banks, condominium-construction changes, and capital investments for affordable housing, economic development and the North Spokane Corridor (N-S Freeway) [S1].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $44,597.02 in contributions and $31,021.29 in expenditures for the 2026 campaign [S2].
