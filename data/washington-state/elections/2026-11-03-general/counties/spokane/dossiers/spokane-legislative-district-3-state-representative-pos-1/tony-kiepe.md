## Background
Tony Kiepe, a Republican, moved to Spokane in 2000 as Director of Sales for Hollister-Stier Laboratories, started his own business in 2006 and currently sells Medicare insurance [S1]. He holds a BS in biology and chemistry and an MBA from the University of Memphis [S1]. He chairs the Spokane County Conservancy Board (member since 2019) and has volunteered with the Boy Scouts of America [S1]. He lists no elected experience [S1]. He sought Spokane City Council appointments in 2016 and 2020, ran for City Council in 2017 and 2019, and lost the 2024 race for this House seat to Natasha Hill [S4]; the Inlander reports Hill's 2024 margin as 17 points [S7]. He finished first in the 2024 primary for the seat [S8]. In the August 4, 2026 primary he received 12,705 votes (30.27%) to Hill's 27,075 (64.50%) [S4].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** wrote that declining downtown tax revenue reflects departing businesses and that "the answer is not tax more but look at current policies"; also wrote that "the easy thing to do is to raise taxes" and that the state should instead "look at priorities and make cuts" [S3].
- **Spending and budget:** website priorities are a "full budget audit" and "fiscal responsibility and budget accountability" [S2]. Supports "a complete government audit," cutting "waste, fraud and abuse," giving programs six months to show results, and cutting programs that do not meet expectations; notes the state budget grew from $30 billion to $80 billion in 15 years [S3].
- **Housing:** website says families deserve to afford a home "without excessive government in the way" and lists affordable housing as a priority [S2]. Supports faster permitting and consulting homebuilders on what to change [S3]. In 2024 he attributed higher Washington building costs to bureaucracy, permitting processes and fees [S5].
- **Public safety and homelessness:** wants to hire and train police, increase police visibility, "enforce our laws by putting and keeping criminals in jail," and "invest in a jail"; calls funding police the first priority [S3]. Says housing is "the wrong goal" for homelessness policy and favors getting people off drugs and into jobs, with hiring incentives for businesses that employ people in recovery [S3]. In 2024 he named safety and law enforcement as the top concern he heard from voters [S5].
- **Economy:** website lists "thriving businesses" as a priority [S2]; in 2024 he called for recruiting higher-paying employers to Spokane in cooperation with county commissioners [S5].
- **Bipartisanship:** his 2026 statement emphasizes bringing people together, "practical solutions" and restoring trust in government, and names housing, public safety and opportunity as priorities [S1].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Parents and schools:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
