## Background

Michael Cathcart, 40, a Republican, has served on the Spokane City Council since winning election in 2019. He was re-elected in 2023 and is its most senior member [S1][S3][S8][S10]. He chairs the Spokane Regional Health District board, the Northeast Public Development Authority and the Growth Management Steering Committee, and sits on the Spokane Transit Authority board [S1]. He has been council president pro tem, Finance Committee chair and Public Safety chair, and chaired the police and fire pension boards [S1]. He chaired the council's Finance & Administration Committee in 2024 [S3]. Before the council he was executive director of the business group Better Spokane, government affairs director of the Spokane Home Builders Association, and a state Senate aide to Michael Baumgartner [S1][S8].

In the August 4 primary he took second place with 28.5% to fellow Republican Dale Whitaker's 25.7% in the election-night count, behind Democrat Callie Gee's 45.6% [S6]. His lead over Whitaker was 2,466 votes in the August 7 count [S7]. He is seeking to succeed Democrat Vicky Dalton, auditor since 1998, who endorsed him [S8][S9].

## Positions

### Elections
- Promises to protect "election integrity" [S1]. At the October 4 debate he said he would maintain local control over elections, protect mail-in voting and expand post-election audits [S9].
- His website proposes livestreaming logic-and-accuracy testing, post-election audits and every Canvassing Board meeting, and expanding risk-limiting and random-batch audits [S2].
- Proposes a notification system that tracks each ballot's chain of custody through certification, restoring the ballot privacy flap, a mobile voter service station for rural areas, and possibly joining a state signature-verification pilot [S8].
- Said deciding how voting should occur "is not the decision of the auditor"; the job is "following and implementing the law" [S8].

### County finances
- Promises to be a "watchdog: scrutinizing finances, strengthening fraud prevention" [S1]. Says "I have been a budget hawk on the City Council" and would "identify efficiencies" while confronting the projected $30 million county deficit [S2].
- Proposes an open-checkbook platform showing in detail how county money is spent, including which legislation authorized each expense [S2][S10].
- His statement says that as Finance Chair he "fought for budget transparency and offered fiscally conservative solutions to budget shortfalls" [S1].

### Taxes
- On his campaign site, endorser Commissioner Josh Kerns says Cathcart opposed "major tax increases" on the City Council and led "the successful effort to ban local income taxes in Spokane" [S2]. His about page says "tax dollars deserve to be treated like what they are, which is money entrusted by taxpayers" [S3].

### Open government and redistricting
- Says he "stopped gerrymandering through redistricting reform" [S1]. His about page says he "led the charge on a charter amendment that took redistricting out of the hands of the politicians who benefit from it" [S3].
- Says he pushed for city rules requiring clear publication of how the city spends tax money [S3].

## Record

- Spokane City Councilmember (elected 2019, re-elected 2023); former council president pro tem and Finance Committee chair [S1][S3].
- Says he led a city charter amendment moving redistricting out of the council's hands [S3].
- Says he has "never missed a vote" [S1].

## Endorsements

Endorsements cross party lines. Two Democrats, retiring Auditor Vicky Dalton and City Council President Betsy Wilkerson, endorse him, as do U.S. Rep. Michael Baumgartner, Sheriff John Nowels, Commissioners Josh Kerns, Mary Brooks and Al French, Clerk Tim Fitzgerald and Assessor Tom Konis [S4][S6]. Organizations: the Spokane and Washington State Republican Party, Spokane Home Builders Association, Associated Builders & Contractors, Associated General Contractors, the Association of Realtors, the Washington State Farm Bureau PAC, City of Spokane Employees Local 270 and Mainstream Republicans of Washington [S4]. Neither Cathcart nor Whitaker had an official Republican Party endorsement before the primary [S6]. In June, county GOP Chair Rob Linebarger criticized Cathcart for accepting Democratic endorsements [S10].

## Campaign finance

PDC summary as of the October 5, 2026 update: $52,549.67 raised, $37,704.22 spent, $2,726.05 in loans [S5]. The Spokesman-Review listed donors including Avista Corp., Goodman Real Estate, the Spokane Association of Realtors and Commissioner Mary Brooks [S8].

- Reform: redistricting charter amendment and proposals to livestream canvassing and expand audits.
