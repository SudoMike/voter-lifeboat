## Background

Mike Valerien has been a Spokane Municipal Court commissioner since his appointment in 2016 [S1][S4][S7]. Before that he spent eight years as a City of Spokane assistant prosecutor, including as the prosecutor assigned to Veteran's Court [S4][S7]. He began his legal career in private practice in Spokane Valley, handling criminal defense, wills, landlord-tenant and real estate matters [S3]. During law school he worked as a 911 emergency communications operator for Spokane County [S3][S4]. He is a former U.S. Air Force officer who came to the area while stationed at Fairchild Air Force Base [S4]. He holds a J.D. from Gonzaga University School of Law and a B.A. from the University of Nebraska [S1][S7]. He is a past president of Local 270PA, the assistant city prosecutors' union [S1].

## Positions

- **Judicial philosophy.** Told The Spokesman-Review: "My philosophy is essentially that the Legislature passes the laws and it's my job to enforce the law as they've written it, as long as it complies with the Constitution." He said that where he has discretion, in sentencing or probation, he would use it "in a way that makes our community better" [S7]. His site says a judge's job "is to fairly apply the law to each case" while the Legislature writes the laws [S2].
- **Public safety and sentencing.** Says he will "hold offenders accountable and apply evidence-based strategies to reduce repeat offenses," and that judges should consider current research in deciding which resources suit each person [S2]. Said that after eight years as a prosecutor he concluded "we are not able to prosecute ourselves from these problems, we have to address those issues in some way" [S7]. His focus, per the Spokesman-Review, is on whether interventions actually work [S7].
- **Due process and resources.** Pledges to follow due process to protect the rights of the accused and to pick "the correct strategy for each case to minimize waste" [S2].
- **Public engagement.** Says elected judges should educate the public about the court's role and be open to feedback on what works [S7].

## Record

- As commissioner he hears misdemeanor, vehicle-impound and civil-infraction matters, authorizes warrants and makes custody and release decisions [S3].
- Has presided over hearings in Spokane Municipal Court's therapeutic courts, including Community Court, DUI Court and Veteran's Court (self-reported) [S3].
- No published rulings or appellate review of his decisions were found.

## Ratings

- Spokane County Bar Association Judicial Candidates Evaluation Panel (2026): "Exceptionally Well Qualified" [S6].
- SCBA bar poll (2026, about 17% of members responding, 1-4 scale): legal ability 2.85, judicial temperament 2.89, integrity 2.91, relevant legal experience 2.96, suitability 2.91 [S6].

## Endorsements

His site lists eight Spokane County Superior Court judges and three commissioners; five District Court judges, including Position 2 incumbent Jeff Smith; two Spokane Municipal Court judges and two commissioners [S5][S7]. Also listed: Spokane County Prosecuting Attorney Preston McCollam, City of Spokane public defender director Nick Antush, the Spokane Police Guild, the Spokane County Deputy Sheriffs Association, the Spokane County Lieutenants and Captains Association, the Spokane Regional Labor Council, AFSCME Council 2 and Local 492, IUPAT District Council 5 and Spokane County Courthouse Employees Local 1553 [S5]. Many judges endorse both candidates in this race [S7].

## Campaign finance

PDC summary (updated Sept. 21, 2026): $26,742 in contributions plus a $7,500 loan; $30,053 spent [S8]. The Spokesman-Review reported about $34,600 raised as of Oct. 5, including his $7,500 loan [S7].

## Scoring notes

- Formation is mainly prosecution, followed by ten years as a full-time commissioner; he also reports therapeutic-court work and an early criminal-defense practice [S3][S4].
- States an apply-the-law-as-written philosophy, with discretion reserved for sentencing and probation [S7].
- Open seat: not an incumbent, but endorsed by the retiring incumbent and runs on a decade of bench service as a commissioner [S7].
