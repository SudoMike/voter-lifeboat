## Background

Deanna Crull is an attorney who has owned the Action Legal Group law firm since 2009 [S2][S6]. She has served as a Spokane County District Court pro tem judge since 2019, an Airway Heights Municipal Court commissioner since 2020 and a Spokane County Superior Court pro tem commissioner since 2023 [S1][S2]. Her tribal-court roles include civil judge for the Kalispel Tribe (2026), justice of the Coeur d'Alene Tribe's Supreme Court (2025), appellate justice for the Nez Perce (2023) and Wind River (2025) tribal courts, and pro tem judge for the Spokane Tribe (2023) [S2][S6]. She is president of the Northwest Tribal Court Judges' Association [S1][S2]. As a lawyer she was a deputy prosecutor for the City of Spokane (2006) and Spokane County (2006-2007) and city prosecutor for Ritzville (2022) [S2]. She has done contract public defense in Lincoln County (2010-2015) and in Adams County Superior and District courts (2023-present), and dependency and termination cases in Adams and Whitman counties [S2][S6]. She holds a J.D. (2001) and B.A. (1997) from Gonzaga and graduated from the Washington Judicial College in 2022 [S1][S2]. She founded the "Ladies in the Law" group in 2019 [S1][S2].

## Positions

- **Judicial approach.** Says "It is easy to be a judge who doles out punishment. It is challenging to be a problem solver – but that's me" [S3]. Told The Spokesman-Review that a judge should weigh each person's history, the circumstances of the offense, community safety and the possibility of rehabilitation, and consider victims' rights alongside due process [S6].
- **Therapeutic courts and accountability.** Her statement says strong courts "require accountability and focus on community safety" but "should offer effective therapeutic alternatives for qualified, motivated individuals, which have been proven to reduce recidivism" [S1]. She says early intervention on underlying issues reduces future court costs, and that "time and money should be spent on individuals who are a danger to the community," using "a balance of deterrence and punishment" [S3]. She told the Spokesman-Review that rehabilitation does not remove the need for accountability and that District Court decides who is and is not a good candidate for therapeutic approaches [S6].
- **Court efficiency.** Says dockets are growing faster than budgets, "the old ways no longer serve the community like they once did," and judges need to be effective and efficient [S3].
- **Access to justice.** Cited a protection-order hearing where she had an interpreter read the petition word for word to a respondent, and said courts must do better at making proceedings understandable [S6].

## Record

- Seven years of part-time judicial service as a pro tem judge and commissioner in Spokane County courts and as a tribal-court judge (self-reported) [S2][S6].
- Ran for Spokane County District Court Position 6 in 2022 and lost to Andrew Biviano, 89,033 votes (48.6%) to 92,952 (50.7%) [S8].
- Has presented CLEs on sentencing alternatives for misdemeanor and felony cases (2019, 2021) [S2].
- No published rulings were found.

## Ratings

- Spokane County Bar Association: did not take part in the 2026 evaluation panel or bar poll and chose to use her 2022 panel rating of "Exceptionally Well Qualified" [S5].

## Endorsements

Her site lists eight Spokane County Superior Court judges or commissioners, six Spokane County District Court judges, out-of-county and tribal judges, and four retired judges [S4]. Community endorsers listed: Spokane County Assessor Tom Konis, Medical Lake Mayor Terri Cooper, Liberty Lake Mayor Cris Kaminskas, Spokane Valley Deputy Mayor Tim Hattenburg and Spokane City Council Member Michael Cathcart. Organizations listed: the Mainstream Republicans of Washington and the Spokane Regional Labor Council [S4]. Valerien's site also lists the Spokane Regional Labor Council; neither claim was checked with the council. Many local judges endorse both candidates [S6].

## Campaign finance

PDC summary (updated Sept. 28, 2026): $15,608 in contributions plus a $7,500 loan; $18,242 spent [S7]. The Spokesman-Review reported about $23,100 raised as of Oct. 5, including her $7,500 loan [S6].
