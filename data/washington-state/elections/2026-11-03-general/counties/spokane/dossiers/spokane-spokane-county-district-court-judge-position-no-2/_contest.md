## What the office does

Spokane County District Court hears misdemeanor and gross-misdemeanor criminal cases, traffic and civil cases, small claims and protection orders, about 62,000 cases a year [S3]. Its judges are elected countywide to four-year terms, so the whole county votes on every position [S4][S7]. In practice a judge's discretion centers on pretrial release, sentencing and probation on misdemeanors, protection orders and therapeutic-court supervision [S3].

## Race dynamics

- **Open seat.** Judge Jeffrey Smith has held Position 2 since 2017 and is retiring at the end of 2026 [S3]. Smith was re-elected unopposed in 2022 [S4] and is not on the 2026 ballot [S1]. Smith endorses Valerien [S3].
- **No primary.** Two candidates filed, so the race goes straight to the November ballot [S1][S7].
- **Candidates.** Mike Valerien has been a full-time Spokane Municipal Court commissioner since 2016, after eight years as a City of Spokane assistant prosecutor [S1][S3]. Deanna Crull has been a District Court pro tem judge since 2019, an Airway Heights Municipal Court commissioner since 2020 and a tribal-court judge; her law practice has included prosecution (2006-2007, 2022) and contract public defense [S1][S3].
- **Prior race.** Crull ran for District Court Position 6 in 2022 and lost to Andrew Biviano, 48.6% to 50.7% [S4].
- **Bar ratings.** The Spokane County Bar Association's evaluation panel rated Valerien "Exceptionally Well Qualified" in 2026. Crull did not take part in the 2026 panel or bar poll and chose to use her 2022 rating, also "Exceptionally Well Qualified." In the 2026 bar poll (about 17% of members responding, 4-point scale), Valerien averaged 2.85 to 2.96 across the five criteria; Crull has no 2026 poll scores [S2].
- **Shared endorsers.** Many local judges endorse both candidates [S3].
- **Money.** The Spokesman-Review, citing the PDC as of Oct. 5, reported Valerien had raised about $34,600 and Crull about $23,100, each including a $7,500 loan from the candidate [S3][S5][S6].

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

- **Judicial approach (`judicial`).** Valerien: "the Legislature passes the laws and it's my job to enforce the law as they've written it, as long as it complies with the Constitution," using discretion in sentencing and probation [S3]. Crull describes herself as a "problem solver" who weighs each person's history, circumstances, victims and community safety, and stresses access to the courts (for example, interpreters in protection-order hearings) [S3].
- **Professional formation (`safety`).** Valerien's career is mainly prosecution (eight years, including Veteran's Court) and then the commissioner bench, with early private practice that included criminal defense [S1][S3]. Crull's career mixes short prosecution stints with long-running contract public defense in Adams and Lincoln counties [S3]. Both support therapeutic courts while stressing accountability; Crull's statement names "therapeutic alternatives for qualified, motivated individuals" [S1][S3]. Neither has a documented bail position.
- **Experience (`experience`).** Neither is an incumbent. Valerien offers about ten years as a full-time commissioner in Spokane Municipal Court [S3]. Crull offers 25 years of practice and seven years of part-time judicial roles across several courts, including tribal appellate benches [S1][S3].

## Not differentiating

- Both emphasize fairness, accountability and community safety [S1].
- Both have Gonzaga law degrees and prosecution experience [S3].
