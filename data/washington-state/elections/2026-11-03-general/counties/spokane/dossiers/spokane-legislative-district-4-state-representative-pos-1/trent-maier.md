## Background
Trent Maier, of Greenacres, is an electrical engineer (BS, Michigan State University, 2003) and co-founder with his family of Trailbreaker Cider in Liberty Lake, plus AM Power Solutions & Services and AM Property Bros [S1][S6]. He served on the Greater Spokane Valley Chamber of Commerce board 2020-2026, including as chair in 2023 and chair of its Government Action Committee, and on the Greater Spokane Incorporated board, chairing its Regional Advocacy Committee 2025-2026 [S1]. He has not held elected office; this is his first run [S1][S4].

The seat is open because Rep. Suzanne Schmidt is running for Spokane County commissioner; Schmidt endorses Maier [S1][S7]. In the August 4 primary Maier placed second among four Republicans with 10,583 votes (30.80%) to Hillary Pham's 11,278 (32.82%); both advanced [S6].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** runs for "lower taxes" [S1]; website says he will "fight against unnecessary tax increases" and keep more money with those who earn it [S2]; names changing tax policy that makes it expensive for small businesses to operate among his top priorities [S5]; told the Spokesman-Review he would focus on lowering taxes [S4]. No statement on the 2026 income tax specifically found.
- **Spending:** website says government "should live within its means" and lists responsible government spending among his commitments [S2].
- **Public safety:** website supports recruiting and retaining more law enforcement officers, "restoring accountability in the criminal justice system," combating fentanyl and keeping "dangerous offenders off the streets" [S2]; named increased capital funding for public safety in Eastern Washington counties as a top priority [S5].
- **Regulation and business:** website pledges to reduce unnecessary regulations, streamline permitting and push back on "government overreach" [S2].
- **Climate and energy:** said it is "irresponsible for legislators to have set expectations through the Climate Commitment Act" and that the state should encourage free-market innovation rather than "heavy-handed mandates"; said hydropower and dams should be protected [S4]. Website opposes policies that drive up utility costs or threaten grid reliability [S2].
- **State structure:** gave a mixed answer on Rep. Chase's autonomous-regions bill, saying "I'm not ready to give up on Washington" [S4].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** asked about abortion rights, did not give a specific position; said "life is deserving of protection" and the state should support mothers and encourage adoption [S4].
- **Parents and schools:** website supports workforce programs linking schools, apprenticeships and community colleges [S2]. No position on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs found.
