## Background
Hillary Pham, of Liberty Lake, is a first-time candidate and the daughter of Vietnamese refugees who ran small businesses [S1][S3]. She served as a U.S. Air Force captain at Fairchild Air Force Base, three years as a health-care administrator and two in financial management, separating in 2023 [S1][S3]. She then worked as an intergovernmental affairs officer in the Spokane County Treasurer's Office, hired by then-Treasurer Michael Baumgartner, and since 2024 as legislative assistant to Rep. Rob Chase, who holds the district's other House seat [S1][S2][S4]. She holds a BA in urban planning from the University of Washington and was working on a master's in public administration [S1][S3]. Her voter-guide community service list includes the Liberty Lake Rotary Club, the Spokane County Veterans Advisory Board, the Spokane County United Way board and the Washington Policy Center [S1].

The seat is open because Rep. Suzanne Schmidt is running for Spokane County commissioner [S3][S6]. In the August 4 primary Pham placed first among four Republicans with 11,278 votes (32.82%), ahead of Trent Maier's 10,583 (30.80%); both advanced [S5].

## Positions
- **Affordability and housing:** says "affordability is a cornerstone of this race" [S3]; names affordability and housing as her top priorities, including better pathways to home ownership [S4]; her statement pledges to "fight for affordability" [S1].
- **Taxes and fees:** said she would focus on keeping grocery bag fees low [S3]. The Spokesman-Review reported that each of the four primary candidates focused on affordability and lowering taxes [S3]. No statement of her own on the 2026 income tax was found.
- **Climate and energy:** said the Climate Commitment Act and gas tax have created affordability issues without measurable progress on climate, and that "questionable climate policy" diverts funds from transportation [S3]; called the act a "huge monster" and said she would change pieces of it rather than repeal it, without specifying which [S4].
- **Public safety:** her statement pledges to "defend public safety" [S1]; her website lists "Stand with Law Enforcement" and "Keep 4th District Neighborhoods Safe" [S2]; she lists the Sheriff's Community Oriented Policing Effort among her volunteer work [S1].
- **Economy and regulation:** website lists cutting unnecessary regulations and supporting small-business owners [S2].
- **State structure:** supported Rep. Chase's bill to create two autonomous regions within Washington, saying it started a valuable conversation about representation and would create a task force to study feasibility [S3].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** asked about abortion rights, she did not give a specific position [S3]. (The primary-stage dossier summarized her as opposing abortion; the article it cited reports no specific position.)
- **Parents and schools:** statement pledges to "restore quality education" [S1]; website lists "Empower Parents with Real Choices" and "Quality Schools for Every Background" without further detail [S2]. No position on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs found.
