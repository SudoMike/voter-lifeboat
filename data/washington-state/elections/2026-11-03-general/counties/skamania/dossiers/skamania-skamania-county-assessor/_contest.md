## What this office does

The county assessor sets the assessed value of property for taxation, calculates the levy limits and rates of the county's taxing districts, and administers exemptions; it is a partisan, county-wide office with a four-year term [S1][S5]. The incumbent describes the work as sales-comparison, cost and income valuation and an appeal process that property owners can follow [S2]. The Department of Revenue audits the assessor's levy calculations [S5].

## Race dynamics

- Incumbent Democrat Gabriel P. (Gabe) Spencer, "the assessor for the last 20 years" in his words (he dates his start to 2002), faces Republican Cora Moser, a county employee and paralegal from Mill A on her first run [S1][S2][S6].
- Spencer was unopposed in 2022 (4,233 votes) [S4]. The 2026 primary was nearly a tie: Moser 2,157, Spencer 2,130 [S3].
- Moser runs on "a fresh perspective": a state homestead exemption, plain-language valuations and opposition to what she calls the incumbent's proposal to sell the county fairgrounds parking lot to an industrial investor [S2][S6]. Spencer runs on accurate, defensible valuations and his work to raise the business personal property tax exemption [S1][S2].
- The Department of Revenue's 2025 levy audit found two requirements and one recommendation for the assessor's office (below); Moser's campaign site cites it [S5][S6].

## Differentiating issue axes

- `taxes`: both favor targeted exemptions over new revenue. Moser pushes a homestead exemption for homeowners; Spencer a higher business personal property exemption.
- `experience`: a two-decade incumbent against a first-time candidate who asks for "new eyes."
- Neither candidate takes positions on housing, climate, spending programs or the structure of political money; those axes stay unscored.
