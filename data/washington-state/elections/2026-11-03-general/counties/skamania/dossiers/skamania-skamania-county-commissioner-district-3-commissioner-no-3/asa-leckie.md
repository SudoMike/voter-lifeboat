## Background

- Incumbent District 3 commissioner, running as a Republican; lives on Cannavina Road, Carson [S1]. Elected in 2022 over Robert J. Hamlin, 3,340 to 2,766, county-wide [S7].
- Chair of the Board of County Commissioners in December 2025 and March 2026 [S3][S4].
- More than two decades in the U.S. Army as a Special Forces (Green Beret) soldier [S2].
- His board assignments include budget and vouchers, the Wind River Business Park and forest issues; he is active in the Washington State Association of Counties [S2].
- Led the District 3 primary 775 to 629 over Paul H. Mosbrucker [S6]. Files with the PDC as a mini reporter (no itemized contributions) [S8].

## Positions

### Taxes and budget

- "We're taxed enough already—I have never voted for or supported the annual 1% property tax increase" [S1]. Says he works for "practical solutions that address our challenges without raising taxes" [S1].
- Wants county budgets to prioritize core services "without unnecessary tax increases that burden employers and families," and calls for "fiscal discipline to fund public safety and services" [S1][S2].
- Describes his approach as "straightforward, populist" [S1].

### Timber, land and county revenue

- Ties the county's money problems to its small taxable land base, the Columbia River Gorge National Scenic Area and the Gifford Pinchot National Forest [S2][S3].
- Proposed to the Gorge Commission's Economic Vitality Committee (Mar. 4, 2026) that local governments be paid for land set aside for habitat and old growth, and that the Forest Service log along paved Gifford Pinchot roads with fire breaks up to 5,000 feet on each side, which he estimated would open 176,000 acres; he said he had raised the idea with staff of Senators Cantwell and Murray [S3].
- Says he will use statewide associations to "fight for policies that protect timber revenue, reduce energy costs, and promote responsible resource management" and to push back on state regulations, taxes and mandates that burden rural businesses [S2].

### Housing and land use

- Says the scarcity of developable land makes affordable housing and commercial space hard to provide [S2].
- Argued for residential zoning in Underwood because state ecology setbacks and designations restrict timber production, and warned that conservation buyers could move more land off the tax rolls: "If we don't diversify, the community is going to continue on a glide path that is unsustainable" [S4].

### Economic development

- Wants a "predictable, low-burden environment" for business, quarterly roundtables with the Chamber and Economic Development Council, public-private partnerships where feasible, and responsible promotion of tourism and outdoor recreation [S2]. Cites his support for an ocean canoe racing manufacturer planning a site with temporary crew housing [S2].

## Record

- Dec. 9, 2025: voted with the full board to zone about 400 acres of previously unzoned Underwood land for homes on 10-acre lots, as the planning commission recommended and over the opposition of all 15 public commenters; asked staff about limiting cluster development [S4].
- Sept. 2025: joined the board's statement opposing the state Commissioner of Public Lands' designation of 77,000 more acres of state timberland as legacy forests, citing lost revenue for schools and public safety [S5].

## Scoring notes

- Explicit, repeated no-new-taxes position, including on the county's own 1% levy increase [S1][S2].
- Restraint-first budgeting ("fiscal discipline," "core services") [S1][S2].
- Favors more timber harvest and lower energy costs, against state forest set-asides [S2][S3][S5].
- Land-use record favors opening land to housing development [S4].
- Sitting board chair with a record to run on [S3][S4].
