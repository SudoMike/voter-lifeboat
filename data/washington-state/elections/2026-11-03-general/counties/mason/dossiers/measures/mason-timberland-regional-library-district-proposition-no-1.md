## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would "restore the District's regular property tax levy rate for library services, operations and maintenance from $0.22 to $0.35 per $1,000 of assessed valuation for both 2027 and 2028, subject to applicable limitations." The 2028 levy amount becomes the base for later limits under chapter 84.55 RCW. The Board of Trustees adopted Resolution No. 26-002 [S1][S2].

- **The district.** Timberland Regional Library (TRL) has 29 branches in Grays Harbor, Lewis, Mason, Pacific and Thurston counties; voters in all five decide the measure [S2]. Mason County's three branches are in Hoodsport, Shelton and Belfair [S1].
- **Who votes in Mason County.** The whole county is inside TRL; this package scopes the measure county-wide (see `pipeline/build_votewa_lite_data.py`, Mason block).
- **What the money would pay for.** The explanatory statement says approval "would provide funding necessary to maintain Library services, staffing, collections, and open hours" [S1][S2]. TRL says it would provide "more open, staffed hours and days throughout the district" [S3].
- **If it fails.** "Further reductions in staffing, books and materials, hours, online services, bookmobile services and programming will begin in 2027," and maintenance and improvements could be delayed [S1][S2]. TRL says branches with Expanded Access would rely on card access during unstaffed hours [S3]. The Mason statement for says the Hoodsport branch "could no longer be staffed" and Belfair and Shelton could face further cuts [S1].

## Cost

- Rate: from $0.228924 to $0.35 per $1,000 of assessed value in 2027 and 2028 [S1][S2]. State law allows up to $0.50 [S2][S3].
- Explanatory statement: about $40.44 a year ($3.37 a month) on a $334,000 home [S1][S2].
- TRL's Mason County estimate: $3.58 a month or $43 a year on the 2025 median home value of $333,773 (revised August 20, 2026, from Department of Revenue data) [S3]. The Mason pamphlet's statement for gives $4.75 a month or $57 a year on a median-priced Mason home of $410,000 [S1].
- TRL says libraries make up 2.82% of Mason County property taxes [S3].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

A levy lid lift on TRL's existing regular levy [S1][S2]. About 96% of TRL's operating revenue comes from local property taxes [S1][S2]. TRL says the 1% annual limit on levy growth has lowered its rate from the $0.50 maximum to $0.22 and that it "has not had a full levy rate in 25 years" [S3]. The explanatory statement says TRL last requested an increase in 2009 and that the resolution was not adopted [S1][S2].

- 2026 budget: in January TRL announced a $3.5 million deficit that put its beginning fund balance below the required threshold, with cuts to new materials and frontline staff [S4]. On February 25, 2026 the trustees voted unanimously to authorize a reduction in force [S5]. In April TRL rescinded most layoffs after negotiating with the staff union: eight involuntary layoffs and 17 voluntary exits [S6].

## Arguments for

Mason pamphlet statement, by the TimberStrong Libraries Committee (Kylie McQuarrie, Jan Morris, Laura Cameron) [S1]:
- Mason's libraries offer free books, music, video, assistive devices, computers, Wi-Fi, printing, tools and book-club kits.
- The last library levy was approved "over 25 years ago"; levy limits have let revenue fall to $0.22 per $1,000.
- Approval "would restore frontline staff, hours and local programming."

VoteWA guide statement for (generic TRL text) and rebuttal: the levy "hasn't increased in 25 years"; "a long overdue cost-of-living raise"; libraries "have endured a 25-year financial drought" and are "safe, welcoming 'third spaces'" [S2].

## Arguments against

Statement against, by Citizens for Accountable TR Libraries (Sean Swope) [S1][S2]:
- A no vote "is a vote for fiscal responsibility, taxpayer accountability, and careful budgeting" while households face rising costs and many levies.
- The library should first cut costs, improve efficiency, seek grants and partnerships and better manage existing resources; the tax "could put future investments in schools and emergency services at risk."
- "Voting 'No' is not a vote against libraries or literacy."
- Rebuttal: "Many services provided by libraries are delivered through nonprofits and community organizations more efficiently and effectively" [S2].
