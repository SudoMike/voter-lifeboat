## Background

Miguel Gutierrez (Prefers Labor Democrat Party) is a Shelton City Council member (S1, S2). He won council seat No. 7 in 2021 over George Blush by five votes, 728 to 723, after a recount (S7). He was re-elected in 2025 with 957 votes (54.5%) over Daniel Velasco (S8). He reports teaching special education, coaching team sports, training and managing AmeriCorps volunteers, community organizing and directing youth camping programs (S1). He holds a BA in political science from UCLA and a special education credential (S1). He serves on the boards of the Rotary Club of Shelton, Shelton Memorial Park and Community Lifeline (S1). His campaign site says he came to the United States with "the immigrant dreams of my father" (S4).

In the certified August 4 primary, open to District 3 voters only, he placed first of seven with 2,156 votes (39.7%), the only Democrat in the field (S6, S14).

## Positions

### County priorities
- Says the county's future "will be brighter if we choose leaders who will enhance our blessings and create a new world through Housing, Healthcare, and Hope" (S1, S2).
- Wants to "transform our government into a meritocracy with opportunity for all: Youth empowerment, living wages, child care, elder care, affordable housing, and security from medical bankruptcy" (S1, S2).
- Envisions that "the rails of our past will become the trails to our future," that water "will be clean and plentiful" and that small businesses "will thrive" (S1).
- Campaign slogan: "Join me in making Mason a SUNRISE county" (S3).

### Housing and homelessness
- In December 2024 he voted for a 0.1% city sales tax, about $310,000 a year, to house people who are homeless and provide them services. The council rejected it 4-3 (S9).
- In February 2025 he voted for a $20,000 city grant to Community Lifeline, the operator of the downtown homeless shelter, after the nonprofit lost access to its grant funds (S10). He serves on Community Lifeline's board (S1).

### Public safety
- In August 2026 he moved to pause the city's Flock Safety camera contract immediately. Only one other council member supported the motion; the council then agreed unanimously to review the contract before it renews (S12).

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- In May 2026 he voted for proclaiming June as Pride Month at the council study session, where the vote tied 3-3. He then moved to place the proclamation on the June 2 agenda, and it passed 4-3. "This is about equality," he said (S11).
