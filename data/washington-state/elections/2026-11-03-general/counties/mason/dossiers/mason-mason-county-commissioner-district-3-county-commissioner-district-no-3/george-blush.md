## Background

George Blush (Prefers Republican Party) is a Shelton City Council member, president of the Shelton Metropolitan Park Board and chairman of the Mason County Housing Authority (S1, S2). He was elected to council seat No. 1 in 2023 with 852 votes (57.6%) (S5); he had lost a 2021 council race to Miguel Gutierrez by five votes (S4). His family has run Nita's of Shelton, a downtown restaurant established in 1952, for the last eight years (S1). He studied social work at SCCC Brentwood, New York (2004) and at Olympic College (2015-2017) (S1). He is executive director of the 5XL Shelton pet food bank and a Crossroads Housing board member (S1).

In the certified August 4 primary, open to District 3 voters only, he placed second of seven with 925 votes (17.0%), ahead of four other Republicans (S3, S10). His statement says he is "the only candidate endorsed by the Mason County Republicans Party" (S1). After Commissioner Sharon Trask resigned on September 15, the Mason County Republican Central Committee named him first of three nominees to fill the seat until the end of the year (S8).

## Positions

### Public safety and code enforcement
- "I will unite Code Enforcement with the Sheriff's Office to address illegal dumping, encampments, and blight — just as we successfully did in Shelton" (S1, S2).
- Would "restore a strong Animal Control program" (S1).
- Says he has fought for "cracking down on crime and encampments" (S1).
- Would improve roads and "expand water access for firefighting, especially as wildfire risks increase" (S1).

### County finances and spending
- "I am committed to ending wasteful spending and living within our means — the same common-sense discipline that has kept Shelton financially stable" (S1, S2).
- Says he has fought for "a cleaner, safer, and more affordable Mason County by opposing garbage rate hikes ... and promoting fiscal responsibility" (S1).

### Housing and homelessness
- In December 2024 he voted against a 0.1% city sales tax, about $310,000 a year, to house people who are homeless and provide them services; it failed 4-3 (S6).

### Other
- Lists "protecting free speech" among the things he has fought for (S1).

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- In May 2026 he voted against proclaiming June as Pride Month, at the study session and in the council's 4-3 vote to issue it. "I don't think that this is the place for a proclamation," he said (S7).
