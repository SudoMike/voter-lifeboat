## What the office does
The three-member Board of Mason County Commissioners is the county's legislative authority: it adopts the county budget, passes ordinances, and manages county property, roads and services (S4). Commissioners are nominated by district in the primary and elected by the voters of the whole county in the general election (S3). Only District 3 voters cast ballots in the August primary; voters county-wide choose the commissioner in November (S6).

## Race dynamics
Open seat: Commissioner Sharon Trask did not seek another term (S6). She later resigned effective September 15 amid a voter registration challenge (S7, S8). The Mason County Republican Central Committee sent the county three names to fill the vacancy until the end of the year, George Blush first (S7). No appointment had been reported in the sources reviewed.

Both finalists sit on the Shelton City Council (S6). In the certified August 4 primary, Miguel Gutierrez (Prefers Labor Democrat Party), the only Democrat in a field of seven, received 2,156 votes (39.7%); George Blush (Prefers Republican Party) was second of five Republicans with 925 (17.0%) (S5, S6). The two met in 2021, when Gutierrez won a council seat by five votes (S6). Write-in candidate Jackie Jewett is also running (S7, S11). By early October Gutierrez had raised $31,040 and Blush $9,632 (S11).

Gutierrez's statement calls for "Housing, Healthcare, and Hope": living wages, child care, elder care, affordable housing and "security from medical bankruptcy" (S1, S2). Blush runs on a "cleaner, safer, and more prosperous" county: code enforcement and the sheriff working together against illegal dumping, encampments and blight, restored animal control, roads and firefighting water, and ending wasteful spending (S1, S2). On the council they split on a 2024 sales tax for homeless housing and services, which Gutierrez backed and Blush opposed (S9). They also split on a 2026 Pride Month proclamation (S10).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Safety:** Blush proposes joint code-enforcement and sheriff action against encampments and crime (S1). Gutierrez backed a homeless-services tax (S9) and shelter funding, and in 2026 moved to pause the city's Flock camera contract (miguel-gutierrez.md S10, S12).
2. **Housing and taxes:** the split vote on the 0.1% homeless-housing sales tax (S9). Gutierrez also names affordable housing as a goal (S1); Blush chairs the Mason County Housing Authority (S1).
3. **Spending:** Blush promises to end wasteful spending and live within means (S1). Gutierrez's goals (living wages, child care, elder care) point to more public investment (S1).
4. **Experience:** both are sitting council members (S6). Gutierrez has served since 2022 and was re-elected in 2025 (miguel-gutierrez.md S7, S8); Blush since 2024, and he is also park board president and housing authority chair (S1; george-blush.md S5).
