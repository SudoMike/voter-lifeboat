## What the office does
The county auditor records deeds and other documents, keeps the county's accounts, issues and registers warrants, and publishes the county's financial reports (S2); the auditor also registers voters and conducts every election in the county (S3). The Mason County statements add licensing, passports and permanent records (S1).

## Race dynamics
Incumbent Steve Duenkel (Prefers Republican Party), who won the seat in 2022 by 360 votes over Democrat Paddy McGuire (S7), faces Tamra "Tam" Ingwaldson (Prefers Democratic Party). Ingwaldson led the certified August 4 primary with 40.68% to Duenkel's 37.10%; a second Republican, Monika Balmer, took 14.58% (S4).

Duenkel runs as the county's financial "watch dog," on internal audits, election security and voter-roll maintenance; in 2026 he publicly disputed commissioners over whether the county meets its reserve policy (S5), and he filed and then withdrew deepfake lawsuits against a commissioner and a primary opponent's campaign treasurer (S6; steve-duenkel.md). Ingwaldson, a former United Way executive director, argues that an auditor who chairs the county Republican Party has a conflict of interest and promises nonpartisan, transparent administration and better support for staff (tamra-tam-ingwaldson.md).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience/record:** a first-term incumbent with a corporate-finance background running on his record against a first-time candidate from nonprofit management.
2. **Spending/accountability:** Duenkel's platform is financial oversight and internal audits, including warnings about county reserves; Ingwaldson states no position on county finances beyond "accurate budget information."
3. Election administration (partisanship of the office, voter-roll maintenance, transparency) is the core disagreement but maps to no rubric axis: it is not money and power in politics (reform).
4. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives: none found for either candidate.
