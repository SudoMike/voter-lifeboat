## Background

Steve Duenkel (Prefers Republican Party) has been Mason County Auditor since January 2023 [S1][S4]. He won the office in 2022, defeating incumbent Paddy McGuire 14,739 votes to 14,379 [S8]. His pamphlet statement lists Precinct Committee Officer and Mason County Republican Party Chair as elected experience; a 37-year career at Boeing Defense, Space & Security, Safeco and a small business, with responsibility for budgets, accounting and financial reporting; chair of the Washington State Republican Party Compensation Committee and member of its Election Integrity Committee; degrees in chemistry and biology (B.S.) and an MBA in finance; and service as a Mason County fire chaplain [S1][S2][S3].

In the certified August 4 primary he placed second with 7,284 votes (37.10%), behind Tamra Ingwaldson's 7,987 (40.68%); Monika Balmer, also a Republican, received 2,863 and Vivian Glass 1,491 [S9].

## Positions

### County finances and oversight
- Describes the auditor as the county's "watch dog" with a duty to "ensure fiscal accountability, contract compliance, and election integrity"; promises to keep working with commissioners to "responsibly steward your tax dollars" and give "regular accounting of our county's financial health" [S1][S4].
- Next-term plan: continue internal audits and internal controls "to ensure your tax dollars are properly spent," strengthen budget development and oversight with the commissioners, and add automated compliance and fraud-control reporting to the financial system [S3].
- In June 2026 he told commissioners the county was below its general fund reserve policy, citing a report the treasurer had presented; Commissioner Pat Tarzwell called that "misinformation" and said the county held about $23 million against a $15-20 million policy range. Duenkel: "Oversight of the county's finances is my job ... I will not be bullied into not performing my statutory duties" [S7].
- His site reports he submitted a 2027 preliminary budget in which the Auditor's Office general fund budget is 7.2% below 2026, mostly from a reorganization of the office [S3].

### Elections administration
- Promises to keep processing ballots "with the highest level of fidelity to protect the rights of every legal voter" and to keep internal auditing "to maintain accurate voter registration data, uncover and address election system vulnerabilities, and prevent fraud" [S1].
- Next-term plan: upgrade tabulation equipment to the Election Assistance Commission's "VVS2.0 compliant test criteria", keep the voter registration database "current and correct," and "protect your right to vote regardless of changes to Federal and State elections law" [S3].
- Claims of record on his site: "15 flawless elections plus 1 manual recount," temporary vote centers in Belfair and the Shelton Senior Center, and 15 drop boxes countywide [S3].

### Recording and licensing
- Offers voluntary title-fraud protection services (Fraud Notify, "Frozen Deed" sign-ups), and plans to seek legislation against deed fraud and to introduce voluntary pet licensing; introduced passport service and free DD-214 recording for veterans [S1][S3].

### Campaign conduct
- In July 2026 he sued Commissioner Sharon Trask, her husband and a primary opponent's campaign treasurer under the state's new synthetic-media law (RCW 29B.35.010) over AI-altered Facebook images of him; the defendants called the suits baseless and an attack on protected political speech [S5]. He dismissed both with prejudice on July 29 and, per the defendants' lawyer, agreed to pay their $8,215 in attorney fees [S6].
