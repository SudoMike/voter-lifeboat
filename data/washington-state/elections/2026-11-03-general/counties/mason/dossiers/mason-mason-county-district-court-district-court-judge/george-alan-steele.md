## Background

- George Alan Steele (nonpartisan office) began his judicial career on February 1, 2009, in Shelton Municipal Court and started his first term as Mason County District Court Judge on January 1, 2019; he was re-elected in 2022 [S1]. He told the Journal in 2022 that he began practicing law in 1982 [S3].
- His pamphlet entries for legal/judicial experience, education and community service read "No information submitted" [S1].
- Elections: unopposed in 2022 (17,796 votes; 753 write-ins) [S2] and in 2026 [S1]. PDC summary as queried on 2026-10-09: $1,000 raised, $25 spent [S7].

## Judicial approach and record

- Statement: "A judge is not a policy maker. A judge's duties are to impartially hear cases, apply the laws to the facts and fairly decide the cases," which "also requires that a judge must be prepared at times to make rulings that the judge does not want to make" [S1].
- Says he has worked to preserve the independence of the courts, has done "everything judicially possible" to mitigate the shortage of public defenders, has commented on proposed court rules and authored two that were adopted [S1].
- Cites Mason County's restart of jury trials in 2020, after the Supreme Court lifted its stay, as a case where "we led the State" [S1].
- Crossbow protection-order case (2025): Steele declined three times to find probable cause that a Belfair man willfully violated an ex parte protection order requiring him to stay 1,000 feet from a neighbor living less than 100 feet away, and released him on personal recognizance. He wrote that the order "merely said stay a thousand feet away with no specific instruction, to leave his home," and that ordering someone out of their home in an ex parte order "is a very unusual situation." He denied Prosecutor Michael Dorcy's affidavit of prejudice as untimely; Dorcy then sought a writ of mandamus [S4]. Superior Court Judge Monty Cobb removed Steele from the case [S5].
- In an August 2025 column he described the District Court's new facility in Building 10, with two courtrooms each with a jury deliberation room [S6].
