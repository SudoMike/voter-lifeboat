## Background
Chelsea Dimas, of Sunnyside, is the Democratic candidate for the 14th District, Position 1, in a rematch of 2024, when Rep. Gloria Mendoza defeated her 54.3% to 45.7% [S5]. She was raised by immigrant farmworkers in the Yakima Valley [S1][S2]. She works for Civil Survival, a nonprofit that assists and advocates for people who are or have been incarcerated [S4][S5], and lists prior work for the Washington State House of Representatives, the Toppenish School District and Habitat for Humanity [S1]. She has served on the Washington State Human Rights Commission and the Washington State Commission on Hispanic Affairs, on the Sunnyside School District DEI committee, and as a United Farm Workers volunteer; she is a Democratic precinct committee officer [S1][S5]. She holds a BFA from Pacific Lutheran University and an AA from Yakima Valley College [S1]. The only elected position her statement lists is Democratic precinct committee officer [S1].

In the August 4, 2026 primary she placed second with 2,910 votes (43.3%, unofficial election-night count) to Mendoza's 3,417; she led in Franklin County and trailed narrowly in Yakima County (1,932 to 1,961) [S6].

## Positions

### Taxes and spending
- Called the 2026 millionaire's tax "a great path forward," while saying it is "worrisome" the law lacks language ensuring it will not affect working people; wants to "relieve taxes off of working people" [S5].
- Says she originally supported the 2025 gas tax but changed her view after seeing its effect on working people in agriculture [S5].
- "110% in support of more funding for schools," including mental-health resources, support staff and livable wages for educators; supports early learning and universal childcare [S4][S5][S2].

### Housing
- Would "build more affordable housing, strengthen protections for renters, provide relief for struggling homeowners, and expand down-payment assistance for first-time and first-generation homebuyers" [S2]; also says increasing housing supply would reduce cost pressure and criticizes out-of-state landlords holding local housing [S5]. The Yakima Herald-Republic lists strengthening tenant protections among her priorities [S6].

### Labor and agriculture
- Supports collective bargaining rights for farmworkers "regardless of immigration status" and lobbied for that bill with the United Farm Workers; says farms are cutting hours under the farmworker overtime law and that collective bargaining would close loopholes [S4].
- Would protect workers from wage theft and retaliation and expand apprenticeships and career and technical education [S2].

### Public safety
- Would fund recruitment and training of law enforcement officers and expand behavioral-health and crisis-response services, and invest in "proven approaches to preventing domestic violence, gun violence, addiction, and youth crime"; says safety requires "accountability, trust, prevention, and effective emergency response" [S2].

### Climate and environment
- Would "hold polluters accountable," protect safe drinking water, and support "clean-energy investments that create good-paying union jobs" and climate preparedness for agricultural workers, firefighters and rural communities [S2].

### Technology and data centers
- "I am completely against data centers"; wants a ban on new data centers until regulations ensure they are built sustainably and that corporations, not communities, pay the costs; spoke against them at a Yakima County Commissioners meeting [S4].

### Immigration
- Would "prevent state and local resources from being used for federal immigration enforcement," protect workers who report unsafe conditions from deportation risk, and expand legal assistance and rapid-response resources for families targeted by immigration enforcement [S2].

### Health care
- Would protect access to preventive and reproductive health care, lower prescription costs and preserve rural services; says care should be available "regardless of income, zip code, language, gender identity, or immigration status" [S2].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Would "protect access to preventive and reproductive healthcare" and says care should be available regardless of gender identity [S2]. No statement on school sports found.

### Parents and schools
- Supports "culturally relevant curricula" among education priorities [S2]; says the Legislature has been distracted by "identity politics" fights rather than school funding [S4]. No position on parental notification, record access or opt-outs found.

## Record
No legislative record. PDC (updated 2026-10-07): $71,477.60 raised and $41,472.88 spent [S7].

## Endorsements
Her campaign lists local officials including Sunnyside Mayor Vicky Frausto and other Sunnyside council and school board members, Mabton Mayor Martha Gonzalez, Pasco and Prosser council members, former Yakima mayor Janice Deccio, and former Toppenish council member Ezequiel Morfin [S3].

## Scoring notes
- First-time officeholder candidate in a rematch against the one-term incumbent [S5].
- Supports the millionaire's tax, more school funding and universal childcare [S4][S5].
- Opposes new data centers outright, in contrast to Mendoza's "in the middle" stance [S4].
- Would limit state cooperation with federal immigration enforcement [S2].
- Supports collective bargaining for farmworkers; Mendoza does not commit [S4].
