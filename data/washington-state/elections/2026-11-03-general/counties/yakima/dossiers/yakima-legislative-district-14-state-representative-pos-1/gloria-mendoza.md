## Background
Gloria Mendoza, of Grandview, is the Republican State Representative for the 14th Legislative District, Position 1, first elected in 2024 when she defeated Chelsea Dimas 54.3% to 45.7% [S16]. She is Assistant Minority Whip and serves on the Transportation (assistant ranking minority member), Environment & Energy, and Postsecondary Education & Workforce committees [S3][S2]. She was a Grandview City Council member (elected 2013) and mayor (elected 2019), eight years in all, and lost her 2023 mayoral re-election bid [S16][S17][S2]. She founded GMC Companies / GMC Training Institute, which offers vocational training such as commercial driver licensing, GED preparation, English classes and forklift certification [S1][S16]. She describes herself as a former seasonal farmworker and graduated from Grandview High School and Yakima Valley Community College [S1][S15]. In January 2025 she delivered the Republican response to Gov. Bob Ferguson's inaugural address [S20].

In the August 4, 2026 primary she placed first with 3,417 votes (50.87%, unofficial election-night count) to Dimas's 2,910 (43.3%); within Yakima County the count was 1,961 to 1,932 [S17].

## Positions

### Taxes and spending
- Statement: during her first term she "fought to ... oppose new taxes and burdensome regulations" [S1]; her website says she will "oppose new taxes and regulations" and stop "runaway price increases and government spending" [S2].
- Says the state budget has grown from $38 billion to over $80 billion, that the majority party does not "have a handle on our budget," and that the state should "look at how we keep what we have without continuing to increase our budget"; skeptical of the schools superintendent's proposal to add $2 billion for schools [S15].
- In her 2025 Republican response she called for eliminating taxes on diapers, baby essentials and food, and spoke against any new taxes [S20].
- Co-wrote that she voted no on the 2025 operating budget and "against the highest tax increases in the history of our state" [S18]; wrote that the governor approved "the largest tax increase in state history" and criticized the 2025 gas-tax increase and the sales-tax expansion to new business activities [S19].
- Her website lists $82 million in 2025-2026 capital funding for district projects (police stations, water systems, a hospital, parks) [S2].

### Climate and energy
- To reduce costs: "Get rid of the Climate Commitment Act," citing fuel costs for farmworkers who commute long distances [S16]; the Yakima Herald-Republic reports she has emphasized repealing the CCA [S17].
- Says "environment and energy policies" strain farms and that energy policies imposed on developers make homes unaffordable [S16].

### Housing
- Attributes high home prices partly to energy policies imposed on developers [S16]. Record: voted against the 2025 rent-stabilization bill (see Record) [S10].

### Public safety
- In her 2025 Republican response: "it is time to finally fund the police"; called for more police funding and staffing, tougher penalties for retail and vehicle theft, and tougher penalties on fentanyl manufacturers and dealers along with more treatment facilities [S20].
- Her website cites support for SB 5868, adding a ninth Yakima County Superior Court judge to reduce backlogs and "improve public safety" [S2], and promises "safer streets" [S2].

### Agriculture and labor
- Opposes the overtime requirement for seasonal farmworkers, saying farms now hire more people and workers are leaving the state; unsure about collective bargaining for farmworkers and concerned current union laws hurt farmworkers [S15].

### Technology and data centers
- Says she is "kind of in the middle" on data centers, has heard positive reports from communities like Quincy, and wants more study and legislation "so that it benefits our communities" [S15].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Statement says she fought to "defend family values" [S1]. Record: voted against SSB 5917 on access to abortion medications [S12].

### Parents and schools
- Co-wrote that she and Rep. Manjarrez "fought to protect parents' rights, including access to records, review materials and receive notifications," and that the majority "had the votes to gut the parental rights initiative" [S18]. Wrote that the governor signed with an emergency clause a bill "that gutted the Parental Bill of Rights" [S19].
- Says unfunded state mandates on schools make their work harder and that schools focus on things "that [don't have] anything to do with the kids" [S16].

### Immigration
- No statement found. Record: voted against the immigrant worker protections bill (see Record) [S11].

## Scoring notes
- One-term incumbent and former two-office Grandview official facing the candidate she defeated in 2024 [S16].
- Recorded no votes on the 2026 income tax and the 2025 B&O, services and transportation revenue bills, plus repeated anti-tax statements [S4][S5][S6][S7][S18][S20].
- Wants the Climate Commitment Act repealed and voted against the clean fuels bill [S9][S16].
- Opposed the 2025 parental-rights amendments and wrote in support of parents' notification and record-access rights [S8][S18].
- Neutral-to-open on data centers, in contrast to Dimas's call for a ban [S15].
