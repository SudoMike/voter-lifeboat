## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including K-12 funding, the tax structure, housing and land-use rules, labor law and criminal-justice statutes. The 14th District covers east Yakima, the Lower Yakima Valley and most of the Yakama Reservation, west to Lyle and east into part of Pasco [S2].

## Race dynamics
A rematch of 2024: Republican Rep. Gloria Mendoza, a former Grandview mayor and vocational-training business owner, faces Democrat Chelsea Dimas of Sunnyside, who works for the nonprofit Civil Survival; Mendoza won in 2024 with 54.3% [S3]. In the August primary Mendoza led 50.9% to 43.3%, and the Yakima County portion was nearly even (gloria-mendoza.md S17). Mendoza has raised about twice as much (gloria-mendoza.md S21; chelsea-dimas.md S7).

The candidates split on taxes (Mendoza voted against the 2026 millionaire's tax and the 2025 tax bills; Dimas calls the millionaire's tax "a great path forward"), climate (Mendoza would repeal the Climate Commitment Act), school funding (Dimas backs more funding and universal childcare; Mendoza wants to hold the budget), data centers (Dimas wants a ban; Mendoza is "in the middle"), farmworker collective bargaining (Dimas supports it; Mendoza is skeptical) and immigration enforcement (Dimas would bar state resources from federal enforcement; Mendoza voted against the 2026 immigrant worker protections bill) [S2][S3] (gloria-mendoza.md S4, S11, S16; chelsea-dimas.md S2). Mendoza opposed the 2025 amendments to the parental-rights law; Dimas has no stated position on parental rights (gloria-mendoza.md S8, S18).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Mendoza's recorded no votes on the 2026 income tax and 2025 revenue bills vs. Dimas's support for the millionaire's tax.
2. **Spending:** Mendoza's budget restraint vs. Dimas's call for more school funding and universal childcare.
3. **Climate and energy:** Mendoza would repeal the Climate Commitment Act; Dimas backs clean-energy investment.
4. **Technology:** Dimas would ban new data centers; Mendoza is undecided and voted against the 2026 large-energy-facilities bill.
5. **Immigration:** Dimas would limit state cooperation with federal enforcement; Mendoza voted against immigrant worker protections.
6. **Record vs. renewal:** one-term incumbent with eight years in Grandview city office vs. a first-time officeholder candidate.
7. **Parents and schools:** Mendoza's record and statements on parental notification and record access; no Dimas position.
