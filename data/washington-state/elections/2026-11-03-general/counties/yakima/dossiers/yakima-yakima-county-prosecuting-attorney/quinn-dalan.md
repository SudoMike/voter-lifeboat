## Background
Quinn Dalan has practiced law in Yakima County since 2008 and has not held elected office [S1][S4]. She began as a deputy prosecuting attorney in the Yakima County Prosecuting Attorney's Office, moved to the special assault unit handling felony, domestic-violence and sexual-assault cases, worked in juvenile court, and left in 2011 as supervisor of the District Court unit [S1][S4]. She cites more than 65 trials in state and federal court [S1]. She has also practiced family law and criminal and juvenile defense, including for the county Department of Assigned Counsel, and has served as a pro tem judge [S2][S4]. The Herald-Republic reports she has been executive director of Central Washington Legal Aid since 2019 [S4][S5]; her voter-guide statement lists "YWCA Yakima, Director of Development" under other professional experience, without saying whether it is current [S1]. She is municipal prosecutor for Wapato (since 2022), Granger and Zillah [S1][S4]. She holds degrees from Gonzaga University and Regent University School of Law and co-chairs the Domestic and Sexual Violence Committee of the state Supreme Court's Gender and Justice Commission [S1][S2].

## Positions
- **Priorities:** her site lists protecting families from violent crime and drug trafficking, restoring "safety, trust, and accountability" in the justice system, and putting "public safety over politics" [S2].
- **Approach:** describes a career focused on "public safety, accountability, and strong partnerships with law enforcement" and "fair and just systems that hold offenders accountable"; says her defense and pro tem judge experience gives her a balanced view of the system [S1][S2].
- **Partisanship:** says she grew up Republican and believes in rule of law, personal responsibility and fiscal responsibility, has donated to candidates of both parties, and thinks the office should serve the community regardless of party [S4].

## Record
- The Yakima County Republican Party, which endorsed her opponent, ran a months-long online campaign against her (graphics, an AI-generated video and a website created by party chair Matt Brown), arguing she is not a Republican based on Democratic presidential-primary declarations in 2020 and 2024, a 2014 donation to Gov. Jay Inslee and her board memberships [S4]. Dalan said the party took things out of context and noted her donations to and endorsements of Republicans [S4].
- The party also posted a police report from a 2011 case in which her now-husband was accused of child rape; a judge dismissed the case with prejudice in 2012 after prosecutors conceded the accuser had recanted some allegations. The report records Dalan, then a deputy prosecutor not assigned to the case, telling a colleague she believed the accuser was lying, using an expletive; Dalan said she regrets the remark, made in a personal capacity, and would recuse herself from any case she felt that way about [S4].
- Her opponent's campaign says she has not prosecuted a felony since leaving the office, which it dates to 2013 (tyler-haueter.md S2); the Herald-Republic dates her departure to 2011 [S4].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $52,494.72 raised and $46,187.48 spent [S6].
