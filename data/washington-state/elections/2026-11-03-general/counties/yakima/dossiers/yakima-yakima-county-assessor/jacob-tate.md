## Background
Jacob Tate is the incumbent Yakima County Assessor, seeking a second four-year term [S1][S2]. He has worked for the county more than 30 years, including as chief deputy assessor under two previous assessors and as property database manager [S1][S2]. He holds the International Association of Assessing Officers' Mass Appraisal Specialist designation and reports more than 500 hours of valuation and assessment training through the state Department of Revenue and the IAAO [S1][S2]. He is a U.S. Navy veteran (nuclear machinist's mate), a lifetime VFW member and vice president of the Washington State Association of County Assessors [S2]. He lists his party preference as Republican [S1][S3].

## Positions
- **Office role and taxes:** says increasing property values does not increase what taxing districts collect and lowering everyone's values does not reduce it; the assessor's job is "accurate and equitable appraisals," not the highest or lowest values [S2]. Encourages taxpayers to look at what their taxing districts request and publishes that levy history online [S2][S4].
- **Transparency and service:** says he helped put county property information online more than 25 years ago; taxpayers can now see the data behind their valuation, search comparable sales and see district requests [S2]. Plans online applications for senior and disability property-tax exemptions [S2]. Keeps an open-door policy [S2].
- **Efficiency:** says the office went from 34 employees when he started to 24 positions (22 filled) through modernization, and is replacing a 30-year-old assessment and tax computer system [S2]. His statement cites "assessment practices applied consistently across all taxpayers" and modernized software [S1].
