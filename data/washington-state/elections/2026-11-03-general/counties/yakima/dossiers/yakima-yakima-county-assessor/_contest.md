## What the office does
The county assessor determines the fair market value of every taxable property each year, keeps the property records and applies property-tax exemptions such as those for qualifying seniors, disabled retirees and disabled veterans [S2][S3]. The assessor does not set the amount of property tax collected: cities, the county, school, fire and other taxing districts decide how much revenue to request within state limits, and the assessor's values divide that total among property owners [S2][S3].

## Race dynamics
Two Republicans are on the November 3 ballot: incumbent Jacob Tate, seeking a second four-year term after more than 30 years in the office, and Keith Effler, a commercial photographer, developer and Republican precinct committee officer endorsed by the Yakima County Republican Party [S1][S2]. The Herald-Republic describes the race as a choice between change and experience [S2]. Tate stresses accurate and equitable appraisal, staff reductions and software modernization; Effler stresses customer service, an online "data-depot" explaining valuations, and a pledge to cut taxpayers' burden as far as the law allows (jacob-tate.md S1, S2; keith-effler.md S1, S2, S3). Both acknowledge that the assessor does not set total tax revenue [S2]. Tate reported raising $14,553; Effler filed for mini-reporting (under $7,000) [S2].

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** a 30-year office veteran and incumbent vs. a first-time candidate from business.
2. **Taxes:** Effler frames the job around minimizing collections and making Yakima County "the cheapest tax base in Washington"; Tate says values do not change what districts collect and the assessor's job is accurate, equitable appraisal.
3. **Spending:** Tate cites cutting the office from 34 employees to 24 positions through modernization.
