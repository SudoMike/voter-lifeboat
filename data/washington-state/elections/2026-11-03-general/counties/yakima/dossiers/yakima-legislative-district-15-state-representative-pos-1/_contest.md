## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The 15th District spans Benton and Yakima counties [S1].

## Race dynamics
Republican Rep. Chris Corry, the House Republican Deputy Minority Leader, who represented the 14th District from 2019 and the 15th since 2025, faces Democrat Jack McEntire of Selah, a sculptor and vice-chair of the Yakima County Democrats [S1][S2]. McEntire previously ran for a 15th District House seat in 2018 and 2020 (jack-mcentire.md S2). Corry has raised $229,050 to McEntire's mini-reporting campaign (chris-corry.md S17; jack-mcentire.md S3).

The contrast is between an incumbent with a long recorded voting record against new taxes, the Climate Commitment Act, the 2021 police-accountability bills, the 2023 reproductive and gender-affirming care shield bill and ESHB 1296 (chris-corry.md S6-S16), and a challenger whose stated platform is universal health care, need-based access to college and vocational education, youth skills programs and treating climate change as an existential threat (jack-mcentire.md S1, S2). McEntire has no stated positions on taxes, housing, public safety, social or parental-rights topics or the 2026 initiatives.

1. **Record vs. renewal:** four-term incumbent and caucus leader vs. a challenger with no government-office record.

4. **Taxes, housing, safety, social, parental rights, immigration:** documented for Corry only (votes and statements); McEntire has no recorded position, so these axes show one-sided evidence.
