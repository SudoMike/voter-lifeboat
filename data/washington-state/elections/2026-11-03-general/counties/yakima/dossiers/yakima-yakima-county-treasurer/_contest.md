## What the office does
The county treasurer acts as the bank for the county, school districts, fire districts and other local governments: it receives, disburses, invests and accounts for their funds, collects property and other taxes, manages cash, debt and the investment portfolio, and handles property-tax refunds and tax foreclosures [S2]. The treasurer collects taxes set by others and does not set tax rates [S2].

## Race dynamics
The seat is open: Treasurer Ilene Thomson, in the office 36 years and treasurer since 2002, did not seek a seventh term and endorsed Stephanie Patterson, the office's senior manager for investments and banking since 2014, who runs as a Republican [S3]. Sam Johnson, a Washington Department of Financial Institutions financial examiner supervisor who oversees credit-union examinations, runs as an independent and says the office benefits from "fresh ideas" and "a broader financial perspective" [S4]. The platforms overlap: both propose centralized receipting, stronger internal controls and protection of public funds (stephanie-patterson.md S2, S3; sam-johnson.md S2). Patterson's case is continuity and office-specific experience; Johnson's is outside regulatory oversight experience [S3][S4]. Johnson did not submit a statement to the general-election voters' guide [S1].

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** the incumbent's chosen successor from inside the office vs. an independent from state financial regulation.
2. **Spending and accountability:** both stress internal controls and safeguarding funds; this does not separate them much.
