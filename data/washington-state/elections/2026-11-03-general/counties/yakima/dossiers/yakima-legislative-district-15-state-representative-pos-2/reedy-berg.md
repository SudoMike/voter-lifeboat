## Background
Reedy Berg, 37, of Yakima, is the Republican candidate for the open 15th District, Position 2 seat held by Rep. Jeremie Dufault, who is running for the state Senate [S3][S12]. He was elected to the Yakima City Council (District 7) in 2023, took office in 2024, and was chosen deputy mayor by the council in 2026 [S1][S2][S3]. He has taught 8th-grade U.S. history at Toppenish Middle School since 2016 and worked as an agricultural produce broker from 2013 to 2015 [S1]. He holds a master's in teaching from Heritage University and a bachelor's from Gonzaga University, where he pitched for the baseball team [S1][S2]. He describes himself as "a conservative Republican committed to smaller government, protecting the rural way of life, slowing tax growth, and greater accountability in Olympia" [S2].

In the August 4 primary he led with 8,271 votes (48.15%) to No Kings candidate Liz Hallock's 5,170 (30.10%); Republican Chase Foster had 21.34% (unofficial election-night count) [S4].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** opposes the "capital gains income tax", calling it "a stepping stone to a broader income tax on working families" and "unconstitutional"; proposes cutting the state sales tax by 0.1% [S2]. Says he would "cut taxes and spending to ease cost-of-living pressures" and oppose "new fees and taxes" [S3].
- **Spending:** pledges to cut "wasteful government spending" and "wasteful administrative overhead" in schools [S2][S3]. Said in hindsight he would have cut more on the city council rather than use one-time money to keep pools and senior centers open [S5].
- **Climate and energy:** wants to repeal the Climate Commitment Act, which he says has "driven up the cost of everything" "without measurable benefit" [S2]; wants to reduce fuel taxes and regulations that hinder energy production [S3].
- **Housing and homelessness:** says "The Housing First model isn't working" and would require mental-health or addiction treatment as a condition of housing assistance [S2]; supports permitting reform to lower housing costs [S3].
- **Public safety:** supports "fully funding law enforcement" and "opposing reckless criminal justice rollbacks" [S2]; "We've got to fund the police as best we can" [S5].
- **Agriculture:** supports preserving the H-2A guest-worker program and raising the farm overtime threshold to 48 hours or exempting peak harvest [S2].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** voted with the 5-2 council majority in May 2024 to reject a Pride Month proclamation, saying "I believe there are 2 genders and science would not support their claim that there are expanding and infinite gender orientations to identify as" [S6]. No stated position on abortion found.
- **Parents and schools:** his platform's first plank, "Empowering Parenthood", says parents "have the right to know what their children are being taught, what medical decisions are being made, and what values are being promoted", and that "Government has overstepped" [S2]. Wants "local control over levies and spending priorities" for schools and to block state mandates on districts [S3].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

## Record
- Yakima City Council: voted against placing the city's $6 million property-tax levy on the November 2025 ballot (placed 4-3) [S7]. Voters rejected the levy (per council member Rick Glenn, quoted in [S8]).
- Moved, in December 2025, to direct staff to cut $1.8 million elsewhere rather than close Fire Station 92 in his district (passed 5-2); the adopted 2026 budget cut police and fire positions [S9].
- April 2026: voted against an open-ended 3% utility tax increase to fund Station 92, then for the increase once it carried a sunset clause tied to a county EMS levy, saying a utility tax "should not be an indefinite solution" [S8].
- July 2026: voted with the 4-3 majority to bar city funding for trolley infrastructure, including the Naches River Bridge [S10].
- May 2024: voted against a Pride Month proclamation (rejected 5-2) [S6].
- PDC (updated 2026-09-28): $15,739.00 raised and $8,542.26 spent [S11].
