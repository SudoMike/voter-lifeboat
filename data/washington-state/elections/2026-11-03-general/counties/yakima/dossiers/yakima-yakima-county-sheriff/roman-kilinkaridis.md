## Background
Roman Kilinkaridis, 36, of Yakima, is a West Richland police officer and owns two Yakima businesses, Icy Tai rolled ice cream and Flame & Brew Pizza; he has not held elected office [S3]. He came to the United States as an immigrant and became a citizen; he says his businesses employ 18 people [S1]. He was a reserve officer in Selah before joining the West Richland Police Department in 2021 [S3]; his statement cites seven years as a police officer [S1]. He describes himself as an outsider to the sheriff's office [S3].

## Positions
- **First-year priorities:** protecting youth, supporting domestic-violence victims, and preventing fraud against seniors [S1][S3].
- **Youth programs:** would create a county Police Athletic League, modeled on Yakima's former program, to steer young people from gangs toward sports and business mentoring; wants deputies in classrooms [S3].
- **Community policing:** says deputies should connect with residents rather than "check a box and leave" when there is no crime [S3].
- **Budget:** names the office's budget deficit as the main problem; would seek grants to fund more deputies and equipment, would trade in the sheriff's $130,000 Ford Bronco, and questions year-end spend-down of budgets [S3]. His site cites the county's 2026 sheriff budget ($18.88 million, up 9.2%) as a baseline voters should question [S2].
- **Automated license plate readers (Flock):** supports plate readers but opposes systems he says approach facial recognition; would deactivate the new cameras on his first day and seek an advisory public vote on their use [S2][S3].
- **Deputy wellness:** voluntary access to a counselor roughly every six months [S2][S3].
- **Accountability:** proposes a public "commitment ledger" tracking campaign promises and corrections [S2]; pledges to donate part of the sheriff's salary to local nonprofits including the YWCA and YMCA [S2][S3].
- **Sheriff decertification law (SB 5974):** says sheriffs must be accountable and one who cannot keep state certification "probably shouldn't be a sheriff," while voters should have a meaningful voice [S3].

## Record
- West Richland police records obtained by the Yakima Herald-Republic show Chief James Fry Jr. demoted him from sergeant to patrol officer in November, eight months after promotion, citing "serious concerns" about his performance, leadership and liability exposure as a probationary sergeant; the documents tie the demotion to his conduct in two pursuits and note admonishments over driving speed, use of the police facility, charging a personal vehicle from a city power pole, and scene supervision [S4].
- Kilinkaridis told the paper the demotion reflected department politics and a chief who wanted more senior sergeants; he said he defended an officer whose pursuit he considered within policy, and acknowledged driving 10 mph over the limit once [S4].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $17,098.56 raised, $14,208.10 spent, $1,993.56 in loans [S5]. The Herald-Republic reported $4,194 of his own money in the campaign [S3].
