## Background
Jake Church, 53, of Zillah, is an administrative lieutenant in the Yakima County Sheriff's Office and has not held elected office [S4]. He taught high school agriculture in Sunnyside (1997-2000), then served with the Toppenish Police Department as an officer and sergeant until 2016, when he joined the sheriff's office as a deputy, later sergeant and lieutenant [S1][S2]. He says he has 26 years in law enforcement, the last 10 with the sheriff's office [S1][S4]. As administrative lieutenant he handles internal investigations, policy and budgeting work; his website says he oversees professional standards, training and policy [S2][S4]. He has worked as a patrol deputy, school resource officer and patrol sergeant, and in Toppenish as a patrol officer, patrol sergeant and detective sergeant [S4].

He holds a B.S. in agricultural education from the University of Idaho and graduated from the FBI National Academy (2023) [S1][S2]. He lists the FBI-LEEDA Command Institute and Command College [S2]. He is immediate past president of Yakima Valley Fraternal Order of Police Lodge 10 and a member of Yakima Downtown Rotary and the Toppenish Lions Club [S1][S2].

## Positions
- **Public safety and staffing:** names budget shortfalls and low staffing as the office's main problem; says Washington ranks last in law-enforcement officers per capita and Yakima is below that; would build relationships with the commissioners to advocate for the office [S4]. Would use more analytics to direct limited staff to the highest-impact crime problems (violent, gang, drug and property crime) [S4]. Says the office needs a good relationship with the prosecutor because arrests that are not charged accomplish little [S4].
- **Prevention and enforcement:** promises evidence-based strategies, crime-prevention programs, and enforcement against drugs, property crime and unsafe driving [S2].
- **Deputy support:** prioritizes training, modern equipment, officer wellness, and recruitment [S2].
- **Automated license plate readers (Flock):** calls them a good tool he would keep using; describes training, keystroke logging, audits and a gross-misdemeanor threshold as safeguards; says if the question reaches the ballot he would respect the voters' decision, including shutting the cameras down [S4].
- **Sheriff decertification law (SB 5974):** calls the law unconstitutional and says the review board's makeup and standards are unclear [S4].
- **Transparency:** promises open communication, accountability and transparent operations [S2].
