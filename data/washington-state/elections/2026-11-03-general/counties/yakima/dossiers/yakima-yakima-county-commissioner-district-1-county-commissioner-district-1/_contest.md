## What the office does
The three county commissioners are Yakima County's legislative and executive authority: they adopt the budget, pass ordinances, set land-use and permitting rules through the comprehensive plan, oversee roads and the county landfill, and sit on the Yakima Health District board. District 1 covers most of the Upper Yakima Valley, including parts of Terrace Heights and Selah [S3]. Since the Aguilar v. Yakima County final order (October 2021), commissioner candidates appear only on ballots within their own district, in the general election as well as the primary [S2].

## Race dynamics
Commissioner Amanda McKinney is running for Congress, leaving the seat open [S3]. Both finalists prefer the Republican Party [S1]. Retiring Sheriff Bob Udell won 65% in the four-way primary; contractor and former Caton Landfill operator Kristoffer Strutner was second with 14% (robert-udell.md S5). Udell runs on 36 years in county government, fiscal responsibility, restructuring and growth, with taxes as a last resort (robert-udell.md S1, S4). Strutner promises "vast and sweeping" cuts to county regulation and staff, an end to the county's well-metering program and faster permits (kristoffer-strutner.md S1, S2, S3). Coverage of Strutner centers on the landfill, which lost its permit, and pending misdemeanor tire-dumping charges he disputes (kristoffer-strutner.md S3). Udell has raised about $31,000; Strutner reports none (robert-udell.md S6; kristoffer-strutner.md S6).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** a 36-year county official and two-term sheriff vs. a first-time outsider.
2. **Housing and land use:** Strutner's deregulation of permitting, rural clustering and water metering to lower building costs; no comparable Udell proposal.
3. **Spending:** Strutner would cut staff and regulation; Udell favors restructuring and growth, taxes as a last resort.
