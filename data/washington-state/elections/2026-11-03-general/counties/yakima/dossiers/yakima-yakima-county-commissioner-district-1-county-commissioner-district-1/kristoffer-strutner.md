## Background
Kristoffer (Kris) Strutner, of Selah, is a heavy civil construction manager who grew up in the Yakima Valley working for farmers and ranchers; he cites more than 25 years in construction, excavation, aggregate production and development [S1][S2][S4]. He graduated from Naches Valley High School and the IUOE Local 302 apprenticeship program [S1]. He runs Naches Wenas Sand and Gravel and the Goldendale-based V7 Excavation [S3]. He has not held elected office and describes himself as having "experience outside the political system" [S1][S2].

He operated the private Caton Landfill near Naches from 2022 until it closed in April 2025, after the state Pollution Control Hearings Board upheld the Yakima Health District's 2023 permit denial; per the Department of Ecology the landfill operated 653 days without a solid-waste permit [S3]. In May 2026, at the county prosecutor's request, the state attorney general filed misdemeanor charges in Yakima District Court against Strutner, Randy Caton and the landfill company alleging illegal tire dumping in 2024; Strutner says he should not have been named and his attorney will seek his removal from the case [S3]. The county also sued the landfill in 2025 [S3].

In the August 4 primary he placed second with 2,629 votes (13.68%) to Bob Udell's 12,562 (65.35%) [S5].

## Positions
- **Water:** would immediately end the Yakima County Water Resource System (well-metering requirement for building permits on exempt wells) and sell its water-right assets into the general fund [S1][S2][S4].
- **Permits and land use:** would cut county permitting and regulation to state legal minimums, create a 30-minute counter approval path for professionally stamped plans, allow rural clustering and heavily modify the comprehensive plan; "Lobbying groups are saying 'People Must Live in Cities' I say no!" [S1][S2][S3].
- **Budget:** would reduce county staff as regulations are cut, change how the Planning and Roads departments contract for infrastructure to save money, and reduce infrastructure spending [S4].
- **Terrace Heights landfill:** says the county landfill has been out of compliance on methane; would bring it into compliance even though "it's going to cost taxpayers money" (the county says monitoring is within thresholds as of 2026) [S3].
- **Surveillance:** cites Flock cameras as an example of expanding government regulation [S1].
- **Animal control:** opposes mandatory spay and neuter rules [S4].
- **Conflicts of interest:** says he would recuse himself only where state law requires; he was unsure he would recuse if the landfill matter returned to the county [S3][S4].
