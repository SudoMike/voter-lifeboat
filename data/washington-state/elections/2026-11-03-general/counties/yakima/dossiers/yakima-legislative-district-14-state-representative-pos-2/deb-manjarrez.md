## Background
Deb Manjarrez, of Wapato, is the Republican State Representative for the 14th Legislative District, Position 2, first elected in 2024 and seeking a second term [S16][S1]. She is assistant ranking minority member of the House Housing Committee and serves on Appropriations and the Joint Legislative Audit & Review Committee [S4]. She is managing partner of the CPA firm Manjarrez and DeLeon PS, treasurer of M&M Orchards and a fourth-generation farmer who grows apples near Wapato [S1][S2][S3]. She was a Yakima County Republican precinct committee officer for 16 years, chaired the Yakima County Republican Central Committee for two terms, and chaired the Wapato Chamber of Commerce for 10 years [S1][S3]. Her website says she was appointed to a governor's housing task force in 2026 [S3]. She holds a BA in accounting from Central Washington University [S1].

In the August 4, 2026 primary she placed first with 3,436 votes (51.15%, unofficial election-night count) to Democrat Ezequiel Morfin's 1,836 (27.33%) and Democrat Tony Sandoval's 1,432 (21.32%) [S18].

## Positions

### Taxes and spending
- Statement: her "business and accounting background qualifies her to fight runaway government spending" [S1]; her website says she used her accounting background "to address concerns regarding government spending" [S2].
- Wants "tighter grant auditing and accounting standards," saying grants are awarded "and we have very little documentation come back" [S17]; the Yakima Herald-Republic reports she is running on lowering the cost of living by limiting government spending [S18].
- Co-wrote that she voted no on the 2025 operating budget and "against the highest tax increases in the history of our state," and stood "against aggressive new taxes, costly climate mandates, reckless spending" [S19].
- Supports the schools superintendent's proposal for $2 billion more for schools if it is restricted to materials, supplies, operating costs and insurance premiums [S16]; says the state is underfunding schools and criticizes cuts to levy equalization [S17].

### Climate and energy
- Says utility bills are rising "because of policy decisions that have come out of Olympia," citing "all of the green agenda" and the Climate Commitment Act; "Everyone wants a clean environment, but at what price?" [S17].
- Wants changes to the Climate Commitment Act so its revenue goes to "direct energy savings, direct infrastructure" instead of grants she says do not benefit the climate [S17][S18].

### Housing and homelessness
- Says "the homelessness crisis is not economic. It's drug and mental illness," and that Washington's approach "has been an absolute failure" [S17]. Record: voted against the 2025 rent-stabilization bill on final passage (see Record) [S11].

### Public safety and drugs
- Sponsored an overdose-mapping software bill and wants to keep babies born to drug-addicted mothers from going home with drug-addicted parents [S17]. Website: public safety and education are the state's "paramount duty" [S2].
- Co-wrote that she opposed a firearm insurance requirement and an ammunition tax, and opposed the 2025 permit-to-purchase law [S19].

### Agriculture and labor
- Wants the farmworker overtime law revisited with a seasonal exemption that workers choose; does not support collective bargaining for farmworkers [S16].

### Technology and data centers
- "I am in support of data centers"; "I'm not so sure the state Legislature should do anything"; wants community input and no increase in utility costs, and opposes a "knee-jerk reaction to ban data centers" [S16].

### Education and tort law
- Wants tort limits and statutes of limitation on suits against schools, citing rising insurance premiums [S17][S16].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No statement found. Record: voted against SSB 5917 on access to abortion medications [S13].

### Parents and schools
- Co-wrote that she and Rep. Mendoza "fought to protect parents' rights, including access to records, review materials and receive notifications," and that the majority "had the votes to gut the parental rights initiative" [S19].

### Immigration
- No statement found. Record: voted against the immigrant worker protections bill (see Record) [S12].

## Scoring notes
- One-term incumbent, CPA and orchardist, former county Republican chair, against a former Toppenish council member [S3][S16].
- Recorded no votes on the 2026 income tax and the 2025 revenue bills; frames her role as fighting runaway spending and tightening grant audits [S5][S6][S7][S8][S17][S19].
- Supports data centers with little state regulation, in contrast to Morfin [S16].
- Opposed the 2025 parental-rights amendments and wrote in support of parents' notification and record-access rights [S9][S19].
- Opposes farmworker collective bargaining; Morfin makes it his main priority [S16].
