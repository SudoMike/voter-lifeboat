## Background
Ezequiel Morfin Jr., of Toppenish, is the Democratic candidate for the 14th District, Position 2 [S5][S6]. He is a former Toppenish City Council member and Toppenish planning commissioner, a small-business owner, and chief electrical inspector for the Yakama Nation; he has also worked as an electrical journeyman and foreman [S1][S4][S5]. He is the son of farmworkers; his family helped found Radio KDNA and his father served on the Yakima Valley Farm Workers Clinic board [S4][S6]. He worked with the United Farm Workers organizing members and representing workers before state agencies, and lists membership in the Yakima May 1st Coalition and the Yakima Immigration Response Network and service as a LULAC vice president [S1][S4]. The Yakima Herald-Republic reports he has been active in protests against the Trump administration's immigration policies [S6][S7]. He studied electrical technology at Yakima Valley College and Perry Technical Institute [S1]. Statement endorsements: Yakima, Franklin and Benton County Democrats and labor unions [S1].

In the August 4, 2026 primary he placed second with 1,836 votes (27.33%, unofficial election-night count) behind Rep. Deb Manjarrez's 3,436; Democrat Tony Sandoval had 1,432 [S7].

## Positions

### Affordability, taxes and housing
- Supports "policies that lower the cost of housing," "fair taxation so working people aren't carrying the load alone," and infrastructure investments that lower long-term costs; says tax systems "continue to favor those at the top" [S2][S3].
- Proposes reducing or eliminating down payments for home purchases, expanding tiny homes, and expanding city boundary lines so cities can grow [S6]; will work on renter protections [S6]. Statement: will fight "for truly affordable housing, family-wage jobs" [S1].

### Labor and agriculture
- Collective bargaining for farmworkers will be his "largest work"; says the farmworker overtime law needs to be revisited because local workers get 30 to 40 hours while overtime goes to H-2A workers, and describes abuse in the H-2A system [S5].
- Supports stronger wage-theft enforcement, safe working conditions, the right to organize without retaliation, and apprenticeships [S3].

### Education and spending
- Supports the schools superintendent's proposal to add $2 billion for schools, saying education was hurt by federal cuts [S5]; supports more funding for childcare and preschool programs [S6].

### Immigration
- Supports "strong state-level protections that limit cooperation with federal immigration enforcement" and keeping schools, hospitals and courthouses accessible to everyone [S3]; statement: families "are living in fear of this federal administration and their unconstitutional attacks against our neighbors" [S1]; says people "are being picked up just based on the color of their skin" [S6].

### Technology and data centers
- "My main answer is no ... until there's more research"; wants accountability and oversight, is concerned about water use, and wants companies building data centers to build substations that also serve the community [S5].

### Government and representation
- Says he will "put working families ahead of corporate donors" and stay accountable to the district, "not Olympia insiders" [S3]; says the incumbent "is absent from the district" [S2].
