## Jurisdiction
Fire Protection District No. 2

## MeasureName
Proposition 1

## ShortDescription
Property Tax Levy For Emergency Medical Services

## BallotTitle
The Board of Clallam County Fire Protection District No. 2, has adopted Resolution No. 2026-03 concerning a proposition to fund emergency medical services.
Will Clallam County Fire Protection District No. 2 be authorized to fund emergency medical services for its citizens by imposing a regular property tax levy of $.50 or less per $1,000.00 of assessed valuation for a period of ten consecutive years to be collected beginning in 2027?

## ExplanatoryStatement
Written by Brian K. Snure, Attorney for Fire District
The elected Board of Commissioners of Clallam County Fire Protection District No. 2 have determined that in order to provide and maintain emergency medical services (EMS) to our citizens the District requires more stable and consistent funding. State law allows fire protection districts to levy an EMS property tax levy of up to $0.50 per thousand of assessed valuation.
If approved by the voters, this measure will authorize a ten-year EMS property tax levy at a rate not to exceed $0.50 per thousand dollars of assessed valuation to be collected beginning in 2027. The District has been funding EMS services out of its regular levy rate and the Board has determined that such an approach is not financially sustainable long term.
Approval of the EMS levy will allow the District to continue to provide paramedic level emergency medical services to the citizens of the District and to, the extent allowed by law, to cover uninsured ambulance transport costs for qualified District residents and taxpayers. The District will use the revenue from this levy exclusively for emergency medical services.

## ArgumentFor
Clallam County Fire District #2 delivers essential EMS services that protect you and your family. EMTs, Paramedics, and Firefighters are ready to assist at any time, but now they need your support. Since 2012, the district has offered advanced Paramedic-level emergency care without receiving ongoing federal, state, or county funding, relying solely on its regular fire levy. At present, patients are billed for each EMS transport, something this new levy would eliminate.
However, using the district’s regular fire levy isn't sustainable because it reduces resources for other vital fire department functions like fire suppression and technical rescue. The funding from a dedicated EMS levy is crucial to maintaining the district’s EMS program given the rising demand for service. Over the last decade, call volumes in the district have increased by more than 42%, with incidents happening simultaneously 32% of the time. This means care can be delayed when emergencies overlap. As demand grows, current staff and volunteers aren't enough to meet the needs of the community. Without the EMS levy, the district can't hire additional personnel to keep up with this demand. Dedicated EMS levy funds will help maintain high-quality emergency care, allow for hiring more staff, and end out-of-pocket ambulance transport costs for district residents. This desired level of prehospital care, free from direct costs to individuals, is already available to most residents outside of Fire District 2 in eastern Clallam County.
Support Clallam County Fire District 2 and Emergency Medical Services by voting YES!
