## BallotTitle
The Board of Clallam County Fire Protection District No. 6 adopted Resolution No. 2026-01 concerning a proposition to maintain and adequately fund District operations.
This proposition authorizes the District to levy a regular property tax at a maximum rate of $1.50 per $1,000 of assessed value in 2026 for collection in 2027.  The funds will maintain fire protection, and emergency services, replace apparatus and equipment, and provide for firefighter safety.  The senior and disabled exemption under RCW 84.36.381 would apply to the additional taxes. The 2026 levy shall serve as the base for computing subsequent levy limitations.

## ExplanatoryStatement
Written by Brian K. Snure, Attorney for Fire District
Clallam County Fire Protection District No. 6 provides fire protection, emergency communications, and emergency response throughout the District. State law generally limits annual growth in regular property tax revenue. Because of that limitation and changes in assessed value, the District's regular levy rate has declined below the statutory maximum.
If approved, this measure would authorize a one-year regular property tax levy at a rate not to exceed $1.50 per $1,000 of assessed value. The levy would be made in 2026 and collected in 2027. Revenue would support fire protection and emergency services, including volunteer recruitment and retention, training, apparatus and equipment, facilities, and District operations.
The dollar amount actually levied for collection in 2027 would become the base used to calculate the District's future levy limits. The senior and disabled property tax exemption authorized by RCW 84.36.381 would apply to the additional regular property taxes authorized by the measure.

## ArgumentFor
The Three Rivers Fire Department (District 6) is the smallest fire district in Clallam County. It serves the communities connected by the La Push, Quillayute Prairie, Mora, and Goodman Mainline roads.  It’s bounded on the west by ONP and the Quileute Nation, and it extends just east of the Prairie / La Push road junction.  While the smallest, it is still beholden to the same National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) standards enforced by major metropolitan districts.  Over the past 16 years, NFPA has added new requirements for PPE cleaning, documentation, SCBA performance, ensemble testing, and staffing readiness.
Basic turnout gear that cost $2,000 in 2010 costs $5,000 today. Overall, NFPA compliance costs over 100% more today than it did when the District last asked for a levy lid lift in 2010.
In 2011, annual District revenue from taxpayers was $31,214. The anticipated revenue for 2026 is $38,941. Over the past 15 years, expenses have risen more than 100% while revenue has risen by 25%. District revenue from property taxes hasn’t even kept up with the national inflation rate.  Additional revenue from timber sales has averaged about $12,000 per year since 2010, but it is not reliable.  The most current DNR projection for 2027 timber revenue is less than $1,000.
Please vote to support our all-volunteer fire department so that we may continue to safely serve you as first responders for fire protection, fire prevention, emergency communications, emergency services and the protection of life and property.
