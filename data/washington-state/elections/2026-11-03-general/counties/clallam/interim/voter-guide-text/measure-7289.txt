## ShortDescription
Charter Amendment Requiring County Commissioner District Town Hall Meetings

## BallotTitle
The Clallam County Charter Review Commission adopted Resolution No. 3 concerning amendment of the charter requiring commissioner district town hall meetings.
This proposition would amend Article II of the Clallam County Charter to require each individual county commissioner to conduct a minimum of one town hall meeting per year in their commissioner district and require the county commissioners, together, to conduct a minimum of one town hall meeting per year in each of the three commissioner districts. These meetings will be required to occur outside normal business hours.

## ExplanatoryStatement
Written by Dee Boughton, Clallam County Deputy Prosecuting Attorney
The Clallam County Charter currently does not require county commissioners to meet with the public in their commissioner districts outside of regular board meetings, which are typically held in Port Angeles during normal working hours. If adopted, this measure would amend Article II of the Clallam County Charter to require each individual county commissioner to hold at least one town hall meeting per year in their commissioner district. It would further require that the county commissioners collectively conduct at least one town hall meeting in each of the three commissioner districts per year. These meetings would be required to occur outside of normal business hours, with notice posted at least 30 days in advance of each meeting.

## ArgumentFor
Vote for this amendment to allow your voice to be heard and your questions regarding county government to be answered by the county commissioners.
This amendment would require two town hall meetings a year in each County Commissioner District outside of normal working hours, one conducted by the district’s commissioner, and one conducted by all the commissioners.  In these meetings people could ask questions, raise concerns and stay informed about county decisions by having their questions answered by the commissioner(s).
The need for this amendment arose from the county commissioners’ practices of having their regular meetings at the county courthouse during normal working hours, far removed from the people in the outlying areas of the county and during times where working people could not easily attend.
The need for this amendment also arose from the county commissioners’ practices of not answering questions presented to them in the public comment portions of their regular meetings.  This typically left people feeling frustrated and the commissioners appearing non-responsive. By addressing these shortcomings of county government, this amendment will facilitate a better-informed public and promote more confidence in county government.

## ArgumentAgainst
Town halls are valuable. Putting their scheduling into the County Charter is not.
A Charter should establish good government, not attempt to prescribe every practice of a good, elected official. Neither the U.S. Constitution nor Washington Constitution requires legislators to conduct constituent town halls. They hold them because listening and responding to citizens are responsibilities of good representation. Clallam County commissioners can—and already do—hold town halls throughout the county without a Charter mandate. Requirements for public engagement can be established through appropriate County policies or rules that can be improved as circumstances, technology, and citizens' needs change.
This amendment would instead permanently place meeting requirements in our foundational Charter while leaving important questions unanswered. It does not define a “town hall,” guarantee questions and answers, establish meaningful standards for public participation, or clearly define “normal working hours” or where the required notice must be posted. Requiring a meeting does not guarantee meaningful public engagement. We support accessible commissioners, district town halls, and vigorous public participation. But good representative practices do not all belong in the County Charter.
Vote NO on putting town hall scheduling into the Charter—and YES to commissioners continuing to meet directly with the people they represent.
