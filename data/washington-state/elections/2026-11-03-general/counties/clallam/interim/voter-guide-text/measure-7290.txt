## BallotTitle
The Clallam County Charter Review Commission adopted Resolution No. 4 concerning establishment of a Clallam County Ethics Review Board.
This proposition would amend Article VIII of the Clallam County Charter to require the establishment of an ethics review board responsible for reviewing complaints of alleged violations of the Clallam County Code of Ethics by elected county officials. Board members would be appointed by designated elected county officials according to eligibility requirements and serve without compensation. The board would adopt rules governing the handling of complaints. Board findings would be published on the county’s website.

## ExplanatoryStatement
Written by Dee Boughton, Clallam County Deputy Prosecuting Attorney
The current Clallam County Code includes a code of ethics that applies to all county personnel, including elected county officials. If adopted, this measure would amend Article VIII of the Clallam County Charter to require the establishment of an independent ethics review board which would be responsible for reviewing complaints of alleged violations of the county’s code of ethics by elected county officials. The ethics review board would consist of three members, one from each of the county commissioner districts. Board members would be appointed by designated independently elected county officials and would serve staggered three-year terms without compensation. The ethics review board would establish rules of procedure governing the submission and handling of complaints, receive and review complaints, determine whether complaints fall within the jurisdiction of the board, conduct impartial reviews, and issue written findings. The board would not impose penalties or sanctions. All findings of the ethics review board would be publicly available and posted on the county website.

## ArgumentFor
A person granting consent to be governed requires faith in the integrity of those who will govern. Some actions taken by county officials, while not illegal, can be unethical. When the people suspect a conflict of interest, a lack of transparency, or a misuse of office, cynicism of their government increases. An independent Ethics Review Board (ERB) would consist of three members of the public, appointed by elected county officials to three-year terms, which will be staggered so that continuity may be ensured.
A summarized version of its purpose will be to: 1. Establish fair rules of procedure; 2. Receive and review written complaints of perceived ethical violations; 3. Determine jurisdiction; 4. Conduct impartial investigations; 5. Issue public written findings.
The goal of an ERB is not to replace the justice system, but rather be investigative and advisory. By gathering facts and shining light on ethical issues, the board would act as a mirror for the county's standards and offer elected officials clear guidance on best practices.
A strong ERB will reinforce the community’s faith in its leadership. “The basis of effective government is public confidence, and that confidence is endangered when ethical standards falter or appear to falter.” — John F. Kennedy

## ArgumentAgainst
Washington already provides multiple safeguards for ethical government, including state law, financial disclosure through the Public Disclosure Commission, the constitutional recall process, and regular elections.
Before creating an additional county ethics board, voters should consider whether these existing tools already provide an effective framework without adding new costs. This proposition would create a county ethics board to hear complaints against elected officials. While accountability is essential, this proposal could create a costly, time-consuming process that becomes politicized, duplicates existing oversight, and diverts staff and funds from core public services.
Clallam County should rely on the accountability measures already in place and allow voters to hold officials accountable at the ballot box.
For these reasons, vote no.
