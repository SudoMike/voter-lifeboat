## Background

Jake Seegers of Port Angeles is running for office for the first time, with a "Prefers Independent Party" preference [S1][S8]. His statement lists his work as portfolio manager for Palomino Investments, real estate investor, founder and former owner of The Natchez Pearl Inn, and buy-side analyst at Wilson Capital Management. Earlier jobs include restaurant manager, substitute teacher, preschool Spanish teacher and ranch hand [S1]. He holds an MBA in investment finance and accounting from Ohio State University and a BS in biomedical sciences from Montana State University [S1][S2]. His campaign site says he worked for a hedge fund in New York City and a futures-trading firm in Texas, and that he launched and later sold a vacation rental business before moving to Washington [S2].

He moved to Port Angeles with his family in 2019. His wife is an emergency-room physician, and he has been the primary homeschool educator for their three children [S2][S8]. His community service includes the 4PA clean-up crew and campus committee, Surfrider and CoastSavers beach clean-ups, and volunteering at Harbor of Hope [S1]. He announced his candidacy in November 2025 [S8].

In the August primary, open only to District 3 voters, he received 3,675 votes (52.7%) to French's 3,288 (47.1%) [S7].

## Positions

### Taxes, spending and the county budget
- Says every decision will be guided by two questions, "Is it essential? Is it effective?", and pledges to "restore oversight to the county budget" with "transparency and measurable results for every taxpayer dollar" [S1][S3].
- His website calls for growing county revenue "without increasing taxes or fees" through economic growth and partnerships, and for requiring nonprofits that receive public funds to report finances and measurable results [S3].
- At the August Sequim forum he said French's approach is "more grants, more taxation, more government spending and more money to (non-governmental organizations)," and that "the answer really is within our budget" [S13].
- At a July forum he said he would support a new sales tax only if another sales tax, such as the one for juvenile justice services, came to an end [S9].
- Ranks balancing the budget third among his priorities, after public safety and economic development, "so the taxpayer can keep more of their hard-earned money" [S10].

### Public safety, homelessness and substance use
- Calls public safety his "top priority" and says "failed policies have fueled homelessness and open drug abuse in our neighborhoods, parks, and watersheds" [S1][S10].
- Wants the county to measure outcomes such as recovery from addiction and "graduation from homelessness to self-sufficiency," not inputs like beds filled or meals served [S1][S3].
- His website pledges to support the Olympic Peninsula Narcotics Enforcement Team (OPNET), "equip officers to address encampments and waste," and expand co-response programs that connect people to treatment and shelter [S3].
- At the Sequim forum he said harm reduction "has not found a balance in our county" and "we are definitely enabling." He proposed returning to a one-for-one syringe exchange while continuing Narcan distribution, said the county lacks local detox services, and favored expanding transitional rather than permanent supportive housing [S13].
- His campaign site's weekly "Sundays With Seegers" posts cover encampments, harm reduction and recovery. One examines records of a proposed county drug-checking program that he says was stopped after a county risk manager intervened [S5].

### Housing, land use and permitting
- Pledges to "push back on costly mandates, work to reduce county fees, and empower local solutions that expand affordability" [S1].
- Says county permitting fees have risen and should go back down. He has also cited higher burn-permit and environmental health fees and land "locked up" from development [S9][S13].
- On the comprehensive plan update he said "the state has already restricted property rights and land use far beyond what is necessary and the county should not add to that," and that land use should be left to property owners within the existing conditional-use process [S17].
- At the September 29 comprehensive plan hearing he encouraged the county to risk noncompliance with the Growth Management Act in order to take more time with the update [S15].
- Would promote revising the state Growth Management Act to make industry easier in the West End [S9].

### Economic development
- Wants to make the county "a magnet for high-quality, high-paying employers" through safe public spaces, streamlined permitting and workforce development [S1][S3].
- Criticized the federal Recompete grant French led, saying it "funnels money to government, special interests and NGOs." He said that during French's tenure private-sector jobs contracted while 500 government jobs were added [S17].
- Called the Port Angeles City Council's data-center moratorium "a knee-jerk reaction" and said each proposal should be judged on its merits [S17].

### Climate, timber and natural resources
- Calls timber a renewable resource vital to the county and says the industry is "under attack" at the local and state levels. He pointed to a carbon-credit-only management system as a policy that would not allow harvesting, and said lawsuits over trust-land harvests were probably imminent [S9][S13].
- Said the county's climate vulnerability assessment was not required by the state and uses extreme assumptions [S17].
- Opposes the proposed federal transfer of the Dungeness Spit and Protection Island refuges into trust for the Jamestown S'Klallam Tribe: "I don't need to see the legislation. ... I don't support it" [S13].

## Record

He has held no elected office [S1][S8].

He has appeared with French at Port Angeles Business Association forums and at a Calico Cat Club forum in Sequim [S9][S13][S17]. The Clallam County Democrats said he declined their invitation to an October forum [S16].

### Campaign conduct and PDC proceedings
- Four complaints were filed with the Public Disclosure Commission between April and July 2026. They concerned sign disclaimers, the Clallam County Watchdog blog run by his then-campaign manager Jeff Tozzer, an over-limit contribution, and his undisclosed role as a director of the Seegers Foundation, a family foundation in Texas. Seegers said the sign and contribution issues were fixed and the foundation role was being disclosed [S11].
- On September 17 the PDC chair found a violation over sponsor identification on blog posts Seegers wrote for Clallam County Watchdog, now run by his campaign treasurer. He imposed a $250 penalty, suspended on the conditions that future posts carry the required disclosure and that there be no further violations for four years. Seegers said he had not decided whether to appeal [S12][S14].
- A fifth complaint, filed September 10, alleges that in-kind billboard contributions exceeded limits. Seegers said the campaign would come into compliance if needed [S14].
- In summer 2026 a video circulated of an October 2024 speech at his church in which he urged voters to "get rid of the skunks," meaning leaders who act against his values. It included a line about God giving "the shotguns." Seegers said the speech was a metaphor about voting and not a call to violence [S11].
