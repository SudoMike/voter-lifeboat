## Background

Mike French is the incumbent District 3 commissioner. His term runs from January 2023 through December 2026 [S2]. He served five years on the Port Angeles City Council before that and owned a downtown Port Angeles business for 15 years, which he later sold to two former employees [S1][S3]. He was born and raised in Port Angeles. He graduated from Port Angeles High School in 2000, earned an associate degree from Peninsula College through Running Start, and holds a BA in music from Central Washington University [S1][S3]. His community service includes the Nor'Wester Rotary Club and six years as Hamilton Elementary PTO vice president. He served six years on the Olympic Peninsula Community Clinic board, including as president, five years on the Port Angeles Chamber of Commerce board, and three years on the Port Angeles School District Capital Advisory Committee [S1][S3].

His county committee assignments include the Board of Health, Canvassing Board, Clallam County Finance Committee, Clallam Transit System and Housing Solutions Committee (alternate) [S2]. His campaign site says he chairs the Board of Health [S4].

In the August primary, open only to District 3 voters, he received 3,288 votes (47.1%) to Seegers' 3,675 (52.7%) [S11]. He said he expected a close race and wanted "a campaign that's focused on the issues, that's not an aggressive, attacking campaign" [S16].

## Positions

### Taxes, spending and the county budget
- Describes himself as "an advocate of conservative budgeting: maintaining services, avoiding debt, and protecting County reserves" [S1].
- At filing he said voters "spoke loud and clear to us last year that they're not willing to provide more property tax than what we're already collecting, so we need to be doing conservative fiscal management for the long term" [S14].
- Asked at a July forum how he would balance the budget, he described the county's earlier exercise of asking departments to model a 7% cut, which included some position reductions, lower supply spending and new revenue sources. He noted that many raises are set by collective bargaining agreements [S15].
- At the same forum he pointed to Port Angeles's new one-tenth of 1% public safety sales tax and said it is an investment he would support at the county level [S15].
- His housing paper says he persuaded the Port Angeles council to put a 0.1% affordable-housing sales tax before voters, and that this led the commissioners to adopt the tax countywide. It now raises about $1.5 million a year [S6][S18].

### Economic development
- Calls economic development his top priority [S5][S16]. He chaired the regional coalition that won $35.6 million in federal Recompete Pilot Program funds for workforce and economic development in Clallam and Jefferson counties [S1][S5].
- Cites county Opportunity Fund support of $935,000 for Forks to recruit Riverside Forest Products to the Forks Industrial Park and $1.75 million for the Port of Port Angeles Marine Trades Center, plus a $3.3 million sewer extension into the western Port Angeles urban growth area [S5][S19].
- Proposes identifying industrial land with proper zoning and utilities, and convening permitting staff with large employers to avoid "permitting traps" [S5].
- On a proposed data center at the former McKinley Mill site, he questioned "what are we making?" and described moratoriums as a normal step while policy is written [S19].

### Housing
- Says "Housing supply is the answer to a housing shortage." He supports private-sector strategies that reduce regulatory barriers alongside public funding for affordable units [S6].
- As a council member he proposed Port Angeles's multifamily property tax exemption and an infill SEPA exemption. He supported ADU and park-model (tiny home) rules and favors removing off-street parking requirements and loosening setbacks and lot sizes in urban growth areas [S6].
- Said at a July forum that "we need to keep making investments in affordable housing" [S15].
- On the comprehensive plan, he said counties must maintain rural density and character and that the plan should give developers clearer detail on what is allowed where [S19].

### Public safety, homelessness and substance use
- His issue paper says "public safety is a core responsibility of local government. Society cannot allow violence or disorder," and also that "housing is a human right" [S7].
- Supports community-oriented policing, co-response units, OPNET, sustainable law-enforcement funding, therapeutic courts and community paramedicine. He says he "would support additional funding for new positions" on Sheriff Brian King's recommendation [S7].
- Supports sanctioned camping facilities, safe parking, transitional housing and outreach. He writes that "criminalizing homelessness doesn't work" [S7].
- At the Sequim forum he said evidence supports harm reduction when the goals are fewer overdose deaths, less spread of disease and more uptake of treatment. He said it should operate alongside treatment, with law enforcement setting boundaries, and called the state's three-year experiment with drug legalization "an abject failure" [S18].

### Climate, environment and natural resources
- Calls climate change "the most important and urgent political issue of our time" and writes that "nearly every policy on every issue" should consider climate mitigation and adaptation [S8].
- Worked on the county's 2023 Climate Action Plan update and the new Community Wildfire Protection Plan, and pushed the county fleet toward hybrids and EVs [S8].
- Defended the county's climate vulnerability assessment as a state requirement "led by best available science, not by ideology" [S19].
- Said the state's habitat conservation plan includes retaining a viable timber industry, and "I don't see a lot of value in one government suing another government" over trust-land harvests [S15][S18].
- On the proposed transfer of the Dungeness refuge into tribal trust, he said the current co-management arrangement is working and that it is difficult to take a position without seeing drafted federal legislation [S15][S18].

## Record

- Commissioner since January 2023, after five years on the Port Angeles City Council [S1][S2].
- Led the Recompete coalition that secured $35.6 million in federal funds; implementation is in its second year [S1][S5].
- Spoke in strong support of a $2 million county allocation to Peninsula Behavioral Health's 36-unit Northview permanent supportive housing project in 2024 [S12].
- In 2025 the commissioners agreed to allocate opioid settlement funds to the Harm Reduction Health Center after the state stopped supplying it: $50,000 for 2025 and $100,000 for 2026. They also allocated $1.39 million in Opportunity Funds to the Port Angeles A Street sewer project, which French tied to housing opportunity [S13].
- After a video of Seegers' 2024 church speech circulated, French issued a statement. He called it "divisive and dehumanizing political rhetoric," urged supporters to "quote him carefully" and "stay civil," and said he was trying to keep his side of the debate on the issues [S17].

## Scoring notes

- Taxes: calls for conservative budgeting, but has a record of advancing a local affordable-housing sales tax and says he would support a county public-safety sales tax [S1][S6][S15].
- Spending: runs on public investment, through Recompete, the Opportunity Fund and housing seed money, as the main economic and housing lever [S5][S6][S18].
- Housing: combines deregulation (MFTE, SEPA exemption, ADUs, parking) with public funding of affordable and supportive housing [S6][S12].
- Safety: pairs support for policing and OPNET with harm reduction, co-response and sanctioned camping [S7][S18].
- Climate: explicit climate-action record and platform [S8][S19].
- Experience: first-term incumbent with nine years in local elected office [S1][S3].
