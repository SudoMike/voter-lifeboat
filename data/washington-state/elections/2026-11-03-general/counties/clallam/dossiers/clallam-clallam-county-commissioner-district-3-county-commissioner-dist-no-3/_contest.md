## What the office does

Clallam County is a home-rule charter county. Its three commissioners hold both the legislative and the executive power of the county: they adopt ordinances and comprehensive plans, levy taxes, appropriate revenue and adopt the budget, set compensation, and hire and supervise the county administrator and appointed departments [S2]. Commissioners are nominated by the voters of their district in the primary and elected by voters countywide for four-year terms [S2]. Commissioners are the only partisan county officials; every other county elected office is nonpartisan [S2].

## Race dynamics

Incumbent Mike French (Prefers Democratic Party), finishing his first term, faces Jake Seegers (Prefers Independent Party), a first-time candidate [S1][S5]. Because the primary was limited to District 3 voters, Sequim-area voters did not see this race in August but will in November [S4]. In the final primary count Seegers received 3,675 votes (52.7%) and French 3,288 (47.1%) [S3].

The candidates have met at a Port Angeles Business Association forum and a capacity-crowd forum at the Sequim Prairie Grange [S5][S6]. The League of Women Voters cancelled its forum for this race under its rule against "empty chair" forums [S8], and the Clallam County Democrats said Seegers declined their forum invitation [S9]. In September the Public Disclosure Commission found a violation by Seegers over sponsor identification on a blog run by his campaign treasurer and imposed a $250 penalty, suspended on conditions [S7].

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Taxes and spending:** French cites "conservative budgeting" and protecting reserves, and has backed local sales taxes for affordable housing and public safety. Seegers pledges to balance the budget "without increasing taxation," lower fees, and test every program for whether it is essential and effective [S1][S5].
2. **Public safety, homelessness and harm reduction:** this produced the sharpest contrast at the Sequim forum. Seegers says the county is "enabling" and wants a one-for-one syringe exchange, transitional rather than permanent supportive housing, and outcome measures. French defends evidence-based harm reduction paired with treatment and law enforcement [S5].
3. **Housing and land use:** French combines regulatory reform with public investment in affordable housing. Seegers wants lower permit fees, less state mandate, and land-use decisions left to property owners within the existing conditional-use process [S5][S6].
4. **Climate and natural resources:** the two disagree on the county's climate vulnerability assessment and on suing over state trust-land timber harvests [S5][S6].
5. **Experience:** a first-term incumbent with five prior years on the Port Angeles City Council, against a first-time candidate who has run on changing the "culture" of county government [S1][S6].
