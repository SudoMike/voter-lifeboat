## What the office does

Clallam PUD No. 1 provides electricity, water, wastewater and wholesale broadband [S9]. A three-member board of commissioners sets utility policy and hires a general manager to carry it out. Commissioners serve staggered six-year terms [S8]. The PUD reports about 30,000 electric customers and 4,500 water connections [S9]. A January 2026 report put its metered electric customers at nearly 37,000 [S10]. It serves all of Clallam County except areas inside the City of Port Angeles, which runs its own electric utility [S9]. Its power comes mostly from the Bonneville Power Administration (BPA): the PUD's fuel mix is 75.53% hydroelectric, 12.78% nuclear and 11.69% unspecified BPA purchases [S9]. In 2024 a commissioner earned $3,238 a month plus a meeting per diem, up to $61,396 a year [S13].

## Who votes

Only voters living in Commissioner District 2 could vote in the primary. Voters of the entire PUD elect the commissioner in the general (RCW 54.12.010(3)) [S2]. The 2026 primary for this seat covered 18 precincts and drew 6,957 votes [S5]. The Sequim Gazette noted that many Sequim-area voters could not vote on the seat in the primary but can in November [S12]. The City of Port Angeles is not in any PUD commissioner district: the county's GIS layer lists no Port Angeles city precinct in Districts 1-3 [S3]. Its voters therefore do not vote on this race. In 2022 the PUD District 1 general-election race drew 28,129 votes, against 39,943 for the countywide commissioner race [S4].

## Race dynamics

- **Open seat.** John Purvis, the former PUD assistant general manager appointed to District 2 in 2023 after Rick Paschall resigned, is not running [S7][S11]. The winner joins District 1 Commissioner Ken Hays and District 3 Commissioner Phyllis Bernard [S6][S8].
- **Primary.** Five candidates ran. Paschall led with 3,025 votes (43.5%), followed by Randy Brackett with 2,132 (30.6%), John W. "Jack" Smith with 653, Timothy Dalton with 601 and Missi Baker with 533 [S5].
- **Candidates.** Paschall, a former BPA mathematician and longtime power-supply and compliance manager at the Pacific Northwest Generating Cooperative, won the seat in 2020 and resigned in early 2023. He has since tied the resignation to a stroke from which he says he has recovered [S1][S6]. Brackett is a licensed professional engineer with 34 years in public works management in Northwest Washington and Alaska, running for office for the first time [S1][S6].
- **Rates context.** The board approved 3.75% electric rate increases for April 2026 and April 2027. BPA power purchases are about 45% of electric costs, and BPA transmission rates rose 19.9% and power rates 6.5% for 2026-28. The PUD reports about $43 million in grants from 2023-25 [S10].
- **Money.** Brackett reported $8,921 raised, $5,500 in loans and $8,209 spent as of Oct. 6 [S15]. Paschall uses the PDC's mini-reporting option (no itemized reports filed) [S14].

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

No rubric axis applies to PublicUtility contests, so nothing is scored. The differences voters will see:

- **Record vs. outsider.** Paschall is a former commissioner who voted on rates in 2020-2023 and stresses BPA and regulatory expertise. Brackett has never held office and stresses public-works budgeting and change management [S1][S6].
- **Rates.** Both make low rates their top goal. Paschall lists cost pressures with rough rate-impact estimates and says the fixed base charge is "high" but "fair." Brackett says rates "cannot continue to grow 3.5% annually" and faults the fixed base charge [S1][S6].
- **Hydropower and energy future.** Paschall strongly supports hydropower and says the reservoir system works as designed. Brackett calls the Columbia dams a success but warns that a warming climate threatens hydro output, and backs local generation such as the planned Sequim solar-and-battery microgrid and geothermal. Both are skeptical of nuclear power [S1][S6].
- **Board conduct.** Brackett questioned Paschall's attendance and a budget-vote abstention during his earlier term. Paschall says he never missed a meeting [S6].
