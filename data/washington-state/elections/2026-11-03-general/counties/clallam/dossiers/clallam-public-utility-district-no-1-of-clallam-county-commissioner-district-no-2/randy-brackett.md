## Background

Randall C. "Randy" Brackett lives in Sequim and has lived in Clallam County for more than 15 years [S2][S3][S7]. He is a licensed professional engineer with a B.S. in civil engineering from the University of Alaska Fairbanks, and sits on an American Public Works Association board [S1]. He reports 34 years in public works management in rural Northwest Washington and Alaska. That work included preparing public works budgets, supervising staff, working with elected officials, a solar-facility risk assessment, Bainbridge's water system plan, water and wastewater treatment projects, and flood and snow emergency response [S1]. He has also said he has experience in the electric-utility field [S2]. He founded and chaired Habitat for Humanity in Jefferson County, has been board member and chair of Clallam Mosaic, and volunteers with Special Olympics [S1]. He has not run for office before [S1][S2].

## Record

- No prior elected office [S1].
- 2026 primary: second of five, with 2,132 votes (30.6%) [S6].
- Says he has attended PUD board meetings and followed its operations for many years [S5].

## Positions

### Utility rates and costs
- Says "customer rates cannot continue to grow 3.5% annually," and promises decisions "that respect every ratepayer dollar" [S1]. At filing his priorities were fiscal accountability, stable rates and clear communication with ratepayers [S2].
- Faults the PUD's fixed monthly base charge, saying some customers pay for more power than they use. He praised existing rebate and low-income assistance programs [S5].
- Noted that Paschall voted for rate increases when he was on the board, and said he would not abstain on a budget vote as he says Paschall did [S5].
- Praises the roughly $43 million in grants the PUD received for transmission work, which he says keeps those costs off ratepayers. He pledges to keep pursuing grants [S1][S5].

### Energy, climate and resilience
- Says a warming climate threatens hydroelectric output. He calls the Columbia River dams a historic success with an uncertain future as snowpack and reservoir levels change [S1][S5].
- Supports exploring local renewable generation for grid resilience during major outages. He backs the PUD's planned solar-and-battery microgrid in Sequim for key facilities during an earthquake or wildfire [S3][S5].
- Skeptical of nuclear power, citing the failed WPPSS program; favors geothermal [S5].
- Wants to work "collaboratively" with BPA and "expand local capacity" to hold down rate increases [S1].
- In the primary field's June forum, joined the others in broadly opposing Snake River dam removal because of hydropower dependence and replacement costs [S3].

### Water supply and regional questions
- Points to Southern California's aquifer recharge and calls for grant-funded research on water flows through the Olympics [S3].
- Called a merger with Port Angeles City Light worth examining. He noted that the city depends on utility revenue and that the denser city service area has a different cost structure [S3].
