## Background

Ricky Thomas "Rick" Paschall lives in Sequim [S5][S12]. He holds an M.S. in economics from Portland State University and a B.S. in mathematics from the University of Oregon, and was honorably discharged from the U.S. Navy [S1]. He reports 31 years in the electric-utility industry [S1]:

- BPA mathematician, then senior power analyst at the Pacific Northwest Utilities Conference Committee [S1].
- At the Pacific Northwest Generating Cooperative: senior power trader, operations manager, then senior manager of compliance [S1]. He provided technical support to public-power general managers there for 20 years [S6].
- Compliance work at Portland General Electric and contract work for the Northwest Power Planning Council [S1].
- Ten years as vice chair of the Western States Power Pool and four on the Western Interconnection Compliance Forum steering committee, per his former PUD biography [S6].

He says his career was mostly in public power, in power supply, transmission and regulatory compliance, with five years at BPA and 25 at organizations he described as the agency's watchdogs [S1][S2].

## Record

- **2020 election.** Won the District 2 seat in November 2020 over Patti Morris, 52.22% to 47.48% [S10]. The seat had been Hugh Haffner's; Haffner's appointed replacement did not run [S6][S9].
- **Snake River dams.** In March 2021 he and Commissioner Will Purser voted for a resolution opposing removal of the four lower Snake River dams; Commissioner Jim Waddell voted no [S8]. Earlier, Paschall and Purser had directed staff to draft it [S8].
- **Rates.** In August 2021 he voted with Purser to approve electric rate increases of 2.25% in 2022 and 2.25% in 2023; Waddell voted no [S7]. On irrigation rates he said irrigators were "not paying their fair share," and he supported telling them about the increase [S7]. At the 2026 forum his opponent noted these rate votes, and Paschall defended them, saying the fixed base rate is "high" but "fair" [S2].
- **Resignation.** He submitted a one-sentence resignation letter in February 2023 that gave no reason, and left in early March, partway through his term [S6]. At the Sept. 2026 forum he tied the departure to a stroke from which he has recovered [S2][S3].
- **Board conduct dispute.** Brackett questioned his attendance during his term and said he abstained on a budget vote; Paschall said he never missed a meeting [S2].
- **2026 primary.** First of five, with 3,025 votes (43.5%) [S11].

## Positions

### Utility rates and costs
- Says his top goal is to keep rates "as low as possible" while keeping service reliable [S1][S2][S3].
- His statement lists pressures with what he calls "back-of-the-envelope" rate-impact estimates: regulatory-compliance obligations (about 7%), BPA contract issues including a surcharge and the Residential Exchange (+2%), low-water years (+2%), plus wildfire risk, clean-building standards and hiring specialized staff [S1].
- Says "Bonneville is our largest creditor," and holding down BPA prices holds down local rates [S2].
- Says he would support infrastructure projects that best deliver power but knows of no significant current needs [S2].
- Plans to work directly with the general manager and senior staff while forming positions [S1].
- Stresses help for customers "barely getting by": "everyone has access to running water, electricity and broadband" [S3].

### Energy and climate
- Strongly supports hydropower and says the reservoir system works as intended [S2]. In 2020 he opposed Snake River dam removal mainly over rates, citing an 8-10% rate increase from the federal EIS [S9]; in 2026 the field broadly opposed removal [S4].
- Skeptical of nuclear power: too expensive, waste left for future generations, and no likely site on the Peninsula [S2].
- On water supply, predicted green lawns and heavy irrigation would disappear and said rate increases may be unavoidable because "you can't create water" [S4].

### Regional coordination
- If a merger with Port Angeles City Light were pursued, he suggested Clallam PUD, Jefferson County PUD and City Light approach BPA together for leverage on rates and outages [S4].
- In 2020 he called the PUD's expulsion from the Public Power Council one of its biggest problems, because it kept the PUD out of BPA contract talks [S9].
