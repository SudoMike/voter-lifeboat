## What the office does

The assessor lists and values all taxable real and personal property in the county every year at its value as of January 1, and keeps those listings open to public inspection [S2]. The assessor sets values; levy rates are set by taxing districts and voters. The office is nonpartisan under the county charter [S3].

## Race dynamics

This is an open seat. The county staff directory lists Pam Rushton as the current assessor [S4], and she is not on the ballot [S1]. Lee Hancock, the office's lead appraiser after 16 years there, filed first. Antonio Price of Sequim, a diesel mechanic, farmer and tow truck driver making his first run for office, filed later in filing week [S1][S7]. With only two candidates, the race skipped the primary. The League of Women Voters scheduled a Zoom forum with both candidates for October 2 [S8].

The office has worked under budget pressure. In the county's 2024 "7 percent exercise" it lost the administrative position that handled senior and disabled exemptions, and in early 2025 it temporarily cut public hours to work through a backlog [S5][S6].

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Experience:** a 16-year staff appraiser who is now lead appraiser, against a first-time candidate with no assessment background who presents himself as a blue-collar outsider [S1].
2. Neither candidate's statement takes positions on taxes, spending or other rubric axes beyond Hancock's goal of making the office "as efficient as possible" [S1].
