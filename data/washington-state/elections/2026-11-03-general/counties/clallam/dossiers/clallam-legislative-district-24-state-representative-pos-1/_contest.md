## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including the tax structure, natural-resource and timber policy, K-12 funding and policy, and criminal-justice statutes. The 24th District covers Clallam and Jefferson counties and most of Grays Harbor County [S2].

## Race dynamics
First-term Democratic incumbent Adam Bernbaum of Port Angeles faces Republican Eric Pratt of Quilcene, a sports-field installation supervisor and Precinct Committee Officer making his first legislative run [S1][S2]. Bernbaum led the four-candidate primary with 60.37% to Pratt's 21.82% in final results [S3].

At their September 8 Port Angeles Business Association forum the two were described as aligned more than divided: both were wary of raising taxes, called for tighter accountability over state spending, and said communities rather than Olympia should decide projects like Highway 101 roundabouts (S2; adam-bernbaum.md S5; eric-w-pratt.md S6). Their sharpest disagreement was the 2026 9.9% tax on income over $1 million: Bernbaum voted for it and defended it as shifting the tax burden toward high earners, while Pratt opposed it and would oppose all new taxes until the state accounts for current revenue; the repeal initiative (I-645) is on the same ballot [S2]. Bernbaum runs on an enacted record (childcare capacity, prescribed-fire insurance, rural middle housing, permit appeals) and endorsements from Planned Parenthood, the State Labor Council and Washington Conservation Action; Pratt lists no endorsements and has raised less than a tenth of Bernbaum's total ($11.9k to $153.7k) (adam-bernbaum.md S1, S2, S16; eric-w-pratt.md S9).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Bernbaum's yes vote on the income tax over $1 million versus Pratt's opposition to all new taxes and 2024 testimony for an income-tax ban.
2. **Record vs. renewal:** one-term incumbent with enacted bills versus a first-time candidate running as a citizen rather than a politician.
3. **Parents and schools:** Bernbaum voted for ESHB 1296, which narrowed several I-2081 parental notification and records rights; Pratt has no stated position beyond concern about "chaos in our schools."
4. **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** Bernbaum's abortion-access plank and Planned Parenthood endorsement; no position found for Pratt.
5. **Spending:** Pratt frames the budget as a spending problem; Bernbaum stresses the spending limits he won and the limits on cutting K-12.
6. **Shared ground:** both lean toward easing housing rules and say local communities should decide local road projects.
