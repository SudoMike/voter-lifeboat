## Background
Eric W. Pratt (Prefers Republican Party) lives in Quilcene, grew up in Port Townsend and graduated from Port Townsend High School in 1997 [S2][S6]. He worked building homes and commercial spaces, then spent about two decades from 2004 managing crews that install sports field surfaces nationwide [S1][S2]. He is a single father of two [S1][S2]. His only elected experience is as a Precinct Committee Officer [S1]. His site says he has studied legislation affecting the district for most of a decade and attends and testifies at legislative hearings [S2][S4]. At a September forum he presented himself "as a citizen in Washington, not as a politician" [S6]. In the August 4 primary he placed second with 21.82% (11,779 votes) to Adam Bernbaum's 60.37% (32,592), per final results printed by The Daily World [S8]; another Republican, Aiden Hamilton, was also in the primary [S6].

## Positions

### Taxes and spending
- **Says:** "I continue standing against burdensome taxes and over-regulation" [S1]. After the primary he said his priorities are to lower costs and reduce taxation, and that the state budget has grown "a little reckless" [S7].
- Said he opposed the 2026 tax on income over $1 million (SB 6346) "from the outset": "Wealthy individuals are important parts of our economy. ... I don't want to see them move away" [S6].
- Said he would oppose all new taxes until the state can better account for the money it already collects, calling additional taxation "the last possible measure" and citing "an ever-increasing spending problem," including school test scores and child-welfare legal payouts [S6].
- **Has done (as a citizen):** testified in February 2024 in support of Initiative 2111, a ban on state and local income taxes: "What we want is protection from future taxation" [S5]. His site lists testimony on SB 6346 and other fiscal bills [S4].

### Housing and cost of living
- Pledges "common-sense policies that increase housing supply" and to "oppose regulations that artificially inflate costs" [S3]. His statement lists lowering the cost of housing, energy, health care and groceries [S1].

### Jobs and economy
- Would "cut red tape that strangles small businesses, oppose tax hikes that drive employers away," and back workforce training [S3].

### Public safety
- "Stands firmly behind our law enforcement officers"; would support funding for community policing and "oppose policies that undermine public safety" [S3].

### Health care
- "Healthcare decisions belong to patients and their doctors — not bureaucrats"; would work to lower prescription drug costs, protect coverage for pre-existing conditions and expand rural health care access [S3].

### Local control and land
- Said decisions such as U.S. Highway 101 roundabouts should rest with communities: "I don't think it's the role of Olympia to tell those people what they need" [S6]. His statement says "you and I will ensure local voices shape the policies that affect our lives" [S1].
- Pledges to defend property rights and public-lands access and ensure state policy reflects "the people who actually live here" [S3].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No public position found as of 2026-10-09.

### Parents and schools
- His site refers to "the increasing chaos in our schools" and says he will work for legislation making "the education of our students ... the principal concern of our education system" [S2][S3]. No position found on parental notification, records access or opt-outs as of 2026-10-09.
