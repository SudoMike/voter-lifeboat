## Background
Adam Bernbaum (Prefers Democratic Party) of Port Angeles is the incumbent in Position 1, first elected in 2024, and is seeking a second two-year term [S1][S5]. He is Vice Chair of the House Transportation Committee and a member of the Early Learning and Human Services and the Agriculture and Natural Resources committees [S1][S2]. Before office he was a nonprofit policy analyst focused on criminal justice and behavioral health policy, a field organizer for Derek Kilmer and legislative assistant to then-Senator Kevin Van De Wege [S1][S2]. He holds a Ph.D. in political science from George Mason University and a B.A. from the University of Washington, and sits on the Olympic View Community Foundation board [S1]. In the August 4 primary he placed first with 60.37% (32,592 votes) to Eric Pratt's 21.82% (11,779), per final results printed by The Daily World [S7]. After the primary he said the dominant theme he hears from voters is a desire for a different style of governing, adding "People are tired of radicalism," and pointed to experience as what sets him apart from Pratt [S6].

## Positions

### Taxes and spending
- **Has done:** voted yes on final passage of ESSB 6346 (the 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million) in the House on March 9, 2026 (passed 51-46) [S8].
- **Says:** defended the vote as "a matter of fairness": "the people who pay the least are high income earners ... it makes sense to me to try and shift the tax burden around so people who are paying a lot right now can pay less and people who are paying little right now pay more" [S5]. Said he won conditions for his vote, including limits on budget spending and protections for small businesses [S5].
- Cautioned that much of the budget is hard to trim because about half is K-12 education, which is protected from cuts [S5]. His site lists "a state government that balances its budget and lives within its means" among shared goals [S2].

### Housing and cost of living
- **Has done:** prime sponsor of HB 2269 (2026), which lets middle housing in limited areas of more intensive rural development in rural counties be served by large on-site sewage systems as well as public sewer; it passed 96-0 and 48-0 [S11].
- **Says:** has pressed his own party to change the state energy code, which he said has raised housing costs, and plans to sponsor a bill to freeze the code for six years [S5]. Pledges to "identify unnecessary administrative burdens and push for permitting and regulatory reform" [S3].
- Prime sponsor of HB 2426 (2026), which allows smaller panels to hear permit appeals before the Pollution Control Hearings Board when parties consent; his site describes it as streamlining business permitting with the Grays Harbor Chamber and environmental groups [S2][S12].

### Transportation and infrastructure
- Calls for investing in roads, bridges, ferry service and the electrical grid; says he secured funding for the US 12 Highway-Rail Separation in Grays Harbor County [S3].
- Said communities rather than Olympia should decide on projects like U.S. Highway 101 roundabouts, calling the Department of Transportation's plan "too ambitious" [S5].

### Health care
- Pledges to work for affordable health care across the Peninsula, including insulin and mental health care, and says he secured funding for Jefferson Healthcare's labor and delivery service [S3]. His statement names supporting hospitals as a priority [S1].

### Childcare and education
- **Has done:** prime sponsor of HB 1351 (2025, ECEAP age requirements), which his site says increased childcare capacity [S2][S13].
- **Says:** will work to bring more childcare slots online and strengthen public schools [S3].

### Climate, environment and wildfire
- **Has done:** prime sponsor of HB 1563 (2025, prescribed fire claims), which his site says stabilized the insurance market for prescribed burns [S2][S14].
- **Says:** "Climate change is real, and our region is uniquely positioned to be part of the solution"; supports habitat preservation and sustainable management of working lands [S3]. Endorsed by Washington Conservation Action [S1].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Platform commits to "protecting access to abortion here in Washington" and to access to contraception, IVF and family planning [S3]. Endorsed by Planned Parenthood [S1].

### Parents and schools
- **Has done:** voted yes on ESHB 1296 (2025) on House passage and on final passage as amended by the Senate (59-39) [S9]. ESHB 1296 amended the parental rights enacted by Initiative 2081: it eliminated the rights to prior notification when medical services are offered and to notification of some medications and services, replaced the 10-day records-copy right with access under federal FERPA within 45 days, narrowed the survey opt-out to the federal PPRA categories, and added a state policy prioritizing student safety, nondiscrimination and privacy [S10].

### Other record
- His site says he worked across the aisle on HB 2239 (family burial grounds; prime sponsor Rep. Abell) [S2][S15].
