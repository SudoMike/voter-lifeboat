## Background

Bruce Emery of Port Angeles has been the elected Director of Community Development since December 2022 [S1][S3]. He worked in the department's planning division as a senior planner from 1993 to 2007 and received the county's 2006 Excellence in Service Award. From 2007 to 2022 he was a project manager for North Pointe Construction, LLC [S1]. He holds a 2012 MBA from Ashford University and a 1992 BS in geography from Central Washington University [S1]. He has served on eight homeowner association boards [S1]. His community service includes the Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation, The Answer for Youth (TAFY), foster parenting, and serving as a church elder at First Presbyterian Church of Port Angeles [S1].

## Positions

### Housing, land use and economic development
- At filing he said: "My first priority is economic development because that goes hand-in-hand with housing ... No. 2 is to work at making changes to help increase the number of housing in general, including multi-family housing, so that we can provide housing that's affordable to everyone" [S3].
- Wants "sound, sustainable growth while protecting our quality of life," and says he continues to look for ways to improve procedures and make permitting more predictable [S1].
- Describing the comprehensive plan update, he said the Growth Management Act limits denser development to urban growth areas. Rural areas are "supposed to be kept rural in nature," and the plan adds density bonuses for multifamily housing where it is allowed [S5].

## Record

- Lists among the department's accomplishments since 2023: expanded agricultural accessory uses for farms, restored working relations with the commissioners, better coordination with other agencies, housing and economic-development policy changes in the comprehensive plan update, and stronger code enforcement [S1].
- In August 2026 he reported that code enforcement closed 87 cases in the second quarter, against an annual goal of 200, and removed 51 junk vehicles [S4].
- He presented the draft comprehensive plan update at the commissioners' September 29 hearing, where public comment ran largely critical. The plan includes a new climate element required by 2023 legislation [S5].
