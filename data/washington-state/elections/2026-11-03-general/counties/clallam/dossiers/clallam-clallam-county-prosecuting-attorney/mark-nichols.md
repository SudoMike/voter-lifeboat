## Background

Mark Nichols has been Clallam County Prosecuting Attorney since his election in 2014; his statement lists the office as "Prosecuting Attorney/Ex-Officio Coroner (2014-2024)" and "Prosecuting Attorney (2025-2026)", and the coroner duties are being moved to an appointed position [S1][S3]. He was elected in 2014, 2018 and 2022, running as a Republican in the contested 2014 and 2018 races and unopposed in 2022. The office became nonpartisan after a 2020 charter amendment he advocated [S3]. He previously served as the county's land use hearings examiner, chief deputy prosecuting attorney, and deputy prosecutor in the criminal and civil divisions. Before law school he was a commissioned U.S. park ranger with the National Park Service at Olympic National Park [S1][S3]. He holds a JD from Seattle University School of Law (2002) and a BA from the University of Washington (1996), along with EMT and law enforcement certifications [S1]. He is vice president of the board of the Washington Association of Prosecuting Attorneys [S3].

## Positions

### Public safety and prosecution
- Says public safety will remain his "top priority" in a fourth term, focused on "prosecuting violent offenders, especially those who target vulnerable members of the community" [S1][S3].
- Says he has "become increasingly concerned about the impact of repeat offenders and will use all legal means to address the disproportionate impact they are having on the criminal justice system" [S1].
- Pledges to advocate for victims' constitutional and statutory rights and to work collaboratively with law enforcement [S1].
- Helped stand up a mental health court, now run by District Court 1, which he described as a restorative approach to underlying mental health and substance use disorders [S3].
- Criticized proposed bail-rule changes before the state Supreme Court, including a "willful flight" standard and a $200 cap on most misdemeanor bail. He said they would produce a "catch and release" cycle [S4].
- Said new indigent-defense caseload standards, adopted without state funding, have "created an affordability crisis" that could lead to dismissed cases. He also said state crime-lab toxicology backlogs of 10 to 20 months are delaying DUI and drug charges [S4].

### Transparency
- Clallam is one of four counties that publish prosecutor data online. He wants to add a dashboard of active cases [S3].

## Record

- According to the office's published data, 501 cases were referred in 2025 and 265 resulted in charges, with seven jury trials, 10 stipulated trials and 46 declined cases. Charged cases have declined from a six-year high of 408 in 2020 [S3].
- As of October 2025, 83 people had been referred to mental health court and 39 had entered a contract [S3].
