## What the office does

A district court has criminal jurisdiction over misdemeanors and gross misdemeanors (up to one year in jail and a $5,000 fine unless a statute says otherwise), hears traffic infractions, holds preliminary hearings, and sets bail and conditions of release [S2]. Schodowski describes District Court 1 as a single-judge court that handles traffic tickets, small claims, civil matters, misdemeanor and gross misdemeanor criminal cases and protection-order petitions [S1][S7]. It covers central and eastern Clallam County, from Lake Crescent to the Jefferson County line, while Forks-based District Court 2 serves the West End [S9]. The county's GIS layer places Port Angeles and Sequim addresses in District Court 1 [S3]. District Court 1 is its own electoral district: in 2022 its judge race drew 23,704 votes, against 1,940 for District Court 2 [S4]. The term is four years [S1].

## Race dynamics

- **Open seat.** Judge David Neupert, in the last year of his second four-year term, is retiring at the end of 2026 [S5]. He was re-elected unopposed in 2022 with 98.37% [S4]. During his tenure the court started a mental health court, now in its third year, and is building a veterans court on that model [S5].
- **Candidates.** Alex Schodowski is a Port Townsend defense and personal-injury lawyer. She has been the court's judge pro tem since 2016 and a Clallam County Superior Court commissioner since 2019, and before that she was a Clallam County deputy prosecutor [S1][S7]. Patrick David Murphy has been a Clallam County deputy prosecuting attorney since 2019, working mainly in District Court 1. He reports 11 years as a lawyer in all [S1][S6].
- **No primary.** With two candidates, the race goes straight to the general election [S1].
- **Debate.** At a Port Angeles Business Association debate on Sept. 1, Murphy said Schodowski's judicial philosophy "prioritizes the accused over the victim" and lets violent offenders be released. He criticized her pro tem rulings, including on conditions of release. Schodowski answered that a judge represents neither party, applies the law to the information before the court, and must impose "the least restrictive alternative" at release [S6]. They also disagreed on how strictly the court screens defendants for indigency before appointing a public defender [S6].
- **Endorsements.** Schodowski lists Judge Neupert, District Court 2 Judge Bruce Hanify, retired Superior Court Judge Chris Melly, about a dozen district and municipal court judges from other counties, the Jefferson County prosecutor and sheriff, and current and former deputy prosecutors [S7][S12]. Murphy lists retired Clallam County Sheriff Bill Benedict, a retired undersheriff, retired deputies and troopers, and a former District Court 1 therapeutic courts manager. He says he is not seeking endorsements from practicing Clallam County attorneys or from prosecutors [S14].
- **Money.** As of mid-September, Schodowski reported $19,729 raised and $12,095 spent [S10], and Murphy $22,398 raised and $9,130 spent [S11].
- **Bar ratings.** No bar-association rating or poll for this race was found.

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

- **Experience (`experience`).** Schodowski runs as the continuity candidate. She cites about ten years of service as the court's pro tem judge, the outgoing judge's endorsement, and the slogan "Ready on Day 1" [S6][S7][S12]. Murphy has no judicial service and says District Court 1 "needs a new, more effective direction" [S1][S13].
- **Release conditions and accountability (`safety`).** Murphy's statements lean toward enforcement. He says release conditions for violent offenses have been insufficient, puts victims and community first, and wants indigency rules for public defense applied strictly. He also backs restorative justice and the therapeutic courts, with consistent sanctions for violations [S6][S8][S13]. Schodowski's background spans prosecution and defense. She stresses that release law requires the least restrictive alternative and pledges to continue the mental health and veterans courts. She also lists community safety as a priority [S6][S12].
- **Judicial philosophy (`judicial`).** Neither candidate takes a position on reading law as written versus pragmatic interpretation. Their disagreement is about bail and release practice and court administration, not interpretive method [S6].

## Not differentiating

- Both live in Sequim and stress fair treatment of everyone who comes to court [S1][S8].
- Both support keeping the court's mental health court [S12][S13].
