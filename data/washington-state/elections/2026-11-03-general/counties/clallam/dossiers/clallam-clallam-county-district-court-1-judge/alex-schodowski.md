## Background

Alexandrea "Alex" Schodowski grew up in Kentucky and holds a B.A. from Morehead State University and a J.D. from Western Michigan University Thomas M. Cooley Law School. She was admitted to the Washington bar in 2011 [S1][S3]. She lives in Sequim [S5]. She began her career as a Clallam County deputy prosecuting attorney and spent more than five years there. She worked in District Courts 1 and 2 and later the felony division, and reports specializing in DUI, vehicular assault, sexual assault, violent crimes and forensic interviewing [S2][S3][S4]. She has also been a contract city prosecutor for Port Townsend [S1]. With her husband she now runs Schodowski Law Inc. PS in Port Townsend, handling criminal defense, DUI defense, personal injury and civil cases [S2][S3][S4]. From 2020 to 2024 she was in-house counsel for the Peninsula Housing Authority [S2]. Her community roles include president (2020-2024) and now secretary of the Olympic Peninsula chapter of Washington Women Lawyers, and a seat on the Swan School board (2021-2023) [S1].

## Judicial service

- Judge pro tem in Clallam County District Court 1 since 2016, filling in for Judge David Neupert during absences, sometimes for two weeks at a time. She has handled every calendar the court runs [S1][S3].
- Clallam County Superior Court commissioner since 2019; pro tem judge in District Court 2 [S1]. She says she has presided over thousands of cases in all [S5].
- No published opinions exist for a pro tem district court judge. At the Sept. 1 debate her opponent criticized some of her pro tem rulings on release conditions. The specific cases were not identified in the coverage [S4].

## Positions

- **Judicial approach.** Says the judge "does not represent either party in the courtroom," and that the judge's job is to listen to both sides and apply current law to the information before the court [S4]. Says she will apply the law "fairly and consistently" and treat people with dignity [S1][S5].
- **Release conditions.** At the debate she said a charged crime "has not been pled or proven," that "the least restrictive alternative should be imposed. That is the law," and that she does not "disregard public safety in any way, shape or form" [S4].
- **Public defense.** Says appointing counsel for indigent defendants is a constitutional requirement, "not a choice." She says the public defender's office is fully staffed for District Court and is meeting the state's new caseload limits [S4].
- **Therapeutic courts.** Pledges to continue the Mental Health Court and Veterans Court developed under Judge Neupert [S2][S3].
- **Court access.** Supports continued electronic filing and procedures that self-represented litigants can follow; promises efficient case management [S2].
- **Community safety.** Lists community safety among her priorities, citing her years prosecuting serious crimes, while "upholding the constitutional rights of all individuals" [S2].

## Endorsements

Judge David Neupert, who said she "has earned my trust to step into my courtroom" [S2][S3]. Also District Court 2 Judge Bruce Hanify, retired Clallam County Superior Court Judge Chris Melly, retired judge Erik Rohrer (listed with Clallam Superior Court and District Court 2 titles), Jefferson County District Court Judge Mindy Walker, and district and municipal court judges from Grays Harbor, King, Mason, San Juan, Skagit, Snohomish, Spokane and Thurston counties and several cities. Her list also names Jefferson County Prosecuting Attorney James Kennedy, Jefferson County Sheriff Andy Pernsteiner, current and former deputy prosecutors, local attorneys, and Port Angeles City Council member Mark Hodgson [S2].
