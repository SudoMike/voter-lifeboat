## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including the tax structure, natural-resource and timber policy, K-12 funding and policy, and criminal-justice statutes. The 24th District covers Clallam and Jefferson counties and most of Grays Harbor County [S2].

## Race dynamics
An open seat: Rep. Steve Tharinger, D-Port Townsend, who has held it since 2011 and chairs the House capital budget committee, is retiring [S2]. Independent Marcia Kelbon of Quilcene, a fire commissioner, engineer, patent attorney and former biotech executive who ran for state Senate as a Republican in 2024, led the five-candidate primary with 37.81%; Democrat Kaylee Kuehn of Sequim, a former legislative aide and high school teacher, was second with 25.98%, with three other Democrats splitting the rest (S1, S3, S4; kaylee-kuehn.md S5).

Party affiliation is itself an issue: Kuehn questions how an independent would get committee assignments or be effective, while Kelbon says she could caucus with either party and that no party or special interest will drive her vote [S2]. On policy, Kelbon opposes the 2026 income tax and calls the budget a spending problem, wants to roll back building regulations and the energy code, restore felony drug possession, and has called for repealing or modifying the Climate Commitment Act; Kuehn calls the tax code regressive and wants a "fairer" system, would keep investing in the Housing Trust Fund, defends Climate Commitment Act investments, and is endorsed by labor, the Washington Education Association and the district's Democratic legislators (S1, S2, S3; marcia-kelbon.md S2, S7; kaylee-kuehn.md S2). At the September League of Women Voters forum both backed marriage equality, but they split on transgender girls in girls' sports [S3].

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Kelbon opposes the 2026 tax on income over $1 million and any state income tax in her statement; Kuehn wants a less regressive tax system without naming a new tax.
2. **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** Kelbon opposes transgender girls on girls' teams; Kuehn supports trans individuals and "100% bodily autonomy."
3. **Climate:** Kelbon's repeal-or-modify stance on the Climate Commitment Act versus Kuehn's defense of its investments.
4. **Public safety:** Kelbon would restore felony drug possession; Kuehn treats addiction as a health issue while revisiting the sentencing grid.
5. **Housing and spending:** Kelbon's deregulation and spending-restraint focus versus Kuehn's mix of Housing Trust Fund investment and permit streamlining.
6. **Immigration:** Kuehn's platform opposes unconstitutional ICE actions; no position found for Kelbon.
7. **Parents and schools:** neither has a stated position on notification or opt-outs; Kelbon has floated splitting school funding with parents who choose private schools.
