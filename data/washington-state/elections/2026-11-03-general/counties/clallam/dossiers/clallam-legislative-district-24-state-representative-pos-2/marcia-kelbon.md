## Background
Marcia Kelbon (States No Party Preference) lives on a tree farm in Quilcene and has been an elected Jefferson County Fire Protection District 2 (Quilcene) commissioner since 2022 [S1][S3]. She holds bachelor's and master's degrees in chemical engineering and a law degree from the University of Washington, worked four years as a U.S. Navy civilian engineer, practiced as a patent attorney for 12 years, and then spent 19 years as general counsel and a senior executive of a biotech company she helped take from start-up to a publicly traded company with an approved product [S1][S3]. She retired from that company about six years ago and was a newspaper columnist until she announced her campaign [S2][S5]. She ran for the 24th District state Senate seat as a Republican in 2024 [S4][S7]. She says she was a Democrat for many years and a Republican for a few years, has resigned from party positions, and now runs as an independent [S2][S4][S5]. She said she could caucus with either party depending on where it made sense, and that the centrist Cascade Party is helping her campaign with voter data, though she is not among its candidates [S5]. In the August 4 primary she placed first among five candidates with 37.81% (19,818 votes), ahead of Kaylee Kuehn's 25.98% (13,617), per final results printed by The Daily World [S9]. After the primary she named economic and business development as her top priorities and called her business experience the clearest difference from Kuehn [S6]. She has not served in the Legislature [S1].

## Positions

### Taxes
- Statement: will promote affordability by "limiting regressive taxes, and ensuring the state does not tax income" [S1].
- Opposes the 2026 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million; said she could see a case for a graduated income tax paired with sales and property tax cuts if put to voters as a constitutional amendment, but "That is not what happened. ... We layered income tax on top of it," and predicted "it's coming to all of us" [S5].
- Her site says she "will work for tax relief and to ensure that an income tax, should it be sustained, is not extended to medium-income earners" [S2]. Proposes taxing business on profits rather than gross receipts, particularly in early growth years [S8].

### Spending and budget
- Calls the state's finances "a spending problem," not a revenue problem; lawmakers should cover essentials first "and then see what's left" [S5].
- Says the state budget "has more than doubled over the last 10 years" and should be reined in, funding education, infrastructure and public health and safety first [S8]; her statement calls for "requiring accountability and effectiveness" [S1].

### Housing
- Statement: will promote affordability "by trimming regulations on housing" [S1].
- Would amend the Growth Management Act to allow smaller lots (outside urban growth areas and in rural centers), roll back a recent energy code update that added construction costs, and offer density bonuses for lower-cost units [S4][S7]. Her site calls for revising the Growth Management Act and energy codes with an emphasis on starter homes [S2].

### Local control
- Says the Legislature has imposed many unfunded mandates on counties and she will work to reduce them [S2].

### Public safety
- Called for restoring felony drug possession, downgraded to a gross misdemeanor after the 2021 Blake decision: "We need to bring back that felony," to steer people into drug court and treatment [S5][S7].
- Says the state should be a stronger partner to law enforcement and prioritize treatment for substance abuse and mental health to address homelessness [S1][S2][S7].

### Climate, energy and timber
- At the July primary forum she was the only Position 2 candidate who said she would repeal the Climate Commitment Act, calling it a regressive tax [S7]. Her site says the act "should at least be modified" and favors tax incentives rather than penalties [S2]. In September she called global warming a "serious problem," said she is not convinced the act has been effective, cited a de facto 50-60 cent per gallon fuel cost, and supports tax incentives for green energy and exploring small modular nuclear reactors [S4][S5].
- Would protect old growth while continuing to harvest younger stands in rotation [S7].

### Health care
- Would push for federal changes to the critical-access hospital definition so Olympic Medical Center would qualify [S4]. At the national level she said she supports "get[ting] insurance companies out of the mix completely" [S4].

### Childcare and education
- Would examine in-home daycare licensing and use underused school buildings for daycare; said she was "distressed" by cuts to Transition to Kindergarten [S4].
- Wants full funding for school facilities and special education, with more emphasis on performance, tutoring and less screen time [S2][S8].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- At the September League of Women Voters forum she said she backs marriage equality and preventing discrimination based on sexuality, but does not support transgender girls competing on girls' sports teams: "I don't think it's fair for a girl to be competing with somebody who developed with the advantage of testosterone" [S4]. She said women need "safe spaces" and that if someone has "fully transitioned, I don't have a problem with anybody being anywhere" [S4].
- Her statement pledges to "respect people from all walks of life" [S1].

### Parents and schools
- Asked about public money going to religious or charter schools, she called herself "a big supporter of public schools" but said "sometimes our public schools are not working for a given child," and wondered whether state funding could be split between public schools and parents who choose private schools [S4]. No position found on parental notification, records access or curriculum opt-outs as of 2026-10-09.
