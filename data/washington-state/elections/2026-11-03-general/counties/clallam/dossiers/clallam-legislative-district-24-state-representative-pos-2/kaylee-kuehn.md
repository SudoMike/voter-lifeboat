## Background
Kaylee Kuehn (Prefers Democratic Party) grew up in Sequim, graduated from Sequim High School in 2015 and lives there with her husband and young daughter [S2][S7]. She earned a bachelor's degree from The Evergreen State College and a master's in education from Grand Canyon University [S1]. She interned in the state Senate and worked as a legislative aide for Senators Manka Dhingra and Steve Conway, then taught high school math, history and government, coached girls wrestling and golf, and advised the speech and debate club [S1][S2]. She now does curriculum and communications work for a nonprofit teaching civic engagement to young people and advocates in Olympia for the Association of Western Pulp & Paper Workers [S1][S2]. She chairs the Sequim High School Career and Technical Education advisory committee [S1]. This is her first run for office [S1]. In the August 4 primary she placed second among five candidates (and first among four Democrats) with 25.98% (13,617 votes) to Marcia Kelbon's 37.81% (19,818), per final results printed by The Daily World [S5][S9]. She describes herself as "a reasonable, more center-left person" [S4].

## Positions

### Taxes and spending
- Platform: Washington "has one of the most regressive tax systems in the country, placing too much of the burden on working people and those on fixed incomes"; she will "fight for a fairer tax system where everyone just pays their fair share," with "greater accountability and transparency" in spending [S2]. Lists addressing the regressive tax code among her top priorities and says she will spend taxpayer dollars carefully [S8].
- Said "throwing more and more money at education is not really the answer," while seeking more special-education funding [S3]. Said there is little the state can do to raise Medicaid reimbursement rates given the budget shortfall [S3].

### Housing
- Would continue state investment in the Housing Trust Fund and avoid "broad-stroke approaches" that ignore local differences [S3]. At the July forum pledged to streamline permitting and reduce bureaucracy [S6]. Platform calls for "more workforce housing" [S2].

### Public safety
- Says the state should work with law enforcement agencies "to ensure the highest standard of public safety" [S2]. At the July forum she agreed addiction is a health issue but wanted to revisit the sentencing grid, which she said has been loosened to the point that police and prosecutors cannot hold offenders accountable [S6].

### Immigration
- Platform: "I do not support unconstitutional actions by ICE or any effort to normalize authoritarian tactics"; "The government should never be able to seize people off the street, deny them due process, or operate outside the limits of the Constitution" [S2].

### Climate and environment
- Will "support policies that reduce emissions, mitigate the risks of climate change, invest in sustainable practices," while keeping energy-intensive, trade-exposed industries and union jobs in-state to avoid emissions leakage, and will work to ensure Climate Commitment Act investments reach rural communities [S2]. Wants Climate Commitment Act revenue kept from being diverted to other budget areas [S3]. At the July forum she urged nuance on the act, noting most of its dollars flow back to rural communities [S6].
- Calls sustainable forestry a top priority, would protect old growth and wants logs milled in-state [S6].

### Technology
- On a statewide data-center moratorium, said she does not support a one-size-fits-all approach but is concerned about the energy and water data centers use [S3].

### Health care
- Would raise state Medicaid and Medicare reimbursement rates to keep rural hospitals open, especially after federal HR 1 cuts, and support the Washington Health Corps for rural recruitment [S2]. Called federal Medicaid cuts "reprehensible" [S3]. In April she said "most countries have this thing figured out" without extreme premiums [S7].

### Childcare and education
- Would expand childcare capacity and eligibility for subsidies, and support shared childcare facilities run with employers and school districts [S2][S3].
- Would fully fund public schools, special education and educators, address rural funding disadvantages and regionalization policies, and expand career and technical education and apprenticeships [S2][S3].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Platform: "defending 100% bodily autonomy, protecting the right to marry who you love" and equal treatment under law [S2].
- At the September forum said she supports "the entirety of the queer community, including trans individuals"; as a girls wrestling coach she supported nonbinary students on her team "to ensure they were treated the same as everyone else" [S3].

### Parents and schools
- No position found on parental notification, records access or curriculum opt-outs as of 2026-10-09.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement found as of 2026-10-09. She calls for a "fairer tax system" and addressing the regressive tax code without naming the 2026 tax [S2][S8].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** no statement found as of 2026-10-09.
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** no statement naming the initiative found as of 2026-10-09. She supports transgender individuals and described including nonbinary athletes on her team [S3].
