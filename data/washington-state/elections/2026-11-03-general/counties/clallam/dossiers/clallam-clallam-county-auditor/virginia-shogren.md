## Background

Virginia Shogren of Sequim filed for auditor during filing week, her first run for county office [S4]. She is a former U.S. Patent and Trademark Office attorney and law firm associate, and principal of her own practice [S1][S2]. She told a business forum that she has run a private law practice since 2005. She said it handles many patent cases, has defended businesses against COVID-19 mandates, and handles "election integrity cases involving access to ballot records and exposure of noncitizen voter registrations" [S5]. She holds a BA and BS with distinction from Stanford University and a JD from the University of Washington School of Law [S1][S2]. Her listed elected experience is "District level California Delegate" [S1]. Her community service includes Sequim Sunrise Rotary and the Port Angeles High School sailing team [S1].

Identity note: the 2022 news reports below concern "Sequim lawyer Virginia Shogren," admitted to the Washington bar in 2003, who represented an election-integrity organization [S6][S7]. That matches the candidate's city, profession and the election-integrity practice she described at the 2026 forum [S5].

## Positions

### Elections administration
- Running "to restore trust in our elections." She calls for "secure, transparent elections with paper ballots you can watch and count" and says the people, "not software or untraceable methods," should maintain oversight [S1][S2].
- Said the county's voter registration list "is a mess," citing a door-to-door survey by Republicans that she said found many problems [S5].
- Raised concerns about election-night chain of custody, saying two people pick up ballot boxes but only one delivers them [S5].
- Opposes ranked-choice voting: "It complicates the ballot for a lot of people. It complicates the tabulation process and could cause an increase in costs at the auditor's office" [S5].
- Would support consolidating elections to once a year, and favors a national holiday for in-person voting [S5].
- In closing remarks she said the county can "cut through this worrying amount of modern digital and financial slavery by returning to constitutional elections that place the political power of Clallam County back into the hands of the people" [S5].

### Spending and fiscal oversight
- Pledges "strong fiscal responsibility. That means watching every county expense closely, cutting waste, and running services efficiently. When government spends less, there's more money left in your pocket" [S1][S2].
- At the August forum she said, "DOGE opened the books and the scale of government waste has become impossible." She described the auditor's combined duties (elections, recording, passports) as efficient but "a single point of failure" [S5].
- Listed high housing costs and "increasing taxes" among the problems county residents face [S5].

## Record

- She has held no county or state office [S1].
- In 2022 the Washington Supreme Court dismissed a petition she filed for the Washington Election Integrity Coalition United against Gov. Jay Inslee. The petition alleged that the Department of Licensing registered noncitizens to vote. The court ordered the organization to pay about $9,600 and Shogren about $18,800 in sanctions for claims the Attorney General's motion called "so devoid of legal merit that there was no reasonable possibility that it would have been successful" [S6].
- The Attorney General's office then filed a complaint against her with the state bar's Office of Disciplinary Counsel. It alleged she "advanced frivolous legal arguments and made allegations of voter fraud with no basis in fact." She did not respond to requests for comment at the time [S7]. The outcome of that complaint was not found as of 2026-10-09.
