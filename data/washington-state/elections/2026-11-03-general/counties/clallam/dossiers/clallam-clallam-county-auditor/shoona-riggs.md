## Background

Shoona Riggs of Port Angeles is the incumbent Clallam County Auditor and filed for re-election in May [S1][S6]. She was elected in November 2014, defeating Kim Yacklin with about 59% of the first-night count, and has held the office since 2015 [S1][S3]. Before that she was the county's chief deputy auditor and elections supervisor [S1][S3]. She cites 36 years of work in the Clallam and Jefferson County auditor's offices, covering elections, recording, vehicle and vessel licensing and finance, including 25 years in elections administration [S1][S7]. She has been a Washington State Certified Election Administrator since 1999. She holds a 2022 Master Certificate in High Performance Leadership from the National Association of Counties and is nearing completion of the Certified Elections/Registration Administrator (CERA) program [S1]. She was president of the Washington State Association of County Auditors in 2021-2022 [S1]. Her other community service includes Klahhane Gymnastics (vice president and board member) and time as a foster parent [S1].

## Positions

### Elections administration
- Says her priority "has always been protecting voter confidence and ensuring every eligible citizen has the opportunity to register and vote." She describes elections "conducted fairly, impartially, and in full compliance" with federal and state constitutions and law [S1].
- To improve election integrity, she said the process should be more public. Her office gives students tours of the election process, and she said offering tours to groups such as the Port Angeles Business Association would build community understanding [S7].
- Responding to her opponent, she said Republican voter-roll survey data reached her office two years after it was gathered and showed only a handful of records needing correction. She said two people always deliver ballot boxes, and that ballots stay in the box if two are not available [S7].
- On ranked-choice voting, she said her opinion does not matter because she would follow the law if it passed. She noted that jurisdictions would choose, and said "it seems to be very complicated" [S7].
- Supports consolidating elections to once a year. She said an April fire district election cost the district $36,000 when it would have paid barely $1,000 at a primary or general [S7].

### Spending and office management
- Says she has "focused on professionalism, accountability, transparency, and responsible stewardship of public resources across all divisions of the office" [S1].

## Record

- Auditor since 2015, previously chief deputy auditor and elections supervisor [S1][S3].
- In January 2017 her office found 125 uncounted ballots from the November 2016 general election in a courthouse walk-up drop box. They had been deposited on election night after 5 p.m. and were not collected [S4]. The results had already been certified, so the ballots were not opened; the voters were notified and credited with voting. The ballots would not have changed any outcome [S4][S5]. Riggs called it an "unfortunate and egregious failure," took responsibility, added drop-box logs and a final post-election sweep, and invited an out-of-cycle review by the Secretary of State's Office [S4][S5]. The county canvassing board found that "no election laws/policies were knowingly, willfully or fraudulently violated" [S5].
