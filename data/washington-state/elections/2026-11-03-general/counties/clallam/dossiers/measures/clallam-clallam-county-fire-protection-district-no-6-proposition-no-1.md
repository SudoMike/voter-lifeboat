## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would let Clallam County Fire Protection District No. 6 levy a regular property tax of up to $1.50 per $1,000 of assessed value in 2026, for collection in 2027 [S1][S4]. This is a one-year levy lid lift. The amount actually levied for 2027 becomes the base for future levy limits, which then grow under the usual limits [S1][S4].

- The explanatory statement says state law limits annual growth in regular property tax revenue. Because of that limit and changes in assessed value, the district's rate "has declined below the statutory maximum" [S1].
- The money would support fire protection and emergency services: volunteer recruitment and retention, training, apparatus and equipment, facilities and operations [S1].
- The senior and disabled exemption (RCW 84.36.381) applies to the added taxes [S1][S4].
- A simple majority is needed to pass (RCW 84.55.050(1)) [S4].
- The district, Three Rivers Fire Department, is the county's smallest fire district and is all-volunteer [S2]. It serves communities along the La Push, Quillayute Prairie, Mora and Goodman Mainline roads, bounded on the west by Olympic National Park and the Quileute Nation [S2].

## Cost

- **Current rate:** for taxes due in 2025, DOR lists $0.47677 per $1,000, raising $37,763 on about $79 million of assessed value [S5]. The statement for puts anticipated 2026 tax revenue at $38,941 [S2].
- **Computed for this dossier from [S1] and [S5]:**
  - At 2025 assessed values, $1.50 per $1,000 would raise about $119,000, roughly 3.1 times the 2025 levy.
  - On a $300,000 property, the district tax would go from about $143 a year to up to $450 in 2027.
  - Actual figures depend on 2026 assessed values and on how much the board levies up to the $1.50 cap.

## Background

- The board adopted Resolution No. 2026-01 on July 21, 2026, by a vote of 2 in favor, 0 opposed, 0 abstaining and 1 absent [S4].
- The resolution says costs "consistently exceed the statutory 101% limitation on tax levy increases" [S4].
- The statement for says the district last asked for a levy lid lift in 2010 [S2]. It says expenses have risen more than 100% over 15 years while revenue rose 25%, and that tax revenue has not kept up with inflation [S2].
- It cites rising costs under National Fire Protection Association standards: basic turnout gear that cost $2,000 in 2010 now costs $5,000 [S2].
- It says timber revenue has averaged about $12,000 a year since 2010, but the latest DNR projection for 2027 is under $1,000 [S2].

## Arguments for

- Chip Keen, Tom Rosmond and Scott Horton wrote the statement for [S2]. Rosmond and Horton sign the resolution as district commissioners [S4].
- The statement asks voters to "support our all-volunteer fire department so that we may continue to safely serve you as first responders" [S2].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote lets the district raise its regular levy from about $0.48 to up to $1.50 per $1,000, roughly tripling its property tax revenue [S1][S5]. The new amount resets the base for future years [S1]. The measure restores rate authority that eroded under the 1% limit, within the statutory maximum, and the dollar amounts are small. That is the case for +1. The size of the rate increase puts it at +2.
- `spending`: the money maintains a volunteer department's equipment and training [S1][S2]; no mapping.
