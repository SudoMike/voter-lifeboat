## What it does

Proposed Charter Amendment No. 3 would amend Article XI so that the full text of every future proposed charter amendment must be printed in the local voters' pamphlet [S1]. It adds one sentence to Section 11.20.10: "The full text of any proposed Charter amendment shall be printed in the local voters' pamphlet" [S5][S8].

- The explanatory statement says Article XI now requires "the text of the amendment" to be printed. The change would require "the full text," "consistent with the treatment of the text of proposed statewide initiatives in the state voters' pamphlet" [S1].
- The commission's findings cite RCW 29A.32.241, which requires the local pamphlet to include "the text of each measure," and RCW 29A.32.070(10), which requires the full text of statewide measures in the state pamphlet [S5].
- The findings say the Auditor "has not always included the full text of proposed Charter amendments." They say the Prosecuting Attorney "has opined that the full text need not be included ... just the text," and that the difference is too vague [S5].
- The commission said it wanted to avoid the risk of a voter-approved amendment "being adjudged void" for missing text and "to err on the side of caution" [S5].
- A simple majority is needed to pass, according to the resolution cover sheet [S4].

## Cost

- No tax or fee is involved, and the VoteWA record has no fiscal statement [S3].
- At the Aug. 18 debate, Ron Richards said the cost would be "essentially nothing" because the pamphlet is printed anyway. Jim Stoffer said the costs are not currently known [S7].
- This year's pamphlet prints the explanatory statement and a link to each resolution for the charter amendments, not the full amendment text [S1].

## Who put it on the ballot

- The 2025 Charter Review Commission approved the resolution Dec. 22, 2025 [S5]. County elections stamped it received Dec. 29, 2025 [S4]. The commission's term ended Dec. 31, 2025 [S6].
- The statement for says the commission approved it 14-0 [S2]. No commission minutes were reviewed to confirm the count.

## Arguments for

- Ron Richards, a commission member, wrote the statement for [S2][S7]. It says that despite state law requiring the text of each measure, Clallam's pamphlet "has not always included the full text of proposed charter amendments," and that "in legal documents the devil is in the details" [S2].
- It cites the 14-0 commission vote as a sign the amendment is reasonable [S2].
- At the debate Richards said the amendment "tells the auditor to do what the state law says" [S7].

## Arguments against

- Jim Stoffer, a commission member, wrote the statement against [S2][S7]. It says state law already requires the text of each measure plus a neutral explanatory statement, and the county makes the full amendment language available [S2].
- It calls the concern that an amendment could be invalidated "precautionary, not established Washington law," and says no appellate decision has been identified invalidating a county charter amendment for this reason [S2].
- It says "the Charter should establish the framework of county government, not dictate how election officials print the voters' pamphlet" [S2].
- At the debate Stoffer said the auditor's office already follows state law, and that "we never went to the county auditor to ask her and her staff their opinion on this" [S7].
