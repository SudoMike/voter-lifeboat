## What it does

Proposed Charter Amendment No. 1 would add a new Section 2.50 to Article II of the Clallam County Charter [S1][S5]. Each county commissioner would have to hold at least one town hall meeting a year in their own commissioner district. The three commissioners together would have to hold at least one town hall a year in each of the three districts [S1][S5]. All of these meetings would have to take place outside normal business hours, with notice posted at least 30 days ahead [S1][S5].

- The explanatory statement says the charter now does not require commissioners to meet the public in their districts outside regular board meetings, "which are typically held in Port Angeles during normal working hours" [S1].
- Commissioners are nominated by district and elected countywide for four-year terms [S10].
- The ballot title cites Charter Review Commission Resolution No. 3 [S1]. The county posts it as CRC2025-003 [S5][S6].
- A simple majority is needed to pass, according to the resolution cover sheet [S4].

## Cost

- No tax or fee is involved, and the VoteWA record has no fiscal statement [S3].
- The amendment itself sets no budget. Opponent Jim Stoffer listed "the staff hours" among the things it does not address [S8].

## Who put it on the ballot

- Voters elected a 15-member Charter Review Commission in November 2024, five from each commissioner district. The commission reviews the charter every five years, a schedule voters adopted in 2015 [S7]. Its term ended Dec. 31, 2025 [S6].
- The commission's resolution says many residents, "especially those residents in rural or outlying areas," feel "disconnected from their local government." It says regular town halls "would strengthen trust in government, increase public participation, and lead to more responsive and thoughtful policymaking" [S5].
- The signed copies are dated October 2025 [S5]. County elections stamped the filed packet received Oct. 23, 2025 [S4].
- The commission's vote count on this resolution was not found in the sources reviewed.
- In a July 14, 2026 press release, the county commissioners said they had held "town hall events in each of the three Districts" while responding to the commission's separate, non-charter recommendations [S9].

## Arguments for

- Ron Richards, a Charter Review Commission member, wrote the pamphlet statement for [S2][S8]. It says the amendment would require "two town hall meetings a year in each County Commissioner District outside of normal working hours" [S2].
- The statement says the amendment responds to commissioners holding regular meetings at the courthouse "during normal working hours, far removed from the people in the outlying areas." It also cites the commissioners' practice of "not answering questions presented to them in the public comment portions of their regular meetings" [S2].
- At an Aug. 18, 2026 Port Angeles Business Association debate, Richards said town halls would give more time to explain issues and hear from the public. He said commission meetings at 9 a.m. Mondays and 10 a.m. Tuesdays are hard for many people to attend [S8].
- The "for" committee first listed Richards and Jeff Carter [S4]. In April 2026 Carter asked to be removed, writing that "new information has become available" and he no longer wanted to co-edit a statement with Richards [S4]. The VoteWA record still lists Carter as a committee member [S3].

## Arguments against

- Jim Stoffer, also a commission member, wrote the statement against [S2][S7][S8]. It says: "Town halls are valuable. Putting their scheduling into the County Charter is not" [S2].
- The statement says commissioners "can—and already do—hold town halls throughout the county without a Charter mandate," and that engagement rules belong in county policies that can change over time [S2].
- It says the amendment does not define a "town hall," does not guarantee questions and answers, and does not clearly define "normal working hours" or where notice must be posted [S2][S8].
- At the August debate Stoffer also listed scheduling, location, the Open Public Meetings Act, technical setup and staff hours as things the resolution does not address [S8].
