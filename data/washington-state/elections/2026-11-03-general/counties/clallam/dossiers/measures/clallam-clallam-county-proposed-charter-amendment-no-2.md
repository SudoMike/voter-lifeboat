## What it does

Proposed Charter Amendment No. 2 would add Sections 8.100 to 8.140 to Article VIII of the charter, creating an "independent Ethics Review Board" [S1][S5]. The board would review complaints that elected county officials violated the Clallam County Code of Ethics [S1][S5].

- **Members.** Three members, one from each commissioner district, serving staggered three-year terms without pay [S1][S5]. No member may be a county employee, an elected official, a candidate for county office, or an immediate family member of one [S5].
- **Who appoints.** Members are appointed in rotation, set by lot, by the County Auditor, Treasurer, Assessor and Director of Community Development [S5]. The county commissioners are not on the list [S5].
- **Duties.** The board would set its own procedures, receive and review written complaints, decide whether each falls within its jurisdiction, conduct "impartial reviews" and issue written findings [S1][S5].
- **Limits.** "The Board shall not impose penalties or sanctions" [S5]. It could not consider conduct from before the amendment takes effect. Complaints must come from people with first-hand knowledge, certified under penalty of perjury; anonymous or second-hand complaints are not accepted [S5].
- **Transparency.** Final findings would be posted on the county website. Meetings could be closed only to protect complainants or witnesses during active investigations [S1][S5].
- A simple majority is needed to pass, according to the resolution cover sheet [S4].

**What exists now.** The explanatory statement says the county code already has a code of ethics covering all county personnel, including elected officials [S1]. The charter requires the commissioners to provide by ordinance for financial disclosure by elected officials and a code of ethics "for other officers and employees" [S9]. The commission's resolution says the existing code "includes no mechanism for the review of ethical concerns involving elected county officials" [S5].

## Cost

- No tax or fee is involved, and the VoteWA record has no fiscal statement [S3]. Board members would serve without pay [S1].
- No estimate of staff or legal costs was found. Opponents say the process would take staff time and money (see below) [S2][S8].

## Who put it on the ballot

- The Charter Review Commission elected in November 2024 proposed it; the commission's term ended Dec. 31, 2025 [S6]. In a May 2025 public survey the commission asked whether the charter should create an ethics board or commission, noting that the charter requires an ethics ordinance "but it does not specify any requirements of the ordinance" [S7].
- The resolution was approved in November 2025 [S5]. County elections stamped it received Dec. 2, 2025 [S4]. Its original title also referred to "Establishing a Code of Ethics for Elected County Officials" [S5].
- The commission's vote count was not found in the sources reviewed.

## Arguments for

- Andrea Williams wrote the statement for [S2]. It says some actions by county officials, "while not illegal, can be unethical," and that suspected conflicts of interest, lack of transparency or misuse of office increase cynicism [S2].
- It describes the board as "investigative and advisory," not a replacement for the justice system, and says it would "offer elected officials clear guidance on best practices" [S2].
- At the Aug. 18 Port Angeles Business Association debate, Williams said adopting a strong ethics code "signals to every resident in our county that integrity, fairness and accountability are our baseline expectations" [S8]. Asked whether the board could be weaponized, she pointed to the jurisdiction and impartial-review steps and said: "Almost everything can be weaponized. I don't know how to stop it" [S8].
- Ron Richards said the county once had an ethics ordinance with misdemeanor and criminal sanctions, that later changes "chipped away at the effectiveness," and that this amendment is meant to strengthen it [S8].
- **The "for" statement was nearly withdrawn.** On Aug. 20, 2026, two days after the debate, Williams emailed the Elections Manager that she was "inclined to agree that Clallam County does NOT need an Ethics Code" and asked whether her statement could be withdrawn [S4]. On Aug. 25 she wrote that she had decided to leave the statement in the pamphlet [S4]. The statement appears in the printed pamphlet [S2].

## Arguments against

- Cindy Kelly wrote the statement against [S2]. It says state law, PDC financial disclosure, recall and regular elections already provide safeguards, and that the board "could create a costly, time-consuming process that becomes politicized, duplicates existing oversight, and diverts staff and funds from core public services" [S2].
- At the debate Kelly said a board would need funding, which might lead to higher taxes [S8].
- Charter Review Commission member Alex Fane also spoke against it: "Everyone supports ethical government. The question is whether Proposition 2 solves an actual problem or creates new problems" [S8]. He said even a dismissed complaint would require "legal analysis, staff preparation, a public meeting, an executive session and records management" [S8].
- Fane said complaints under the existing ethics code, which covers county employees, go through human resources [S8].
