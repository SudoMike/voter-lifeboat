## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would let Quillayute Valley School District No. 402 (Forks) issue $34,000,000 in general obligation bonds maturing within 25 years. The bonds would pay for a new middle school replacing Forks Middle School, repaid by annual excess property taxes [S1][S3].

- The new school would replace three existing buildings on the Forks Middle School site [S1]. It may include flexible learning spaces, a single secure main entrance, a reworked parking and drop-off area, a full-size gym and cafeteria for student and community use, and outdoor play space [S1].
- News coverage describes the current campus as four separate buildings that students walk between outdoors [S4][S5]. The pamphlet says three [S1].
- The building dates from 1952 and was first used as the elementary school [S5][S6]. The district says it does not meet current safety, seismic and energy codes, and that any renovation would trigger code upgrades throughout, so replacement is more cost-effective [S4][S5].
- The resolution also authorizes the district to seek the state's school bond credit enhancement guarantee (chapter 39.98 RCW) [S3].
- The district lies in Clallam and Jefferson counties [S3].
- **Passing requires at least 60% yes votes and a minimum turnout** (validation), according to the cover sheet [S3].

## Cost

- The ballot materials do not estimate a tax rate [S1][S2].
- **District estimate:** about $1.94 per $1,000 of assessed value. That is almost $680 a year, or about $56 a month, on a home assessed at $350,000 [S4][S5].
- **Timing:** the district says the new bond would phase in during 2028 as the Forks High School bond is paid off at the end of 2028 [S4][S5].
- **The outgoing bond's rate:** for taxes due in 2025, the district's bond levy was $1.34127 per $1,000 ($1,012,999) on the Clallam County portion of the district. Its enrichment levy was $0.8374 per $1,000 [S7]. On a $350,000 home the 2025 bond rate comes to about $469 a year (computed from [S7]).
- **"Will not increase the tax level."** In March, board chair Bill Rohde said the middle school bond "would be a replacement for the outgoing Forks High School Bond and will not increase the tax level" [S6]. The district's later estimate of $1.94 is higher than the outgoing bond's 2025 rate of $1.34. No source reviewed explains the difference. Assessed values and the 2028 starting point are uncertain (comparison computed from [S4], [S5] and [S7]).
- **State money and timber revenue:** the district says passing in November 2026 qualifies it for about $21 million in state construction assistance (SCAP). Waiting until 2027 would cut that to about $17 million [S5]. In March the superintendent put the November 2026 figure at about $22 million [S6]. The district says it must have a voter-approved bond in place to collect timber sales revenue. It received about $9 million in timber revenue from 2008 to 2025 [S5].

## Background

- The board voted unanimously July 28, 2026 to put the bond on the ballot [S3][S5]. A Forks Middle School predesign committee of parents, staff, community members and local business owners developed the proposal [S5][S6]. The committee began meeting in September 2025 and surveyed the community in spring 2026 [S5][S6].
- In November 2025 the board unanimously adopted a resolution for the replacement project after learning it could receive state funding if a bond passed in November 2026 [S6].
- Problems the committee listed include campus security with multiple entrances, drop-off traffic on Spartan Avenue, roofing and drainage at the end of their useful life, limited classroom electrical capacity, and an electric heating system more than 37 years old [S6].
- The district calls the project one step in its long-range plan for all three Quillayute Valley schools [S6].

## Arguments for

- No one filed a statement for the measure [S1][S2].
- The district's case: safety under one roof with a single secure entrance, code compliance, lower long-run cost than remodeling, and capturing state matching money and timber revenue [S4][S5][S6].
- Board chair Bill Rohde: "Placing this measure on the November ballot gives our community the opportunity to maximize available state construction funding if voters approve the bond" [S5].
