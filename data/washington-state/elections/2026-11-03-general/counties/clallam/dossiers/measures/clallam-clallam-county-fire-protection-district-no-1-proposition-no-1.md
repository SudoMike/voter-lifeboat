## What it does

Proposition No. 1 is a multi-year levy lid lift for Clallam County Fire Protection District No. 1 [S1][S3]. It would set the district's regular property tax levy at $1.00 per $1,000 of assessed value, levied in 2026 for collection in 2027 [S1][S3]. For each of the next nine years, the allowed levy would grow by the greater of 1% or the June-to-June West Region CPI-U, instead of the usual 1% limit [S1][S3]. The 2035 levy (collected in 2036) becomes the base for later limits [S1][S3].

- Fire districts may levy up to $1.50 per $1,000, and the CPI growth stays subject to that cap [S1][S3].
- After the ten-year period, absent another vote, the levy returns to the statutory 1% growth limit [S1].
- The money pays for fire protection and emergency medical services. The resolution lists operating emergency vehicles, acquiring and improving stations and other capital facilities, and retaining trained personnel [S1][S3].
- Exemptions for qualifying seniors, veterans and others under chapter 84.36 RCW apply [S1].
- A simple majority is needed to pass (RCW 84.55.050(1)) [S3].

## Cost

- **Current rate.** The resolution says the district's regular levy is now "approximately $.47 per $1,000.00" [S3]. For taxes due in 2025, DOR lists $0.47059 per $1,000, raising $296,325 on about $630 million of assessed value [S5].
- **Proposed rate.** $1.00 per $1,000 for 2027 collection [S1].
- **Computed for this dossier from [S1] and [S5]:**
  - At 2025 assessed values, $1.00 per $1,000 would raise about $630,000, roughly 2.1 times the 2025 levy.
  - On a $300,000 property, the district tax would go from about $141 a year to $300 a year in 2027.
  - Actual figures depend on 2026 assessed values, and the CPI factor lets the levy grow faster than 1% a year after that.

## Background

- The resolution says rising demand and costs require money for fire operations, replacing apparatus and equipment, staffing and EMS "in excess of those which can be provided by the District's regular tax revenue levied at the current rate" [S3].
- It says annual cost increases "consistently exceed the statutory 101% limitation" [S3].
- The Forks Forum, printing the district's explanation, says the district is funded mainly by property taxes, that inflation has exceeded 1% for years, and that costs have risen faster than revenue [S4].
- The cover sheet calls the measure a "Multi Year Permanent Levy Lid Lift" [S3].
- The adoption date and the board's vote are illegible on the scanned resolution. Elections stamped the packet received July 30, 2026 [S3].

## Arguments for

- No one filed a statement for the measure [S1][S2].
- The district's case: maintain the current level of fire protection, fire suppression and emergency medical services and provide for firefighter safety [S1]. It also wants to keep up with inflation after years of 1% limits [S4].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote more than doubles the district's regular levy rate, from about $0.47 to $1.00 per $1,000 [S1][S3][S5]. It also replaces the 1% growth limit with a CPI-based limit for nine years [S1]. The lid lift restores taxing room the district already has under the $1.50 statutory cap; that framing is the case for +1. But the size of the increase and the multi-year inflation escalator put it at +2.
- `spending`: the district frames the money as maintaining current service levels [S1]; no mapping.
