## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would authorize Clallam County Fire Protection District No. 2 (Clallam 2 Fire-Rescue) to levy an emergency medical services property tax of up to $0.50 per $1,000 of assessed value [S1][S4]. The levy would run ten consecutive years, levied 2026-2035 and collected 2027-2036 [S1][S4]. It would be in addition to the district's regular levy [S4].

- The money could be used only for emergency medical services: personnel, training, equipment, supplies, vehicles and structures [S1][S4].
- To the extent the law allows, it would also cover uninsured ambulance transport costs for qualified district residents and taxpayers [S1][S4].
- The district covers about 85 square miles and an estimated 9,800 residents around Port Angeles, from Deer Park to Lake Sutherland, including the Lower Elwha Klallam Tribe. The city of Port Angeles runs its own fire department [S5][S7].
- **This is the second try in 2026.** The same levy appeared on the Aug. 4, 2026 primary ballot [S6][S7]. It received 1,824 yes votes and 1,541 no (54.2% yes, computed from [S9]), short of the 60% required [S8][S9].
- The district had already filed this resolution as a "provisional" general-election measure, to be withdrawn if the August vote passed [S4][S8].
- An earlier Fire District 2 EMS levy failed in August 2024, 47.08% yes to 52.9% no [S6].
- **Passing requires 60% yes votes plus validation.** Total turnout must exceed 40% of the turnout at the preceding general election; otherwise the yes votes must equal at least 60% of that 40% figure (RCW 84.52.069) [S4].

## Cost

- **Maximum rate:** up to $0.50 per $1,000 a year for ten years [S1].
- **Per household:** Chief Jake Patterson said the levy would cost about $300 a year on a home assessed at about $600,000 [S7]. At the same rate, a $300,000 home would pay $150 a year (computed from [S1]).
- **Revenue:** about $1.2 million a year [S7][S8]. DOR's 2025 figures give the district about $2.32 billion in assessed value; $0.50 per $1,000 on that is about $1.16 million (computed from [S11]).
- **Existing levy:** the district's regular levy is at the $1.50 statutory maximum [S11]. Voters raised it from 96 cents in November 2024 [S5]; that lid lift passed with 59.88% (3,650 to 2,446) [S10]. The EMS levy would bring the combined fire district rate to up to $2.00 per $1,000 (computed from [S1] and [S11]).

## Background

- The district has offered paramedic-level care since 2012, paid from its regular fire levy. It has never had a dedicated EMS levy [S2][S5][S7].
- Patterson said using the fire levy for EMS draws money from firefighting needs such as replacing vehicles and equipment [S7]. The explanatory statement says that approach "is not financially sustainable long term" [S1].
- The district answered 2,094 calls in 2025, its most ever. Calls are up 42% over a decade, and 68% were EMS [S7]. About 32% of the time, a new call comes in while crews are already committed [S2][S7].
- Patterson said Olympic Ambulance, the private partner, has no unit available in the district about 45% of the time [S7].
- The levy would pay to staff two response units around the clock and build a capital fund for ambulances and cardiac monitors [S7]. It would also let the district stop billing residents for what insurance does not cover [S7][S8].
- The board adopted the general-election resolution April 21, 2026 [S4]. Patterson said the district chose August first because heavy November turnout would raise the validation bar [S7].

## Arguments for

- Dr. Paul Craven, Kolby Konopaski and Dan McKeen wrote the statement for [S2]. It says the district has provided paramedic care since 2012 "without receiving ongoing federal, state, or county funding, relying solely on its regular fire levy" [S2].
- It says call volume rose more than 42% in a decade, with incidents overlapping 32% of the time, and that without the levy "the district can't hire additional personnel" [S2].
- It says the levy would "end out-of-pocket ambulance transport costs for district residents," a level of care "already available to most residents outside of Fire District 2 in eastern Clallam County" [S2].
- Patterson: "Right now, it's not a good time to ask people to raise their taxes, but it usually never is a good time" [S7].

## Arguments against

- No one filed a statement against the measure [S2][S3].
- No organized opposition was found in news coverage [S5][S6][S7][S8].
- The August result (45.8% no) and the 2024 result (52.9% no) show substantial opposition at the polls [S6][S9].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote creates the district's first EMS levy, a new property tax of up to $0.50 per $1,000 for ten years, about $1.2 million a year. It sits on top of a regular levy already at the $1.50 maximum [S1][S7][S11]. A new tax: +2.
- `spending`: the stated purpose includes hiring more staff and ending resident transport bills [S2][S7], which leans toward the invest-and-expand pole. The primary-election scoring mapped spending +1. This pass maps only `taxes`, consistent with other fire-district EMS levies in this election. The dossier records the staffing evidence.
