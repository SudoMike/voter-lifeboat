## Background

Brian King has been Clallam County Sheriff since 2023 [S1]. He is a 31-year law enforcement veteran. His assignments have included patrol, K-9, school resource officer, training coordinator, detective and sergeant. He was promoted to Chief Criminal Deputy in 2015 and then commanded patrol, investigations and the Olympic Peninsula Narcotics Enforcement Team (OPNET) [S1]. Before his election he had worked for the Sheriff's Office for 25 years [S3]. He was born and raised in Clallam County, graduated from Forks High School, and attended Peninsula College and Washington State University. He completed the state Basic Law Enforcement Academy in 1997 and the FBI National Academy in 2017 [S1]. His community service includes Port Angeles Noon Club Rotary, the Serenity House of Clallam County board and the Forks Elks Lodge [S1].

## Positions

### Public safety and policing
- Says "decisions made at the state level have created significant challenges for public safety." He calls for strengthening behavioral health systems, supporting drug enforcement, and "enough well-trained officers ... Not fewer" [S1][S5].
- Pledges "professional, service-oriented law enforcement," partnerships with other agencies, "holding offenders accountable," and proactive community policing [S1][S5].
- Priorities for a second term include stabilizing staff and collective bargaining agreements, retaining deputies, and maintaining accreditation standards; the office was reaccredited in 2025 [S3].
- Writes that "accountability and recovery are not competing goals, they are complementary." He supports jail-based case management, peer recovery specialists, re-entry planning, and drug and mental health courts [S6].

### State legislation affecting sheriffs
- Opposed SB 5974, which would let the Criminal Justice Training Commission decertify an elected sheriff. He supported HB 2387, backed by the Washington State Sheriffs' Association, which would tie decertification of an elected sheriff to recall procedures [S4].

## Record

- Lists first-term accomplishments: expanded jail clinical and re-entry services, a digital forensics lab, body-worn cameras, a crisis response team and armored vehicle, drones, and online crime reporting. He also says the office kept OPNET running despite the loss of state funding and "largely overcame an unprecedented staffing crisis" [S5].
- Has promoted the Joint Public Safety Facility for county emergency management and PenCom 911 dispatch, which broke ground September 15, 2026 [S7].
- After a September 2026 jury verdict of about $30.2 million against the county in a former deputy's lawsuit over a 2020 training injury, King said the county had been advised by counsel not to comment while it explores its options [S8].
