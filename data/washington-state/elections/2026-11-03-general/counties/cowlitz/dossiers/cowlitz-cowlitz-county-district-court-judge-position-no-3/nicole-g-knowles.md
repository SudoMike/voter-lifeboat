## Background

Nicole G. Knowles is Lead Prosecutor for the City of Longview, supervising attorneys who prosecute misdemeanors in District Court, and serves as a judge pro tempore of Cowlitz County District Court [S1][S3]. Her campaign says her whole nine-year legal career has been in Cowlitz County [S2][S3]. She was the county's Senior Deputy Prosecuting Attorney for child sexual abuse cases for five years and says she has tried more than 50 cases in District and Superior Court [S1][S3]. She grew up in Ridgefield, earned a B.A. in political science from Seattle University (2012) and a J.D. from Gonzaga University School of Law (2015) [S3]. She has been secretary of the Children's Justice & Advocacy Center board since 2023 [S3]. The race is nonpartisan [S1].

## Positions

### Judicial approach
- "A courtroom should be the one place where every person is heard with patience and judged with impartiality... justice will be delivered without fear or favor." [S2]
- Pamphlet: committed to "serving and protecting local families with compassion, accountability, professionalism, and dedication to justice" [S1].

### Therapeutic court (diversion)
- Says she helped create and continues to grow the Cowlitz Municipal Therapeutic Court, "developing solutions that address addiction and mental health challenges while improving public safety and reducing recidivism" [S1]. Her campaign says she helped establish it in March 2024, months after becoming lead prosecutor, as "a program built on accountability, treatment, and second chances" [S2][S3].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- Judicial candidate. Positions on I-645, I-1 and I-638 are not researched or recorded for judges.

## Record

- Senior Deputy Prosecuting Attorney (child sexual abuse cases), Cowlitz County, five years; Lead Prosecutor, City of Longview; pro tem judge, Cowlitz County District Court [S1][S3].

## Ratings

- A 2026 judicial evaluation poll (63 respondents for this race) rated her legal ability: 29 "exceptionally well qualified," 17 "well qualified," 7 "qualified," 2 "not qualified" and 8 "insufficient information." On choice for appointment, 83.3% (50 of 60) chose Knowles and 16.7% (10) chose Christopher Eastwood [S5]. The poll was published on Superior Court Judge Jill Karmy's campaign site; Karmy endorses Knowles [S4]. The document does not name the polling organization.

## Endorsements

Presiding Superior Court Judge Patricia Fassett; Superior Court Judges Thad Scudder and Jill Karmy; Court of Appeals Judge Anne Cruser; retired Judges Stephen Warning, Marilyn Haan and Ed Putka (District Court); Superior Court Commissioner Jamie Foster; retired Sheriff Mark Nelson; and others [S4].

## Campaign finance

PDC summary (updated 2026-10-05): $17,600.36 raised, $10,708.97 spent [S6].

- Experience: nine years of practice, a pro tem judge, and broad endorsements from the local bench, in an open-seat race [S3][S4].
