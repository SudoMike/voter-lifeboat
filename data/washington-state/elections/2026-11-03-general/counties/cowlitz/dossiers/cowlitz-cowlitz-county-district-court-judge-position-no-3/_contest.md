## What the office does

Cowlitz County District Court is a county-wide court of limited jurisdiction. Eastwood describes it as handling mostly criminal cases plus some civil matters, and as "where most people have their closest contact with the justice system" (christopher-eastwood.md S2) [S1]. It has three elected judges, each serving a four-year term, and the whole county votes. In 2022 the three positions drew about 30,400 votes each county-wide [S2].

## Race dynamics

Position 3 is an open seat. John Hays won it unopposed in 2022 [S2] and is not on the 2026 ballot [S1]. Both candidates are Longview prosecutors who sit as District Court pro tem judges [S1]. Christopher Eastwood stresses nearly two decades of varied practice: prosecution, public defense and civil litigation, in nine counties (christopher-eastwood.md S1, S2). Nicole Knowles stresses nine years of Cowlitz prosecution, including child sexual abuse cases. She founded the city's therapeutic court and is endorsed by most of the local Superior Court bench (nicole-g-knowles.md S1, S3, S4). In a 2026 judicial evaluation poll published on Judge Karmy's campaign site, 83.3% of respondents chose Knowles for appointment and 16.7% chose Eastwood (nicole-g-knowles.md S5). Knowles has raised more (nicole-g-knowles.md S6; christopher-eastwood.md S5).

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Professional formation (safety, narrowly):** Eastwood mixes defense and prosecution. Knowles is a prosecutor who built a treatment-based diversion court.
2. **Experience:** a longer, broader career (Eastwood) against deeper local roots and institutional endorsements (Knowles). Neither is an incumbent.
3. Neither states an interpretive judicial philosophy beyond fairness and due process, so the `judicial` axis does not separate them.
