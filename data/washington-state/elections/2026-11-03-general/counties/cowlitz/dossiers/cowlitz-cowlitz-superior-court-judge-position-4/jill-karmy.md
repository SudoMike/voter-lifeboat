## Background

Jill Karmy is the sitting Cowlitz County Superior Court judge in Department 4 and is running for the two-year unexpired term [S1][S2]. Governor Bob Ferguson appointed her in September 2025 to replace retiring Judge Marilyn Haan [S4][S7]. She had served more than three years as a Cowlitz County Superior Court commissioner, beginning in 2022 [S1][S4]. Before the bench she ran a solo civil practice representing both plaintiffs and defendants, and she was the criminal prosecutor for Battle Ground, Ridgefield and La Center in Battle Ground Municipal Court [S1][S4]. She reports more than 22 years of legal experience, including trial and appellate work up to the Washington Supreme Court [S1][S2]. She graduated from Lewis & Clark Law School in 2003, with a certificate in federal taxation law, and earned a B.A. in communications from Pacific Union College [S1]. She served on the Washington State Bar Association Board of Governors from 2014 to 2017 and as WSBA treasurer from 2016 to 2017 [S1]. The office is nonpartisan [S1].

## Positions

### Judicial approach
- Pamphlet: "applying the law fairly, impartially, and with integrity," with "decisions grounded in the law, not politics or ideology" [S1].
- Pamphlet: "Judicial experience matters. Proven judicial temperament, balanced decision-making, and experience managing complex cases are essential." [S1]
- Website: she "believes the law must be applied fairly, consistently, and impartially to every individual who appears before the court" [S3].

### Courts, diversion and access to justice
- Says that as a prosecutor she co-created the first therapeutic court in Battle Ground [S2].
- Serves on a statewide Justice Gap workgroup on access to justice [S2].
- Created the "Justice in Action" mock trial program for local teenagers and the court's first high-school mock trial summer camp, held in summer 2026 [S1][S2].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- Judicial candidate. Positions on I-645, I-1 and I-638 are not researched or recorded for judges.

## Record

- Superior Court commissioner from 2022; appointed judge in 2025; now Presiding Juvenile Judge, hearing civil, domestic and criminal matters as one of five judges [S1][S2][S4].
- On her appointment, Judge Haan said Karmy "has consistently embodied these qualities as a Court Commissioner," referring to hearing courteously, answering wisely, considering soberly and deciding impartially [S4].

## Ratings

- A 2026 judicial evaluation poll of 65 respondents, published on her campaign site, asked who should be chosen for appointment. 87.7% (57) chose Karmy and 12.3% (8) chose Jacob Lervold [S5]. The document does not name the polling organization.

## Endorsements

Her campaign says all current sitting Cowlitz County Superior Court judges endorse her [S1][S3]. Its list includes Presiding Judge Patricia Fassett, Judges Thad Scudder, Gary Bashor and Michael Evans, retired Judges Marilyn Haan and Stephen Warning, District Court Presiding Judge M. Jamie Imboden and Judge John Hays, Court of Appeals Judges Anne Cruser and Ian Birk, and judges from Clark, Whatcom and Grant counties [S2][S3].

## Scoring notes

- Experience: sitting appointed judge with prior commissioner service and endorsements from the whole local bench [S1][S2][S4].
- Professional formation is mixed: civil litigator, municipal prosecutor, and co-creator of a therapeutic (diversion) court [S1][S2][S4].
- She describes her approach in general fairness terms only and states no interpretive philosophy [S1][S3].
