## Background

Jacob Lervold is a Senior Deputy Prosecuting Attorney in the Cowlitz County Prosecutor's Office and has spent his whole legal career as a criminal deputy prosecutor [S1]. He was earlier the serious traffic crimes prosecutor in the Thurston County Prosecutor's Office, and before law he owned a business and was a regional manager for a property management company [S1]. He holds a B.A. in political science and philosophy from the University of Washington and a J.D. cum laude from Arizona State University law school, where he was selected to the Order of the Barristers [S1][S2]. He has not held judicial office [S1][S2]. The race is nonpartisan [S1].

## Positions

### Judicial approach
- "The role of a judge is to follow the law, not make it." His stated philosophy: "follow the law, respect its limits, and decide each case on its individual facts. A judge should respect the constitution and apply the law as written, without allowing politics or personal preferences to determine the outcome." [S2]
- Pamphlet: "There is no place in our courts for politics, personal opinion, or favoritism. Our courts need to be fair while also holding people accountable." [S1]
- Says "the rule of law protects both public safety and individual rights" [S1].

### Public safety (professional formation)
- Assigned prosecutor for the Longview Street Crimes Unit (illegal drug use and distribution) and for the Cowlitz County Sheriff's Office Crime Reduction Team; member of the county's Multi-Disciplinary Team on crimes against children [S1][S2].
- Says he "has spent his entire career fighting for victim rights and trying to make the community safer" [S1].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- Judicial candidate. Positions on I-645, I-1 and I-638 are not researched or recorded for judges.

## Record

- Attorney of record on more than 450 felony cases in Cowlitz County Superior Court and more than 50 trials, he says, with a caseload of homicides, child sexual assault, drug distribution, felony assault, domestic violence and gun violence [S1][S2].
- Says he has filed about 20 substantive briefs in the Court of Appeals and Supreme Court in the past three years, and represents his office on the Washington Association of Prosecuting Attorneys' appellate committee [S1][S2].
- Prosecuted the 2025 second-degree murder trial over the death of Longview restaurant owner Grant Hadler [S3].

## Ratings

- A 2026 judicial evaluation poll of 65 respondents, published on his opponent's site, rated his legal ability: 4 "exceptionally well qualified," 6 "well qualified," 19 "qualified," 23 "not qualified" and 13 "insufficient information." On choice for appointment, 12.3% (8) chose him and 87.7% (57) chose Jill Karmy [S4]. The document does not name the polling organization.

## Endorsements

His site lists Lewis County Prosecuting Attorney Jonathan Meyer, Thurston County Prosecuting Attorney Jon Tunheim, Wahkiakum County Prosecuting Attorney Dan Bigelow, State Rep. Ed Orcutt, the International Association of Machinists and Aerospace Workers District 751, Kalama Police Guild President Mike Castellanoz, and other officers, prosecutors and residents [S2]. Meyer's endorsement criticizes "the tendency to appoint people who support certain policies" and urges voters to "say ENOUGH" [S2]. That is the endorser's statement, not the candidate's.
