## What it does

The Longview City Council adopted Resolution No. 2624 to put this levy lid lift on the ballot [S1][S8]. If approved, it would "increase the regular property tax rate by $1.15 per $1,000 of assessed valuation beginning in 2027, and the voter-approved 2027 levy would become the base for future levy-limit calculations under RCW 84.55" [S1][S2][S8]. The explanatory statement says it "does not create a new separate tax but changes the maximum amount the City may collect through its regular property tax levy" [S1][S2]. The ballot title sets no end date [S1][S8].

The explanatory statement lists what the money would pay for: more firefighter paramedics/EMTs, a prevention specialist and an administrative officer; replacing aging emergency response and life-safety equipment; another fire engine with staff; an additional fire station; and operational support for emergency medical, fire suppression and prevention services [S1][S2].
- The statement in favor names four deliverables: Station 83 on Ocean Beach Highway, eight firefighter paramedic/EMTs, a fourth frontline engine, and replacement safety equipment [S1]. KLOG reports the station site as the 2700 block of Ocean Beach Highway [S5].
- At the June 2026 council meeting, Fire Chief Brad Hannig said the levy would raise minimum daily staffing from nine to 12 firefighters [S4].
- The city says a third station would close a five-minute response gap covering Columbia Heights, City View, Northlake, the Olympic neighborhoods and the Highlands [S3].

## Cost

- Up to $1.15 more per $1,000 of assessed value from 2027 [S1][S8].
- The city estimates "about $32 per month for a median home in Longview" [S3]. KLOG reported about $33.50 a month on a $350,000 home [S4].
- The statement against says the city's property-tax rate would go from $1.955 to $3.105 per $1,000, "a 58.8% leap," and calls the increase permanent because it becomes the base for future increases [S1].
- No annual revenue estimate appears in the ballot materials or on the city's page [S1][S3].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- This is a lid lift of the city's regular levy, not an expiring levy renewal. The 2027 amount becomes the base for later levy-limit calculations [S1][S2].
- The city says Washington's 1% annual limit on property tax growth restricts revenue as fire and EMS costs rise [S3].
- Background: a 2012 deployment and staffing study called for two more stations and 18 more firefighters. The city says it has since added only three firefighters and lost a fire inspector position [S3]. Longview still works from two stations built in 1975 and 1979 [S1].
- In June 2025 the fire chief floated forming a separate fire protection district with its own levy and told the council the department was "not sustainable in its current form" [S6]. The 2026 measure is a city levy lid lift instead [S1].
- Qualifying low-income seniors and disabled residents remain eligible for county property-tax exemptions, per the statement in favor [S1].

## Arguments for

The statement in favor (committee: Fire Chief Brad Hannig, Karen Bergquist, Shannon McLain) [S1][S2][S4]:
- "When you call 911, every second matters." Families may have about four minutes to escape a modern house fire, and brain damage can begin four to six minutes into a cardiac arrest.
- Call volume has doubled in 20 years with the same two stations and the same minimum daily staffing.
- "Proposition 1 is a dedicated investment, not a blank check," and Resolution 2624 legally restricts the funds to fire and EMS.

## Arguments against

The statement against (committee: Erik Wrzesinski) [S1][S2]:
- The increase is permanent and raises the rate 58.8%, on top of a school levy approved in February 2026 and higher water rates. It also adds to housing costs for owners and renters.
- "Major capital improvements should have a defined price, purpose and funding period." The city should give firm costs, find efficiencies and consider a limited-duration measure.
- Rebuttal: the 2012 study's comparisons included industrial property outside Longview, and the city could "reduce the money it already spends" on fire/EMS and shift it elsewhere. "Demand the complete financial picture." (VoteWA files this text under RebuttalFor; the printed pamphlet labels it "Rebuttal of Argument For" [S1][S2].)
- The proponents filed no rebuttal [S1].

## Council vote and campaign

- The council placed the measure on the ballot with strong support. Mayor Erik Halvorson cast the lone no vote, saying the cost may be hard for residents right now and citing a survey suggesting voters were unlikely to support it [S4].
- The city recruited the pamphlet's for and against committees by public notice [S9].
- PDC: a committee named SAFE Longview, with Erik Halvorson as treasurer, is registered as a local ballot measure committee whose stance on "Prop 1" is listed as "for." It reports $15 raised and $15 spent as of September 24, 2026 [S7]. This record does not show why the mayor who voted against placing the measure on the ballot is treasurer of a committee registered in support.

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote permanently raises the city's regular property-tax rate by $1.15 per $1,000 (by opponents' figures, from $1.955 to $3.105) [S1]. It is a new increase, not a renewal. +2.
- `spending`: the levy pays for an explicit expansion of service (a third station, a fourth engine, eight more firefighter paramedic/EMTs, daily staffing from nine to 12) [S1][S4]. That is investment-and-expand rather than restraint. +1, consistent with the Spokane Fire District 3 staffing levy.
- `local-control`: no state mandate or regional-authority question is involved. No mapping.
