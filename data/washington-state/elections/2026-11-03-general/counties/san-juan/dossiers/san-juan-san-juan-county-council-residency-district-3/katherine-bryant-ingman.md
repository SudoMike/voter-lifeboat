## Background

- Senior Manager of Membership at the Washington Women's Foundation since 2022 [S1][S7].
- President of the Catherine Washburn Medical Association, which she says has a $4.5 million budget and led the Lopez Clinic's transition from UW Medicine to Sea Mar Community Health Centers [S5][S7][S9].
- Founding staff member and manager of the Lopez Food Share during the pandemic; property manager for Housing Lopez (2021-2022); Lopez Island Prevention Coalition coordinator; program director, Leadership San Juan Islands [S1][S7].
- Community advocate on the Washington State Ferries schedule task force; volunteer track coach at Lopez Island School; president of the Lopez Island Community Tennis Association [S1][S6][S7].
- No elected experience [S1]. M.A. in theology (Fuller Seminary), B.A. (Whitworth University), nonprofit management certificate (University of Washington) [S1].
- Has lived on Lopez Island for ten years [S7]. Second in the Aug. 4, 2026 primary with 2,501 votes (39%) [S10].

## Positions

### County budget and spending
- "Budgets are moral documents"; would focus spending on frontline services, public safety, health and infrastructure "before cutting deeper into programs people rely on daily" [S5].
- Protect emergency response, roads and services for children and vulnerable residents; take care of county infrastructure now ("Deferred maintenance always costs more later"); "Keep investing in affordable housing. The county's Home Fund is a unique tool we need to protect" [S5].
- Would review management and administrative costs before cutting frontline services, set clear criteria per program rather than equal percentage cuts, and pursue grants and partnerships [S4][S5].
- Says she believes in "transparency, balanced budgets" [S1].

### Taxes and revenue
- "To do that without asking islanders to shoulder more, I'd look at revenue tools already available: updated lodging taxes, modernized Airbnb and short-term rental permitting, an additional 1/10 of 1% sales tax for law and justice that frees up general fund dollars, and the exploration of voluntary visitors taxes" [S5].
- On the failed April 2026 county levy lid lift: she was disappointed it did not pass; if it returns to the ballot, "there needs to be broad listening sessions, community awareness and a communications campaign" first [S9].
- Personally voted for the Lopez recreation levy [S9].

### Housing
- Supports more housing for low-income, moderate-income and working residents; would "explore tools including ADU reforms, pre-approved building plans, permit streamlining" and review land-use capacity against projected needs [S4].
- Wants to protect and keep funding the county's Home Fund [S5].

### Environment and climate
- Protect clean water, forests, farmland; "Increase resilience against environmental and climate-related challenges. Help integrate the San Juan County Climate Action Plan across county departments and programs" [S4].
- Encourage on-island connectivity "through greater EV use" [S6].

### Ferries and transportation
- Advocate in Olympia and through regional coalitions for reliable ferry service; prioritize critical users; build a backup plan for disruptions; more ADA-compliant transportation [S6].

## Endorsements

- San Juan County Democrats [S8].
- Former county commissioners Tom Cowan (District 3) and Lovel Pratt (District 1), former councilmember Jamie Stephens (District 3), former Auditor Milene Henley, Port of Lopez commissioner Stephen Adams, and individual islanders [S8].
- Told the Journal she was endorsed by the National Women's Political Caucus [S9].

## Scoring notes

- First-time candidate who stresses generational change: "With age doesn't always come more experience – sometimes we need to shake things up" [S9].
- Names specific new revenue sources (lodging, sales and visitor taxes) while ruling out asking islanders to "shoulder more" [S5].
- Would protect and invest in frontline services and infrastructure, finding savings in administration first [S5].
