## What the office does

San Juan County is a charter county governed by a three-member County Council, with a county manager running daily operations [S4][S9]; voters elected a charter review commission in 2020 [S9]. The council adopts the county budget and sets policy; it received the Auditor's 2027 preliminary budget in August 2026 and held the public hearing on Oct. 5 [S6]. Each member must live in one of three residency districts; District 3 is Lopez, Shaw, Decatur and the nearby outer islands (the precincts that elected the District 3 charter review commissioners in 2020) [S9]. Every county voter votes for every seat, in the primary as well as the general: the District 3 race was counted on all 23 units in the Aug. 4, 2026 primary [S2] and on all 23 precincts in 2022 [S3]. Nonpartisan, four-year term [S1].

## Race dynamics

No incumbent is on the ballot [S1]. Jennifer Swanson of Shaw Island led the primary with 2,944 votes to Katherine Bryant Ingman's 2,501 (Renee J Koplan 887) [S2]. Both candidates live in District 3 (Swanson on Shaw, Bryant Ingman on Lopez) [S5], and the San Juan County Democrats endorsed both in September [S10].

The county budget dominates. Voters rejected a levy lid lift on April 28, 2026, 3,914 to 2,528, and the county faced a $3.7 million gap for 2027 [S4]. Swanson says "this is not the time to expand county services" and promises to "live within our means," looking for "alternative ways the county can increase revenue" for 2028 [S7]. Bryant Ingman would protect frontline services, review management and administrative costs first, and names new revenue tools: updated lodging taxes, short-term rental permitting, an added 0.1% sales tax for law and justice, and voluntary visitor taxes [S8].

The other contrast is experience: Swanson, 11 years an elected school board director and 7 years a superintendent, calls herself "the longest track record and ... most experienced, seasoned candidate"; Bryant Ingman has not held office and stresses her generation's stake and "fresh perspective" [S5].

## Differentiating issue axes

- `experience`: elected and administrative record against a first-time candidate pitching generational change [S5].
- `spending`: restraint ("not the time to expand county services") against protecting and investing in frontline services and infrastructure [S7][S8].
- `taxes`: Bryant Ingman names specific new revenue sources; Swanson defers to the levy vote and promises unspecified alternative revenue for 2028 [S7][S8].
- `housing` and `climate`: both favor affordable housing and climate resilience; the difference is emphasis, not direction.
