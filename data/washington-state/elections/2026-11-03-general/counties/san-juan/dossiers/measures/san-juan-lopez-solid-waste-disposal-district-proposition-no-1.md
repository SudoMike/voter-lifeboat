## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would let the Lopez Solid Waste Disposal District collect $210,000 through a one-year excess property tax levy, estimated at $0.091 per $1,000 of assessed value, for tax year 2027, to fund the district's operations and capital improvements [S1][S2]. The ballot title cites Resolution No. 20-2026; the explanatory statement calls it Resolution No. 2026-02 [S2]. A 60% majority is needed [S2].

- Disposal districts may seek levy funding only one year at a time, so the district puts a levy on the ballot every year [S1][S3].
- The district runs the Lopez Island drop-box facility ("the Dump") at 2419 Fisherman Bay Rd, with free self-sorted recycling and the Take It Or Leave It exchange [S3][S4].
- It was created in 2013, after the county stopped operating solid waste facilities in 2012 and 1,418 Lopez residents petitioned for a district [S4].
- Its levy has been voted on in the three Lopez precincts only (Lopez North, Northwest and South), not Decatur [S6].

## Cost

- **This levy:** $210,000 for 2027 only, about $0.091 per $1,000 [S1][S2]. The district puts it at about $45.50 a year on a property with $500,000 of taxable value, and says the rate is the same as last year's [S3].
- **Current levy:** for taxes due in 2025 the district's excess levy was $210,000, $0.09464 per $1,000 on $2,218,902,086 of assessed value [S5].
- **History:** $105,000 in 2014, about 40% of operating costs; by 2023 the levy covered only 19%; the district raised it to $210,000 from 2024 [S3]. The levy usually covers a third or less of annual expenses; fees, recyclables and volunteer hours cover the rest [S1][S3].

## Background

- The district says disposal charges have risen 30% since 2023, diesel 47% in the last year, and its new interlocal agreement with the county adds leasing fees [S3].
- If the levy fails, the district says it would operate on about 67% of the money needed, with options including reduced services, ending free recycling and higher disposal fees [S3].
- Recent results: approved with 82.8% (2020), 77.5% (2022), 82.3% (2023), 79.6% (2024) and 77.8% (2025) [S6].

## Arguments for

- Clauda Costa, Mehgan Alexandra and Carey Chenoweth: the levy is one of several recurring supports for current services, including free recycling and Take It Or Leave It; "Support your local Dump"; the team keeps the levy "at a minimal level year after year" [S1][S2].

## Arguments against

- None filed. The Auditor publicly sought writers in August 2026 [S7], and the pamphlet says that "after repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement against the ballot measure" [S1].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote renews a one-year excess property tax at the same $210,000 as the current levy (about $0.09 per $1,000) [S1][S3][S5]. A same-amount renewal of a small levy: +1.
- `spending`: framed as sustaining current services; no clear mapping.
