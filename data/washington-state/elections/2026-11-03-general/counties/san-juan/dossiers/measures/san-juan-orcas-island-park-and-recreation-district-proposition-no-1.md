## What it does

Proposition No. 1, under Resolution 2026-07-09, would impose the Orcas Island Park and Recreation District's regular property tax levy at 15 cents per $1,000 of assessed value for six years, subject to the limits of chapter 84.55 RCW, to fund district programs and services [S1][S2]. The explanatory statement says the district must get voter approval of its regular levy every six years, that voters renewed it in 2021, and that this proposition would "restore" the levy to $0.15 per $1,000 for collection in 2027 [S1][S2].

- Spending purposes: facilities and improvements, programs, services and activities [S2].
- The argument for says passage needs 60% approval and turnout of at least 40% of the prior general election [S1].
- The district is Orcas Island; the outer islands of Orcas Island School District (Blakely, Waldron and others) are not in it [S5][S6].

## Cost

- **Current rate:** for taxes due in 2025 the district levied $0.10259 per $1,000 on $4,807,263,936 of assessed value, $493,176 [S3].
- **Proposed rate:** $0.15 per $1,000 for 2027 collection, for six years [S1][S2].
- The argument for: about $120 a year, or $10 a month, on an $800,000 home [S1].
- **Computed for this dossier from [S1] and [S3]:** the same $800,000 home paid about $82 at the 2025 rate. At 2025 assessed values the levy would be about $721,000 instead of $493,000. The proponents call it "not a new tax" because it renews an existing levy; the rate is higher than the one now collected, because the 1% limit has eroded the old rate [S1][S2][S3].

## Background

- The district serves up to 3,000 islanders a year through nearly 300 programs over 15 years and maintains Buck Park's playground, ballfields, tennis and pickleball courts, skatepark and dog park [S1].
- Its first levy, in 2010, got almost 55% but failed for lack of a 60% supermajority [S4].
- The levy funds operations and a modest buffer, not major capital projects [S1].

## Arguments for

- Ed Andrews, Jason Bradshaw and Barb Skotte: renews the levy that funds operations and maintenance; past levies passed with strong support; failure would mean budget cuts, deferred maintenance, fewer hours, program cuts and staffing impacts [S1][S2].

## Arguments against

- Nathan Carter: program costs vary and are better covered by grants, endowments and volunteer giving "according to their values, and within their means"; the levy burdens "citizens regardless of their present situation"; the 1% limit makes the revenue stagnate; the district could instead request a budgeted levy for earmarked recurring costs and rely on local support for the rest [S1][S2].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote renews a six-year property tax at a rate about half again what the district collects now ($0.15 against $0.10259 per $1,000) [S1][S3]. It is a renewal of an existing levy, the case for +1; the rate increase supports +2. +1 or +2 are both defensible; +1 fits "renewal" best.
- `spending`: the proponents frame it as sustaining current operations; the opponent wants programs funded by donations and grants instead. Weak mapping at most.
