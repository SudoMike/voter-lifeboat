## What it does

Proposition No. 1, under Port Commission Resolution No. 2026-2, would lengthen the term of Port of Lopez commissioners from four years to six [S1][S2]. Sitting commissioners keep their current terms; six-year terms apply only to commissioners elected afterward [S1][S2]. With six-year terms one of the three seats would be on the ballot every other election cycle; under four-year terms two seats come up together every fourth year [S2].

- State law sets port commissioner terms at six or four years outside large countywide ports, and provides a ballot proposition for reducing six-year terms to four [S4].
- The Port of Lopez elected Commissioners 1 and 2 in November 2023 and Commissioner 3 in November 2025, each unopposed [S5].
- The pamphlet does not state the approval threshold [S1].

## Cost

No tax or spending. The port argues that six-year terms would lower its election costs, since fewer seats would be on each ballot [S1]. The pamphlet gives no dollar figure.

## Arguments for

- Wil Catlin and Kenn Aufderhar: most of Washington's 75 ports have three commissioners on six-year terms; the Port of Lopez is one of seven with four-year terms; four-year terms put two-thirds of the commission up at once, which "undermines the stability of port management while doubling the election cost on that cycle"; six-year terms preserve institutional knowledge [S1][S2].

## Arguments against

- None filed. The Auditor publicly sought writers in August 2026 [S3], and the pamphlet says that "after repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement against the ballot measure" [S1].
- Not stated in the pamphlet, but the trade-off is direct: longer terms mean voters can replace each commissioner less often [S1][S2].
