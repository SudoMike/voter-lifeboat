## What it does

The Methow Valley Emergency Medical Services District asks its voters for a one-year excess property tax levy of $0.10 per $1,000 of assessed value, collected in 2027, "to prevent the impairment of emergency medical care and services, including ambulance services," under RCW 84.52.052 [S1][S7]. The money may be used only for operations and maintenance of emergency medical care and ambulance services, including personnel, equipment, supplies, vehicles and structures [S1]. Aero Methow Rescue Service, a nonprofit, is the district's contracted provider [S1][S2].

- **What it pays for.** Aero Methow says it would hire three paramedics so a second advanced-life-support ambulance can be staffed 24 hours a day all year; in 2026 it staffed a second ambulance only from April to October, through contracts, at a cost overrun of about $310,000 [S2][S3][S4].
- **Who votes.** The district's rural Methow Valley voters (4,093 registered in November 2025); the towns of Twisp and Winthrop vote on matching propositions of their own [S2][S8].
- **Vote needed.** A 60% supermajority, with the turnout conditions of Article VII, Section 2(a) of the state constitution [S1][S3].

## Cost

$0.10 per $1,000 of assessed value, collected in 2027 only [S1]. Aero Methow's estimate is about $47 a year on a $500,000 house, bringing that house's total Aero Methow levies to about $300 [S2][S3]. Of the three jurisdictions' levies, about $25,000 would come from Twisp and $20,000 from Winthrop, with the rest from the rural district [S2][S4].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This is new money on top of the regular EMS levy, not a renewal [S2]. The regular levy is already at the 50-cent legal maximum and was renewed for ten years in the rural district and Winthrop in 2022 (Twisp renewed its share in February 2026); all three come up again in 2032 [S2][S5]. Operations and maintenance excess levies last one year, so Aero Methow expects to run this request every year "until we can find a more sustainable way to fund growth and meet demand," Executive Director Derek Van Marter said [S2]. The extra levy would appear as its own line on property tax statements [S2].

Aero Methow says the regular levy funds about 60% of its operations, transport reimbursement about 25% and fundraising and grants about 15% [S2][S3]. Calls rose from 531 in 2015 to 911 in 2025, and more than half do not end in a transport, which is not reimbursed [S2][S4]. Because the valley is more than an hour from the hospitals in Brewster and Omak, the state requires Aero Methow to provide advanced life support, which needs paramedics [S2][S3].

## Arguments for

No statement for was submitted [S1]. Van Marter told the Winthrop council that Aero Methow has become "a de facto urgent care center" and that the organization "feel[s] obligated to prepare to respond" to rising demand [S4]. The Okanogan County commissioners voted unanimously to place the measure on the ballot, after the Twisp and Winthrop councils endorsed it [S3]. A Methow Valley News editorial called Aero Methow "established, popular and absolutely vital to this rural community" [S6].

## Arguments against

No statement against was submitted [S1], and no organized opposition or PDC committee was found [S9].
