## What it does

The Board of Commissioners of Public Hospital District No. 1, Okanogan and Douglas Counties, which operates Three Rivers Hospital in Brewster, adopted Resolution No. 2026-10 to ask voters for up to $48,000,000 of general obligation bonds maturing within 30 years, repaid by annual excess property tax levies [S1]. The money would construct, equip, remodel, renovate and furnish improvements to Three Rivers Hospital and pay for other capital improvements the commission deems necessary [S1].

- **What it pays for.** The hospital's description lists a centralized entrance for hospital and clinic services, a dedicated ambulance bay connected to the emergency room, private showers in inpatient rooms, expanded operating rooms, expanded radiology space, an upgraded electronic medical record system, replacement of aging electrical, plumbing, medical gas and HVAC systems, and moving the laboratory [S2]. It would add a two-story addition connecting the hospital to Three Rivers Family Medicine [S2]. Construction is expected to take about two years [S2].
- **Who votes.** The district covers the Methow Valley, Brewster, Bridgeport, Mansfield and Pateros, in Okanogan and Douglas counties [S2][S4]; 8,066 of its registered voters were in Okanogan County in November 2025 [S7].
- **Vote needed.** Bond measures need a 60% supermajority; the 2025 bond failed with a majority below that threshold [S3][S4].

## Cost

Up to $48 million in bonds over up to 30 years [S1]. The explanatory statement estimates the levy at 73 cents per $1,000 of assessed value, about $18 a month for a $300,000 home [S1]; the Methow Valley News gives about $219 a year for a $300,000 home [S2]. Tax exemptions may be available to certain homeowners [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This is a new bond levy, not a renewal [S1]. It is the hospital's third capital request in four years: a $72 million complete rebuild in 2023 failed to win a majority, and a $45 million "hybrid renovation" bond in November 2025 won 52% in Okanogan County and 50.5% in Douglas County but missed the 60% threshold [S2][S3]. The 2025 bond would have collected 78 cents per $1,000 [S3]. The hospital district separately runs a one-year special levy each year for emergency room services; voters renewed it in the August 4, 2026 primary with 65.08% [S5][S8].

Three Rivers is a 25-bed critical-access hospital providing 24-hour emergency care; its facility is 77 years old [S2]. The hospital says it will keep pursuing grants and loans to offset the project's cost [S2].

## Arguments for

Statement for, by Michael Pruett (committee: Michael Pruett) [S1]: "Vote yes to keep quality healthcare close to home"; the modernization updates patient care spaces, technology, privacy and infrastructure to today's standards; strong local health care is part of the region's resilience during wildfires; the estimated cost is $0.73 per $1,000 of assessed value. Pruett chairs the hospital board [S2]. Chief Executive Officer Scott Graham said the hospital has "a responsibility to make thoughtful investments" so it can remain for the people who depend on it [S2].

## Arguments against

Statement against, by Melvin Sorensen (committee: Melvin Sorensen, planokanoganhealth.net) [S1]: voters should not commit $48 million and 30 years of higher property taxes without county-wide data showing this is the best use of the money; a county-wide study might favor a central facility with outlying 24-hour urgent care centers. His rebuttal says Okanogan County's three small hospitals have few overnight patients and compete for patients and staff, that new Medicaid reductions add financial strain, and that "If Three Rivers Hospital fails, residents get decades of increased taxes, but no hospital" [S1]. In 2025 the Okanogan Health Care Planning Group, retired health care professionals based in the Methow Valley, opposed the earlier bond and proposed a single central hospital, most likely in the Omak area [S2][S3]. No PDC committee for or against the measure was found [S6].
