## Background

Gisberth "Giz" Gonzalez is an Okanogan County Sheriff's Office deputy (S1, S4). Born in Guatemala, he served as a reserve soldier in the Guatemalan Army, immigrated at 19 and became a U.S. citizen (S1, S3). He worked as a farm hand and for the City of Wenatchee parks department before being hired as a corrections officer at the Okanogan County Jail in 2007, then as a field deputy months later (S3). He has served on the Special Response Team and as a Field Training Officer, and has worked shifts in the jail during staffing shortages (S1, S2, S3). He reports no prior elected office (S1). He coaches youth soccer and says he has worked to bridge the Hispanic community and local law enforcement (S1, S3).

He sought the interim appointment after Sheriff Budrow's resignation; the county Republican Party interviewed him but did not include him among its three nominees (S5).

## Positions

### Public safety and the jail
- His site calls the jail "the number one issue facing the sheriff's office": staffing has fallen to about 8 to 10 corrections officers from 22 to 24, which limits how many people the 188-bed jail can hold (S2).
- He attributes a crime "surge" partly to Olympia legislation requiring "a more hands-off approach or other alternatives to incarceration" (S2).
- "There needs to be deterrents for criminals... That deterrent can, at times, be incarceration. Incarceration should be used as a deterrent even though there are other alternatives" (S2).
- He supports "proactive policing" (S2) and says he will enhance training and standards so deputies can be "proactive in the community" (S1).
- He proposes revisiting contract inmates (housing other jurisdictions' inmates for revenue), with grants and lobbying, as a long-term jail solution (S1, S2). His site says the program's end cost the county about $2.5 to $3 million a year (S2).

### Leadership and staffing
- Says "the community's trust in the Sheriff's Office has deteriorated, with staffing issues persisting, training not taken seriously" (S1).
- Proposes "servant leadership" and restoring trust through transparency and outreach (S1, S2, S3). At the forum he named deputy retention as the office's biggest problem and attributed staffing problems mainly to leadership (S6).
- Acknowledged at the forum that he has less budgeting experience: "I have the desire and the drive to learn how to do a budget" (S6).

### State law and the constitution
- In answers to the county Republican Party questionnaire on his site, he says a sheriff who believes a law is unconstitutional "should disregard it," while noting the courts' role (S2).
- He describes SB 5974 as requiring background checks and five years' police experience for sheriffs and giving the state authority to remove a sheriff for misconduct (S2).
