## What the office does
The sheriff is the county's chief law enforcement officer: the office runs patrol, investigations, search and rescue, 911 dispatch and the county jail (S2, S3, S4). The sheriff is elected countywide to a four-year term as a partisan office (S1, S2).

## Race dynamics
Sheriff Paul Budrow, elected in 2022, resigned effective April 6, 2026 to return as Twisp's police chief (S2). The commissioners appointed Chief Criminal Deputy Jodie Barcus from the county Republican Party's three nominees (S3). The ballot carries both the short term (to the end of 2026) and the full term (S1). Both candidates prefer the Republican Party (S1). Barcus led the August primary with 65.4% to Gonzalez's 31.7% (S5).

Barcus runs on continuity: staffing gains in patrol, a jail re-entry program linking inmates to mental-health and substance treatment, employee wellness, and budget care; she calls the aging jail the most pressing problem (S4). Gonzalez, a deputy since 2007, runs on leadership, morale and transparency, calls jail staffing the top issue, wants incarceration used as a deterrent, and proposes reviving contract inmates for revenue, which Barcus says is no longer possible (S4, S6). At the forum Barcus disputed his account of multi-agency responses (S4).

## Differentiating issue axes
- `experience`: an appointed sheriff with command experience vs. a line deputy seeking his first supervisory and elected post.
- `safety`: both emphasize enforcement, but Barcus also cites diversion, drug court and treatment-based re-entry, while Gonzalez frames incarceration as a deterrent and blames state laws for "alternatives to incarceration."
- `spending`: Barcus stresses staying within budget and grant caution; Gonzalez concedes less budget experience and proposes revenue from contract inmates.
- `reform`: Gonzalez says trust in the office has "deteriorated" and calls for leadership change; Barcus runs on stability.
