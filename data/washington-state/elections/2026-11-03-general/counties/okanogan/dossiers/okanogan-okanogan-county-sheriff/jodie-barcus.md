## Background

Jodie Barcus is the appointed Okanogan County Sheriff, running for the short and full terms (S1). She owned a real estate business from 2006 to 2009, graduated from the state Basic Law Enforcement Academy in 2010, and was a Colville Tribal Police officer from 2009 to 2017, including as a narcotics K9 handler and a narcotics detective with the North Central Washington Narcotics Task Force (2013-2017) (S1). She joined the Okanogan County Sheriff's Office as a deputy in 2017, became a sergeant in 2022 and was chief criminal deputy from 2023, overseeing patrol, major criminal investigations and search and rescue (S1, S2). She reports no prior elected office (S1).

She announced her candidacy on Feb. 20, 2026, before Sheriff Paul Budrow resigned to return as Twisp's police chief (S2, S3). The Okanogan County Republican Party nominated three people for the interim appointment, ranking Undersheriff Dave Yarnell first and Barcus second (S4). The county commissioners appointed Barcus on March 31, and she took office April 6 to serve the rest of Budrow's term (S5, S6). She is the first woman to serve as sheriff in the county (S6). She promoted Mike Worden, a 31-year veteran of the office, to undersheriff (S6).

## Positions

### Public safety and the jail
- In her appointment interview she "highlighted education and diversion programs" on substance use, said "people have come out of drug court as successful human beings," and said outreach programs giving people with substance use disorder and mental health problems access to treatment rather than jail would reduce crime (S5).
- At the July candidate forum she cited the jail's re-entry initiative connecting inmates with mental health and substance abuse treatment before release: "The jail is not a psych ward" (S9).
- She lists continued commitment to "keeping drugs off the streets," investigation of crimes against children, and raising jail staffing as priorities (S7).
- She calls the aging jail the office's most pressing concern: "If we lose this jail, we will never have one again" (S9). She says "the days of contract inmates ... are all gone" because newer regional jails have been built (S9).
- She wants at least two full-time detectives (S7).

### Staffing, training and wellness
- Her campaign announcement centers on deputy retention, training and recruitment of experienced staff (S2). She says patrol staffing reached levels "we haven't seen since Frank Rogers" (S7).
- She named employee wellness her top improvement and cited a $20,000 fitness-equipment grant and expanded mental-health access; the office trains its own instructors to meet state training mandates (S9).

### Budget
- "We have to be very careful ... and be very budget conscious and stay within our budget" (S7). She cites buying quality used patrol vehicles and cautions against treating grants as "free money" (S9).

### State law and federal cooperation
- She called SB 5974, the 2026 law on sheriff qualifications and removal, "so unconstitutional" and "overstepping horribly," and "the first step in trying to have a state ran law enforcement entity" (S7).
- She told the forum she opposed a state firearms-related bill on constitutional grounds and supported joining litigation against it (S9).
- She said the office will continue to collaborate with federal partners "in accordance with applicable state and federal law" (S8).

### Communication
- She promised more public communication, including news releases on investigations and education on children's phone parental controls (S6).
- After an inmate died in the jail on July 24, 2026, she requested an independent Washington State Patrol investigation, as state law requires (S10).
