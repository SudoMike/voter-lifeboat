## What the office does
Okanogan County District Court hears misdemeanor and gross-misdemeanor criminal cases, traffic infractions, civil cases of limited value, small claims, protection orders and felony preliminary matters (S1, S2). The county's two District Court judges are elected county-wide to four-year terms; in 2022 both seats were on every precinct in the county (S4).

## Race dynamics
Byron Moore was appointed by the county commissioners in October 2025 to replace Judge Chancey Crowell, who resigned; by law he must win this election to keep the seat, and the ballot carries the short and full term (S1, S2). Robert W. Grim, the current Winthrop mayor, held this District Court seat before going into private practice and later served three years on the Superior Court, resigning in 2025 to run for mayor (S1, S3). Nonpartisan; with two candidates there was no primary (S3).

Moore stresses judicial restraint and a "constitutional originalist" reading of the law plus experience as prosecutor, defense lawyer and Ferry County pro tem. Grim stresses about a decade on the bench and some 70 jury trials. In 2025 Grim wrote a recommendation for Moore's appointment (S2).

## Differentiating issue axes
- `experience`: Grim has the longer judicial record (District and Superior Court, about ten years); Moore has under a year on the bench plus pro tem service. Both are former or current judges, so the contrast is degree, not insider vs outsider.
- `judicial`: Moore describes an explicit "as written" / originalist approach; Grim's statement speaks of constitutional rights and even-handed administration without a stated interpretive philosophy.
- `safety`: both mention public safety in general terms; no documented bail, diversion or sentencing record distinguishes them beyond Moore's remark about community service as an alternative to fines.
