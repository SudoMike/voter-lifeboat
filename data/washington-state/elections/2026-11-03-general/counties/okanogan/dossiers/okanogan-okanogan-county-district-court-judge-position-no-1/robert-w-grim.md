## Background

Robert W. Grim is the current mayor of the Town of Winthrop and a former Okanogan County judge seeking to return to the District Court [S1][S3]. His statement lists service as an Okanogan County District Court judge (2015-2022) and Superior Court judge (2022-2025) [S1]. Local reporting says he served four years on the District Court before resigning in 2021 to go into private practice, and that Gov. Jay Inslee appointed him to the Superior Court in 2022 when Judge Chris Culp retired [S2][S5]. He was elected to that Superior Court seat later in 2022 and again in 2024, unopposed both times [S2][S6].

He resigned from the Superior Court effective July 1, 2025, to run, unopposed, for mayor of Winthrop, a position he holds now [S2][S3]. The Methow Valley News notes he would need to resign as mayor if elected judge [S4]. Before the bench he was an associate attorney at Kottkamp & Yedinak, PLLC, in Wenatchee (2012-2015) [S1].

He holds a J.D. from Gonzaga University School of Law (2012) and a B.A. in criminal justice from Washington State University (2007), and graduated from Liberty Bell High School (2003) [S1]. He was president of the board of Room One in Twisp from 2017 to 2022 [S1].

## Positions

### Judicial approach
- Calls District Court "the legal forum that members of the public are most likely to encounter" and says those before it must "understand its processes and have their constitutional rights protected," with the law "administered in an ethical, efficient, and even-handed manner" [S1].
- Says he would run "a court that prioritizes public safety, public service, and promoting trust and confidence in our local government" [S1].

### Public safety
- Lists public safety first among his court priorities [S1]. No more specific statement on bail, sentencing or diversion was found.

## Record

- Says that in a decade as an elected judge he presided over approximately 70 jury trials, on charges ranging from DUI to first-degree murder, plus bench trials and other civil and criminal hearings [S1].
- Re-elected to Superior Court Position 2 in 2024 with 12,899 votes, unopposed [S6].
- In 2025 he wrote a letter of recommendation for Byron Moore, now his opponent, when Moore applied for the District Court appointment [S5].
- He did not attend the July 15, 2026 Oroville candidate forum [S7].
- No bar ratings or disciplinary records were found in the sources reviewed.

## Endorsements

- His statement says "law enforcement, victim advocates, jurors, litigants, and attorneys of every stripe have commended my judgment, skill, and temperament," without naming any [S1]. No campaign website or endorsement list was found.
