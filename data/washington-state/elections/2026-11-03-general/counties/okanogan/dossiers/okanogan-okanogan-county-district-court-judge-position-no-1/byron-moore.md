## Background

Byron Moore is the sitting Okanogan County District Court judge in Position 1, appointed by the county commissioners in October 2025 from four applicants to replace Judge Chancey Crowell, who resigned; the appointment began Nov. 1, 2025 and runs to this election [S3][S2]. He is running for the short and full term [S1][S7].

Before the appointment he was a judge pro tem in Ferry County District Court from 2023, presiding over misdemeanors, traffic matters, felony preliminary hearings and protection orders [S3][S4]. He was an Okanogan County deputy prosecuting attorney in 2022-23, handling a caseload of up to 700 cases and filing cases from a misdemeanor-review backlog that risked the statute of limitations [S3]. He told the governor's office in 2025 that he left the prosecutor's office because of "differing philosophies" [S4]. Through Moore Law, PLLC, in Wenatchee he has practiced criminal defense, real estate, family, business, estate and water-rights law [S1][S3]. Earlier he served in a quasi-judicial role for the New Mexico Board of Nursing [S4].

He holds a J.D. from Cleveland State University's Cleveland-Marshall College of Law and a Ph.D. in human services from Walden University, with a focus on family studies and intervention strategies [S1][S3]. He applied to the governor for Okanogan County Superior Court vacancies in 2023 and 2025 [S3][S4].

## Positions

### Judicial approach
- Describes his judicial philosophy as "one of restraint," applying statutes and legal principles as written "rather than legislating from the bench" [S3].
- Calls himself a "constitutional originalist" who applies "the law as written — without activism or personal agenda" [S2].
- Says access to the courts "must be made more readily available for every citizen" [S2].
- Says his human-services doctorate informs his handling of domestic-violence matters, "reinforcing my commitment to a just and balanced outcome for all parties" [S3].

### Public safety
- Lists public safety among his pillars: "protecting public safety while upholding individual rights" [S2].
- At a July 2026 forum he said recent Washington Supreme Court decisions limit judges' ability to impose financial penalties, making alternatives such as community service increasingly important: "Being fair and using common sense" [S5].

## Record

- Appointed District Court judge by the Okanogan County commissioners in October 2025 [S3].
- References submitted with his application: Rep. Hunter Abell (R-7th), who as a Ferry County District Court judge had chosen Moore as his pro tem; former judge Robert Grim (now his opponent); then-Sheriff Paul Budrow; and Superior Court Judge Kathryn Burke [S3].
