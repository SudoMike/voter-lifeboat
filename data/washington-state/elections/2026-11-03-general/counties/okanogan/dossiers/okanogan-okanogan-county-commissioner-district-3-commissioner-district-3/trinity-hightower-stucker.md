## Background

Trinity Hightower Stucker of Tonasket is a farm owner-operator and homeschooling parent who prefers the Republican Party (S1, S4). She is a Washington State Farm Bureau board director, a past president of the Okanogan County Farm Bureau, a 7th Legislative District committee board member, an Okanogan County Republican precinct committee officer, and a WRIA 49 (watershed) board member (S1, S4). She was a Washington State Senate session aide, working for then-Sen. Bob Morton (S1, S4, S5). She holds a B.A. in government/pre-law with a minor in economics from Eastern Washington University and an A.A. from Wenatchee Valley College (S1). She has not held county office (S1).

## Positions

### Taxes and the county budget
- Her statement lists "proactive budgeting and spending, lower taxes, respect for taxpayers" (S1).
- Opposed the county's 2026 levy lid lift as "a Band-Aid": "My plan is for that levy to fail" (S5, S6).
- Says a volunteer budget-review group she joined (Farm Bureau and Republican Party members, at the commissioners' request) found more than $3 million in potential cuts that the commissioners did not adopt; it proposed a 10% pay cut for commissioners and department heads and turning two of three fairgrounds positions into floating jobs, and did not touch the sheriff, jail or prosecutor (S5, S6).
- Would take "a 10% decrease in my pay" to lead by example (S5).
- Favors efficiency audits over relying on grants or new revenue (S6). Platform: tighten the budget "to more sustainable levels," review department fees (S3).
- On union wage requests: "That is ... union negotiations ... If we are [paying beyond agreements], why?" (S6).
- Opposes shutting down the county fair (S4, S5).

### State government and regulation
- Says businesses are "going under due to overregulation, taxes, different changes in the workforce laws" and that unfunded state mandates must be documented and pushed back on in Olympia (S5).
- Says the jail's costs are driven partly by state mandates and that she would wait for the sheriff's proposals on jail improvements (S5).

### Growth, land and water
- Platform: streamline permitting timelines, "protect rural character while expanding opportunity," support agriculture, "balance growth with infrastructure capacity" (S3).
- Supports private property rights; on legal cannabis production, "The voters have spoken... I would support that" (S6).
- Worked with her campaign strategist on a committee on mitigation for permit-exempt wells (S4).

### Wildfire and emergency preparedness
- Platform pillar: year-round fuel reduction, evacuation route planning, support for volunteer fire districts, better coordination with state and federal agencies (S2, S3).

### Transparency
- Calls for "transparent government" and says residents feel they are "paying more and getting less" (S6). Platform: transparency around infrastructure spending (S3).
