## Background

Jon Neal of Oroville is the incumbent Okanogan County Commissioner for District 3 (S1). He was born in Tonasket in 1963, is a third-generation county resident and owned Neal's Autobody & Glass for 34 years (S1). He was previously mayor of Oroville and an Oroville City Council member, and a volunteer firefighter for 19 years (S1). On the commission he lists roles as the county's state legislative steering committee representative, Emergency Management Board chair, Public Health Board vice chair, and Council of Governments chair, among others (S1). He won the open seat in 2022 with 6,954 votes to Lloyd E. Caton Jr.'s 6,370 (S9).

## Positions

### Taxes and the county budget
- Voted with the other two commissioners, "unanimously — but reluctantly," to put a levy lid lift (from 88 cents to up to $1.15 per $1,000) on the August ballot to close a projected $2-million-plus deficit: "I'm not in a happy place, but I'm good with where we're at" (S2, S3).
- At the July forum he said "I honestly am not in support of the levy lid lift either," but that the county's finances left few alternatives (S5).
- After voters rejected it about 70-30: "Well, time for some cuts, I guess. The people have spoken" (S4).
- Notes about 70% of the county is nontaxable public land and federal payments are uncertain (S2).
- On union wage requests: "If you go too far with increases, you start cutting manpower" (S5).
- At the Lincoln Day Dinner he named public safety and "using your tax dollars wisely and as fiscally responsibly as we can" as commission priorities (S6).

### State government
- His statement pledges to "continue to fight Olympia against state government overreach regarding excessive taxation and over regulation" (S1).
- Estimates state unfunded mandates cost the county about $3.5 million a year (S3, S5).
- Lobbied in Olympia against the sheriff bill and the "millionaire's tax" (S6).

### Public safety and services
- Calls public safety (EMS, law enforcement, firefighting) a top priority and pledges to protect First and Second Amendment rights (S1).
- Says the county has expanded partnerships with Okanogan Behavioral Healthcare and crisis response teams: "I wish there was more money" (S5).
- Working to bring a Veterans Affairs clinic to the county and to get federal aid for struggling orchardists and farmers (S5).

### Growth and land use
- "Change needs to be done in a controlled fashion so we do not lose our rural way of life" (S5).
- Says property owners should generally be able to use their land so long as neighbors are not adversely affected; on cannabis farms, says conflicts stem from operators not following regulations (S5).

## Record

- Elected in November 2022 (S9); in office as District 3 commissioner (S1).
- 2026: voted for the levy lid lift resolution with the full commission (S2, S3).
- Criticized Rep. Dan Newhouse's absence from a meeting on Secure Rural Schools funding as "very disappointing" (S8).
- August 4, 2026 primary (District 3 voters only): 1,793 votes (47.7%) to Stucker's 1,919 (51.0%) (S10).

## Endorsements
