## What the office does
Okanogan County PUD is a consumer-owned electric and wholesale broadband utility governed by a three-member elected Board of Commissioners, to which the general manager reports (S2). It serves 2,808 of the county's 5,315 square miles and nearly 17,000 customers; two electric cooperatives serve the rest of the county over PUD infrastructure (S2). The board sets rates and the budget. Commissioners serve six-year terms, are nominated by district and elected by the whole PUD in the general (S1, S4).

The PUD's electorate is not the whole county: 26,809 registered voters are in "OKANOGAN PUD COUNTY" and 325 northeastern voters are instead in Ferry County PUD No. 1, whose Commissioner #3 race is on their ballots (S3). In 2024 the Okanogan PUD race was on 247 of 248 precincts (S4).

## Race dynamics
Incumbent Scott Vejraska, an 11-year commissioner, board president, lineman and rancher, faces Steven Gadd, an assistant state auditor and former Okanogan City Council member (S1, S7). Nonpartisan; no primary.

Vejraska runs on rate restraint ("a firm NO VOTE" on unnecessary increases) and reserves rebuilt after wildfire damage (S1). Gadd runs on change after 12 years and closer oversight, without a stated rate position (S1). Recent board issues include an April 2025 average 3% rate increase cushioned with Climate Commitment Act funds (S5) and the possible removal of the PUD's dormant Enloe Dam on the Similkameen River, for which the PUD requires full indemnity (S6). Neither candidate's statement addresses Enloe Dam.
