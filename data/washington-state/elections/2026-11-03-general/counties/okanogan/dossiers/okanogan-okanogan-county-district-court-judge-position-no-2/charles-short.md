## Background

Charles Short has been an Okanogan County District Court judge since 2013 and is the court's presiding judge, supervising its judicial business [S1][S2]. He says voters retained him in 2014, 2018 and 2022 [S1]; in 2022 he was unopposed, with 9,385 votes [S4]. Before the bench he was a litigator in criminal defense, prosecution, child support enforcement, family law, personal injury and general civil litigation; his first job after law school was as judicial law clerk to the Chelan County Superior Court judges [S1][S2].

He is dean of the Washington State Judicial College, which trains all new judges in the state; past president of the District and Municipal Court Judges' Association; and a past member of the Board for Judicial Administration [S1][S2]. He is faculty for the National Council of Juvenile & Family Court Judges, the Center for Justice Innovation and the National Judicial Institute on Domestic Violence, and a judicial coach certified by the National Center for State Courts [S1][S2]. In 2024 the District and Municipal Court Judges' Association gave him the David A. Steiner Judicial Leadership Award [S1][S2].

He holds a J.D. with honors from the University of Montana School of Law and a B.A. in philosophy, magna cum laude, from Central Washington University's William O. Douglas Honors College; he graduated from Lake Roosevelt High School [S1][S2]. He was born in Omak and grew up on his family's cattle ranch near Nespelem [S1][S2].
