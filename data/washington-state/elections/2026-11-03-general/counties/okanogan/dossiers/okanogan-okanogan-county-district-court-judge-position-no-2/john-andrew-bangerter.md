## Background

John Andrew Bangerter is a felony deputy prosecutor in the Okanogan County Prosecuting Attorney's Office, where he earlier handled District Court matters; he has also been a felony prosecutor in Chelan and Grays Harbor counties and a financial civil-litigation attorney at a private firm [S1][S2]. From 2017 to 2021 he managed a team of intelligence analysts and security professionals protecting Microsoft assets worldwide, as a security operations manager under a Pinkerton contract [S1][S2]. He has lived in Okanogan County about five years with his wife and three children [S1].

He was born in Tacoma, grew up partly in the Netherlands, and graduated from Curtis High School in University Place [S2]. He holds a J.D. and a Master of International Affairs from Pennsylvania State University and a B.A. from Brigham Young University-Idaho [S1][S2]. He serves as Sunday School president at his church in Omak [S1].

In October 2025 he was one of four applicants the county commissioners interviewed for the District Court appointment that went to Byron Moore [S6].

## Positions

### Judicial approach
- Promises "calm, patient, and respectful leadership" and a courtroom where "every person is treated with the dignity and courtesy they deserve" [S1][S3].
- "Every decision will be based on facts, evidence, and the law" [S1]; "committed to applying the law evenly, and without favoritism, bias, or preconception" [S3].
- Offers "fresh leadership focused on timely justice," moving cases "without rushing or unnecessary delay"; his statement says "It is time for a fresh perspective and a positive change" and asks voters to "restore confidence, efficiency, and justice" in the court [S1][S3].
- At a July 2026 forum he said judicial decisions require careful interpretation of the law while balancing punishment, treatment options and community safety, and: "What judges do in other counties doesn't matter ... As long as I'm following the law...being impartial" [S5].

### Public safety
- "Convicted offenders will be held responsible, victims will be heard and protected, and due process will always be upheld in a fair, firm, and consistent manner" [S1].
- Pledges "a strong commitment to public safety from the bench," holding convicted offenders "accountable under the law in a fair, consistent, and decisive manner" [S3].
- Names substance abuse, domestic violence and impaired driving as harms he wants the community free of; says domestic violence has become one of his greatest concerns in five years as a prosecutor [S1][S2][S5].

## Record

- Okanogan County deputy prosecutor (District Court, then felony cases in Superior Court) [S1][S2].
- Former Chelan County colleagues describe him prosecuting serious felonies including sexual assault and rape at trial [S4].
- No judicial experience; no bar ratings or disciplinary records were found in the sources reviewed.

## Endorsements

- Chelan County Prosecuting Attorney Robert Sealby and Chief Deputy Prosecutor Ryan Valaas; former Chelan County District Court Judge Kyle Mott; criminal defense attorney Herman Bylenga; Okanogan County Department of Corrections supervisor John Lyles; victim advocates in Okanogan and Chelan counties [S4].
