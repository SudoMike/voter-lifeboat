## Background

Gabriel Frase has worked for the Lewis County Sheriff's Office since 2002. He served as patrol deputy, field training officer, search and rescue coordinator, K9 handler, detective, patrol, detective and administrative sergeant, and Special Services Chief, and is one of the commanders of the Regional Independent Investigation Team (S1). The Chronicle reports he was promoted to special services chief in 2024. In that role he manages the civil side of the office and oversees its detective sergeant and its community service officers (prisoner transport, animal control). He is one of the office's three chiefs (S5). He served in the U.S. Marine Corps from 1998 to 2002 and holds an associate degree in criminal justice from Centralia College (S1). He prefers the Republican Party (S1). He lists no elected experience (S1).

He filed during candidate filing week in May 2026, after other candidates had been campaigning for months. He told The Chronicle that none of the declared candidates were "the right fit" for the office (S5). Former candidate Kenneth Cheeseman withdrew at filing week and endorsed him (S11). In the certified August 4 primary he finished first with 8,158 votes (36.15%) (S8).

## Positions

### Public safety and policing
- Priorities include "Prioritize Victims Over Career Criminals" and focusing resources on "investigations, enforcement, and proactive efforts" to hold criminals accountable and reduce repeat criminal activity (S3).
- Supports working with local agencies to "re-establish a combined SWAT team and drug unit" (S3). He has said relationships with local agencies need rebuilding after the regional SWAT team broke down and the Sheriff's Office left the Joint Narcotics Enforcement Team (S5).
- Says the office must "uphold the law while protecting the constitutional rights of every citizen" (S3). His statement says he believes "in the supremacy of the US Constitution" (S1).
- At the Adna forum all four candidates opposed the blocked state law (SB 5974) that would have allowed decertified sheriffs to be removed from office, and all said they supported the Second Amendment (S6).

### Budget and management
- Names the budget, transparency and inter-agency relationships as the office's three biggest challenges (S5).
- Says taxpayer dollars "should be managed responsibly and directed toward services, training, equipment, and operations that best promote public safety" (S3). His statement says he will "prioritize the use of the funding entrusted to us" (S1).

### Transparency and accountability
- Says the office has withheld information out of caution and "could have definitely done a better job", and that it needs a better balance between protecting investigations and informing the public, victims and families (S5).
- At the Adna forum he proposed building relationships with trusted community and media members who could receive information as it becomes available (S6). He said he has no Brady letter (S6). At the Republican forum he said a sheriff with a Brady letter would hurt "trust with the community" (S7).

### Continuity
- Described himself as supportive of Sheriff Rob Snaza: "I support the sheriff and what he is doing." He said the office is in many places operating as it should, with room for improvement (S5).
