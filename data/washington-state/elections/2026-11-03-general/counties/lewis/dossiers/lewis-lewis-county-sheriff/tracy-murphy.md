## Background

Tracy Murphy served with the Centralia Police Department from 1990 to 2026, starting as a police cadet. He worked as patrol, motors and bike officer, K-9 handler and trainer, firearms instructor, field training officer, SWAT member and union shop steward. From 2016 he supervised patrol, K-9 and field training, and from 2019 to 2026 he was a detective sergeant and JNET/DEA supervisor (S1). He graduated from the Washington State Basic Law Enforcement Academy in 1995 and attended Centralia College (1993-1995) and Ecola Bible College (S1). He prefers the Republican Party. He ran for sheriff in 2022 and lost to incumbent Rob Snaza in the general election (S1, S6).

Centralia Police Chief Andy Caldwell fired Murphy on April 29, 2026. Caldwell concluded that Murphy had made false statements and omitted material information during an internal investigation (S6, S7). That investigation followed an anonymous January complaint to the state Criminal Justice Training Commission (CJTC). It examined whether Murphy improperly disclosed protected information about unverified allegations involving Sheriff Snaza, or used his position for campaign purposes. A third-party investigator did not sustain those allegations but took issue with Murphy's "categorical denial" (S7, S9). Murphy says he withheld information because of a non-disclosure agreement with the DEA, calls the firing politically motivated and is challenging it through Teamsters Local 252. An arbitration hearing is scheduled for November 19-20, after the election (S7, S8). Prosecuting Attorney Jonathan Meyer issued a potential impeachment disclosure ("Brady letter") about him in May. As of September 18 the CJTC complaint was still at intake review (S7). Murphy has said he believes the Brady letter will be retracted (S9).

In the certified August 4 primary he finished second with 7,735 votes (34.28%) (S10).

## Positions

### Public safety and drugs
- "As your sheriff, I will make combating illegal drugs a priority. We will aggressively target the sources bringing drugs into Lewis County, work to dry up that supply, and hold accountable those profiting from the drugs" (S5). His site promises to "identify, disrupt, and dismantle drug trafficking organizations" (S3).
- Promises a volunteer Cold Case Unit of retired law enforcement professionals and new investigative technology (S5). His statement says "No case will be ignored, and no one will be above the law" (S1).
- At the Adna forum all four candidates opposed the blocked state law (SB 5974) that would have allowed decertified sheriffs to be removed from office, and all said they supported the Second Amendment (S8).

### Jail and recidivism
- Says the jail "must be safe, secure, and well-managed." He will support partnerships that provide "effective mental health and substance abuse programs to reduce recidivism," and supports bringing back faith-based and mentoring programs for inmates (S1).
- Promises to plan for a "safe, secure, properly staffed facility capable of holding offenders accountable while utilizing proven programs that reduce recidivism" (S5).

### Budget and management
- "I will be responsible with your tax dollars. Before asking taxpayers for more, I will examine our administration, staffing, programs, and resources to determine where we can operate more efficiently" (S5).
- Will select his administration "by merit and capability, not campaign promises," people "who share a commitment to fiscal responsibility". He would pursue professional accreditation for the office and restore volunteer programs, which he calls "force multipliers" that keep costs down (S3).

### Transparency and accountability
- Promises press releases, community meetings and social media updates. He says internal investigations should be "fair, documented, and when appropriate, shared in summary form with the public" (S3).
- At the Republican Party forum he disputed the county party chair's claim that allegations against Sheriff Snaza had been fully investigated by the FBI (S9).
- Frames the race as "a choice between the status quo and a new direction" (S5). His statement says "Lewis County doesn't need another administrator; it needs a leader" (S1).
