## What the office does
The coroner has jurisdiction over the bodies of people who die suddenly, violently, under suspicious or unnatural circumstances, or of unknown causes. The office determines cause and manner of death (S2, S1). In Lewis County the coroner has been a full-time position since 2014. Besides the coroner, the office has two full-time and one part-time county employees, eight on-call deputies, eight removal technicians and two reserve deputies (S4).

## Race dynamics
Warren McLeod (Prefers Republican Party) has been coroner since January 2011 and ran unopposed after his first election. He says this will be his last term (S4). Jessica Stickley (Prefers Independent Party) was a Lewis County on-call deputy coroner from 2009 to 2019. She later worked as a coroner investigator in Cowlitz County and now works as a medicolegal death investigator in Columbia County (S1, S5). Stickley cites concerns about controversial death investigations, including the 2022 Aron Christensen case, and about transparency (S5). The Christensen family's April 2026 complaint to the state names both the Sheriff's Office and the Coroner's Office (S7). Records about Stickley's resignations from Lewis and Cowlitz counties have circulated. She disputes them and says she was forced to resign from Lewis County (S5).

In the certified August 4 primary McLeod received 12,708 votes (54.38%), Stickley 6,434 (27.53%) and Chief Deputy Coroner Chelsie Niemcziek 4,188 (17.92%) (S3). McLeod raised and spent nothing before the primary; Stickley raised more than $10,000 (S6).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience/record:** a 16-year incumbent who cites the office's accreditation since 2012 and staff certification, against a challenger running on change, "transparency and accountability for mistakes made", and a first-woman-coroner pitch.
2. **Spending:** McLeod wants to convert casual on-call staff to benefited county employees, which needs more county funding. Stickley lists "lawsuits costing taxpayers" among community concerns and pledges "good stewardship of public resources".
3. **Reform/accountability:** Stickley campaigns on transparency, accountability for mistakes and repairing relationships with first responders. McLeod cites the office's 2011 transparency vision and its accreditation.
4. Most rubric axes (taxes, housing, climate) do not apply to this office. Neither candidate takes positions on them.
5. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives: none found for either candidate.
