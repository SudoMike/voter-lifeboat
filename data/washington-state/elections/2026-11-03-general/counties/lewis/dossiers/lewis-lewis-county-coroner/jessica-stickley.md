## Background

Jessica Stickley has lived in Lewis County since 2005 and lives in Toledo (S1, S2, S4). She was an on-call deputy coroner with the Lewis County Coroner's Office from 2009 to 2019 and a coroner investigator in Cowlitz County from 2023 to 2025. She currently works as a medicolegal death investigator in Columbia County (S1, S4). She is a diplomate of the American Board of Medicolegal Death Investigators, a Washington State certified forensic investigator and a certified psychological autopsy investigator (S1, S2, S3). She holds a Master of Science in Studies in Human Behavior from Capella University (2013) and a BA from Saint Martin's University (2008) (S1). She has worked in behavioral health since 2008 (S1, S2). She is a registered member of the Native Village of Afognak (S4). This is her first run for office, as an independent (S1, S7).

The Chronicle reports that records on her resignations from the Lewis County and Cowlitz County coroner's offices have circulated during the campaign, some released by Stickley herself. She says she was forced to resign from Lewis County after actions she says followed county policy and had McLeod's verbal approval, and that she left Cowlitz County because of a toxic work environment. She disputes documents in her file and has asked through counsel for certain personnel records to be destroyed (S4).

In the certified August 4 primary she finished second with 6,434 votes (27.53%) (S5).

## Positions

### Investigations, transparency and accountability
- Cites concerns over controversial death investigations, including the Aron Christensen case, and over transparency as reasons for running (S4).
- Lists concerns she has heard: "deficits in investigative professionalism and competency," "lawsuits costing taxpayers," communication deficiencies, "lack of transparency and accountability for mistakes made," and limited aftercare for families. She pledges to strengthen investigative standards, repair professional partnerships and increase transparency and accountability (S2).
- Wants investigative practices brought up to national standards "to ensure families are receiving accurate investigations, every case, every time" (S2).
- Says elected officials "should be partners in accountability and willing to ask difficult questions, accept questions themselves, acknowledge mistakes" (S3).

### Prevention and family support
- Says the office should lead community death-prevention efforts, including suicide prevention, overdose awareness, water safety, heat illness, impaired driving and senior fall prevention (S2, S4).
- Proposes meaningful aftercare resources for families after a death (S2, S3).

### Independence and stewardship
- Running as an independent "because I believe the coroner should serve the entire community without political bias" (S3).
- States a belief in "good stewardship of public resources" (S2).
- Says that if elected she would be Lewis County's first female coroner (S2).
