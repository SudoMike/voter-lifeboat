## Background

Mitch Townsend, of Silver Creek, has lived in Lewis County for more than 30 years (S1, S4). He chairs the Mossyrock School District Board of Directors and lists Lewis County Republican Party chairman and precinct committee officer as elected experience. He formerly chaired the Lewis County Republican Party, resigning that post in June 2025 (S1, S3). He reports 35 years as an owner or executive of several businesses, including Pacific Precious Metals, more than 25 years of accounting experience, work as a digital engineer, and teaching at Centralia College and Lower Columbia College (S1, S9). He reports 14 years in Army infantry units, active, reserve and National Guard, and service on the Lewis County Budget Advisory Committee and as archaeology representative on the Gifford Pinchot National Forest resource advisory committee (S1, S8). He lists bachelor's and master's programs at the University of Puget Sound, Pepperdine and Gonzaga (organizational leadership) and a doctoral program at the University of Calgary (S1, S8). He is not a CPA (S3). He prefers the Republican Party (S1).

He said he first considered running for county commissioner but chose the auditor race after talks with party members and Commissioners Brummer and Swope (S3). In the certified August 4 primary he placed second with 7,597 votes (37.07%) (S7).

## Positions

### Elections and election reform
- "Contract with Lewis County": audit the voter rolls "to ensure 100% accuracy using existing methods and AI," matching every vote with a registered voter by name, address, signature and phone number (S1, S2).
- Will "strongly advocate for State Election Reform" from the auditor's office, working with other county auditors on legislation (S1, S2).
- Told The Chronicle he supports voter identification along the lines of the federal SAVE Act and a state initiative backed by Rep. Jim Walsh (proof of citizenship when registering), ending acceptance of ballots that arrive after election night, final results by the end of election day, and easier ways for voters to verify their ballots were counted (S3).
- "There's no reason we can't audit every vote with every voter after every election and match a voter with a vote" (S3).
- Says every ballot that needs to be "cured" is proof the county's voter rolls contain inaccuracies (S2). His letter asks: "Is your vote one of the 822 votes not counted in the primary election?" (S4).
- Promises voter education and registration programs to raise participation (S2).
- Says Grove did a "great job" but it is time to "supercharge" the office (S3).

### County finances and spending
- "Audit and Open the Books": a full financial re-examination of all county departments, and a top-to-bottom review of every practice and process in the auditor's office within 30 days, with results shared for public comment (S1, S2).
- Says citizens increasingly question the county budget process "against the reality of rapidly rising taxes and what many perceive as unclear priorities" (S2).
- Describes the auditor as "the Citizens Watchdog" (S1, S2, S4).

### Qualifications
- Argues the job "does not legally require a financial CPA certification" and is an executive position requiring leadership experience and graduate education in organizational leadership (S1, S3, S4).
