## Background

Scott Brummer has been the District 3 Lewis County commissioner since late 2022. He won the November 2022 general election against Harry Bhagwandin and took office on certification, succeeding interim Commissioner Lee Grose, who had been appointed after the previous District 3 commissioner died of COVID-19 in September 2021 (S3). He has lived in Lewis County about 30 years and lives south of Winlock (S2, S3, S4). He worked as a fish biologist for the Lewis County and Thurston County conservation districts and then for about 21 years at the Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife. He left WDFW after declining COVID-19 vaccination under the state worker mandate (S3). He is senior pastor of God's Place church in Ethel, and he and his family run Patriot Farm and Feed in Winlock (S1, S3). He holds a bachelor of science degree (S1). He prefers the Republican Party (S1).

In the certified August 4 primary, held only in District 3, he placed first with 3,084 votes (33.60%) (S9, S10).

## Positions

### Taxes and county budget
- Describes himself as a supporter of keeping tax increases to a minimum. He says that in four budgets the board took the allowed property-tax revenue increase only once, using banked capacity: "we've kept our spending down and made hard choices" (S3).
- Campaign site: "Keeping county levy rates down despite massive inflation," holding the line on spending while seeking business growth "without raising property taxes" (S2).
- On a proposed public safety sales tax: "not sold on it yet." He wants a new sheriff to rebuild public trust for at least a year before voters are asked, and any tax to go 100% to criminal justice (S3, S4).
- Opposed ending the long-running transfer of about $1.1 million to $1.5 million a year from the county road fund to the sheriff's office if it would cut deputies: "I'm absolutely not going to advocate that we cut any of those positions" (S6).

### Public safety
- Promises to "continue to support law enforcement" (S1). His site cites support for "the successful 911 sales tax measure" (S2).
- Defended the county's syringe-exchange ordinance, struck down in court at a cost of hundreds of thousands of dollars, as "very sound legislation" and called the ruling "a judge ... legislating from his bench" (S3).
- Voted against the August 2026 ordinance allowing limited cannabis sales in unincorporated Lewis County (Packwood), citing increased underage use (S5).

### Flood control, land use and resources
- Chairs the Office of Chehalis Basin board and the Chehalis River Basin Flood Control Zone District, and supports the proposed flow-through flood-retention facility near Pe Ell as "absolutely the process that we need to continue" (S1, S3, S4).
- Campaign site: pushes back "against excessive state mandates to protect local control over land use," while encouraging affordable housing and reducing homelessness; favors reducing regulations on farmers and timber owners and increasing timber harvests (S2).
- Skeptical of data centers; says county planning limits them to the former TransAlta mine industrial site (S4).

### Government and reform
- Calls himself a property-rights advocate and a proponent of small government (S3). "I believe our elected representatives must hold firm to our Constitution and the founding principles of our Nation" (S2).

## Record

- Lewis County Commissioner, District 3, since 2022 (S3); his campaign site says he has twice chaired the board (S2).
- Voted on four county budgets; board took the allowed property-tax increase once in that period (S3).
- Voted no on the 2026 cannabis-sales ordinance, which passed 2-1 (S5).
- Board defended the syringe-exchange ordinance in court and lost (S3).
- County representative on the Chehalis Basin Board (chair), Lower Columbia Fish Recovery Board, 911 governance, Veterans Advisory Board, Economic Alliance, Lewis County Transit and the Southwest Washington Fair board (S1, S3).
