## What the office does
The three-member Board of Lewis County Commissioners is the county's legislative authority: it adopts the budget, sets county policy and ordinances and oversees county departments (S3). The board has employed a county manager since 2018 (S6). Commissioners are nominated by the voters of their own district (S2). In Lewis County the District 3 primary was held only in the district, and the general election is county-wide (S5). District 3 covers East Lewis County and part of the south county, including Vader and Toledo (S6).

## Race dynamics
Both finalists prefer the Republican Party (S1). Incumbent Scott Brummer, first elected in 2022, faces Lewis County PUD Commissioner Mike Hadaller (S5). In the certified primary Brummer received 3,084 votes (33.60%) and Hadaller 2,907 (31.67%), ahead of Democrat Zac Eckstein (21.56%), Republican Tim Toerber (7.27%) and independent Kyle Wheeler (5.87%) (S4).

Brummer runs on his record: flood-control and basin board chairmanships, holding down property-tax increases, and support for law enforcement (S1). Hadaller runs as a lifelong East County resident and "rural conservative" promising audits of every agency that receives county money, eliminating the county manager position, and an end to "expensive political lawsuits" (S1, S6; michael-hadaller.md S3). At the July forum both opposed cannabis sales in unincorporated areas and a public safety tax for now. They split on the proposed Chehalis River flow-through dam, which Brummer supports and Hadaller opposes (S6). Hadaller had raised about five times as much as Brummer by mid-August (S7).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience/record:** a one-term incumbent with a voting record and regional board roles against a challenger who has served one PUD term and promises an East County voice and change in how the county is run.
2. **Spending/accountability:** both campaign on restraint; Hadaller emphasizes audits of every funded entity and cutting the county manager; Brummer points to rarely taking the allowed property-tax increase and argues against cutting deputies to end the road-fund transfer.
3. **Climate/flood infrastructure:** the flow-through dam divides them.
4. Taxes: both oppose a public safety tax for now; Brummer is "not sold on it yet."
5. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives: none found for either candidate.
