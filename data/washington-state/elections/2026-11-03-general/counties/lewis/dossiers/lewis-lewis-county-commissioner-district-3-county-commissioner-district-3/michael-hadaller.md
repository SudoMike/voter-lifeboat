## Background

Mike Hadaller, of Salkum, is the Lewis County PUD commissioner for PUD District No. 3. He lists "2022 PUD commissioner" as his elected experience, was sworn in at the end of December 2022, and serves as board president (S1, S2, S3, S9). He grew up in Ethel, attended school in Onalaska and graduated from Mossyrock High School, then followed his father into logging (S1, S3). He describes himself as a business owner. Secretary of State records reviewed by The Chronicle show three businesses since 2014 (a trucking company and Mike's Stump Buster, LLC among them), all now dissolved or inactive (S1, S3). He prefers the Republican Party (S1).

At the July forum he acknowledged a 1999 felony conviction for attempting to elude police, which he said was later expunged. He linked his conduct then to the unsolved 1985 murder of his grandparents and said, "I wasn't a model citizen back there, and the last few years, I've grown up" (S2, S3). The Chronicle reports his most recent legal matter in the records it searched was a 2003 bankruptcy (S3). He declined a one-on-one interview with The Chronicle after the forum (S3).

In the certified August 4 primary, held only in District 3, he placed second with 2,907 votes (31.67%) (S8).

## Positions

### Spending, audits and county government
- Calls for a "common-sense" budget and more budget transparency. "With any tax or any county entity getting our tax dollars, they should be open for an audit period ... Don't matter if it's the sheriff, don't matter if it's the Lewis County Alliance or anybody" (S3).
- Would eliminate the county manager position: "First thing I do is get rid of the county manager, with $177,000 (salary) that we don't need" (S2).
- Declined to name specific cuts: "I think you just need to tighten the belt." Opposes the annual road-fund transfer to the sheriff: "I sure ain't gonna take $1,250,000 away from the road maintenance crew to give it to our sheriff" (S3).
- "No more expensive political lawsuits. Just serve the public" (S1). After the primary he said voters are "not happy with all these lawsuits" (S5).
- His letter: "Sometimes, that means paying for government responsibilities like roads and safety. Many times, it just means getting government out of the way" (S4).

### Taxes
- Against a public safety tax for now. He wants to wait for a new sheriff and to audit the sheriff's office and other major departments before they get more funding (S2).

### Economy and permitting
- "I want Lewis County to thrive again with good paying jobs by making Lewis County a place where businesses want to be"; lists expedited permitting among county needs (S1). Promises to lower regulatory hurdles (S3).

### Public safety
- "Our residents shouldn't have to be worried about being burglarized while they are at work or have to tolerate known drug dealers and drug houses" (S1). His letter calls for rooting out "the drug traffickers and addicts" (S4).
- Opposed cannabis sales in unincorporated Lewis County, citing access by minors (S2).

### Flood control and land use
- Opposes the proposed Chehalis River flow-through dam and proposed pumping extra water out of the basin instead (S2).
- Skeptical of data centers, citing water and energy use (S2).

### Representation
- Promises an "open-door policy," to return calls and to attend city council meetings in the south and east county (S1, S2, S3).

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No public position found in the sources reviewed.

### Parents and schools
- No public position found in the sources reviewed. His letter mentions improving schools as part of economic competitiveness (S4).

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

## Record

- Lewis County PUD Commissioner, District No. 3, since December 2022; board president (S2, S3, S9). His statement says he has served on the PUD board with Dave Muller and David Fenn (S1).

## Endorsements

Commissioner Lindsey Pollock has campaigned for him (S3). His statement says "Commissioner Pollock and I will provide that leadership" (S1).

## Campaign finance

PDC summary as of the September 17, 2026 update: $31,100.88 raised, $30,311.83 spent (S6). The Chronicle reports roughly $15,000 of his pre-primary total came from himself in cash and in-kind contributions, nearly $10,000 from individuals and $3,700 from businesses (S7).
