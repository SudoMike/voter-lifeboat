## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would "restore the District's regular property tax levy rate for library services, operations and maintenance from $0.22 to $0.35 per $1,000 of assessed valuation for both 2027 and 2028, subject to applicable limitations." The 2028 levy amount becomes the base for later limits under chapter 84.55 RCW. The Board of Trustees adopted Resolution No. 26-002 [S1][S2].

- **The district.** Timberland Regional Library (TRL) serves Grays Harbor, Lewis, Mason, Pacific and Thurston counties through 29 libraries [S1][S4]. Its filing with Lewis County Elections lists the measure as a lid lift under RCW 84.55.050 needing a simple majority [S3]. The Chronicle reports the measure goes to voters in all five counties [S6].
- **Who votes in Lewis County.** The Chronicle describes it as the one countywide measure on Lewis ballots [S6], but the district does not take in every Lewis city; this package scopes it to the TRL district (see `pipeline/build_votewa_lite_data.py`, Lewis block).
- **What the money would pay for.** The explanatory statement says approval "would provide funding necessary to maintain Library services, staffing, collections, and open hours" [S1][S3]. TRL says the levy would provide more open, staffed hours and days throughout the district [S4]. Board President Brian Mittge told the Lewis County commissioners it would let TRL "resume full service based on what we had budgeted to do and increase it modestly but meaningfully with more hours and branches resuming our collections and some more activities" [S5].
- **If it fails.** Reductions in staffing, materials, hours, online services, bookmobile service and programming "will begin in 2027," and maintenance could be delayed [S1][S3]. Mittge: "We will still keep the doors open... But some of the staff, some of those small libraries, might be unstaffed. So keycard access only, and there would be fewer hours in the branches that do stay open" [S5].

## Cost

- Rate: from $0.228924 to $0.35 per $1,000 of assessed value in 2027 and 2028 [S1][S3]. State law allows up to $0.50 [S1][S4].
- Explanatory statement: about $40.44 a year ($3.37 a month) on a $334,000 home [S1][S3].
- TRL's Lewis County estimate: $46 a year ($3.83 a month) on the 2025 Lewis median home value of $357,400 [S4]; The Chronicle reported the same $46 average for county residents [S5].
- Revenue: about $14 million more districtwide in 2027 than without a lid lift [S4][S5].
- At The Chronicle's downtown Centralia building, TRL's rate is the lowest of 10 levies; at $0.35 it would be second-lowest, above the Port of Centralia ($0.29) and below the Riverside Regional Fire Authority ($0.40) [S6].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This is a levy lid lift on TRL's existing regular levy [S3][S4]. About 96% of TRL's revenue comes from it [S1][S7]. TRL says the 1% annual limit on revenue growth has lowered its rate from the $0.50 maximum to $0.22 and that it "has not had a full levy rate in 25 years" [S4].

- TRL last asked voters in 2009, and that request failed; if this one passes it would be the first lift approved since 1987, per TRL's records [S1][S6].
- 2026 budget: Mittge told the commissioners TRL closed a $3.8 million deficit with about $2.23 million in cuts to new books, programs, travel and training, plus about $300,000 from administrative salary changes, restructuring and furloughs. He called the cuts "not a particularly sustainable response for years ahead" [S5]. TRL reports districtwide staffing fell from about 251 FTE in 2016 to 182 FTE as of August 2026 [S4].
- Lewis branches: the manager of the Centralia and Chehalis libraries said Centralia went from 17 staff to six in nine years; Winlock and Salkum have run leaner, and further cuts would reduce Anywhere Library mobile visits [S5].

## Arguments for

The statement for (no author listed in the Lewis guide) [S1]:
- TRL's levy "hasn't increased in 25 years" and its value has been "nibbled away by higher prices"; state law caps growth at 1% a year "until voters lift the lid."
- Approval would let TRL "restore hours and staffing" and expand collections and programs; it calls the increase "a long overdue cost-of-living raise."
- Rebuttal: libraries "have endured a 25-year financial drought" and are an affordable resource for families, students, job-seekers and seniors.

Four former trustees, two from Lewis County (Edna Fund, Brian Zylstra), wrote that rural residents "in far-flung areas" could lose access and several branches could go unstaffed if it fails, and that the owner of a $400,000 home would pay $52 more a year [S7].

## Arguments against

The statement against (no author listed in the Lewis guide) [S1]:
- A no vote "is a vote for fiscal responsibility, taxpayer accountability, and careful budgeting" as owners face rising bills from many levies.
- The library should first show it has cut costs, sought grants and partnerships and better managed existing resources; the tax "could put future investments in schools and emergency services at risk."
- Rebuttal: many library services "are delivered through nonprofits and community organizations more efficiently and effectively"; "Voting 'No' is not a vote against libraries or literacy."

Lewis County Commissioner Sean Swope, in an email to The Chronicle after the interim executive director missed the commissioners' annual TRL meeting, asked why someone who "can afford to only vote yes on one property tax increase" should choose TRL "over schools or public safety" [S5].

## Endorsements and campaign

- Former TRL trustees Edna Fund, Bob Hall, Corby Varness and Brian Zylstra support it [S7].
- PDC: a committee named "Yes For Libraries" (Olympia; jurisdiction Thurston County; stance for) reports $46,855.18 raised. Its record does not name TRL. No committee for or against filed with Lewis County as its jurisdiction was found [S8].
