## Background

Cori McKean is the Kittitas County Coroner. A dispatcher (Ellensburg Police, then KITTCOM) before joining the Coroner's Office in 2019, recruited by Coroner Nick Henderson, she rose to chief deputy coroner [S1][S2]. Henderson credited her with making the office's accreditation by the International Association of Coroners & Medical Examiners a priority; the office was accredited in 2024 after a five-year review [S4]. When Henderson died in October 2025, the commissioners named her acting coroner [S5]; after the county Republican Party recommended her (the only applicant), they appointed her to the rest of his term, which ends in December 2026 [S6]. She reports state and national medicolegal death-investigation certifications [S2] and 35 years of service in Kittitas County [S1]. She prefers the Republican Party [S1].

In the August 4 primary she received 7,393 votes (64.5%) to Charlie Divine's 4,024 (35.1%) [S9].

## Record

- In March 2026 a deputy coroner filed a complaint with the commissioners about her hiring of a chief deputy coroner without an application, after the four deputy coroners had sent her a letter describing an "untenable working environment," untrained duties (X-rays, blood draws, fingerprinting) and "punitive language" in draft policies [S7].
- An outside investigation by Senti Law (September 2026) examined 10 issues and found no wrongdoing: the hire was a re-hire not subject to competitive hiring, the duties were in draft policies, and the decisions were "within Coroner McKean's ultimate discretion and authority" [S8]. She acknowledged more training was needed before the new duties became expectations [S8].
- Says that as coroner she has launched family-support and outreach efforts, an investigator peer-support program and wider collaboration with the Health Department on prevention [S2].

## Positions

### Office direction
- Running to "retain" her: commitments to thorough, respectful work, support for families, continuous learning, and transparency and trust [S1].
- Proposes the county's first dedicated morgue, citing a recent feasibility study; describes it as "a practical, rural solution that keeps costs down" in place of relying on funeral homes and other counties' autopsy suites [S3].
- Keeping accreditation, continuing education and the Central Washington University internship program [S3].
