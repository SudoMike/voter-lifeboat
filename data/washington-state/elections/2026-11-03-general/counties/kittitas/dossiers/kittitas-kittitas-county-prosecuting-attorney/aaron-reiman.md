## Background

Aaron Reiman is an Ellensburg attorney running as an independent; he has held no elected office [S1]. He served as a Navy JAG officer (1999-2004), including an appointment as a Special Assistant U.S. Attorney in Puerto Rico, then in the U.S. Coast Guard (2004-2020), retiring as a commander [S1][S2]. He was a City of Yakima prosecutor after moving to Ellensburg in 2014 and became the City of Ellensburg's prosecuting attorney in February 2020 [S6]. He is now a public defender [S1][S4]. He reports 25 years of prosecutorial experience [S1].

Education: B.A., University of Washington; J.D., Gonzaga University; master's, University of Oklahoma [S1][S2]. Community service listed includes Ellensburg downtown Rotary, the American Legion, Boy Scouts Pack 489 and school activities [S1]. He states his party preference as Independent [S1].

In the August 4 primary he placed second with 4,032 votes (34.3%) [S7]. Unlike his opponents he has never worked in the county prosecutor's office [S4].

## Positions

### Office direction
- Running as an independent: "I made the choice to not bring national politics into this role" [S4]; the position should be "free from partisan politics" [S1].
- Says he can come in "with a fresh slate, fresh eyes" and "figure out what's been working, what hasn't been working" [S4].
- Describes himself as data-driven; says he tracked more data than required as Ellensburg's city prosecutor [S4].

### Public safety
- Promises to "aggressively prosecute violent and repeat offenders" and strengthen partnerships with law enforcement, while balancing accountability "with smart prevention, diversion, and rehabilitation programs to reduce recidivism" [S1][S3].
- Names gang activity as a pressing concern, citing the 2024 Jack in the Box murder and continuing tagging [S4].
- Wants pre-filing diversion; says he raised it with Hammond and Zempel after becoming a public defender and would raise it again with the court [S4].
- Would explore lowering costs for indigent defendants (treatment evaluations, stay-of-proceedings fees) so they can use diversion options, apply for grants for those programs, and expand recovery-navigator work for lower-level drug offenses such as simple possession [S4][S5].
- Victim rights: says victim advocacy would be a core priority of the office [S3].

### Staffing and budget
- Calls staff retention a critical challenge; proposes mentoring, career pathways, workload limits and advocacy for competitive pay (which the commissioners set) [S2][S5].
- Promises to "manage the office budget responsibly, reduce waste" and transparent operations [S3].
