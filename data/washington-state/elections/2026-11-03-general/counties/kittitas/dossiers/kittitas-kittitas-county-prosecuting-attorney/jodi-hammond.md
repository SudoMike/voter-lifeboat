## Background

Jodi Hammond is the Chief Criminal Deputy Prosecuting Attorney of Kittitas County, a post she has held for nine years; she has never held or sought elected office [S1]. She joined the county prosecutor's office in 2011 and became chief criminal deputy in 2018 [S4]. She reports more than 20 years as a prosecutor, handling cases from misdemeanors to homicide and sexual assault [S1]; the Daily Record puts it at 22 years as a criminal prosecutor, nine of them on retiring Prosecutor Greg Zempel's management team [S6].

She holds B.A. degrees in English and in Law & Justice from Central Washington University (2002) and a J.D. from Arizona State University (2005) [S1]. She serves on the Washington Association of Prosecuting Attorneys appellate and training committees, teaches at statewide prosecutor trainings, and is an adjunct law professor at CWU [S1]. Community service listed includes Ellensburg Morning Rotary, Ellensburg School District volunteering, Valley Theater Company, and former board seats at Bright Beginnings (Head Start) and Wellspring Christian Fellowship [S1]. She prefers the Republican Party [S1].

In the August 4 primary she placed first with 4,380 votes (37.2%), ahead of Aaron Reiman (4,032) and fellow deputy prosecutor Patricia Todd (3,333) [S7].

## Positions

### Office direction
- Says her top priority would be to uphold the values set under Zempel: "fighting for victims in ways that no one else will do," providing legal representation for county departments through the civil division, and "holding offenders accountable" [S5].
- Runs on experience: "with a new candidate ... there's going to be a steep learning curve ... for me ... it's just shifting gears" [S6]. Her statement describes leading changes in the office "to improve productivity and efficiency" [S1].

### Public safety
- Says Kittitas County has seen a rise in violent crime, which she describes as resource-intensive for law enforcement and prosecutors [S6].
- Describes the role as protecting public safety and standing up for victims, with justice that is "fair" and "principled" [S2]. Says she wants every person, "regardless of their race, ethnicity, sexual orientation," to feel comfortable reporting crime and engaging with law enforcement and the courts [S6].
- On access to justice, says the prosecutor's office does not interact directly with the public and should work through "great partnerships with those who actually are interacting with the public" [S5].

### Staffing
- Says the last three attorneys who left the office endorse her and left for reasons unrelated to office culture or leadership; attributes the statewide prosecutor shortage to generational and career factors [S5].
- Says she would give retention bonuses to every employee "if money were no option" [S5]; aims to keep caseloads below 50 for the domestic-violence and sexual-assault specialist, and encourages staff to use mental-health benefits [S6].
