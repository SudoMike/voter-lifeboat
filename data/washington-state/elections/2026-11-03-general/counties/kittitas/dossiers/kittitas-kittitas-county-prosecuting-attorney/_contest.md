## What the office does
The prosecuting attorney runs the county's criminal prosecutions, represents county departments in civil matters through the office's civil division, and works with victims and law enforcement [S1][S4].

## Race dynamics
Open seat: Greg Zempel, prosecutor since he was first elected in 1994, did not seek a ninth term and endorsed his chief criminal deputy, Jodi Hammond [S3]. Hammond (prefers Republican) runs on continuity and experience inside the office; Aaron Reiman, a former Navy JAG and Coast Guard lawyer, former Ellensburg city prosecutor and now a public defender, runs as an independent promising a "fresh slate" and more diversion [S1][S5]. In the August 4 primary Hammond received 4,380 votes (37.2%), Reiman 4,032 (34.3%) and deputy prosecutor Patricia Todd 3,333 (28.3%) [S2]. Turnover among the office's attorneys was a forum topic; Todd, now eliminated, called it unacceptable [S4].

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience:** the office's chief criminal deputy against a lawyer with long prosecution and defense experience who has never worked in the office.
2. **Safety:** both promise to hold violent and repeat offenders accountable; Reiman adds pre-filing diversion, lower diversion costs for indigent defendants and expanded recovery-navigator referrals.
3. **Reform:** keep Zempel's approach versus review "what's been working, what hasn't".
4. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives, social policy or parents-and-schools: none found for either candidate.
