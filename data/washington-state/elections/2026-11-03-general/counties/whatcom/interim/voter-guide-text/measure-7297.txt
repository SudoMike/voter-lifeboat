# City of Bellingham - Proposition 2026-07 Streamlining the City's Contract Review Process to Allow Electronic Signatures

## BallotTitle
Proposed Charter Amendment Proposition 2026-07 would streamline the contract process and allow for electronic signatures.
This amendment would streamline and update the City's contract process to allow the use of electronic signatures. Currently, the Charter requires the Mayor to sign all city contracts, bonds, and instruments of any kind, and for the finance director to attest the Mayor’s signature and affix the City's seal to the document. This amendment would remove these inefficient and out-of-date requirements, authorize the Mayor's designee to sign contracts, and allow the City to use electronic signatures.
Should this amendment be enacted into law?

## ExplanatoryStatement
This proposed Charter amendment would streamline and modernize the City's contract process to remove requirements for wet ink signatures and allow the use of electronic signatures. Currently, the Charter requires the Mayor to physically sign "all written contracts, bonds and instruments of every kind and description to which the City shall be a party" with a wet ink signature and requires the finance director to attest to the Mayor's signature and affix the city seal. These out-of-date requirements create inefficiencies in the City's contracting process and complicate the use of electronic signatures by requiring sequential signing, attestation of a prior signature, and physical attachment of the city seal. These requirements also prohibit the City from transitioning to a paperless contract process, which would improve efficiency, reduce costs, and support environmental sustainability. If adopted, this amendment would allow the Mayor to assign a designee to execute contracts in the name of the City and remove the requirement that the finance director attests to the Mayor's signature and affix the seal. The Mayor would remain responsible for ensuring that all city contracts are faithfully kept and performed.

## ArgumentFor
This charter amendment will help your local government work more efficiently. 
Current charter language prevents the City of Bellingham from using electronic contracts and signatures – resulting in the literal shuffling of paper from one place to another. 
When you sign a document today, you’re likely signing electronically. It's faster, since you don’t have to coordinate schedules to pass a document along. It’s also more secure, since digital signatures are verified through multiple factors. 
At the City, our current charter requirement means every contract is printed on paper, mailed out and back, then physically carried to various locations for at least four individual hand signatures from city officials. The final signer is the finance director, who attests that the mayor’s signature is authentic and crimps the paper with the official City seal. Only then is the document scanned and stored in our electronic filing system. This antiquated process adds time and cost to every contract without adding value or accountability. It verges on absurdity. Vote Yes on this amendment to approve the use of electronic signatures to streamline the City’s process for contracts and other documents: saving paper, time and helping your government work more efficiently for you.
