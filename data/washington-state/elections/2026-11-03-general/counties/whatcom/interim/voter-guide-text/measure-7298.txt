# City of Bellingham - Initiative 26-01 Prohibition of Algorithmic Price-Fixing in the Rental Market

## BallotTitle
This measure would prohibit two or more landlords from entering into an agreement to establish rental prices, prohibit the use of paid algorithmic coordinating services that use systems or software to analyze public and private data about the rental market to recommend rental prices, lease renewal terms, or occupancy levels to multiple landlords, create a private right of action, establish whistleblower and anti-retaliation protections for tenants and employees, and authorize civil and criminal penalties.
Should this measure be enacted into law?

## ExplanatoryStatement
This measure would add a new chapter to the Bellingham Municipal Code prohibiting algorithmic price-fixing in the rental housing market. It defines "coordinating services" as tools that collect rental pricing and occupancy data from multiple landlords and use algorithms to recommend future rental prices or terms. The measure would make it unlawful for landlords to agree to set rental prices, to pay for or use coordinating services, or for service providers to offer such services to multiple landlords. Certain activities, including recordkeeping tools, aggregated market reports, or information used for financing or appraisals, would still be allowed.
The measure establishes civil remedies for tenants or others harmed by violations, including damages and attorney fees. It authorizes enforcement by the City Attorney and creates civil infractions and potential misdemeanor penalties for repeated or willful violations. The measure also includes whistleblower and anti-retaliation protections for tenants and employees who report violations. The measure would not apply to hotels, motels, or short-term rentals. The measure includes a severability clause.

## ArgumentFor
Initiative 26-01 protects Bellingham’s rental market from technologies that explicitly increase rent prices in both the short and long term. Algorithmic pricing tools combine proprietary market data from subscribing property managers and landlords to calculate rent prices. These third-party platforms have the ability to increase the cost of living by suggesting that clients raise their prices collectively. When done by people, this is illegal price-fixing. Automation should not be a loophole to federal law.
Coordinated pricing fundamentally undermines fair markets. The negative effects of these technologies are well-documented. According to nationwide reporting, algorithmically priced units are, on average, $70/month more expensive than their non-algorithmically priced counterparts. Rental price-fixing does real and lasting damage to communities. Affordable housing is not optional. It’s an essential need, both for people and a thriving Bellingham.
26-01 is based on a proven model that has already been successfully implemented across the country. Bellingham has the chance to join cities like Seattle, Portland, Philadelphia, Minneapolis, and Washington, D.C., in leading the fight against these predatory technologies.
