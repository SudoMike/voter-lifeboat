# City of Bellingham - Proposition 2026-06 Authorizing the City's Salary Commission to Set the Mayor's Salary

## BallotTitle
Proposed Charter Amendment Proposition 2026-06 would make a salary commission responsible for setting the Mayor's salary.
This amendment would change the Charter's requirements concerning the Mayor's salary. Currently, the Charter provides that the Mayor's salary should at no time be less than that of the highest paid City official or employee. This amendment would delete that requirement and instead assign responsibility for setting the Mayor's salary to the City's independent salary commission, which was established by ordinance to periodically review and fix the salaries of City Council members.
Should this amendment be enacted into law?

## ExplanatoryStatement
This proposed Charter amendment would change the process for setting the Mayor’s salary. Currently, the Charter requires that “the Mayor’s salary should at no time be less than that of the highest paid City official or employee.” The City negotiates and sets salaries for employees, including department heads, based on market factors related to recruitment and retention. Increases in salaries for City employees that are driven by the market automatically increase the Mayor’s salary. This amendment would remove the requirement that the Mayor’s salary be the highest salary in the City and assign the responsibility for determining the Mayor’s salary to an independent salary commission.
The City’s salary commission was established by ordinance to periodically review and fix the salaries for members of the City Council. The City’s salary commission provides an established public process for reviewing and, if necessary, adjusting the salaries for Council members. This proposed change would have the City’s salary commission review and fix the salary of the Mayor. Whatcom County has an independent salary commission to review and fix salaries for elected officials at Whatcom County. The State of Washington also has a citizens' commission on salaries for elected officials.

## ArgumentFor
This charter amendment will bring a more transparent, sensible process to how the mayor’s salary is set. 
Current charter language ties the mayor’s salary to the highest-paid City employee. This method sounds reasonable, but in practice it means the mayor’s pay is driven by salary compression: as wages rise throughout the City workforce, labor contracts require supervisors and managers to receive corresponding increases. Those adjustments cascade upward, ultimately driving up the mayor’s salary. 
This amendment would instead have the mayor’s salary set by an independent salary commission, the same transparent process which is in use for the Bellingham City Council, and the process used for elected officials across Whatcom County. It’s also the norm statewide based on a review of similarly sized and larger Washington cities - not one set the mayor’s salary in relation to the salaries of their staff. Bellingham’s current method is the lone outlier. 
A salary commission allows the public direct conversation about compensation for elected officials, rather than leaving it to an indirect formula. Vote Yes to eliminate Bellingham’s outdated system, align with best practices and create a more transparent process to determining the mayor's salary.
