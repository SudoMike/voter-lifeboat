## Background
Alex Ramel, of Bellingham, is the incumbent Democratic State Representative for the 40th Legislative District, Position 2, in his third term; House Democrats elected him Majority Whip [S1][S2][S8]. He has held the seat since 2020 [S8]. He sits on the House Environment and Energy, Finance, Transportation and Rules committees, the Energy Strategy Advisory Committee and the State Building Code Council [S2]. Before the Legislature he was a clean-energy and environmental organizer; as program director at Sustainable Connections he founded the Community Energy Challenge, an energy-efficiency program in Whatcom, Skagit and San Juan counties [S1]. He is a past president of the Kulshan Community Land Trust and served on a worker-retraining advisory board during the 2008 recession [S1]. He holds a BA in environmental policy and planning from Western Washington University [S1].

In the August 4 primary he led a four-candidate field with 68.3% in Whatcom County returns as of August 7; Joseph Segault (12.9%) advanced with him [S9].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** supports the tax on high earners and stresses that it is paired with tax cuts [S8]. His statement says "we taxed millionaires to provide free lunches for kids, healthcare, and tax relief for small businesses and low-income families" [S1]. On the projected deficit, he hopes to close shortfalls through "thoughtful, targeted" budget cuts and closing tax loopholes rather than more taxes, and says his "line in the sand" for any new tax is that it be progressive [S8].
- **Housing:** says he "helped cap rent increases" and helped fund a record number of affordable homes [S1]; names his work on a rent stabilization bill among his proudest accomplishments [S8]. Co-sponsored and voted for EHB 1217 (see Record) [S7].
- **Climate and energy:** "I fight for bold climate action" [S1]; names building decarbonization among his proudest accomplishments and says lawmakers "need to be finding smart ways to say yes" to clean-energy development, citing his 2026 bill taxing renewable-energy facilities to benefit local governments [S8]. Says the state developed an energy strategy to reduce pollution and energy costs [S1]. His website says "it's time for leadership to confront the climate crisis" [S11].
- **Technology and data centers:** would allow data-center construction while keeping the energy costs from falling on the state or neighboring ratepayers [S8].
- **Health care:** his website says "healthcare is a human right" [S11]; his statement cites funding for health care and mental-health treatment in Whatcom and Skagit counties [S1].
- **Labor:** his website says all workers "deserve to be respected and protected" [S11]; endorsed by unions representing nurses and firefighters [S1].
- **Public safety:** his website says "gun violence is preventable" [S11].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** "I'm 100% pro-choice" [S1]. Says he is voting no on all statewide initiatives, which include I-638 [S1].
- **Parents and schools:** voted for ESHB 1296 (see Record) [S4]; says he is voting no on all statewide initiatives, which include I-1 [S1].
- **Government process:** describes his approach as collaborative, working on colleagues' priorities as whip and with some Republicans [S8].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** "I'm voting no on all statewide initiatives and ask you to join me" [S1]. Record: voted for ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S3].
- **I-1 (parental rights):** same statement: voting no [S1]. Record: voted for ESHB 1296 (2025), the amendments to the I-2081 parental-rights law that I-1 would undo [S4].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** same statement: voting no [S1].

## Record
- Voted yea on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million (2026-03-09, passed 51-46) [S3].
- Voted yea on House final passage of EHB 2081, business and occupation tax increases (2025-04-22, passed 50-48) [S5], and on ESSB 5814, excise taxes on select services (2025-04-23, passed 50-47) [S6].
- Voted yea on ESHB 1296 ("promoting a safe and supportive public education system") on House passage (2025-03-12, 56-37) and on final passage as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24, 59-39) [S4].
- Secondary sponsor of HB 1217 and voted yea on final passage of EHB 1217, limiting residential rent and fee increases, as recommended by the conference committee (2025-04-27, 54-44) [S7].
- Co-sponsor, with Reps. Joe Timmons and Debra Lekanoff, of the 2026 county ferry district bill sought by Whatcom County for the Lummi Island ferry [S10].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $83,798.13 raised and $72,211.14 spent [S12].

## Endorsements
Washington Conservation Action and unions representing nurses and firefighters [S1]. His website quotes individual supporters, including Port of Bellingham Commissioner Michael Shepard and former San Juan County Councilmember Lovel Pratt [S11].
