## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, ferries, energy policy, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The 40th District runs from south Bellingham to a northwest portion of Skagit County and includes all of San Juan County [S2]. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the two candidates who advanced from the August primary.

## Race dynamics
Democratic incumbent Alex Ramel, in the seat since 2020 and now House Majority Whip, faces Joseph Segault, an Orcas Island stone mason and political newcomer who states no party preference [S1][S2]. Ramel led the four-candidate primary with 68.3% in Whatcom County returns as of August 7; Segault was second with 12.9% [S3]. Segault has no campaign website and reports no fundraising, against $83.8k raised by Ramel (joseph-segault.md S5; alex-ramel.md S12).

The main contrast is taxes and the state budget. Ramel voted for the 2026 tax on income over $1 million and the 2025 business and occupation and services tax bills, supports keeping the high-earner tax and says any new tax must be progressive (alex-ramel.md S3, S5, S6, S8). Segault opposes rising taxes, calls state budgets bloated and says a tax like the income tax should go to a vote of the people [S1][S2]. Ramel's stated focus is climate, building decarbonization and housing, including the 2025 rent-increase cap he co-sponsored (alex-ramel.md S7, S8); Segault says restrictive zoning makes housing less affordable [S2]. Ramel says he is voting no on all statewide initiatives, including I-645, I-1 and I-638, and describes himself as 100% pro-choice [S1]; Segault has no stated position on the initiatives or on social policy.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Ramel's yes votes on the income tax and 2025 tax bills and no on I-645 vs. Segault's opposition to rising taxes and call for a public vote on the income tax.
2. **Record vs. renewal:** third-term incumbent and Majority Whip vs. a newcomer with no prior office.
3. **Housing:** Ramel's rent-stabilization record vs. Segault's zoning-and-supply framing.
4. **Spending:** Segault calls state budgets bloated; Ramel prefers targeted cuts and closing tax loopholes before new taxes.
5. **Climate, social and parents and schools:** Ramel's climate record, pro-choice stance and no votes on I-1 and I-638; no Segault positions found on these.
