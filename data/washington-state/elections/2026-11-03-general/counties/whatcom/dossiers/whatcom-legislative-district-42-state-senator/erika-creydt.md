## Background
Erika Creydt, of Blaine, is the Republican candidate for the open 42nd District Senate seat [S13][S15]. She describes herself as the longest-serving Blaine School Board director; Cascadia Daily News reported in July 2026 that she has served two terms [S1][S15]. She is a clinical psychologist and founder and clinical director of TouchStone Counseling, with 22 years as a youth counselor; she conducted psychological assessments for special-needs students in Chicago Catholic schools and taught graduate classes at Adler University [S1][S3]. She holds a BA from California Lutheran University and an MA and PsyD from Adler University, graduated from Blaine schools in 1999, and is a third-generation Blaine resident [S1][S13]. Her community service list includes founding TouchStone Behavioral Health Clinics, the Healthy Youth Coalition, the Everson Youth Crisis Facility and the Youth Resource Center; her campaign also lists membership on the Whatcom County Behavioral Health Advisory Committee [S1][S11]. She announced her run on January 17, 2026, citing her work in youth mental health [S13].

She has not served in the Legislature. In the August 4 primary she placed first with 22,125 votes, about 44.6% of the 49,654 votes counted for candidates and write-ins, ahead of Democrats Michael Shepard (13,125) and Eamonn Collins (13,121) and unaffiliated Ryan Bowman (1,248) [S17].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** opposes a state income tax; her website headline is "Say No to a State Income Tax," argues that once an income tax exists it is easier to extend to other brackets, and says Washington has a spending problem rather than a revenue problem [S4]. Says the state should repeal the 9.9% tax on income above $1 million enacted under Senate Bill 6346 [S5]. Calls the tax unconstitutional and says income is property under state case law [S6]. Criticized the sales taxes on services enacted "last October" and says small businesses should be consulted before new taxes [S4]. Told Cascadia Daily News she wants fewer taxes and regulations to support businesses [S15]. Her website states that "my opponent" has publicly supported an income tax on households earning $125,000; the page does not name the opponent or cite a source [S4].
- **Spending and accountability:** says programs that do not show a return on investment should be reviewed, reformed or eliminated, and that state spending should adjust to the end of federal COVID-19 relief [S4]. Supports measuring whether budget programs achieve intended outcomes and redirecting money from ineffective ones [S5]. Her statement calls for "accountability so tax dollars pay for what was promised" [S1].
- **Housing:** supports increasing housing supply by streamlining permitting and removing barriers, while concentrating development where infrastructure exists and protecting farmland and water [S5][S9].
- **Business, agriculture and water:** says layers of regulation are limiting innovation and supports a state economic development plan [S15]. Opposes the state water adjudication requiring about 30,000 Whatcom County landowners to prove their water rights, citing costs to farmers and property owners [S7].
- **Healthcare and mental health:** says the state should find ways to keep people insured after federal cuts and should address provider shortages so residents can get timely physical, behavioral and mental health care [S9][S15].
- **Public safety:** says voters raise concerns about not feeling safe in downtown Bellingham [S15]; lists "supporting public safety" among spending priorities [S4]. A former Whatcom County undersheriff spoke at her kickoff about "the Sheriff Bill" [S11]. No specific public-safety proposals found.
- **Parents and schools:** says enrichment levies now cover core costs the state formula does not fully fund, such as counselors, nurses and special education [S8]; supports stable school funding, special education, career and technical programs and apprenticeships [S9]. No position on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs found.
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Climate and immigration:** no stated position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement naming the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. She says the tax enacted under Senate Bill 6346, which I-645 would repeal, should be repealed [S5], and calls it unconstitutional [S6]. No legislative record (she has not served in the Legislature).
- **I-1 (parental rights):** no statement found as of 2026-10-08. No legislative record on ESHB 1296.
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no statement found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- Blaine School Board director (two terms per CDN) [S1][S15]. In 2025 she supported pausing the Blaine school consolidation for a year and asked the new superintendent to cut "things that are wasteful" from the budget [S18]. Her campaign says she supported a 2026 board resolution urging the Legislature to reverse a proposed 25% cut to the Transition to Kindergarten program [S12].
- No legislative votes; she has not held state office.
- PDC (updated 2026-10-04): $265,795.14 raised and $221,385.14 spent [S19]. As of July 15, much of her fundraising was in-kind and cash support from the Senate Republican Campaign Committee and the state Republican Party [S15].

## Endorsements
