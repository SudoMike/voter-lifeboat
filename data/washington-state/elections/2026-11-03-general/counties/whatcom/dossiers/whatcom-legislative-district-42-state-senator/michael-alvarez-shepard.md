## Background
Michael Alvarez Shepard, of Bellingham, is the Democratic candidate for the open 42nd District Senate seat [S15][S18]. He is in his third term as a Port of Bellingham commissioner, elected countywide, and is a former Democratic precinct committee officer [S1][S2]. He teaches in the Western Washington University anthropology department and in a prison education program, and previously worked as eLearning director at Whatcom Community College and cultural documentation coordinator at Northwest Indian College [S1][S2]. He holds a PhD in anthropology from the University of British Columbia and an MA and BA from Western Washington University [S1]. His community service includes the Whatcom County Economic Investment Board and the Kulshan Community Land Trust board [S1]. He said that if elected he would resign from the port, and would teach at Western only in quarters when the Legislature is not in session [S12].

He has not served in the Legislature. Sen. Sharon Shewmake, who is retiring, supports him [S1][S15]. In the August 4 primary he finished second with 13,125 votes, four ahead of fellow Democrat Eamonn Collins (13,121); a mandatory hand recount on August 25-26 matched the machine count [S17][S18]. Cascadia Daily News described him as the establishment-backed Democrat and Collins as the further-left candidate [S16][S17].

## Positions
- **Taxes and revenue:** told Cascadia Daily News he would look for "revenue-positive opportunities" from growing the business community, citing clean energy [S15]. His website says government too often stands in the way of businesses with "outdated regulations, high taxes, lengthy permit times, and confusing rules" [S5]. No position on the 2026 income tax on income over $1 million found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Spending and education:** pledges to "champion increased funding for K-12 education and affordable early childhood education," affordable college tuition and apprenticeships [S8]; his statement says he will invest in Pre-K through postsecondary education and adult retraining [S1]. At a May 2026 WWU union town hall he said legislators must better communicate higher education's role as an economic driver [S13].
- **Housing and homelessness:** supports incentivizing workforce housing, "responsible density near transit" and cutting red tape to build housing faster [S4]; supports expanding homelessness prevention and addressing mental health and substance use [S7]. His campaign lists his support, as commissioner, for the Millworks low-income family housing development [S2].
- **Healthcare:** proposes that the state move to a self-insurance model for Medicare and Medicaid to remove the insurance middleman, modeled on the port's employee plan; supports lowering prescription drug prices and expanding affordable coverage options [S4][S15].
- **Public safety:** "I do NOT support 'defund the police'"; says law enforcement needs more resources [S6]. His statement says he strongly supports public safety, and he lists the Whatcom County sheriff among endorsers [S1].
- **Climate and environment:** says "Climate change is real, and we must continue to lead in reducing greenhouse gas emissions, as voters have mandated," while supporting energy and conservation policies "without loading extra costs on families" [S10]. Pledges to work with tribal governments on salmon recovery [S10].
- **Immigration and federal policy:** expressed concern about the "imposition" of Immigration and Customs Enforcement and said he was proud to vote to limit ICE operations on port property [S15]. His website frames the state as "our first line of defense" against federal cuts and policy [S9].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** describes himself as "100% pro-choice" [S1]; supports access to safe and legal abortion and birth control and protecting health data and providers of reproductive care [S11]. No position on LGBTQ+ policy or school sports found.
- **Parents and schools:** no position on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs found as of 2026-10-08. At the WWU town hall he said public-records requests have been "weaponized" against port staff, supported records-act reforms, and said faculty need protection to teach "topics that they care about, not just some whim of the current administration in D.C." [S13].

## Record
- Port of Bellingham commissioner, third term [S1][S2]. On June 9, 2026 he voted with Commissioner Carly James, in a 2-1 vote, for a resolution barring federal immigration agents from detaining people on private port property without a judicial warrant and barring civil immigration detention facilities or staging areas on port property; he also voted against tabling it [S14].
- His campaign lists port actions during his tenure including adopting a Climate Action Plan for net-zero carbon emissions by 2050, expanding open-access broadband into rural Whatcom County, reactivating the Bellingham Shipping Terminal, a sovereignty and permitting agreement with Lummi Nation for Squalicum Harbor, ending the Harcourt master developer contract, and the Blaine Harbor cleanup [S1][S2].
- No legislative votes; he has not held state office.
- PDC (updated 2026-10-07): $150,052.10 raised and $100,489.94 spent [S19].
