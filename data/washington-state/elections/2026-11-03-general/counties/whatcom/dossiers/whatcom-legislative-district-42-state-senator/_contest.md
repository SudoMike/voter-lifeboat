## What the office does
A Washington State Senator is the one senator elected from each of the 49 legislative districts to the State Senate, for a four-year term [S1]. Senators write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the two candidates who advanced from the August primary.

## Race dynamics
The seat is open: Democratic Sen. Sharon Shewmake announced in December 2025 that she would not seek another term, so neither candidate is an incumbent and neither has served in the Legislature [S2][S4]. The district covers north Bellingham and north and east Whatcom County and has alternated between Republican and Democratic senators; Republican Doug Ericksen held it from 2011 until his death in 2021, and Shewmake defeated appointed Republican Simon Sefzik in 2022 [S2].

Republican Erika Creydt, a clinical psychologist and Blaine School Board director, finished first in the August 4 primary with 22,125 votes [S4]. Second place required a mandatory hand recount: Democrats Michael Shepard and Eamonn Collins were separated by four votes after certification, and the hand count, held August 25-26, confirmed Shepard 13,125 to Collins 13,121; unaffiliated Ryan Bowman had 1,248 [S3][S4]. Shepard is a three-term Port of Bellingham commissioner and Western Washington University anthropology faculty member, endorsed by Shewmake [S1][S2]. He has said he would resign from the port if elected [S5].

Both candidates center affordability. Creydt calls for lower taxes and fewer regulations, accountability for state spending, and repeal of the 2026 tax on income over $1 million (erika-creydt.md S4, S5) [S2]. Shepard emphasizes economic development from his port tenure, a self-insurance model for Medicare and Medicaid, education investment, abortion rights and limits on federal immigration enforcement, and he voted for a port resolution restricting civil immigration detentions on port property [S1][S2] (michael-alvarez-shepard.md S14). As of early October, Creydt reported $265.8k raised and Shepard $150.1k (erika-creydt.md S19; michael-alvarez-shepard.md S19).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Creydt opposes the 2026 income tax on income over $1 million and calls for its repeal; no stated Shepard position on the income tax or I-645 was found.
2. **Spending:** Creydt's "spending problem" and program-accountability framing vs. Shepard's pledge to increase K-12 and early-learning funding.
3. **Immigration:** Shepard voted to limit federal civil immigration enforcement on port property; no Creydt position found.
4. **Social (reproductive policy):** Shepard calls himself "100% pro-choice"; no Creydt position found.
5. **Climate:** Shepard backs continued greenhouse-gas reduction and adopted a port net-zero plan; Creydt has no stated climate position beyond regulatory relief for farms and businesses.
6. **Record vs. renewal:** both hold local office (port commissioner vs. school board director); Shepard is endorsed by the retiring senator and Democratic legislative leaders, and Creydt frames her run as a challenge to current state policy.
