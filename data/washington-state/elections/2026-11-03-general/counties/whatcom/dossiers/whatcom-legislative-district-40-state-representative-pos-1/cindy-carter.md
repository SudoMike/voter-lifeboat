## Background
Cindy Carter, of Orcas Island (Eastsound), is the Republican candidate for the 40th Legislative District, Position 1; her campaign uses the name Cindy "No Income Tax" Carter [S1][S2][S7]. She grew up on a South Dakota farm, holds a BS in medical technology (ASCP-certified), worked five years in the chemistry laboratory at Virginia Mason in Seattle, and later held secretarial and office-management roles on Orcas Island, where she has lived for 39 years [S1][S5]. She homeschooled her four children and has tutored homeschool students [S1][S5]. She has served as a precinct committee officer, chair and state committeewoman of the San Juan County Republican Party and a four-year term on the Washington State Republican Party executive committee [S5]. Community service listed: the Buck Park Tennis Association and Buck Park Association, girls' softball coaching and leadership roles at Orcas Island Community Church [S1]. No elected public office is documented [S1][S5].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** her central pledge is to repeal the 2026 state income tax, which she calls unconstitutional; she criticizes her opponent's vote for it [S1][S2][S4]. Says Washington "doesn't have a revenue problem, it has a spending problem," that there is "no need to increase any taxes," and that she would look to decrease existing taxes, give small business a break and study Florida's approach to eliminating taxes [S3]. Her stated top budget priority is to cut taxes on working families by eliminating the state income tax and the "Carbon Emissions Tax" [S3].
- **Spending and accountability:** cites a state audit finding that $1.3 billion in child-care spending could not be fully audited and lost COVID funds, and asks "why give more money when the state demonstrates zero accountability" [S2][S3]. Lists eliminating fraud as a priority [S3]. Pledges to "bring fiscal responsibility back to the state" [S1].
- **Climate and energy:** proposes eliminating the "Carbon Emissions Tax" to lower gasoline and food prices [S3].
- **Health care:** under a "Universal Healthcare" heading, says "I support universal health" in the sense of addressing root causes of illness rather than relying on pharmaceuticals, then argues against a universal health-care system, citing Canadian wait times; favors catastrophic insurance plans, interstate competition among insurers, patient choice and more private medical practice [S3].
- **Public safety:** says the best way to improve public safety is "to get criminals off of the streets," with local, state and federal law enforcement working together; also lists drug treatment, laws that discourage drug use and enforcement against drug cartels [S3].
- **Immigration and federal cooperation:** says "coming into compliance with federal law" and working with federal authorities would improve public safety [S3]. On redistricting, favors counting only citizens for representation [S3].
- **Local control and regulation:** says one-size-fits-all water rules are damaging in the district and criticizes the state's water-rights adjudication approach; calls "overbearing regulation" the district's main challenge and wants to "roll back the restrictions" [S3]. Pledges to "put government back in the hands of the people" [S1].
- **Transportation and infrastructure:** prioritizes ferry and bridge repairs, highways and disaster-resilient roads [S1][S2].
- **Elections:** supports in-person voting with ID [S3].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no statement found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Parents and schools:** wants to restore Washington's education to "national excellence" [S1]. Her education page cites declining proficiency rankings and growth in non-teaching staff and says that "by decreasing administrative requirements, and removing government from our schools, we can return the education of our children to local schools" [S6]. No statement on parental notification, record access or opt-outs found.
