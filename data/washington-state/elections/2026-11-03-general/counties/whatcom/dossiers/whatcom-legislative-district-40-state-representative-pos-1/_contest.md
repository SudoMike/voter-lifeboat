## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, ferries, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The 40th District runs from south Bellingham to a northwest portion of Skagit County and includes all of San Juan County [S2]. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the two candidates who advanced from the August primary.

## Race dynamics
Democratic incumbent Debra Lekanoff, completing eight years in the House and vice chair of the Health Care and Wellness Committee, faces Republican Cindy Carter of Orcas Island, a former hospital laboratory technologist and San Juan County Republican Party officer with no documented elected office [S1] (debra-lekanoff.md S2; cindy-carter.md S5).

Carter's campaign is built around repealing the 2026 tax on income over $1 million: her campaign name is "No Income Tax Carter," and her statement attacks Lekanoff's vote for the tax [S1] (cindy-carter.md S2, S4). Lekanoff voted for ESSB 6346, the 2025 business and occupation and services tax bills, ESHB 1296 and the 2025 rent-increase limit, which she co-sponsored (debra-lekanoff.md S3-S7). Carter also argues the budget has a spending and accountability problem, would eliminate the carbon tax and opposes a universal health-care system (cindy-carter.md S3). Lekanoff's statement stresses ferries, salmon and the Salish Sea, unions, reproductive freedom and the fentanyl and homelessness crises [S1]. Neither candidate has stated a position on I-645, I-1 or I-638 as of 2026-10-08. Lekanoff reports $77.3k raised to Carter's $27.9k (debra-lekanoff.md S10; cindy-carter.md S7).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Lekanoff's yes votes on the 2026 income tax and 2025 tax bills vs. Carter's pledge to repeal the income tax and cut taxes.
2. **Spending and accountability:** Carter's audit-and-restraint platform vs. Lekanoff's program-investment priorities.
3. **Record vs. renewal:** eight-year incumbent vs. a challenger who has not held elected office.
4. **Climate and energy:** Carter would eliminate the carbon tax; Lekanoff's platform centers environmental protection and green building.
5. **Parents and schools / social:** Lekanoff voted for ESHB 1296 and backs reproductive freedom; Carter has no stated position on either topic.
