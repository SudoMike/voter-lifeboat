## Background
Debra Lekanoff is the incumbent Democratic State Representative for the 40th Legislative District, Position 1, finishing her eighth year in the House [S1][S2]. She is vice chair of the House Health Care and Wellness Committee and sits on the Housing, Appropriations and Rules committees, the Joint Legislative Committee on Water Supply During Drought, the Pacific Fisheries Legislative Task Force and the Task Force for Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women and People [S1][S2]. Her statement lists more than 30 years of public-service work in health care, salmon recovery, climate, infrastructure, water, treaty rights, education, housing, workforce and unions, and a BA in business administration and finance from Central Washington University [S1]. She is a member of the National Caucus of Environmental Legislators and a past board member of Earthjustice [S1].

## Positions
- **Taxes and budget:** lists balanced budgets and affordability among her priorities [S1]. Voted for the 2026 tax on income over $1 million and the 2025 business and occupation and services tax bills (see Record) [S3][S5][S6]. No separate public statement on the income tax found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Housing:** lists housing among her priorities [S1]; her website calls for "affordable housing for all," support for middle housing and low-income housing, and state support for city and county growth-management plans on zoning and infrastructure [S8]. Co-sponsored and voted for EHB 1217, the 2025 limit on residential rent and fee increases [S7].
- **Climate and environment:** pledges to protect salmon, forests, parks and the Salish Sea [S1]. Her website calls for green-building standards for new construction, incentives for retrofits and renewable energy, statewide water-efficiency standards and planning that addresses climate change [S8].
- **Public safety, addiction and homelessness:** pledges to address the fentanyl and homelessness crises [S1]. Her website proposes prevention, recovery and aftercare investments; a requirement that people receiving state-funded homelessness services enroll in recovery and wraparound services; a minimum 180-day implementation period for new public-safety laws; and training scholarships for first responders [S8]. The website also says she "invests in laws for gun control, and safe schools" [S8].
- **Health care:** lists health care as a priority [S1]; her website says she is committed to "healthcare for all" without naming a mechanism [S8].
- **Labor and transportation:** supports unions and jobs [S1]; says she helped fund three new state ferries and accelerated ferry hiring [S1]. Co-sponsored the 2026 bill letting a county create a ferry taxing district, which Whatcom County sought for the Lummi Island ferry [S9].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** pledges to defend "reproductive freedom" [S1]; her website lists "the right to choose" [S8]. No statement on LGBTQ+ policy or school sports found.
- **Parents and schools:** her website proposes free school meals and tutoring for all students and treating sports, music, art and physical, social and wellness programs as basic education [S8]. Voted for ESHB 1296 (see Record) [S4]. No statement on parental notification, record access or opt-outs found.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted for ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S3].
- **I-1 (parental rights):** no statement found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted for ESHB 1296 (2025), the amendments to the I-2081 parental-rights law that I-1 would undo [S4].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no statement found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- Voted yea on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million (2026-03-09, passed 51-46) [S3].
- Voted yea on House final passage of EHB 2081, business and occupation tax increases (2025-04-22, passed 50-48) [S5], and on ESSB 5814, excise taxes on select services (2025-04-23, passed 50-47) [S6].
- Voted yea on ESHB 1296 ("promoting a safe and supportive public education system") on House passage (2025-03-12, 56-37) and on final passage as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24, 59-39) [S4].
- Secondary sponsor of HB 1217 and voted yea on final passage of EHB 1217 as recommended by the conference committee (2025-04-27, 54-44) [S7].
- Co-sponsor, with Reps. Joe Timmons and Alex Ramel, of the 2026 county ferry district bill, which lets a county council levy up to 10 cents per $1,000 of assessed value without a public vote [S9].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $77,274.32 raised and $27,136.81 spent [S10].

## Endorsements
Gov. Bob Ferguson, the Washington State Labor Council, Lummi Nation, Washington Conservation Action, the Washington State Nurses Association and Whatcom County Firefighters [S1].
