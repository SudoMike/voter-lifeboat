## What it does

Proposition 2026-07 is a proposed amendment to the Bellingham city charter about how the city signs contracts [S1]. Today the charter requires the mayor to sign "all written contracts, bonds and instruments of every kind and description to which the City shall be a party," with the finance director attesting the signature and affixing the city seal [S1]. The amendment would [S1]:
- let the mayor name a designee to sign contracts in the city's name;
- remove the requirement that the finance director attest the mayor's signature and affix the seal;
- allow electronic signatures and a paperless contract process.

The mayor would remain responsible for seeing that city contracts "are faithfully kept and performed" [S1].

CDN reports that because city charter amendments are rare, the city put this proposal on the same ballot as the mayor's-salary amendment (Proposition 2026-06) [S2].

## Cost

- No tax or rate [S1]. The voters' guide has no fiscal statement [S1].
- The explanatory statement says a paperless process "would improve efficiency, reduce costs, and support environmental sustainability," but gives no dollar figure [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

The explanatory statement says the current wet-ink rules require "sequential signing, attestation of a prior signature, and physical attachment of the city seal," which complicates electronic signing [S1]. The statement for describes the current process: each contract is printed, mailed out and back, and carried to "at least four individual hand signatures from city officials," with the finance director signing last and crimping the seal before the document is scanned [S1].

## Arguments for

The statement for (committee: Kim Lund and Edwin "Skip" Williams) says [S1]:
- The charter prevents the city from using electronic contracts and signatures.
- Electronic signing is faster and "more secure, since digital signatures are verified through multiple factors."
- The current process "adds time and cost to every contract without adding value or accountability."

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed: "No person in the jurisdiction contacted the Auditor's Office to provide a statement" [S1]. No committee against was formed [S1]. No published opposition was found.

The amendment does move signing authority: a mayor's designee could sign contracts, and the finance director's attestation step would end [S1]. No source found discusses this change beyond the voters' guide.
