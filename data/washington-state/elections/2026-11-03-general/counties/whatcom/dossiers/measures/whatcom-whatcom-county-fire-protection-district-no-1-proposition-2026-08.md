## What it does

Proposition 2026-08 is a multi-year levy lid lift for Whatcom County Fire Protection District 1's regular property tax levy [S1]. It would [S1]:
- set the district's regular levy for collection in 2027 at no more than $1.48 per $1,000 of assessed value;
- allow the levy to grow up to 6% a year (a limit factor of 106%) for the following nine years;
- make the final year's maximum levy the base for later limits under chapter 84.55 RCW. After the ten years, without another vote, the levy returns to the 1% limit.

The board adopted Resolution No. 2026-07 to place it on the ballot [S1]. The explanatory statement says the measure "will replace the final year of the levy lid lift approved by the voters in 2021" [S1]. Senior, veteran and other exemptions under chapter 84.36 RCW apply [S1].

The district serves Everson, Nooksack, Deming, Nugents Corner and the Nooksack Indian Tribe [S4][S7]. The statement for gives 75 square miles, 12,700 residents and about 1,100 calls a year, about 800 of them EMS [S1]; CDN gave about 106 square miles in July 2026 [S4]. Brian Atkins became fire chief on June 1, 2026, replacing Rachel Carlson [S10].

## Why it is on the ballot again

**The same measure failed in August.** In the August 4, 2026 primary, the district asked for Proposition 2026-02 (Resolution No. 2026-03) [S2]. Its ballot title and explanatory statement match this measure's word for word except for the resolution number: $1.48 per $1,000, a 106% limit factor for nine years, replacing the final year of the 2021 lid lift [S1][S2]. In the primary voters' guide, no statement for or against was filed and no committee formed on either side [S2].

Official results: 1,620 approved and 1,744 rejected [S3], or 48.2% yes (computed for this dossier). The Lynden Tribune reported that 124 votes separated the measure from approval [S7]. The two other Whatcom fire district lid lifts on the same ballot, Glacier Fire & Rescue (District 19) and North Whatcom Fire & Rescue (District 21), passed [S3][S5]. CDN's August 12 story about a lid lift that "gets a yes" after almost 20 years concerns North Whatcom Fire & Rescue (District 21), not District 1 [S6].

**What changed for November, per the district** [S7]:
- Chief Atkins said his impression was not that voters doubted the need but that "times are tight and, financially, it will be just one more burden."
- He said many residents are new to the area and that some people believe the department has more staff than it does.
- The district plans more resident education, a refined social media campaign and an educational mailer. Because the district cannot ask for a yes vote, it is relying on the firefighters' labor group to campaign.
- Unlike the primary, this time a statement for was filed and a committee for formed (Anne Isenhart) [S1][S2].

**Earlier requests.** In 2024 the district twice asked voters for a $13 million, 20-year bond (about $0.39 per $1,000) to build a new station on Everson Goshen Road, because the Everson station sits on a floodplain and lacks sleeping quarters [S8][S9]. The bond needed 60% and received 50.21% in August 2024 [S9]; CDN reports it failed both times in 2024 [S5].

## Cost

- The district's statement for says the current rate is $1.12 per $1,000 [S1]; the Lynden Tribune and CDN also report about $1.12 [S4][S7]. The measure sets $1.48 for 2027 [S1].
- The increase is $0.36 per $1,000: about $180 a year on a $500,000 property, for a total district regular levy of about $740 (computed for this dossier from [S1]). No district estimate per home was found.
- After 2027, the levy amount may grow up to 6% a year for nine years [S1]. State law caps fire district regular levies at $1.50 per $1,000 [S1][S7].
- The $1.48 rate is higher than the $1.40 rate voters approved in 2021 [S1][S4].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

Fire district revenue from the regular levy can grow only 1% a year without voter approval, so the rate falls as property values rise [S1][S4]. The district says its rate has dropped from the $1.40 approved in 2021 to $1.12 [S1]. Atkins told the Lynden Tribune that much of the district's property was devalued after the last flood, reducing levy revenue [S7].

This measure replaces the final collection year of the 2021 lid lift and starts a new ten-year schedule [S1]. It raises the rate rather than renewing it at the current level [S1].

**What the money would pay for, per the district** [S1][S7]:
- Maintaining staffing, equipment and facilities against rising costs.
- Vehicle repair and maintenance to extend the life of apparatus.
- Keeping recently added positions, many of them funded by three-year grants. Atkins said several positions would have to be cut without the levy.

Atkins said $1.48 is "about as low as the department can go" without returning to voters again in a couple of years [S7].

## Arguments for

The statement for (committee: Anne Isenhart) says [S1]:
- The district has 10 full-time and 3 part-time paid employees, 8 active volunteers and 9 newly graduated volunteers.
- Revenue growth is limited to 1% a year while inflation, call volume and demand rise faster.
- "A levy lid lift is not a new tax. It restores or adjusts the existing fire district levy rate."
- The district "has done more with less for years" and periodically asks voters to reset the rate.

Lt. Nikki Campbell told the Lynden Tribune: "I want the community to know we're there" [S7].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed: "No person in the jurisdiction contacted the Auditor's Office to provide a statement" [S1]. No committee against was formed [S1], and the PDC query found no committee for or against [S11].
- Voters rejected the identical measure in August, 1,744 to 1,620 [S3].
- Atkins said the burden of another tax in tight times was the reason he heard for the August result [S7].
- For comparison (not an argument made by any source found): the new rate is above the 2021 voter-approved $1.40 [S1][S4], and the 6% annual growth allowance is larger than the 3% North Whatcom's August lid lift allows [S1][S6].

The district calls it a restoration and "not a new tax" [S1], but the new rate is above both the current rate and the 2021 voter-approved rate. Larger tax: +2.
