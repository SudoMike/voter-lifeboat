## What the office does

The Port of Bellingham is a county-wide port district [S5]. The commission sets policy for its economic-development work and assets, including the Bellingham Shipping Terminal, Bellingham International Airport, the Fairhaven cruise terminal and the downtown Bellingham waterfront [S10]. Voters expanded the commission from three to five members on Nov. 4, 2025, 65.16% to 34.84% [S8]. Each commissioner is paid just under $63,000 a year [S7]. District 5 is east Whatcom County, from Everson and Sumas to the Skagit County line. It matches County Council District 3; the numbers differ because state law requires the three sitting commissioners to hold port districts 1 through 3 [S9][S10][S4].

## Who votes and how long the term is

- **County-wide general.** Only District 5 voters took part in the August primary. In November every voter in the port district, which is all of Whatcom County, elects the District 5 commissioner [S3][S5][S6].
- **Term length depends on the vote count.** VoteWA lists both new seats as "1 Year Term" [S1][S2]. Under RCW 53.12.130, the new commissioner (District 4 or District 5) who receives more votes in this even-year election gets a three-year term and the other gets a one-year term, counted from Jan. 1, 2027. The port's FAQ and CDN describe the same rule, which puts both seats on the odd-year cycle of the other commissioners [S4][S6][S7][S8]. So the District 5 winner will serve through 2027 or through 2029, depending on how their vote total compares with the District 4 winner's. No source found explains why VoteWA shows "1 Year Term" for both seats.
- **Taking office.** The new commissioners take office as soon as the Auditor certifies the results and they take the oath [S4][S6].

## Race dynamics

- **Primary.** Six candidates ran. In the returns CDN reported, Chris Elder had 41.4% and Shawn Ensley 21.4% [S11]. The results were certified Aug. 18 [S13]. CDN describes Elder as "Democratic-endorsed" [S12]. The port office is nonpartisan, and neither candidate lists a party preference [S2].
- **Backgrounds.** Elder, 44, of Acme, is a farmer and natural-resources planner. He worked on farmland preservation and watershed planning at Whatcom County, now manages King County's farmland-preservation program, and owns a natural-resource consulting firm (chris-elder.md S1, S2, S5). Ensley, 48, of Deming, is a 21-year Air Force Reserve veteran in airport logistics and an instrumentation and electrical shop foreman at the BP Cherry Point refinery (shawn-ensley.md S1, S2, S4).
- **Messages.** Elder pairs economic development in working lands, trade and the airport with habitat restoration, cleanup of legacy contamination and climate resilience. He also calls for port investment in east-county communities (chris-elder.md S1, S3, S6). Ensley focuses on the port's mission statement, living-wage jobs, keeping the waterfront "working," air service and responsible development (shawn-ensley.md S1, S3, S5).
- **Endorsements.** Elder lists Sen. Sharon Shewmake, Reps. Alex Ramel and Joe Timmons, five County Council members, all three PUD commissioners, the Whatcom and 42nd District Democrats, the Sierra Club, the Lummi Nation, Firefighters Local 106 and Teamsters Local 231 (chris-elder.md S4). Ensley's endorsement page shows only logo images, which the page's alt text identifies as the Whatcom County Association of Realtors and BUILD PAC (shawn-ensley.md S7).
- **Money.** The PDC lists about $11,000 raised for Elder and about $8,500 for Ensley (chris-elder.md S9; shawn-ensley.md S8).

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

- **Emphasis.** Elder links port economic development to agriculture, forestry, salmon habitat and climate resilience, and to services for flood-affected east-county towns. Ensley frames the port around its mission statement, industrial jobs and transportation gateways [S1].
- **Background.** Elder: 12 years in county government and natural-resource planning. Ensley: refinery trades and military logistics. Both are first-time candidates [S1].
- **Coalitions.** Elder's endorsers are mostly Democratic officials and party, environmental and labor groups. Ensley's endorsement page shows real-estate and building-industry logos (chris-elder.md S4; shawn-ensley.md S7).
