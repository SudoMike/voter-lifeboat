## Background

Chris Elder, 44, of Acme, is running for office for the first time [S1][S5]. He moved to the South Fork Valley to start a farm and has lived in Whatcom County for 20 years [S1][S6]. He holds a BA in environmental biology from Western Colorado University and an MS in agriculture from Washington State University [S1]. At Whatcom County he administered the farmland-preservation (purchase of development rights) program in Planning and Development Services and later worked as senior watershed management planner in Public Works [S2][S6]. He now manages King County's Farmland Preservation Program [S2][S6]. He has owned the natural-resource consulting firm Rivershed SPC for six years and has taught for WSU Extension and as adjunct faculty at Western Washington University [S1][S2]. He co-founded and chaired the Twin Sisters Market and the Coastal Farmland Trust, is administrative chair of the Washington State Food Policy Forum, and volunteered in Nooksack River flood response [S1][S2]. He says he supported the preservation of about 1,000 acres of farmland and the restoration of more than 300 acres of watershed habitat [S6].

He placed first of six in the August primary, with 41.4% in the returns CDN reported [S8]. CDN describes him as "Democratic-endorsed"; the office is nonpartisan [S7].

Identity note: the PDC lists this candidate as Christopher Carson Elder of Acme. A different Christopher Michael Elder is a Republican candidate in the 44th Legislative District [S9].

## Positions

- **Working lands and rural investment.** Wants the port to invest in agriculture, forestry, Nooksack tribal communities and businesses, and flood-affected towns such as Sumas, Nooksack, Everson, Deming and Acme [S6]. After the primary he said there are "a lot of needs out in Kendall, Maple Falls and Deming" [S7]. His website calls for "meaningful representation" for rural communities [S3].
- **Shipping terminal and fisheries.** Sees the shipping terminal's location between Seattle and Vancouver as a reason to use its light-industrial zone. Wants better fisheries infrastructure and value-added processing for fishers [S5]. Supports increasing trade at the shipping terminal and airport [S1][S6].
- **Waterfront.** Wants to pair the working waterfront with public access [S5][S6].
- **Environment.** Pledges to keep supporting cleanup of legacy contamination and to "hold new development to the highest standards as we face rising seas and weather extremes." Calls environmental stewardship "essential" to economic growth and supports salmon-habitat restoration [S1].
- **Public needs.** Names affordable food, affordable housing, transit and trails, and living-wage jobs as underserved needs [S6].
- **Port policy.** Supports the work to reactivate the shipping terminal and the port's role as lead of the Regional Economic Partnership [S6]. Supported expanding the commission to five members [S3][S6].
- **Transparency.** Says he will push his priorities "with transparency and accountability" [S1].

## Record

No elected office [S1]. Public-employment record as Whatcom County farmland-preservation administrator and watershed planner, and now King County farmland-preservation manager [S2][S6]. He says he has secured over $3.3 million in state and federal funding for community priorities [S2]. He served on the Whatcom Forest Resilience Task Force, the Climate Impact Advisory Committee and the Agricultural Advisory Committee [S6].
