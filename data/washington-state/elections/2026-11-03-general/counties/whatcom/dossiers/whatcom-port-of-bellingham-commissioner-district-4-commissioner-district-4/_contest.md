## What the office does

The Port of Bellingham is a county-wide port district: the state's port-district layer has a single Whatcom feature, and point queries from Point Roberts to Newhalem all fall inside it [S5]. The commission sets policy for the port's economic-development work and its assets, among them the Bellingham Shipping Terminal, Bellingham International Airport and the downtown Bellingham waterfront [S10]. Voters approved expanding the commission from three members to five on Nov. 4, 2025, 65.16% to 34.84% [S8]. The port estimated the two added seats cost about $126,000 a year, with each commissioner paid just under $63,000 [S7]. In December 2025 the commission adopted a five-district map that mirrors the County Council map, except that districts 3 and 5 swap numbers. State law requires the three sitting commissioners to hold districts 1 through 3 [S9][S4]. District 4 matches County Council District 4: part of northwest Bellingham, parts of Ferndale, and north to Lynden [S10].

## Who votes and how long the term is

- **County-wide general.** Only District 4 voters took part in the August primary. In November every voter in the port district, which is all of Whatcom County, elects the District 4 commissioner [S3][S5][S6].
- **Term length depends on the vote count.** VoteWA lists both new seats (District 4 and District 5) as "1 Year Term" [S1][S2]. Under RCW 53.12.130, when the two added seats are first filled in an even-numbered year in a port with four-year terms, the new commissioner who receives more votes gets a three-year term and the other gets a one-year term, counted from Jan. 1, 2027 [S4]. The port's FAQ and CDN's reporting say the same thing: the higher vote-getter of the two winners serves three years and the other one year, which puts both seats on the odd-year cycle of the other three commissioners [S6][S7][S8]. So the District 4 winner will serve either through 2027 or through 2029, depending on whether they draw more votes than the District 5 winner. No source found explains why VoteWA shows "1 Year Term" for both seats.
- **Taking office.** The new commissioners take office as soon as the Auditor certifies the results and they take the oath [S4][S6].
- **A note on the port's FAQ.** The port's FAQ page gives the approval date as Nov. 3, 2026, and names District 3 instead of District 4 for the primary. CDN reports that the vote was on Nov. 4, 2025 [S6][S8].

## Race dynamics

- **Primary.** Six candidates ran [S10]. In the returns CDN reported after election week, Paul Burrill led with 30.2% and Tor Benson was second with 24.2%. Samantha Cruz-Mendoza, who had an endorsement from Sen. Sharon Shewmake, finished third at 19.8% [S11][S12]. The results were certified Aug. 18 [S13].
- **Two maritime backgrounds.** CDN describes both finalists as fishermen [S12]. Burrill, 52, of Lynden, has spent 30-plus years in commercial fishing, is a seafood-company shareholder, owns an interstate trucking company, and has chaired the port's Marine Advisory Committee since 2018 (paul-burrill.md S1, S2). Benson, 36, of Bellingham, is a Certified Financial Planner and runs a financial-planning firm. He skippered Alaska fishing boats for about a decade and now coordinates cargo and tanker port calls in Puget Sound (tor-benson.md S1, S4, S6).
- **Messages.** Burrill stresses the working waterfront, keeping some waterfront land industrial, airport cargo (including lengthening the runway), local contractors, and a heavy-industry advisory committee [S10] (paul-burrill.md S1, S5, S6). Benson stresses airport finances and air service, a 5% airport marketing budget, public communication after the ABC Recycling episode, and a review of port-owned property (tor-benson.md S1, S5, S6, S7).
- **Endorsements.** Burrill lists Port Commissioner Bobby Briscoe, two former port commissioners, the Whatcom County Association of Realtors, Firefighters Local 106 and AGC Washington (paul-burrill.md S3). Benson lists the Whatcom Commercial Fishermen's Association and the Nooksack Indian Tribe, plus individual endorsers listed by title (tor-benson.md S8).
- **Money.** The two campaigns have raised similar amounts: about $12,900 for Burrill and about $13,100 for Benson, per the PDC (paul-burrill.md S7; tor-benson.md S9).

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

- **Emphasis.** Burrill puts seafood, cold storage, trucking and keeping industrial land industrial first. Benson puts airport finances, financial planning and public communication first. Both name family-wage jobs as the core of the port's mission [S1].
- **Background.** Burrill: decades of port advisory service and business ownership. Benson: finance, ship logistics, and a past seat on a planning and zoning board in Alaska [S1].
