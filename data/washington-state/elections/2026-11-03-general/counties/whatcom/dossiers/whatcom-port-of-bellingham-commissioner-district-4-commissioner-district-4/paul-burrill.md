## Background

Paul Burrill, 52, of Lynden, is running for office for the first time [S1][S2]. He reports more than 30 years as a commercial fisherman, is a shareholder in a Washington-based seafood company, and owns a small interstate trucking company that hauls seafood and local farm products [S1][S3]. He says he has been self-employed since 18 [S6]. He has chaired the Port of Bellingham's advisory committee for the marina fleet since 2018; his statement calls it the Marine Advisory Committee and his website and questionnaire call it the Marina Advisory Committee [S1][S3][S6]. He lists board seats on the county Business and Commerce Committee, the Purse Seine Vessel Owners Association and Whatcom Family Farmers, and past service on the Whale Entanglement Task Force and crab advisory and management committees [S1][S5]. He says he would step down as advisory committee chair if elected, because the committee serves at the commissioners' pleasure [S5]. CDN calls him a "former-fisherman-turned-seafood-logistics-businessman" [S2].

He placed first of six in the August primary, with 30.2% in the returns CDN reported after election week [S8].

## Positions

- **Working waterfront and land use.** Says some waterfront land, such as the Whatcom Waterway and parts of Fairhaven, should stay permanently industrial: "If we lose our industry here and it's converted to housing, we're never getting that back" [S5]. His website lists support for maritime trades, agriculture, aviation and small businesses [S3].
- **Industry the port already has.** Wants the port to build on seafood, cold storage, trucking and Alaska-linked logistics, possibly through public-private partnerships in which private firms build facilities on long-term leases [S5]. Says Whatcom should try to win pieces of shipbuilding work such as modular components for state ferries [S5].
- **Airport.** Thinks lengthening the runway at Bellingham International Airport could open cargo traffic, including seasonal seafood freight from Alaska [S2].
- **Public process.** Proposes a heavy-industry advisory committee to vet industrial proposals early, after the ABC Recycling dispute. Says advisory committees are not always taken seriously [S5]. He told Bellingham Metro News that some industrial companies see the port as "closed for business"; the outlet notes this is his characterization [S5].
- **Contracting.** Wants qualified local contractors to have "every opportunity to compete" for major port construction projects [S6].
- **Jobs and community needs.** His statement says port industries can create family-wage jobs while supporting priorities such as affordable housing and childcare. It also backs "protecting longshore jobs by limiting the use of AI in port operations" [S1]. He names affordable housing and childcare as underserved needs that the port can address indirectly through jobs [S6].
- **Public access and workforce.** Pledges better public access and upland storage for recreational users, and workforce development in marine trades, aviation and first-responder services [S1].
- **Federal presence at port facilities.** Asked about ICE and Border Patrol use of port facilities, he tied the federal presence to the airport and port of entry and said, "Let's have that conversation" [S5].
- **Commission expansion.** Supported the expansion to five commissioners, saying more districts give more residents a voice [S5].
