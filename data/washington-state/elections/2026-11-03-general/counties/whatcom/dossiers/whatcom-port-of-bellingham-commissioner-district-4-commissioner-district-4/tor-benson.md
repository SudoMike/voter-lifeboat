## Background

Tor Benson, 36, of Bellingham, is a Certified Financial Planner and founder of Streamline Advisory, a local financial-planning firm [S1][S2][S4]. He was born in Petersburg, Alaska, grew up near Olympia, and graduated from Oregon State University with degrees in biology and education [S1][S4][S6]. He says he owned and skippered commercial fishing boats in Alaska for about ten years (his statement and questionnaire put his fishing experience at 15 years) [S1][S4][S7]. He also prepared tax returns for a CPA for three seasons [S4]. He works as a logistics manager for GAC North America, coordinating tanker and cargo-ship port calls in Puget Sound, including at local refineries and the Bellingham Shipping Terminal [S4][S6]. Asked about a possible conflict of interest, he told Bellingham Metro News he is a third party who does not award contracts and would declare any issue that came up [S6].

His prior public service is four years on the Petersburg, Alaska, Planning and Zoning Committee from 2016, which his statement lists as elected, and an appointed one-year term on the Southeast Alaska Power Agency board [S1][S4]. He is treasurer of the Bellingham Bay Rotary Club and has been a member of the Whatcom Working Waterfront Coalition since 2024 [S1][S4].

He placed second of six in the August primary, with 24.2% in the returns CDN reported after election week [S10].

## Positions

- **Jobs and finances.** Calls family-wage jobs his top priority. Says the airport and shipping terminal are budgeted to run at a loss for a third straight year and pledges to spend tax dollars "responsibly and with transparency" [S1].
- **Airport.** Supports spending 5% of the airport's gross revenue on marketing, retiming Seattle connections, bringing back a Denver route and seeking seasonal Alaska and Hawaii flights [S5][S6][S7]. Wants the airport's planned month-long runway closure in July 2027 used to relaunch it with new routes [S5].
- **Moorage rates.** Disagrees with how the port set recreational moorage rates by benchmarking to other harbors. He calls that "arbitrary" and says rates should be worked back from the money the port needs [S6].
- **Shipping terminal and communication.** Says ABC Recycling was "neither a good fit nor an ideal partner" and that it eroded community trust [S2][S3]. Supported the port's hiring of a communications director [S5][S7].
- **Waterfront.** Proposes a quarterly "trackside advisory committee" for the Container Village and pump track area and a public process to rename it [S5]. Wants waterfront planning that includes housing, industry, agriculture, recreation, commerce and Lummi and Nooksack fishermen [S1].
- **Port property.** Proposes a review of port-owned property to buy or sell parcels, including reviewing port-owned lots on Cliffside Drive near the airport for possible return to residential use [S5].
- **Agriculture.** Wants the port to keep exploring agricultural projects such as the Boxx Berry Farm research site [S5][S6].
- **District numbering and name.** Wants port district numbers to match County Council district numbers. Says he would consider renaming the port the "Port of Whatcom" [S3][S5].
