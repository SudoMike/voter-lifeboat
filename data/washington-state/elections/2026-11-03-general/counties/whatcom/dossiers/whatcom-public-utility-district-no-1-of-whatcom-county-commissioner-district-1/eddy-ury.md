## Background

Eddy Ury is the incumbent District 1 commissioner and the Whatcom PUD commission's 2026 president [S1][S2]. He won a 2024 special election for the remaining two years of the District 1 term, defeating Jeremiah Goggins with 59.3% in CDN's late count; his term expires Dec. 31, 2026 [S2][S4]. He has lived in Bellingham for 15 years [S2]. He majored in sociology at Western Washington University and took post-graduate courses in energy law and policy [S1]. He reports a decade in the nonprofit sector on sustainability, development and energy policy [S1]. In 2024 he was climate and energy policy manager at the Bellingham nonprofit RE Sources [S3]. The Northwest Energy Coalition gave him a "4 under Forty" award in 2019 [S2]. He is vice-chair of the Whatcom County Climate Impact Advisory Committee and sits on the board of North Coast Credit Union [S1].

His campaign website, ury4pud.org, showed only a "Private Site" page on 2026-10-08 [S9].

## Positions

- **No taxes.** Says the PUD's capital program is going ahead "without levying any taxes, and I am committed to keeping it that way" [S1].
- **Infrastructure and growth.** Points to rapidly growing demand, capacity constraints and development pressure. Pledges to "improve and expand core services while protecting and restoring natural resources" [S1].
- **Clean electricity.** Backs replacing fossil-fuel generation with clean electricity through new transmission lines and a substation [S1]. In 2024 he said he would work to advance clean energy, support the economy and improve broadband access [S3][S4].
- **Water supply.** Pledges to "sustain freshwater supplies for farms, fisheries, industries, and every household" [S1]. In 2024 he cited the Nooksack water-rights adjudication and climate change as challenges for water planning [S3].

## Record

- PUD commissioner since his 2024 election; 2026 commission president [S2][S4].
- Says that in his first two years the commission approved large capital projects to engineer and build a non-potable water plant, power transmission lines and a substation [S1].
- In February 2026 the commission he presides over signed a memorandum of understanding with the Nooksack Indian Tribe to explore geothermal power near Mount Baker [S2][S7].
- Before taking office, he says he helped bring Cherry Point industrial stakeholders and environmental advocates to agreement on the county's Cherry Point development rules [S3].
- He is listed among the endorsers of Port of Bellingham District 5 candidate Chris Elder [S8].
