## What the office does

Public Utility District No. 1 of Whatcom County (Whatcom PUD) is governed by three commissioners elected on a nonpartisan basis. One seat is on the ballot every two years, for a six-year term [S5]. The commission sets PUD policy and rates, adopts system plans for the electric and water utilities, approves revenue obligations and appoints the general manager, who runs the PUD with 24 employees [S5]. The PUD was founded in 1937, holds water rights to 53 million gallons per day, and serves one external electric customer, the Phillips 66 refinery at Cherry Point, along with its own water treatment plants [S8][S9]. It does not collect taxes and is funded by its utility customers [S8]. It has partnered with the Port of Bellingham and Whatcom County on broadband for underserved areas [S9]. In February 2026 the commission signed a memorandum of understanding with the Nooksack Indian Tribe to explore geothermal power near Mount Baker [S14].

## Who votes

- **County-wide PUD.** The PUD's boundary is all of Whatcom County: the state's PUD layer has a single Whatcom feature, and point queries from Point Roberts to Newhalem all fall inside it [S4].
- **Every PUD voter votes in the general.** Under RCW 54.12.010(3), only district voters nominate in a primary, but voters of the entire PUD elect each commissioner in the general [S3]. The PUD describes its commissioners as elected "by Whatcom County residents" [S5]. In 2024, CDN described District 1 as spanning the southeast half of the county [S8][S10].
- **No primary this year.** Only two candidates filed, so the race went straight to the general [S11][S12][S13].

## Race dynamics

- **Incumbent.** Eddy Ury won a 2024 special election, with 59.3% in CDN's late count, for the remaining two years of the District 1 term. Todd Lagestee had held the seat since May 2024, when he was appointed to complete Christine Grant's term [S8][S10]. Ury is the commission's 2026 president, and his term expires Dec. 31, 2026 [S6][S7]. He is now running for a full six-year term [S1][S2].
- **Challenger.** Frank Imhof filed on the last day of filing week, May 8, 2026 [S2]. He submitted no voters'-guide statement and lists no website [S1]. The PDC shows his campaign as "Candidate discontinued campaign" as of May 15, 2026, with no reports filed [S13]. That came after the May 11 deadline to withdraw from the ballot [S11]. VoteWA still lists him as an active candidate on the general ballot [S2]. No public announcement explaining the PDC status was found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Campaign activity.** Ury registered under the PDC's "mini" reporting option and had filed no reports [S12]. No news coverage of the 2026 race was found beyond candidate-filing lists as of 2026-10-08 [S11].

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

- **Record vs. unknown.** Ury has two years on the commission and a background in nonprofit clean-energy policy. He pledges to keep the PUD from levying taxes and to expand services while protecting natural resources [S1][S6][S9]. No public positions were found for Imhof [S1][S13].
