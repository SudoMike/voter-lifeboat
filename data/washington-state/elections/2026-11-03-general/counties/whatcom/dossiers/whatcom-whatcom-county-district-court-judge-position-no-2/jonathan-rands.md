## Background
- Jonathan Rands was elected to District Court Position 2 in 2022 and took office in January 2023; he is the court's presiding judge [S1][S2][S6].
- Before the bench he spent 20 years as a trial attorney and small-business owner, representing clients in five counties and more than 20 courts and trying over 200 cases to verdict [S1][S2]. His practice focused largely on DUI defense [S4].
- J.D. from Seattle University; B.A. in criminal justice from Simon Fraser University [S1].
- In 2022 he won the open seat with 62.25% of the preliminary count over Deputy Prosecuting Attorney Gordon Jenkins, after leading a three-way primary with 42.3%; he campaigned on adopting a therapeutic community court for low-level misdemeanors [S3].
- Lists a five-year term as invited faculty at the state Judicial College, membership on therapeutic courts and court rules committees, and founding District Court's first Youth Court [S1].

## Positions
- Says he has modernized local rules and expanded remote court access [S1]. With Superior Court Presiding Judge David Freeman, he opened Zoom viewing of court hearings to members of the public in 2026, which both judges said was consistent with state law and court rules [S6].
- Says each District Court case, from traffic infractions to DUI, drug and domestic-violence cases to small claims, deserves careful attention and "practical, problem-solving approaches within the rule of law" [S2].

## Record
- In January 2023, days after he took office, the county prosecutor's office filed 123 affidavits of prejudice seeking to remove him from DUI cases, citing his history with "Kitsap motions" on breath-test evidence as a defense attorney and comments he allegedly made about rehearing such a motion. In a January 18 order Rands found 50 of the affidavits untimely and did not recuse, saying he could agree not to hear a Kitsap motion but should not be barred from the cases entirely [S4][S5].
- On February 13, 2023, Superior Court Judge Robert Olson found the affidavits timely, held that Rands acted in error, and issued writs moving the 123 cases to Judge Angela Anderson and voiding his discretionary rulings in them [S5].

## Endorsements
In 2022 he was endorsed by both the Whatcom County Republicans and the 42nd District Democrats, unions and the Lummi Indian Business Council [S3]. No 2026 endorsements were researched for this uncontested race.
