## Background
Ryan Swinburnson states no party preference [S1][S11]. He grew up in Whatcom County, graduated from Blaine High School, Central Washington University and Willamette University College of Law, and lists more than 26 years of criminal and civil practice as a prosecutor and defense attorney, plus work as civil counsel for two counties [S1][S3]. He began his career as a prosecutor in Benton County [S6]. He now leads the county's assigned counsel (conflict) office in the Whatcom County Courthouse, which represents defendants who have multiple open cases or co-defendants [S6].

He has served on the Blaine School Board since 2024 and is its president in 2026 [S1][S10]. PDC records show he won the November 2025 general election for Blaine School District Position 4 [S12]. He entered the prosecutor race weeks before the filing deadline [S6].

In the August 4 primary, four candidates ran for the open seat being vacated by Prosecutor Eric Richey; Swinburnson, the only candidate not running as a Democrat, led with 38.4% as of the Thursday count, and he and Jesse Corkern advanced [S8][S9]. He said running without a party label "helped keep doors open" and that voters responded to "somebody that isn't looking at [the prosecutor's office] from a political ideology" [S8].

## Positions
- **Public safety and charging:** says "violent offenders, repeat offenders, and drug dealers must be held accountable while protecting the constitutional rights of every person," with consistent charging decisions and outcomes that "serve as deterrents" [S2][S4]. His statement says violent offenders "must be held more accountable" [S1].
- **Treatment, diversion and therapeutic courts:** supports "evidence-based programs that improve outcomes and reduce recidivism," and programs for people with mental illness and addiction "that also require accountability"; would strengthen Recovery Court and Mental Health Court and create a Veterans Court [S1][S4]. Told CDN he sees diversion as a path to accountability and wants to change how juvenile court is handled [S6].
- **Case backlog and victims:** wants to get deputy prosecutors out of "triage mode," have supervisors attend regular court appearances, review charges properly and have an initial plea offer ready early, while saying that does not mean he would "plead everything out" [S6]. Says victims should not wait years for resolution, which requires reducing the backlog [S4]. As a defense attorney he told CDN that newer prosecutors are "way in over their heads" for lack of mentorship and that survivors sometimes call him when they cannot reach a prosecutor [S7].
- **Office management:** lists mentorship and training of deputy prosecutors as a top priority and says staff turnover makes the county less safe [S4][S6].
- **Law enforcement:** pledges timely charging decisions, clear communication and training for police on changes in the law [S4].
- **Equal protection:** says he will ensure members of marginalized communities "receive the same protection and respect as everyone else" [S2].
- **Civil division:** says county departments deserve competent and responsive legal counsel [S4].
- **Party label:** says he runs with no party preference "to serve every person in Whatcom County" and calls his priorities "not political" [S2][S4].
- **Parents and schools:** as Blaine School Board president he presented the board's evaluation of the interim superintendent and the new contract in June 2026 [S10]. No position on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no position found as of 2026-10-08.
