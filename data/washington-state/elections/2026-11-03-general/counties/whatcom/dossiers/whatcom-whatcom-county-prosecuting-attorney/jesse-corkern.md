## Background
Jesse Corkern prefers the Democratic Party and is a first-time candidate [S1][S9]. He holds a J.D. from Willamette University College of Law (2006) and a B.A. from Portland State University (2000) [S1]. He was a deputy prosecutor in Snohomish County, ran a private criminal and civil practice for nearly a decade, and has been a prosecutor in Whatcom County since 2019 [S1][S3]. He prosecuted misdemeanors and felonies in the criminal division, then moved to the civil division about two years ago after what he described as an unmanageable caseload, a lack of case standardization and minimal support [S3][S5]. He is now a senior deputy prosecuting attorney and the county's civil litigator, representing county departments and employees [S1].

In 2018 he received a Washington State Bar Association admonition for failing to reply to a complaint in a timely manner; the complaint involved a former client's allegation that he had not communicated about vacating a conviction. Corkern returned the client's money, accepted the admonition and told CDN it was "a client communication issue" with no ethics, money or dishonesty element [S5].

He advanced from the four-way August 4 primary with Ryan Swinburnson; as of the Thursday count Corkern, Dona Bracke and Erik Sigmar were within 3 percentage points of one another behind Swinburnson's 38.4% [S7][S8].

## Positions
- **Office management and turnover:** says his leadership "will end the constant flow of attorney resignations" through attorney specialization (for example, prosecutors who handle only sex crimes), improved training and supervision, and leadership "based on merit over seniority" [S1][S2][S3][S5]. Told CDN that District Court prosecutors "are not getting mentored, trained and supported in ways that they need" [S6].
- **Charging standards:** would adopt consistent charging standards for Superior Court cases, applied "equally and consistently," to set expectations for prosecutors and defense attorneys and shorten the time to trial [S1][S3][S5].
- **Public safety:** would "establish zero-tolerance policies for gun violence" and strengthen relationships with law enforcement [S1][S2].
- **Diversion and treatment:** would work with community partners on addiction and mental-health problems that lead to repeated police contacts, and would work with the courts to create a Community Court for non-violent offenders [S1][S3].
- **Civil division:** would add civil attorneys and support staff such as paralegals to reduce outsourcing of county legal work, which he says can be expensive [S5].
- **Caseload:** said he does not plan to carry a criminal caseload as prosecutor but may take part in particular cases [S5].
- **Parents and schools:** no position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no stated position found as of 2026-10-08; his endorsement list includes Planned Parenthood Advocates of Greater Washington and North Idaho and Pro-Choice Washington [S4].
