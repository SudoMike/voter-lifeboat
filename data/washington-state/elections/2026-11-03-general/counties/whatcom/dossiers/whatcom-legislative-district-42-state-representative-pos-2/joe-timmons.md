## Background
Joe Timmons, of Bellingham, is the incumbent Democratic State Representative for the 42nd Legislative District, Position 2, now in his second term [S1][S6]. He was first elected in 2022, won re-election in 2024 against Republican Kamal Bhachu, and serves as Assistant Speaker Pro Tempore [S17]. He holds a bachelor's degree from Western Washington University and a master's from the University of Washington [S1]. He worked as a preschool teacher, as WWU's assistant director of government relations, and in Gov. Jay Inslee's office representing the governor in Northwest Washington; his website cites 15 years of work in local, state and federal government [S1][S4]. His father was a union pipefitter and his parents started a mechanical contracting company [S1][S4].

He sits on the House Housing, Postsecondary Education & Workforce, Transportation and Rules committees [S6]. In the August 4 primary he received 53.9% of the vote; Republican Justin Pike also advanced [S18].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** before the 2026 vote he was hesitant to support a millionaires' tax bill he had not yet read [S15]. After voting no, he said Washington's tax structure is flawed by its reliance on sales tax and that he understands the merit of a tax on millionaires, but that the bill did not provide enough tax relief while adding a new revenue source; he said the state needs to be "drastically" cutting sales, B&O and property taxes if it seeks new revenue, and that the state of the tax code and current spending levels contributed to his decision [S14]. Prime sponsor of HB 1751, a sales-tax exemption for required course materials at public colleges [S11].
- **Spending and higher education:** prime sponsor of HB 2070 for state funding parity for Western Washington University, which he says receives about $1,000 less per student than any other four-year state university [S11][S15]. At a May 2026 town hall he said lawmakers will "ultimately" need a higher-education funding formula that adequately funds all institutions [S16]. His statement and website cite state funding secured for Whatcom County projects, including $13 million for Nooksack floodplain projects, $51 million for a Whatcom Community College center and $1 million for a Bellingham homeless day shelter [S1][S2]. Also said he and Rule want to look more closely at how the state currently spends its money [S14].
- **Housing:** website says tackling housing affordability "starts with building more housing," including increasing supply in cities and around transit lines, and cites his family's 35% rent increase in 2021 [S3]. Voted for the 2025 rent-increase limits in EHB 1217 (see Record) [S12]. Website cites $8 million for a Kulshan Community Land Trust homeownership project [S2].
- **Public safety and behavioral health:** website says he supports law enforcement, firefighters and first responders and their resources and training [S3]. He backs investment in behavioral-health and substance-use treatment, citing $2.35 million for the Whatcom 23-Hour Crisis Relief Center [S3]. Prime sponsor of ESHB 2532 on the sale and distribution of nitrous oxide (chapter 188, Laws of 2026) [S11].
- **Climate and environment:** "Joe believes climate change is real" and supports "continued efforts to reduce emissions, improve energy efficiency, and strengthen Washington's clean energy economy"; also supports keeping public lands in public hands and salmon habitat recovery [S3].
- **Health care:** says he supported efforts to lower the cost of medications such as EpiPens and inhalers [S3]; prime sponsor of HB 1632 on medical debt [S11].
- **Immigration:** voted yes on SB 5855 on law enforcement face coverings (see Record) [S13]. No campaign statement on immigration found.
- **Labor:** prime sponsor of SHB 1570 on collective bargaining for student employees at public colleges (chapter 113, Laws of 2026) [S11].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** "Joe is proudly 100% Pro-Choice and believes decisions about pregnancy, abortion, contraception, and family planning should remain between a patient and their healthcare provider" [S3]. No statement on transgender sports participation or gender-affirming care found.
- **Parents and schools:** voted yes on ESHB 1296, the 2025 rewrite of the I-2081 parental-rights law (see Record) [S8]. Website says he voted to increase special-education funding and supports early learning and career-connected learning [S3]. No statement on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs found.
- **Public records:** at the May 2026 town hall he said he was open to Public Records Act reforms to protect state employees, echoing Rep. Alex Ramel's view that public access is positive [S16].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted against ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S7], while saying he understands the merit of a millionaires' tax and wanted larger cuts to other taxes [S14].
- **I-1 (parental rights):** no statement found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted yes on ESHB 1296 (2025) on House passage and final passage, the amendments to the I-2081 parental-rights law that I-1 would undo [S8].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no statement found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- Voted nay on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million (2026-03-09, passed 51-46) [S7].
- Voted nay on House final passage of EHB 2081, business and occupation tax increases (2025-04-22, passed 50-48) [S9], and on ESSB 5814, excise taxes on select services (2025-04-23, passed 50-47) [S10].
- Voted yea on ESHB 1296 on House passage (2025-03-12, 56-37) and on final passage as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24, 59-39) [S8].
- EHB 1217 (limits on residential rent and fee increases): voted yea on House passage (2025-03-10, 53-42) and on final passage of the conference report (2025-04-27, 54-44); voted nay on concurring in the Senate amendments (2025-04-23, failed 48-50) [S12].
- Voted yea on House final passage of SB 5855 on face coverings worn by law enforcement officers (2026-03-03, 56-37) [S13].
- Prime sponsor of 18 bills and resolutions in 2025-26; enacted bills include SHB 1418 (transit authority governing bodies), HB 1540 (homelessness and tribal colleges), SHB 1570, SHB 2467 (fifth-wheel travel trailers), ESHB 2532 and EHB 2588 (county ferry districts) [S11].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $205,503.77 in contributions, $11,584.47 carried forward and $111,699.47 in expenditures [S19].
