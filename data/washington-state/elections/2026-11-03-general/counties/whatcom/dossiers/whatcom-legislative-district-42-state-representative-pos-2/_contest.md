## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the two candidates who advanced from the August primary.

## Race dynamics
Democratic incumbent Joe Timmons, a second-term representative and former aide to Gov. Jay Inslee, faces Republican Justin Pike, a Whatcom County Sheriff's detective, National Guard soldier and Ferndale laundromat owner running for office for the first time [S1] (joe-timmons.md S4, S17; justin-pike.md S6). Timmons received 53.9% in the primary; with only two candidates filed, both advanced [S2]. Pike reports $197.2k in contributions, close to Timmons's $205.5k (joe-timmons.md S19; justin-pike.md S10).

Both candidates center affordability. Timmons voted against the 2026 tax on income over $1 million and the 2025 B&O and services tax bills, saying he sees merit in a millionaires' tax but wanted larger cuts to sales, B&O and property taxes alongside it (joe-timmons.md S7, S9, S10, S14). Pike opposes new taxes and frames his platform around budget accountability; at a May town hall with Timmons he said, "What I've heard here today is more taxes, more taxes, more taxes" [S3]. On housing, Timmons emphasizes supply near transit and voted for the 2025 rent-increase limits, while Pike would speed permitting, reassess energy-efficiency mandates and loosen Growth Management Act boundary schedules (joe-timmons.md S3, S12; justin-pike.md S3). Timmons states pro-choice and climate-action positions and voted for ESHB 1296, the parental-rights rewrite I-1 would undo; Pike criticizes the Climate Commitment Act's cost and has no stated position on abortion or the initiatives (joe-timmons.md S3, S8; justin-pike.md S8). Both cite behavioral-health treatment as part of public safety; Pike adds accountability for repeat offenders (joe-timmons.md S3; justin-pike.md S2, S3).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** two-term incumbent and House leadership member vs. a first-time candidate.
2. **Spending:** Timmons's focus on state investment in local projects and higher-education funding vs. Pike's budget-accountability and spending-restraint platform.
3. **Housing and land use:** supply near transit plus rent limits vs. permitting speed, fewer energy mandates and Growth Management Act changes.
4. **Climate and energy:** Timmons's emissions-reduction plank vs. Pike's criticism of Climate Commitment Act costs.
5. **Gender and reproductive policy; parents and schools:** Timmons's pro-choice statement and ESHB 1296 vote; no Pike position found.
6. **Taxes:** both call for cutting existing taxes; Timmons voted against the 2026 income tax but sees merit in a millionaires' tax, while Pike opposes new taxes.
