## Background
Justin Pike, of Ferndale, is a Republican running for public office for the first time [S1][S6]. He owns Ferndale Laundry and is a detective with the Whatcom County Sheriff's Office; he previously served with the Ferndale Police Department and the Lummi Nation Police Department [S6]. His statement cites more than 23 years of military service and 17 years in law enforcement; he spent 23 years in the U.S. Army, including four on active duty, and serves in the Washington Army National Guard [S1][S6]. He was elected vice president and president of a law enforcement guild [S1]. He graduated from Ferndale High School and is a youth sports coach in Ferndale and a father of four [S1][S4].

He told the Ferndale Record that he often finds himself "more toward the center of the spectrum," and that if elected he would take leave from his detective role during legislative sessions [S8]. In the August 4 primary Rep. Joe Timmons received 53.9% of the vote, and Pike advanced with him to the general election [S9].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** platform calls for "rebalancing our state tax structure to lower the cost of living for working families" and says "Excessive taxation, meanwhile, is holding back local business expansion and hiring" [S3]. At a May 2026 town hall, the only Republican present, he disagreed with raising taxes: "What I've heard here today is more taxes, more taxes, more taxes. What I can tell you, as a business owner, at some point I can no longer keep those costs" [S7]. He said the Climate Commitment Act has "taxed people out of their home" without required reports on how the money is spent [S8].
- **Spending:** platform starts government accountability "with the budget": transparent budgets, responsible spending and measurable results [S2][S3]. He criticized the state's use of one-time funding for long-term programs and creating programs it cannot support [S7], and said overspending leads to cuts to K-12 education and Medicaid instead of lower-impact programs [S8].
- **Housing and land use:** would use technology to speed plan reviews, "reassess energy efficiency mandates that place unrealistic financial burdens on homeowners," and supports "updating the Growth Management Act so municipal boundaries can expand based on actual community growth, not rigid, multi-decade schedules" [S3].
- **Public safety:** supports "effective law enforcement, accountability for repeat offenders, and better access to behavioral health and addiction treatment" [S2]. Platform calls for "compassion with accountability": shelter and treatment with clear expectations for program participants, and community service or other consequences for minor offenses rather than cycling people through the system [S3]. His statement frames public safety around addressing the behavioral health crisis [S1].
- **Climate and energy:** criticizes the Climate Commitment Act's cost and reporting [S8] and would reassess energy efficiency mandates on homeowners [S3].
- **Public records:** wants more protection for victims under the Public Records Act [S7].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no position found.
- **Parents and schools:** no position on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs found.
