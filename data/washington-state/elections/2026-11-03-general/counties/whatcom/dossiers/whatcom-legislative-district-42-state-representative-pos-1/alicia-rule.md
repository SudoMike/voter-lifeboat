## Background
Alicia Rule, of Blaine, is the incumbent Democratic State Representative for the 42nd Legislative District, Position 1, and describes herself as a third-term representative and former Blaine City Councilmember [S1][S5]. She is a social worker and mental health professional who owns Rule and Associates, a small therapy practice, and she founded and served as president of the Blaine Downtown Development Association [S1][S3]. She graduated from Meridian High School and holds a bachelor's degree from the University of Washington and a master's degree from San Diego State [S1]. Her family has lived in Whatcom County for five generations [S1][S3].

In the 2025-26 Legislature she is vice chair of the House Health Care & Wellness Committee and a member of the Capital Budget and Education committees [S5]. In the August 4 primary she received 56.2% of the vote; Republican Misty Flowers also advanced [S15].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** her statement says she "worked to cut taxes for small businesses and voted no on tax increases, because these costs just get passed on to the consumer" [S1]; her website says "we reduced taxes on small businesses" [S2]. On the 2026 tax on income over $1 million, she said before the vote that it could open the door to an income tax affecting all residents ("I worry that we will end up shouldering this in future years") [S14]. After voting no she said she lacked assurance the revenue would close education funding gaps, that her attempt to direct 40% of it to the Education Legacy Trust Account failed, and that the bill's tax relief was insufficient; the same report says she and Timmons agree Washington's tax system is "regressive" and needs fixing [S13].
- **Spending:** said she wants lawmakers to fund programs that "work for people" and to look more closely at how the state spends its money [S13]; on education she said "We are failing" to fully fund schools and that full funding "doesn't mean just dollars. It means using them well" [S14].
- **Housing:** says she "passed laws to cap rent increases and changed zoning laws to build more housing" [S1]; website cites increased housing working families can afford and services for the homeless [S2].
- **Public safety and behavioral health:** website says homelessness and behavioral health need more support and that she "worked closely with police to improve public safety" and brought behavioral-health funding [S2]. She was prime sponsor of 2025 bills on violent offenses, endangerment with a controlled substance, cannabis retail robberies and county and municipal criminal justice assistance accounts [S10].
- **Immigration and data privacy:** says she "passed laws to stop ICE agents from hiding behind masks" and to "prevent the state from sending your private data to DC" [S1]; voted yes on SB 5855 on law enforcement face coverings (see Record) [S12].
- **Health care:** her prime-sponsored bills include prior authorization, medical loss ratio, maternity services costs and anesthesia services [S10].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** "I'm 100% pro-choice and introduced the bill to protect access to IVF"; says she passed a law to replace Planned Parenthood funding cut by the federal government [S1]. No statement on transgender sports participation or gender-affirming care found.
- **Parents and schools:** voted yes on final passage of ESHB 1296, the 2025 rewrite of the I-2081 parental-rights law (see Record) [S7]. Prime sponsor of bills on youth mental health in schools, school library information and technology programs, teacher-librarian funding and cardiac emergency plans in schools [S10]. No statement on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs found.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted against ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S6], one of eight House Democrats to do so, citing uncertainty about the law's future, education funding and insufficient tax relief [S13].
- **I-1 (parental rights):** no statement found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted yes on ESHB 1296 (2025), the amendments to the I-2081 parental-rights law that I-1 would undo; she was excused on the House's first vote [S7].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no statement found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- Voted nay on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million (2026-03-09, passed 51-46) [S6].
- Voted nay on House final passage of EHB 2081, business and occupation tax increases (2025-04-22, passed 50-48) [S8], and on ESSB 5814, excise taxes on select services (2025-04-23, passed 50-47) [S9].
- ESHB 1296: excused on House passage (2025-03-12, 56-37); voted yea on concurrence in Senate amendments and final passage (2025-04-24, 59-39) [S7].
- EHB 1217 (limits on residential rent and fee increases): voted yea on House passage (2025-03-10, 53-42) and on final passage of the conference report (2025-04-27, 54-44); voted nay on concurring in the Senate amendments (2025-04-23, failed 48-50) [S11].
- Voted yea on House final passage of SB 5855 on face coverings worn by law enforcement officers (2026-03-03, 56-37) [S12].
- Prime sponsor of 39 bills and resolutions in 2025-26, including HB 1287 on health information coordination (chapter 83, Laws of 2025) and HB 1318, a sales-tax exemption for children's diapers [S10].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $180,706.97 in contributions, $32,412.64 carried forward and $85,399.64 in expenditures [S16].
