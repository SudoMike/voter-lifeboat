## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The 42nd District covers nearly all of Whatcom County except part of Bellingham [S3]. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the two candidates who advanced from the August primary.

## Race dynamics
Democratic incumbent Alicia Rule, a third-term representative, social worker and former Blaine councilmember, faces Republican Misty Flowers, a small-business owner and Whatcom County Republican Party chair who lost a 2025 county council race [S1] (misty-flowers.md S5, S9). Rule received 56.2% in the primary; with only two candidates filed, both advanced [S2].

On taxes, both candidates describe opposition to increases. Rule was one of eight House Democrats to vote against the 2026 tax on income over $1 million, and she also voted against the 2025 B&O and services tax bills; her statement says she "voted no on tax increases" [S1][S3] (alicia-rule.md S6, S8, S9). Flowers's statement refers to "Washington's tax-raising Rule" and pledges to lower property, sales and state income taxes and restrain state spending [S1] (misty-flowers.md S2). The sharper recorded contrasts are on social and immigration policy, where Rule campaigns as "100% pro-choice," cites a law against masked ICE agents and voted for the 2025 parental-rights rewrite (ESHB 1296) that I-1 would undo, and on public safety, where Flowers pledges expanded jail capacity for violent offenders (alicia-rule.md S1, S7, S12; misty-flowers.md S2, S5). No statement by either candidate on the three statewide initiatives was found as of 2026-10-08. Rule reports $180.7k in contributions to Flowers's $17.2k (alicia-rule.md S16; misty-flowers.md S8).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** third-term incumbent vs. a challenger who has not held office.
2. **Public safety:** Flowers's jail-capacity and accountability plank vs. Rule's behavioral-health and police-partnership framing.
3. **Immigration:** Rule's vote and statement on masked federal agents vs. Flowers's view that immigration is a federal matter.
4. **Gender and reproductive policy:** Rule's pro-choice and IVF record; no Flowers position found.
5. **Taxes and spending:** both say they oppose tax increases; Flowers adds explicit cuts to property, sales and income taxes and spending restraint.
6. **Parents and schools:** Rule voted for ESHB 1296; no Flowers position found.
