## Background
Misty Flowers is the Republican candidate for the 42nd Legislative District, Position 1; her statement lists no elected experience and describes her as "Fresh Leadership for Fiscal Responsibility" [S1][S2]. She lists work as a small business owner, in business and office management, as a wellness educator and as a performing musician; her campaign is based in Ferndale [S1][S2]. She studied at the Southwest Institute of Healing Arts, Whatcom Community College and Shasta Community College [S1][S3]. She moved to Whatcom County 23 years ago from California [S3].

Her community service list includes vice chair and chair of the Whatcom County Republican Party, lead facilitator for Be Brave WA, county coordinator for Informed Choice WA, child and family advocate, and volunteer garden work for the Bellingham Food Bank [S1][S3]. In 2025, as Whatcom County Republican Party chair, she ran for Whatcom County Council at-large against incumbent Barry Buchanan; she told Cascadia Daily News she first became involved in state politics in 2016 and in local politics around 2020 [S5]. Buchanan won with about 63% of the vote to her 37% [S9]. In the August 4, 2026 primary she advanced to the general election against Rep. Alicia Rule, who received 56.2% [S7].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** statement says "It is time to restrain Olympia's spending" and pledges "lowering the tax burden" [S1]. Website pledges to "Lower property, sales, and state income taxes responsibly to reduce the cost of living" and says "Washington is taxed out" [S2]; says families and businesses have left the state because of building rules and rising taxes [S3]. In her 2025 council race she said the county should use taxes wisely and not increase them regularly [S5].
- **Spending:** website describes the state budget as "bloated and unaccountable" and pledges to "Demand fiscal responsibility and transparency so every tax dollar is spent efficiently" [S2]. In 2025 she called for more accountability in county contracts with nonprofits, with requirements to show whether programs work [S5].
- **Housing:** website pledges to "Align local building codes with state standards to cut delays and expand affordable, environmentally responsible housing" [S2]. In 2025 she said taxes and zoning had expanded the housing crisis and proposed reallocating county taxes to low-interest loans for single-family homes and rent subsidies [S6].
- **Public safety:** website pledges to "Expand jail capacity for violent offenders and support addiction recovery programs focused on personal responsibility" [S2]. In 2025 she criticized drug decriminalization in Bellingham and the 22 North housing-first facility, called the incarceration system "completely broken," and advocated jail time as part of breaking the cycle of addiction-driven crime alongside recovery resources [S5].
- **Jobs and economy:** pledges to simplify permitting and invest in marine trades, tourism, agriculture and expanded ferry service [S2].
- **Environment:** says industries can partner with government to protect the environment "without having to sacrifice one for the other" [S3]; in 2025 she favored regenerative agriculture and clearing forest underbrush to reduce wildfire [S6].
- **Elections:** in 2025 she called ranked-choice voting "confusing, more expensive and time consuming to count" and said election resources should go to ensuring only U.S. citizens vote [S6].
- **Immigration:** in 2025, asked about protecting immigrants from unconstitutional actions, she said immigration is federal jurisdiction and changes must go through the proper channels, citing 8 U.S. Code Chapter 12 [S6].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no position found.
- **Parents and schools:** her statement says she is committed to leadership that protects "your pocketbook, your property and your progeny" [S1]. No position on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs found.
