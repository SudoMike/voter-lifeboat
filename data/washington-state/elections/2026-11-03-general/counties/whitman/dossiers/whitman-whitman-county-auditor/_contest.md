## What the office does
Under state law the county auditor records deeds and other instruments, keeps an account current with the county treasurer, reports the state fund account to the State Auditor and publishes an exhibit of the county's prior-year finances [S3]. The auditor is also the officer with overall responsibility for voter registration and for conducting state and local elections in the county [S4]. Whitman County's Auditor department page lists document recording, elections, voter registration, marriage licenses and vehicle and vessel licensing as its responsibilities [S5]. The term is four years [S1].

## Race dynamics
The seat is open. Auditor Sandy Jamison, auditor since 2019, is not seeking re-election; her letter in the pamphlet says she is retiring at the end of the year [S2][S7]. This is the only contested county office on the 2026 Whitman ballot; both candidates live in Colfax and filed without a party preference [S1][S8].

Crystn Guenthner is the office's Senior Deputy Auditor and has served on the Colfax City Council since 2018. Jamison endorses her, and she runs on "Trusted Experience. Proven Integrity." [S1]. Kenneth Millar works in the county's Information Technology department and has worked as a poll worker and as a 2010 Census recruiting manager. He runs on accuracy, consistency and stewardship of taxpayer resources, and says public service is "a duty, not a career" [S1]. In the fully reported August 4 primary, Guenthner received 6,041 votes and Millar 1,794, with 50 write-ins [S6].

The Gazette asked both candidates about "concerns about ballot mailing errors" under the current administration. Millar said he would add verification checkpoints and process reviews. Guenthner said she would keep current quality-control procedures, investigate problems with the Postal Service and report them publicly [S9][S10]. On primary night Millar said he hoped the winner could "shake things up a little bit and make the changes that need to be made" [S7].

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience/record:** the main difference. Guenthner is the office's senior deputy and has the outgoing auditor's endorsement, so she is the continuity candidate. Millar comes from another county department, has not held elected office and calls for changes in the office.
2. **Spending/accountability:** both use the language of accuracy and accountability. Millar adds "responsible stewardship of taxpayer resources" but has no specific audit or spending program. Neither candidate is clearly separated on this axis.
3. **Reform:** no candidate proposes changes to election law or campaign-finance rules. Their differences are about how the office runs: Millar would add ballot verification checkpoints; Guenthner would cross-train staff and work in state workgroups.
4. Taxes, housing, public safety and climate: no positions found for either candidate. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives: none found for either candidate.
