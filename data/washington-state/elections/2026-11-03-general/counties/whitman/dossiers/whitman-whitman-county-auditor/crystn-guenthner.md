## Background

Crystn Guenthner, 38, of Colfax, is Whitman County's Senior Deputy Auditor, a post she has held since 2019 [S1][S3]. She has served on the Colfax City Council since 2018 [S1][S3]; PDC records show her winning a Colfax council seat (position 5) in the 2023 general election [S7]. She lists private business accounting experience and is a certified licensing agent through the Washington State Department of Licensing [S1]. She attended Colfax High School and Lewis-Clark State College, where she studied psychology and anthropology and co-authored a published psychology research paper [S1]. She says Whitman County has been her home since she was 12; she is married with two sons [S1][S3]. Her civic activities include church volunteering in children's and women's ministries [S3]. She filed stating no party preference [S2][S8].

She said she has worked in the main areas of the auditor's office: elections, recording and licensing [S1]. The Gazette profile adds county financial controls to that list [S3].

In the fully reported August 4, 2026 primary she placed first with 6,041 votes, to Kenneth Millar's 1,794 and 50 write-ins [S5]. "I am grateful to every voter who came to return their ballot," she said on primary night [S4].

## Positions

### Auditor's office administration
- Running "because I believe experience, accountability, and steady leadership matter"; her slogan is "Trusted Experience. Proven Integrity." [S1]
- Describes the office's work as "secure and transparent elections," "preserving public records dating back to the 1800s" and licensing services [S1].
- Says the office has "an exceptional staff" and faces "increasing workloads, limited budgets, and evolving technology"; her stated priority is "preserving institutional knowledge" by cross-training staff so service stays consistent through retirements and transitions [S3].
- Says the county benefits when staff take part in state workgroups, committees and professional organizations, and that this helps the office "adopt proven best practices" [S3]. She says she participates in statewide workgroups and committees on licensing accuracy, recording standards and legislative review [S1].

### Elections
- On concerns about ballot mailing errors: says she "will continue using strong quality-control procedures throughout ballot preparation and mailing." Once ballots enter the postal system the office relies on the USPS. She says she cannot direct another agency, but would investigate any problems, work with partners on improvements and "communicate openly with the public about what happened" [S3].
