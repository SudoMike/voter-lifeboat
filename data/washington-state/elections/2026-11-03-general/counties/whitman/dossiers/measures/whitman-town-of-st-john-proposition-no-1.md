## What it does

Renews the town's special property tax levy for street improvements and maintenance, collected in 2027 [S1][S2]. The explanatory statement says the money would help fund "the maintenance, repair, improvement, and replacement of streets, sidewalks, and related public infrastructure," including chip sealing, pothole repair, manhole cover replacement and street sweeping, and that recent work has included each of those [S1][S2]. The measure is titled "Street Improvements Levy" in VoteWA and "Street Improvement Levy" in the printed pamphlet [S1][S2].

## Cost

- $90,000, an estimated $1.89 per $1,000 of assessed value, collected in 2027 [S1][S2].
- In 2024 the Daily News reported the town's street levy request as $80,000 at $200 per $100,000 ($2.00 per $1,000) [S4]. This year's amount is $10,000 higher at a slightly lower estimated rate.
- Proposition No. 2 on the same ballot asks for an $80,000 water and sewer levy at an estimated $1.68 per $1,000 [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

MRSC describes excess levies under RCW 84.52.052 as separate from the regular levy, expiring after one year for all agencies except fire protection districts, and requiring a 60% majority [S9].

- **A recurring street levy.** St. John voters approved a street levy in November 2025 (163 yes, 77 no, 67.9%) [S7] and November 2024 (243 yes, 110 no, 68.8%) [S3], and town Propositions No. 1 and No. 2 in November 2023 (69.4% and 71.2% yes) [S6]. The Gazette reported that one of the town's 2025 measures, Proposition No. 2, totaled $80,000 at an estimated $1.85 per $1,000; the visible part of the story does not name its purpose [S5].
- For 2025 collection the Department of Revenue lists a St. John "Special" levy of $140,000 at $3.14 per $1,000, the total of the 2024 street ($80,000) and water and sewer ($60,000) requests [S8][S4].
