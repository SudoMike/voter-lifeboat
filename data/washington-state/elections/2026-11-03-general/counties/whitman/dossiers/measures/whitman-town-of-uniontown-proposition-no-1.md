## What it does

A one-year excess levy, levied in 2026 for collection in 2027, to raise about $30,000 "for General Operations, Improvements of the Town Water/Sewer Systems, and General Maintenance, not to include employee wages" [S1][S2]. The town submitted no explanatory statement [S1][S2].

- **Council action.** The town council voted unanimously on June 10, 2026 to put a maintenance and operations levy on the November ballot. When Councilman Jim Barry asked whether it could cover payroll, the council amended the motion to exclude payroll [S4].

## Cost

- About $30,000, approximately $0.64 per $1,000 of assessed value, collected in 2027 [S1][S2].
- The Gazette reported that the owner of a $350,000 home would pay $224 [S4].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

MRSC describes excess levies under RCW 84.52.052 as separate from the regular levy, expiring after one year for all agencies except fire protection districts, and requiring a 60% majority [S8].

- **Earlier Uniontown levies.** In November 2024 the town asked for two-year park and library levies for 2025 and 2026, at $75 and $25 per $100,000 [S5]. The library levy passed (120 yes, 74 no, 61.9%); the park levy received 100 yes, 94 no (51.5%) [S3]. No Uniontown town measure was on the November 2025 ballot [S6].
- The Department of Revenue lists a Uniontown "Special" levy of $65,000 at $2.14 per $1,000 for 2025 collection, alongside the regular levy of $79,172 at $2.58 per $1,000 [S7]. The sources found do not itemize the $65,000.
- This year's measure is for general operations, water and sewer and maintenance, a different purpose from the 2024 park and library levies [S1][S5].

## Arguments for

After repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement for the ballot measure [S1][S2].

## Arguments against

After repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement against the ballot measure [S1][S2]. At the June meeting Councilman Barry said, "The county has a property tax raise in 2027, and we have a continuing school tax, so it might be a hard sell to the town" [S4].
