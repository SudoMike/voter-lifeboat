## What it does

Renews the town's special property tax levy "for water and sewer upgrades and capital improvements," collected in 2027 [S1][S2]. The explanatory statement says the money would help fund operation, maintenance, repair and improvement of the town's wells and water system and its sewer treatment plant and related infrastructure, and that recent work has included replacement pump motors, a well monitoring system and "specialized professional services" [S1][S2].

## Cost

- $80,000, an estimated $1.68 per $1,000 of assessed value, collected in 2027 [S1][S2].
- In 2024 the Daily News reported the town's water and sewer levy request as $60,000 at $150 per $100,000 ($1.50 per $1,000) [S4]. The Gazette reported a 2025 St. John Proposition No. 2 of $80,000 at an estimated $1.85 per $1,000; the visible part of the story does not name its purpose [S5].
- Proposition No. 1 on the same ballot asks for a $90,000 street levy at an estimated $1.89 per $1,000 [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

MRSC describes excess levies under RCW 84.52.052 as separate from the regular levy, expiring after one year for all agencies except fire protection districts, and requiring a 60% majority [S9].

- **A recurring levy.** St. John voters approved a water and sewer levy in November 2025 (175 yes, 66 no, 72.6%) [S7] and a "Water & Sewer Upgrade Levy" in November 2024 (248 yes, 106 no, 70.1%) [S3]; town Propositions No. 1 and No. 2 passed in November 2023 (69.4% and 71.2% yes) [S6].
- For 2025 collection the Department of Revenue lists a St. John "Special" levy of $140,000 at $3.14 per $1,000, the total of the 2024 street ($80,000) and water and sewer ($60,000) requests [S8][S4].
