## What it does

A one-year special property tax levy "for ongoing oiling of gravel streets, chip sealing roads, replacing asphalt or repairing and replacing sidewalks," levied on 2026 assessed value for collection in 2027 [S1][S2]. The explanatory statement says the levy would pay for sidewalk replacement or the match for a sidewalk grant, the match for a street grant, chip sealing paved roads, or oiling gravel streets. It says the city will seal coat most streets this summer with a Transportation Improvement Board (TIB) grant, has applied for a TIB grant to replace the Main Street sidewalk in 2027, and has "a large number of sidewalks that need replaced" [S1][S2].

- **Council action.** After a public hearing on July 20, 2026, the council approved Street Levy Resolution No. 26-02 for $50,000; all members voted yes except Council Member Thomas [S5]. At the same meeting it approved a TIB application to replace sidewalks on Main Street and between Crosby and Ramsey Streets: $187,600 from TIB and $40,000 from the street levy fund, $227,600 in all [S5]. In June the clerk had asked the council to consider using levy money as the match for a TIB grant to replace "the worst sidewalks in Tekoa" [S4].
- **Street levy fund.** On August 17 the mayor reported about $10,000 in the street levy fund; the council voted to take $15,000 from it to add chip sealing of Alder Street (Howard to Lake) to the TIB project, with Council Members Smith and Thomas voting no [S6].

## Cost

- $50,000, an estimated $1.23 per $1,000 of assessed value, collected in 2027 [S1][S2].
- In 2024 the Daily News reported the city's $50,000 street levy at $150 per $100,000 ($1.50 per $1,000) [S7]; the Department of Revenue lists Tekoa's 2025 special levy as $50,000 at $1.36 per $1,000 [S10].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

MRSC describes excess levies under RCW 84.52.052 as separate from the regular levy, expiring after one year for all agencies except fire protection districts, and requiring a 60% majority [S11].

- **Prior street levies.** The November 2024 street levy passed with 252 yes, 164 no (60.6%) [S3], and appears in the 2025 tax roll as Tekoa's $50,000 special levy [S10]. The November 2025 street levy received 173 yes, 124 no (58.2%) [S9], below the 60% MRSC cites [S11]. A city Proposition No. 1 passed in November 2023 (204 yes, 85 no, 70.6%) [S8].
- The Daily News described the 2024 levy as continuing street and sidewalk work, including paving College, Lake and Howard streets and chip sealing on the northwest side of town [S7].

## Arguments for

Doug Palmer's statement for: "Any improvements that will improve the city streets and sidewalks will benefit all the citizens in Tekoa. This keeps our city safe!" [S1][S2]

## Arguments against

Jequetta Lucki's statement against: "Streets that are more difficult to work on do not get the same attention as other streets." [S1][S2] One council member voted against the levy resolution [S5].

## Endorsements and campaign

No committee was found in PDC filings [S12]. The statements for and against were written by individual residents [S1].
