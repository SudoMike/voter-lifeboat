## What it does

Proposition 1 would replace Cheney School District's educational programs and operation (EP&O) levy, which expires in 2027, with a three-year levy collected in 2028-2030, under Resolution No. 27-25-26 [S1][S2]. The explanatory statement lists expenses the state does not fund: athletics, extracurricular activities and electives; class size reduction, counselors and mental health supports; staffing above the state's prototypical model and substitutes; safety and security, instructional technology and transportation; nutrition and special education costs above state and federal funding; nursing above funded levels; and early learning [S1][S2].

- The district's page lists athletics and activities, staffing above the prototypical model, counselors, paraeducators, nurses, custodians, special education and "YES Officers" [S3].
- Superintendent Ben Ferney said the levy is nearly 20% of an annual budget of about $105 million [S5]; an earlier report put it at 16.5% of operating revenue the previous year [S4].
- The board voted unanimously to place it on the ballot; it needs a simple majority [S4]. The district moved its levies from February to November hoping for higher turnout [S5].
- **Who votes in Whitman County.** The district is based in Spokane County; only a sliver of it lies in Whitman County. In the February 2024 levy election four Whitman County ballots were counted on the EP&O levy (3 yes, 1 no) [S8], and Cheney director races in November 2025 drew five or fewer Whitman votes [S9]. The outcome is decided districtwide.

## Cost

| Collection year | Estimated rate per $1,000 | Levy amount |
|---|---|---|
| 2028 | $2.10 | $18,450,000 |
| 2029 | $2.10 | $19,000,000 |
| 2030 | $2.10 | $19,550,000 |

Figures from the ballot title [S1][S2]; about $57 million over three years [S4]. Ferney said $2.10 is "well below" the $2.50 state maximum, and that the district collects dollar amounts, so the rate falls as assessed values rise [S5]. The voters' guide gives no household example [S1]. A July report said collection would begin in 2027 [S4]; the ballot title's first collection year is 2028 [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

The expiring levy went to voters in February 2024: more than $51 million over three years at an estimated $1.97 per $1,000, up from $1.30 in 2023, as federal pandemic funding ended [S6]. Cheney's levies passed that February [S7]. No source found gives the rate actually collected.

- Compared with that estimate, the new levy is about $6 million (roughly 12%) larger over three years and $0.13 higher per $1,000 (computed for this dossier from [S1][S6]).
- With both 2026 levies and the existing bond, the district projects a combined school tax rate of $3.90 per $1,000, compared with $3.93 now; the board's goal is to stay under $4 [S4][S5].
- Ferney: "It's not new taxes. It's to continue the programs we have now" [S5].

## Arguments for

Statement for, by the Committee for Great Schools (Meridth Lemelin, Nicole Williams, Amy Pruett) [S1]:
- The levy "allows CSD to maintain the exceptional educational and extra-curricular programs currently offered."
- "Failure to pass this measure would result in drastic cuts to educational programs, student support, and services."
- "This levy will replace the current levy ... This is not a new tax."

## Arguments against

No statement against was submitted: "After repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement against the ballot measure" [S1][S2]. No opposition was found.
