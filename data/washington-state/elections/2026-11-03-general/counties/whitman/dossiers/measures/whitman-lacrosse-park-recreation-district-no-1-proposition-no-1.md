## What it does

Proposition No. 1 asks for a one-year special levy of $75,000 on all real property in LaCrosse Parks and Recreation District 1, collected in 2027, for the operation, maintenance and capital improvements of the swimming pool and existing buildings [S1][S2].

- **What it pays for.** The explanatory statement lists lifeguard salaries, pool chemicals, utilities and office supplies (operations) and professional services, repairs, construction and painting supplies (maintenance). It cites recent work: resealing the expansion joints, installing a second new pool filter, and painting. The levy also supports district-sponsored youth programs: swim team, soccer, football, basketball and baseball [S1][S2].

## Cost

- $75,000, an estimated $0.31 per $1,000 of assessed value, collected in 2027 [S1][S2].
- Computed from the estimated rate: about $93 on a $300,000 property.

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- The explanatory statement says the levy "will continue to support" the pool and buildings [S1].
- The district has asked for a one-year levy each year: voters approved it in November 2023 (186 to 81, 69.7%), November 2024 (258 to 125, 67.4%) and November 2025 (162 to 93, 63.5%) [S3][S4][S5].
- The DOR levy detail shows the district's special levy at $125,000 ($0.51515 per $1,000) for taxes due in 2024 and $75,000 ($0.29537) for 2025 [S6][S7]. This request matches the 2025 amount.

## Arguments for

Katie Bailey's statement says the levy "makes it possible to continue operating our pool" and supports youth programs; it calls the pool "the lifeblood of our community in the summer months," cites the Tri-County Swim League team, swim lessons and water aerobics, and notes that the pool employs local youth in "our small, rural community" [S1][S2].

## Arguments against

Jerry Chastain's statement: "I understand that the pool is a big deal for our community; however, I believe our tax dollars should be prioritized elsewhere" [S1][S2].
