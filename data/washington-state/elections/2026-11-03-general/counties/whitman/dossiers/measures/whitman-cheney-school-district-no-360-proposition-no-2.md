## What it does

Proposition 2 would replace Cheney School District's capital levy, which expires in 2027, with a three-year capital levy collected in 2028-2030, under Resolution No. 28-25-26 [S1][S2]. The money would pay for (1) instructional technology equipment, infrastructure and systems; (2) security cameras, entry and access controls, door locks and other safety systems; and (3) other safety infrastructure, "including bleachers, locker rooms, heating and cooling systems and equipment, electrical, plumbing, roofing, lighting and fencing" [S1][S2].

- The district's page lists technology upgrades, security improvements, facility infrastructure, furniture and equipment, and implementation and ongoing support [S3].
- Superintendent Ben Ferney said the money goes to technology such as security cameras and replacement Chromebooks, and to smaller capital items such as a roof, carpeting or parts of HVAC systems [S5]. Most of the 2024 bond went to the new Skyway Elementary in Airway Heights; Ferney said there is "always a component of capital levy that's needed to maintain buildings" [S4].
- The board voted unanimously to place it on the ballot; it needs a simple majority [S4].
- **Who votes in Whitman County.** The district is based in Spokane County; only a sliver of it lies in Whitman County. In the February 2024 levy election four Whitman County ballots were counted on the EP&O levy (3 yes, 1 no) and four on the companion levy (2 yes, 2 no) [S8], and Cheney director races in November 2025 drew five or fewer Whitman votes [S9]. The outcome is decided districtwide.

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

The expiring capital levy went to voters in February 2024 and would "renew to 9 cents per $1,000," about $2.3 million over three years [S6]. Cheney's levies passed that February [S7]. No source found gives the rate actually collected.

- Compared with that estimate, the new levy is about $1.8 million larger over three years, and its rate rises from about $0.09 to $0.10-$0.20 per $1,000 (computed for this dossier from [S1][S6]).
- With both 2026 levies and the existing bond, the district projects a combined school tax rate of $3.90 per $1,000, compared with $3.93 now [S4][S5].

## Arguments for

Statement for, by the Committee for Great Schools (Meridth Lemelin, Nicole Williams, Amy Pruett) [S1]:
- "This is a replacement levy, not a new tax."
- It funds technology "such as digital whiteboards," code compliance, roof repairs, HVAC, fire alarm systems, security cameras, access controls and door locks.
- "Failure to pass Proposition 2 would result in deferred maintenance and would only cost more money down the road."

## Arguments against

No statement against was submitted: "After repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement against the ballot measure" [S1][S2]. No opposition was found.
