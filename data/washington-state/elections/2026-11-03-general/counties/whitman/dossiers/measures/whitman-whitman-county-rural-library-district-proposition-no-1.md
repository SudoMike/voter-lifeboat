## What it does

Proposition 1 would "restore the Rural Library District's regular property tax levy rate to $0.45 per $1,000 of assessed value in 2026, for collection in 2027 and maintain that rate authorization for each of the nine succeeding years." The actual rate may be set below that maximum in any year, and the 2036 levy amount becomes the base for later limits under chapter 84.55 RCW [S1][S2]. It is a levy lid lift on the district's existing regular levy and needs a simple majority [S3].

- **The district.** It serves 14 communities [S1][S2]. Voters in unincorporated Whitman County and in Albion, Colfax, Endicott, Farmington, Garfield, LaCrosse, Lamont, Malden, Oakesdale, Palouse, St. John and Tekoa decide it. Colton, Rosalia and Uniontown contract with the library for service and are not in the district; Pullman has its own library (Neill Public Library) [S3].
- **What the money would pay for.** The district says its "first priority will be to stop the bleed": close its current budget gap and keep existing services, while continuing to seek grants for programs and special projects [S3]. Its estimate of where property tax money goes: staff 59%, benefits 15%, programs and technology 9%, operations 8%, collection 4%, insurance 3%, loan repayments 2% [S3].
- **If it fails.** The district says it would have to make "difficult choices," "potentially reducing hours, programs, services, or other investments" [S3].
- **Why ten years.** The district says levy elections are costly and a ten-year authorization avoids returning to voters every few years [S3].

## Cost

- Rate: from about $0.396 per $1,000 now to a maximum of $0.45, below the $0.50 statutory maximum [S3][S4]. The statement for rounds the current rate to $0.39 [S1].
- Explanatory statement: $14.30 more a year on a $275,000 home [S1][S2].
- District table: $5.20 more a year per $100,000 of assessed value ($45.00 versus $39.80), $15.60 more on a $300,000 home [S3]. The Gazette reports $112.50 a year on a $250,000 property, an increase of $13.50 [S4].
- Senior and disabled-person property tax exemptions may apply [S3].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

The district's regular levy is limited to 1% annual revenue growth, so its rate falls as assessed values rise; the district says costs are rising faster than revenue and it now faces a budget deficit [S3]. The explanatory statement says rising costs and state tax limitations have created "a gap between revenue and the cost of services" [S1][S2].

This is the latest in a series of requests to restore the rate:
- Voters approved a 50-cent rate in 1984. It fell after Initiative 747 (2001); voters restored it to 50 cents in 2010, after which it fell to about 43 cents, and the board asked voters in February 2014 to restore it again [S7][S8]. The 2014 result was not found.
- In February 2018 the district again asked to return the levy to 50 cents after freezing wages and cutting books and online materials by 20% [S6]. Voters approved it 3,159 to 1,088 (74%) [S5].
- Since then the rate has fallen to about 39.6 cents [S3][S4]. This request is for 45 cents, not the full 50, and for a ten-year authorization [S1][S3].

## Arguments for

Statement for, by Blaine Golden [S1][S2]:
- The library is, for many, "the only free, welcoming public space where people can gather to learn, connect, attend programs, and build community." In 2025 the district hosted 2,264 programs and circulated 57,173 physical items.
- Libraries "drive downtown activity," provide free wireless internet, support jobseekers and "reduce crime by engaging youth outside of school."
- State and federal library funding "is unpredictable and may come with requirements that do not reflect our communities' values"; "Local taxation preserves local control." The rate would let the district "maintain a balanced budget."

The district says "This is not a new tax" but a restoration of an existing levy that has declined [S3].

## Arguments against

No statement against was submitted: "After repeated recruitment attempts, no volunteers came forward to write a statement against the ballot measure" [S1][S2]. No opposition committee or published opposition was found [S9]. The district acknowledges that a property tax increase is "something people will need to consider carefully" [S3].

## Endorsements and campaign

No PDC committee for or against this measure was found; the library committees in the 2026 PDC data are in other counties [S9]. The district is holding "Library Levy Community Conversations" around the county [S3].
