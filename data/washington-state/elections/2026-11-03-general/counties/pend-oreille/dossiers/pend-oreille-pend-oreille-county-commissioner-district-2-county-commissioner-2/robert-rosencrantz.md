## Background

- Robert Rosencrantz, of Newport, prefers the Republican Party. He was appointed District 2 commissioner in September 2021 and elected to a full term in November 2022; before that he sat on the Newport Hospital board (2020-2021) and the Pend Oreille County Library District board (2018-2021) [S1][S6].
- He owns Rose Properties, a commercial real estate business, since 1984; he holds a B.A. in economics from the University of Washington (1978) [S1]. He moved to the county in 2004 [S5].
- In 2004 he was executive director of the Northwest Association for Housing Affordability for a one-year turnaround assignment [S1][S6].
- He chairs the Tri County Economic Development District governing board and was finance chair of the Rural Resources governing board [S1][S6].
- In 2022 he won the seat 3,214 to 2,884 over Becky Walrath [S9]. In the August 2026 primary, which only District 2 voters could vote in, he led Brandy Parker Warren 670 to 669, with 18 write-ins [S10].
- Full PDC reporting: $10,662.61 raised and $10,110.78 spent as of September 28, 2026 [S11].

## Positions

### Data centers and the Usk mill

- Says his first reason for running is "to stop data centers from being built here"; argues they would raise power costs, strain water supplies and threaten property rights for few jobs [S1][S3][S4].
- Says counties face developers with far more resources and that data centers are often pushed through "with no meaningful public input"; calls for "smart growth" and "open government" instead [S4].
- On the proposal to turn the former Ponderay Newsprint mill in Usk into a data center drawing up to 225 megawatts: "We've heard absolutely nothing ... If they are such a good idea, how come people aren't talking to us about it?" and "I do not want Pend Oreille County to become a data center corridor" [S7]. He acknowledged the use "may be a permitted use" under the site's existing conditional use permit [S7].

### Taxes and spending

- Says he wants the county to stay as affordable as possible by "maintaining essential services while holding taxes down" and making sure county government "spends carefully" and "remains accountable to taxpayers" [S4].
- Opposes road levy shifts (moving road levy money to balance the general budget), calling deferred road maintenance "a short-term fix that leads to long-term problems" [S1][S3].
- Says roads, snowplowing and public safety should stay top priorities [S3].

### Housing and land use

- Cites his housing background in securing nearly $5 million to renovate the Kaniksu Village Apartments in Metaline Falls [S1][S6].
- Petitioned the State Board of Health to restore a 100-foot septic setback from the Pend Oreille River after it was raised to 200 feet in April 2025, a change he said would block riverfront building; the board approved the petition statewide [S6].

### Other

- Says he has spoken out against Medicare cuts [S1][S3].

## Record

- In June 2025 he said he was "deeply disappointed" that the Usk mill's owners had not kept their promises to restore paper mill jobs, and said county leaders must make sure development at the site serves the public [S8].
- Lists securing additional funding for road maintenance and snowplowing [S1][S3].

## Endorsements

None listed on his campaign site [S3][S4][S5][S6].

## Scoring notes

- Incumbent running on his record against a first-time candidate [S1].
- Opposes data centers; the Spokesman-Review reported that his opponent's husband works as a manager at Merkle Standard, the company seeking power for the Usk data center [S7].
- Fiscal statements favor holding taxes down and careful spending [S4]; his housing record includes both public housing investment and loosening a septic setback rule [S6].
