## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would let Pend Oreille County Public Hospital District No. 1, which runs Newport Hospital and Health Services, issue up to $51,000,000 of general obligation bonds maturing within 30 years, repaid by an annual excess property tax, to expand and renovate Newport Community Hospital under Resolution No. 2026-06 (S1, S2). The work relocates the main entrance and admitting area and expands imaging, laboratory, rehabilitative therapy, surgical, emergency, trauma and behavioral health services (S1).

The district covers roughly the southern half of the county (S3); Ione and Metaline Falls are in Hospital District No. 2 and do not vote on it (builder notes, WA DOR HSP2025).

Hospital officials describe a three-story addition replacing the 1958 wing, three surgical operating rooms, an emergency department growing from seven to up to 12 beds with behavioral health rooms, and an in-house MRI suite (S3, S5, S6). The total project is $55 million; the district would pay $4 million from cash, a lease and grants (S5, S6). The district says the project would add 20 to 25 jobs (S6).

This is a re-run. The same bonds failed in the August 4 primary, 1,719 approved to 2,084 rejected (45.2%), with 60% needed (S6, S7, S8).

## Cost

- Estimated $1.47 per $1,000 of assessed value a year, about $36.75 a month for a $300,000 home, per the explanatory statement (S1).
- The Miner's estimates: $25 a month on a $200,000 assessment, $37 on $300,000 and $49 on $400,000; interest 4.75% to 5.5% over up to 30 years (S5, S6).
- District taxpayers can apply for up to $750 a year in credits toward hospital bills and Seeber's Pharmacy prescriptions, based on the bond taxes they paid (S5, S6). An April report put the waiver at up to $500 (S3).
- In 2025 the district already levied a regular levy ($0.26494 per $1,000) and a separate "Hospital #1 Bond" levy ($0.30533 per $1,000, $659,410) (S10).
- Needs 60% approval (S3, S5, S11).

## Arguments for

- The statement for (Taylor Stewart, JoAnn Bell, Kevin McNamee) says the bond keeps care close to home and cuts trips to Spokane and Coeur d'Alene, expands emergency, therapy and specialist services, and helps recruit staff; it says every $1 rural hospitals spend generates $2.30 in local business activity (S1).
- Hospital leaders say the building is at capacity: about 10,000 emergency visits a year in space for seven patients, rehab therapy and lab space outgrown, surgeries that cannot be scheduled (S3, S4).
- CFO Justin Peters says the same project would cost $94 million in ten years and that piecemeal work would strain departments already at capacity; the district now has approval for a progressive design-build contract with a $55 million guaranteed maximum price (S6).
- The hospital says it is financially sound (about 112 days of cash in October 2025; lost money one year this decade) (S3, S4).
- The Newport City Council passed a resolution supporting the bond, 2-1 with one abstention, on October 5 (S7). A "Yes for Hospital Expansion" committee reported $7,104.04 raised (S9).

## Arguments against

- No statement against was submitted; the pamphlet says no volunteers came forward (S1).
- Voters rejected the bonds in August (54.8% no) (S8).
- About 30% of the hospital's patients live in Idaho, a point raised about charity care and who benefits; CEO Kim Manus says Idaho business supports staff and services that also serve county residents (S3, S6).
- A Newport council member who abstained said nearby hospital services are "extremely helpful" rather than "essential" (S7).

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote adds a new 30-year excess property tax of about $1.47 per $1,000 (S1, S3), well above the district's current levies (S10).
- `spending`: a yes vote expands hospital capacity and services and adds staff (S1, S5, S6).
