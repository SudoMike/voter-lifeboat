52 Local Voters’ Pamphlet Hospital District No. 1 | Proposition No. 1 Hospital District No. 1 Proposition No. 1 Bonds for Expansion and Renovation of Newport Community Hospital The Commission of Pend Oreille County Public Hospital District No. 1 adopted Resolution No. 2026-06 concerning a proposition to finance improvements to Newport Community Hospital. This proposition would authorize the District to expand and renovate Newport Community Hospital, including relocating the main entrance/admitting area and expanding imaging, laboratory, rehabilitative therapy, surgical, emergency, trauma, and behavioral health care services, together with other related capital improvements deemed necessary or advisable by the Commission; issue no more than $51,000,000 of general obligation bonds maturing within 30 years; and levy annual excess property taxes to repay the bonds, all as provided in Resolution No. 2026-06. Should this proposition be: [ ] Approved [ ] Rejected Pend Oreille County Public Hospital District No. 1 (the “District”) operates Newport Hospital and Health Services (the “Hospital”). The District has determined that the existing condition of the Hospital requires that the District expand and renovate the Hospital by relocating the main entrance and admitting area and expanding imaging, laboratory, rehabilitative therapy, surgical, emergency, trauma, and behavioral health care services, together with other related capital improvements deemed necessary or advisable by the Commission (the “Project”). To pay for the costs of the Project, the District is proposing a ballot proposition to be voted on by the voters of the District. This proposition would authorize the District to issue no more than $51,000,000 of general obligation bonds maturing within 30 years to be repaid from an annual excess property tax levy at an estimated rate of $1.47 per $1,000 of assessed value, or an estimated $36.75 per month for a $300,000 home. Exemptions from taxes may be available to certain homeowners. For more information, please call the Assessor’s Office in Pend Oreille County 509-447-6474. 53 Local Voters’ Pamphlet Argument For Approving this bond measure will ensure Newport Hospital District can continue providing safe, modern, and reliable healthcare services close to home. As a community, we need upgrades to meet our growing demands of healthcare. With this bond, the local dollars spent on healthcare will be put back into our community where they are needed. Without these improvements, patients may face delays in care and have more need to travel to Spokane and Coeur d’Alene for essential services. Expanding local capabilities helps keep families together and reduces the burden of travel during times of medical need. This measure prioritizes expansion of emergency department services, helping ensure shorter wait times, improved capacity, and high-quality care during urgent and life-threatening situations. It also strengthens physical and occupational therapy services, supporting recovery for patients of all ages, and improves access to visiting specialists so residents can receive advanced care locally. This bond is a responsible, long-term investment, both for community health and local economy. For every $1 spent by rural hospitals, an additional $2.30 is generated in local business activity. This bond will help recruit and retain healthcare professionals, support timely emergency care, and expand access to rehabilitation and specialty services. Without it, the district may rely more on uncertain state and federal funding with restrictive requirements that may not align with local needs and values. Supporting this bond means better care, closer to home, for Pend Oreille County—now and in the future. 57 Local Voters’ Pamphlet Argument For “Why the O&M Levy is Needed. The Sacheen Water and Sewer District (W&SD) functions on a combination of (1) the balance (after loan payment) of monthly fees paid by local W&SD users and (2) the operations and maintenance (O&M) funds obtained from the annual levy. The levy ensures that a portion of your existing property taxes are allocated to the Sacheen Water and Sewer District. The levy funds enable combatting the invasive weed species in Sacheen Lake, pay for land access and landowner support to combat high lake level due to beaver activity on the West Branch of the Little Spokane River, as well as paying for other O&M costs incurred by the W&SD. Invasive weed management efforts include a survey of the lake to determine where to apply which herbicide, placement of notifications where treatment will occur, and application of the herbicide(s) all performed by a contractor to the W&SD. It is critical that the herbicidal application occurs at a time of the year when it is most effective against the invasive weed(s) it targets thus requiring multiple, cost-incurring applications during the boating season. Without invasive weed treatment, these weeds begin to choke off portions of the lake thus having a significant impact on the lake’s ecosystem and our ability to enjoy Sacheen Lake. The land access agreement allows a designated lead and a volunteer to access the river, clear existing culverts, and remove new start-up dams, and supports storage of the equipment and burning of the debris by the landowner. The Sacheen Lake Association is a strong advocate of the W&SD O&M levy.
