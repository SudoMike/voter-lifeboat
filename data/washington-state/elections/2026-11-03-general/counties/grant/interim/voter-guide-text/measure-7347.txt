## Jurisdiction
City of Moses Lake

## MeasureName
Proposition No. 1

## ShortDescription
PUBLIC SAFETY SALES AND USE TAX

## BallotTitle
The Moses Lake City Council adopted Resolution No. 4042 concerning funding for public safety services.
This proposition would increase the sales and use tax rate by 1/10th of 1% (0.1%, or one cent per $10 sale) to provide funding for public safety services to meet increased demand and calls for service; paying public safety operations, maintenance and capital; providing for law enforcement staffing and retention; and providing other criminal justice City operations and services as permitted under RCW 82.14.450.
Should this proposition be approved?

## ExplanatoryStatement
The City of Moses Lake provides public safety and related services to its residents and businesses through its own, fully-staffed, police department.  The City has identified a need to retain and provide dedicated police officers to keep up with the rising population within the City and increasing calls for service.  In order to pay for public safety expenses, including maintaining current staffing levels and increasing law enforcement costs generally, the City Council has approved a Public Safety Sales Tax ballot measure.  If approved by voters, the City would impose a 0.1% sales tax on retail sales within the City that would be paid by all retail sales consumers, including non-residents. New sales tax revenue would pay costs associated with: providing public safety services; paying public safety operations, maintenance and capital; providing for law enforcement staffing and retention; and providing other public safety operations and services authorized by RCW 82.14.450.  If this measure is approved, the additional 0.1% sales tax would generate approximately $1.2 million dollars in 2027.

## ArgumentFor
MLPD is a full-service police department, providing 24-hour-a-day response to all matters, including those that are not strictly  “police problems.” The department is forecasting three vacancies (one lateral transfer and two retirements) in 2026 that it is not planning to replace due to citywide budget cuts. It is important to maintain staffing so the department can continue to provide these services that make Moses Lake a great place to live.
A 0.1% sales tax increase ($1 on every $1,000 spent) is one of the most equitable ways to maintain vital funding for the police department. As a sales tax, it does not apply to groceries or gasoline, and the burden is shared broadly, including by visitors, shoppers, and those who live outside city limits, all of whom use city services and roads, but don’t pay Moses Lake property taxes.
