## Background

Kelly Raap, 55, of rural Ephrata, prefers the Republican Party and is a first-time candidate for elected office [S1][S4]. She has co-owned and run Raap Land & Livestock Inc., her family's farm and ranch, for 33 years [S1]. Her husband is a sixth-generation Grant County farmer [S4]. She provides bookkeeping and payroll services to businesses statewide through a Ritzville-based accounting firm [S4]. Her pamphlet lists five years as an accounting assistant at Leffel, Otis & Warwick, P.S., and 12 years with the Ephrata School District as a para-educator, middle-school secretary and payroll assistant [S1]. She also worked as an auto claims insurance adjuster [S4]. She holds an AA from Walla Walla Community College and also attended Central Wyoming College [S1][S4]. She serves on the Washington Cattlemen's Association Endowment Trust Fund committee and the Grant County Cattlewomen's Association. She volunteers with the Moses Lake Round-Up Demolition Derby and the Columbia Basin Cancer Foundation auction [S1][S4].

She led the certified District 3 primary with 1,377 votes to Shannon Durfee's 1,233. Janice Flynn (805) and Seth Weeks (790) were eliminated [S5].

## Positions

### County finances
- Says agriculture and bookkeeping taught her "careful financial management" and that she knows how to "balance a budget, make practical decisions and plan for the future without wasting valuable resources" [S1].
- "I take an approach that just because you have money in the bank does not mean it has to be spent" [S6].
- Wants residents to know "how decisions are made and how taxpayer dollars are spent" [S1].

### Economy, infrastructure and public safety
- Pledges to protect the rural economy and to support "farmers, ranchers, small and large businesses and working families" [S1][S2].
- "It's important to invest in public safety, roads, and infrastructure so Grant County remains a great place to live and raise a family" [S4].
- Names economic development, infrastructure, public safety and county spending as major concerns but declined to rank one first [S6].

### Approach to the office
- Would start by learning county operations and building relationships with partner agencies: "I'm more of a let's learn how things are going and then decide how adjustments are to be made" [S6].
- Promises to make "decisions based on facts, not politics" and to be accessible to residents [S4].
