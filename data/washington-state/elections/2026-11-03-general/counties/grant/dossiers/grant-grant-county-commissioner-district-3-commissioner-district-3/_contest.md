## What the office does
The three Grant County commissioners adopt and monitor the county budget ($325.5 million in 2026 across about two dozen offices and departments and nearly 800 employees). They take part in labor negotiations, adopt traffic, zoning, planning and public-safety ordinances for unincorporated areas, oversee roads and public works, and sit on regional boards including the Board of Health and Grant Transit Authority (S5). The winner takes office Jan. 1, 2027 for four years, serving with incumbents Kevin Burgess and Rob Jones (S5).

## Who votes
Under RCW 36.32.040, only a commissioner district's voters nominate its commissioner candidates "to be voted for at the following general election" (S4). Only District 3 voters (the southwest of the county, including Quincy, Royal City, Mattawa and Desert Aire) voted in the August primary. The general election is county-wide (S5, S6). VoteWA's general-election candidate list files the race as "Countywide" (S3), its voters' guide titles it "Countywide Commissioner District #3" (S1), and the county's sample ballot lists it under County Partisan Offices on the published sample ballot (precinct 0001.01) (S2).

## Race dynamics
This is an open seat: Commissioner Cindy Carter of Royal City is leaving after five terms and 20 years (S5). Both finalists prefer the Republican Party and are first-time candidates for county office (S1). Kelly Raap of rural Ephrata, a ranch co-owner and bookkeeper, led the certified District 3 primary with 1,377 votes. Shannon Durfee of Quincy, a Quincy School Board director, small-business owner and hospital-foundation director, followed with 1,233 (S6). Janice Flynn (805), whom Carter endorsed, and Seth Weeks (790) were eliminated (S5, S6). Both finalists list similar priorities: fiscal responsibility, infrastructure, public safety, economic development and planning for growth (S5). Neither has published detailed policy proposals.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** neither has held county office. Durfee holds an elected school board seat and leads the Quincy Rotary Club. Raap is a first-time candidate for any office.
2. **Spending:** Raap's restraint language is more specific: "just because you have money in the bank does not mean it has to be spent." Durfee pledges fiscal responsibility in general terms.
3. **Growth:** Durfee stresses managing growth so it benefits current residents and seizing economic opportunities. Raap stresses protecting the rural and agricultural economy. Neither has a stated position on zoning or housing.
4. **Taxes, housing, climate, reform, safety:** no differentiating evidence found.
