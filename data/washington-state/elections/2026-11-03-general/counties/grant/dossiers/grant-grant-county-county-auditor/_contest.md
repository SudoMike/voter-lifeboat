## What the office does

The county auditor records land documents such as deeds and surveys, issues marriage licenses, runs vehicle licensing under a contract with the state, administers elections, and manages the county's financial reporting and accounting systems [S6]. The Grant County auditor has a staff of 14 [S4]. The ballot lists the race as a short and full four-year term: the winner finishes the remainder of the current term and then serves the new term beginning January 1, 2027 [S2][S3].

## Race dynamics

Auditor Michele Jaderlund, a Republican first elected in 2014, retired June 30, six months before her term ended [S3]. Because the office is partisan, state law let the county's Republican precinct committee officers nominate up to three candidates for the appointment [S3]. After public interviews, the county commissioners appointed Shannon Falstad as interim auditor effective July 1 [S3][S6]. Falstad, Darci Homesley and Andrew Koeppen, all Republicans, ran in the primary. GCJ.news reported that the Grant County Republican Party Central Committee endorsed both Falstad and Homesley [S4]. In the certified count Falstad led with 7,188 votes to Homesley's 4,283; Koeppen, a former county GOP chair, had 3,905 and was eliminated [S4][S5].

The two finalists are both Republicans from inside county government. Falstad has about 30 years in county offices, including 20 in the auditor's office, and stresses continuity and staff retention [S1][S6]. Homesley cites 30-plus years as a business owner and five years working with Grant County. She campaigns on fiscal responsibility, internal controls and a "strictly non-partisan" office, and did not answer GCJ.news's candidate questionnaire [S4].

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Record vs. renewal:** the appointed incumbent with two decades in the auditor's office, against a challenger who has not worked in the office and runs on business and accounting experience.
2. **Spending:** Homesley's platform centers on internal controls to prevent waste, auditing county expenditures and "living within a budget." Falstad's statement stresses efficient operations and reviewing policies but makes no spending-restraint pledge.
3. **Elections administration:** both pledge transparency. Homesley pledges a "strictly non-partisan" approach to election oversight. Falstad stresses continuity in election administration. Neither has published a detailed elections platform.
