## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would let Grant County Public Hospital District No. 4, which operates McKay Healthcare & Rehabilitation Center in Soap Lake, issue up to $9,940,000 in general obligation bonds maturing within 30 years, repaid by an annual excess property tax [S1][S2]. The money would help pay for "a 16-bed assisted living unit, a 16-bed memory care unit, and other capital improvements" [S1].

- The explanatory statement says the other improvements are planned to include "an intergenerational community wellness center with licensed daycare, meeting, and classroom spaces and dining" for residents and the community [S1].
- The commission adopted Resolution 2026-4 on May 19, 2026. Commissioner Kim Randolph-Meaney moved, Steve Wellein seconded, and it passed with all commissioners present in support [S5].
- RCW 70.44.110 lets a hospital district issue general obligation bonds for health care facilities, maturing in no more than 30 years [S11].
- Passage needs a 60% yes vote, and turnout of at least 40% of those who voted in the last state general election, under RCW 84.52.056 [S12].

**The project, per the district.**
- It is the first phase of a planned "Continuing Care Retirement Community" campus in Soap Lake, with future phases adding independent living and affordable housing [S3].
- The page describes "more than 40,000 square feet of community areas and private apartments," and a community center for gatherings, art classes or child day care [S3].
- The district projects nearly 30 permanent jobs and 160 construction jobs [S3][S4].
- Administrator Audra Gutierrez said: "Memory care is a coveted resource, and there are just not many openings available" [S4].
- The district reported that residents 65 and older were about 11.6% of Grant County's population in 2000 and about 15% in 2024 [S4].

## Cost

- The estimated levy is $0.61 per $1,000 of assessed value, or about $15.27 a month for a $300,000 home [S1]. The Herald reports this as $183.24 a year [S4].
- The bonds may run up to 30 years [S1].
- The explanatory statement says property-tax exemptions may be available to some homeowners through the Grant County Assessor [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This is a new bond levy. The statement for says McKay "has never had a bond increase" since 1938 [S1]. No existing district bond levy is mentioned in the pamphlet, the district's page or the news coverage [S1][S3][S4].

**Other funding.** The explanatory statement says "the majority of the costs of the Project will be funded from grants and low interest loans," with the bonds paying the remainder [S1]. No source found states the total project cost. District minutes describe the other sources being sought:
- A Feb. 23, 2026 presentation by D.A. Davidson covered debt capacity, "a two-year LTGO line of credit during construction," and "projected non-voted USDA and voted bond payments" [S6].
- A $319,500 "SIP" award was recommended in March to cover project management costs [S7].
- A "$3M CDS request" (federal congressionally directed spending) was being started through Rep. Dan Newhouse in April [S8].
- In May the board heard an update on "the USDA loan application and CDBG grant writing" [S5].
- In March the board discussed "the reduced funding available under the tax-levy bond" [S7].

Gutierrez told the Herald the bond amount was chosen "because we could assign funding from other sources to get us to the number we needed, but also because we felt like it wasn't a giant ask." She said the district hopes "to backfill everything with grants and congressional funding" [S4]. If the measure fails, she said, the district would "have to seek other funding sources, which will just delay the project longer" [S4].

**Same-ballot timing with Fire District 7.** The Fire District 7 chief wrote to the hospital board about the timing of the fire district's EMS levy on the same general-election ballot [S9]. At the fire district's June 17 meeting, a commenter named Cliff Sears said that putting the EMS levy and "the McKay bond" on the same ballot "could create financial hesitation and potentially result in a hard 'no' vote" [S10]. The hospital district's minutes list a Cliff Sears as its legal counsel [S5][S7]; this research did not confirm they are the same person.

## Arguments for

The statement in favor (no author or committee is listed) says [S1]:
- "70% of our population over the age of 65 will require skilled services at some point," and the bond keeps those services "close to home."
- The project would add "daycare options for children and elderly respite, where none are otherwise available."
- It would "create jobs, and provide an annual boost of $4.5 million to our local economy."
- McKay "has been a responsible steward serving our community since 1938 and has never had a bond increase," and has pursued "the majority of funding independently."

## Arguments against

The VoteWA record has no statement against and lists no committee against [S1]. The board's April planning listed "a pro and con statement for the voter's pamphlet" among its outreach items [S8]. No organized opposition was found in news coverage [S4].

## Endorsements and campaign

- PDC lists a local ballot measure committee, "Team McKay," registered June 1, 2026 to support Hospital District 4 Proposition No. 1, treasurer Matt Eisen, mini reporting [S13].
- Corinne Isaak of the Columbia Basin Foundation presented the bond timeline, community outreach plan, talking points, billboard and rack-card designs to the board [S8].
- No endorsements were found.

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote adds a new excess property tax of about $0.61 per $1,000 for up to 30 years to repay up to $9.94 million in bonds; the district has had no bond before [S1]. A new bond levy maps to +2.
- `spending`: the bonds pay for new senior-care capacity and community space, but the district says most of the cost comes from grants and loans [S1][S3]; no mapping.
