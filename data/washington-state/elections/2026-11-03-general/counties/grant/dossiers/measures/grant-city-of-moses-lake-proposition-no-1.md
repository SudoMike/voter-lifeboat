## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would raise the sales and use tax in the City of Moses Lake by 0.1% ("one cent per $10 sale") under RCW 82.14.450 [S1]. The money would pay for "public safety services to meet increased demand and calls for service; paying public safety operations, maintenance and capital; providing for law enforcement staffing and retention; and providing other criminal justice City operations and services" [S1]. The ballot title sets no expiration date [S1].

- The City Council approved sending the measure to voters 7-0 on June 9, 2026 [S3]. The ballot title cites Resolution No. 4042 [S1], as did Source ONE News's preview of the meeting [S4]. The resolution copy filed with Grant County Elections is headed "Resolution 4051" but carries the same ballot title [S2]. This research did not resolve the difference in numbers.
- A city press release quoted by the Herald says the money would go to "police department operations, including retaining police officers for patrol, responding to 911 calls, and criminal investigations" [S3].
- City attorney Katherine Kenison said the money would have to be spent on public safety services [S3].
- Passage needs a simple majority, per the city's filing [S2] and RCW 82.14.450(2)(a) [S9].

**Why on the ballot.** The filed resolution says House Bill 2015 (2025) "authorizes the City Council to impose the public safety sales and use tax councilmanically, without a vote of the people; however, the City Council wishes to submit the issue to the voters for approval" [S2]. Source ONE News reported that city documents say the city "prefers to seek voter approval rather than adopt the tax directly" [S4].

## Cost

- One cent on a $10 purchase, or "$1 on every $1,000 spent" in the statement for [S1].
- The city estimates about $1.2 million in 2027 [S1][S3].
- The Department of Revenue's Q4 2026 table lists Moses Lake (location code 1309) at 8.5% combined (2.0% local plus 6.5% state) [S8]. The measure would raise it to 8.6% (computed for this dossier).
- RCW 82.14.450 exempts motor-vehicle sales and leases of up to 36 months [S9]. The statement for says the tax "does not apply to groceries or gasoline" [S1].
- Under RCW 82.14.450(7), a city keeps 85% of the proceeds and sends 15% to the county [S9]. No source says whether the $1.2 million estimate is before or after the county's share [S1][S3].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This is a new tax, not a renewal; the city would "impose a 0.1% sales tax" [S1]. No existing Moses Lake tax under RCW 82.14.450 is mentioned in the pamphlet, the resolution or the news coverage [S1][S2][S3].

**The city's budget gap.**
- The filed resolution says about 33% of the general fund goes to police and criminal justice. It says the city faces "a structural deficit" from state property-tax growth limits, state mandates, inflation, population growth and rising service demands [S2].
- City Manager Rob Karlinsey said the city had already cut about $4 million "without affecting service levels," but projected a $4.6 million deficit in 2027, growing to $8 million by 2031 without action [S7].
- The Herald reported a 2026 combined general-fund and street-fund deficit of about $1 million, paid from reserves [S3].
- The statement for says the police department expects three vacancies in 2026 (one lateral transfer and two retirements) "that it is not planning to replace due to citywide budget cuts" [S1].

**How the council got here.**
- In mid-April the city said outreach (a survey, an online budget tool and a town hall) showed residents open to "a balanced approach that includes both service level reductions and modest tax increases." Council members indicated a 0.1% police sales tax "would likely be placed before voters" [S6].
- A city survey (March 18 to April 1, Fulcrum Strategy Group) found 73% support for a 0.1% sales tax for fire and police operations; the consultant cautioned that "I'm not telling you it would get 70 at the ballot box" [S5].
- In April the council leaned toward a budget plan without a property-tax levy lid lift. Mayor Dustin Swartz favored the no-levy option and was open to a public safety sales tax if paired with additional savings, the outlet reported [S7].
- Councilman Victor Lombardi said property taxes should be a last resort: "I'm just here to say that should be the last resort" [S7].
- Councilman Jeremy Davis said "I believe we can tighten our belts" and the city should avoid new taxes if possible [S7].
- Councilman Joel Graves said "I am okay with a sales tax increase because I think all of our users participate in it" [S7].
- At the June 9 vote, Councilman Mark Fancher said "I don't like having to charge more taxes to anybody," but that a sales tax spreads the cost to visitors and residents of the urban growth area, unlike a property tax [S3].
- The same sustainability plan includes moving the Larson Recreation Center, museum and ice rink to nonprofit operators by 2029 [S7].

## Arguments for

The statement in favor (no author or committee is listed) says [S1]:
- The Moses Lake Police Department is "a full-service police department, providing 24-hour-a-day response."
- The department forecasts three vacancies in 2026 it is not planning to fill because of budget cuts, and "it is important to maintain staffing."
- A 0.1% sales tax "is one of the most equitable ways to maintain vital funding," shared by "visitors, shoppers, and those who live outside city limits... but don't pay Moses Lake property taxes."

## Arguments against

The VoteWA record has no statement against and lists no committee against [S1]. No organized opposition was found in news coverage [S3][S4]. During budget planning, two council members said they preferred cuts and efficiencies to new taxes, though both spoke mainly about property taxes [S7].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote creates a new 0.1% sales tax with no stated end date, raising Moses Lake's combined rate from 8.5% to 8.6% and about $1.2 million a year [S1][S8]. New public safety sales taxes elsewhere (Liberty Lake, Milton, SeaTac) map to +2. Scored +2.
- `spending`: the city frames the money as keeping current police staffing amid a structural deficit and after $4 million in cuts, not as expansion [S1][S2][S7]; no mapping.
- `local-control`: the council could have imposed a councilmanic tax under HB 2015 but chose a public vote [S2][S4]. That is a choice of process, not a shift between local and state decisions; no mapping.
- `safety`: the measure funds police staffing, but the rubric does not apply the `safety` axis to measures; no mapping.
