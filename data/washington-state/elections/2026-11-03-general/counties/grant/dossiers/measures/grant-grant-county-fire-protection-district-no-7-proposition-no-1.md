## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would replace the last two years of Grant County Fire Protection District No. 7's existing emergency medical services (EMS) levy with a new six-year EMS levy of up to $0.50 per $1,000 of assessed value [S1]. It would be first levied in 2026 and collected from 2027 [S1]. The district, based in Soap Lake, says the money is "exclusively for emergency medical services," including basic, intermediate and advanced life support [S1].

- The board adopted Resolution No. 07082026-A on July 8, 2026. Commissioner Burts moved, Commissioner Wilson seconded, and the motion carried [S12]. The adoption date on the filed copy is left blank [S2].
- The resolution says that if the measure passes, the 2022 EMS levy ends two years early and the new $0.50 levy runs six years. "If the voters do not approve this measure, the existing EMS levy will be levied 2026 and 2027 for collection in each succeeding year" [S2]. (The resolution's heading says it replaces "the final year" of the current levy; its Section 4 and the ballot title say the final two years [S1][S2].)
- RCW 84.52.069 allows EMS levies of up to $0.50 per $1,000 for six or ten years or permanently. The money may be used only for emergency medical care and services [S14].
- The resolution treats this as a "subsequent approval" needing a simple majority under RCW 84.52.069(2) [S2]. The statute requires 60% for a first six-year levy and a simple majority for a later one [S14].

## Cost

- The pamphlet says the levy "would not exceed $150.00 each year or approximately $12.50 per month" on a $300,000 property [S1].
- Chief Erick Brittain said the $0.50 levy would raise about $250,000 a year, and the $0.25 levy about $125,000 [S3]. The March board minutes give the same figures [S9].
- **What a yes vote changes, compared with a no vote.** If the measure fails, the district resumes the $0.25 levy for 2027 and 2028 collections [S2][S3]. Against that baseline, a yes vote adds about $0.25 per $1,000 ($75 a year on $300,000) for those two years. It then continues $0.50 per $1,000 through 2032 collections, after the 2022 levy would have ended (computed for this dossier from [S1], [S2] and [S5]).
- **A conflicting figure.** The June Herald article says the 50-cent levy is "about $75 per year for a $300,000 property" [S3]. The pamphlet's figure for the $0.50 rate is $150 [S1]; $75 matches the $0.25 rate in the 2022 pamphlet [S5].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

**The 2022 EMS levy.** In November 2022 voters approved a six-year EMS levy of up to $0.25 per $1,000, collected from 2023, at about $75 a year on a $300,000 home [S5]. It passed with 68.66% (1,010 yes, 461 no) [S6].

**The 2023 fire levy and the suspension.** In November 2023 the district asked voters to raise its regular property tax levy from $0.57 to $1.30 per $1,000 for 2024 collection, with a 6% limit factor for five years [S7]. That pamphlet said: "If approved the Board would no longer levy the $.25 EMS levy approved by the voters in 2022 thereby reducing the impact on District taxpayers" [S7]. The lid lift passed with 64.64% (554 to 303) [S8]. The 2026 pamphlet says the board "temporarily suspend[ed]" the EMS levy in 2024 [S1]. The Herald reported that the district stopped collecting it after voters approved the fire levy [S3].

**The 2026 plan.**
- In March the board approved a resolution for the August ballot to "replace current EMS Levy from uncollected $0.25 to $0.50 for six years." The minutes say the levy "would help us fund almost 2 full time positions and get away from the grant funding that is currently shut down and not reliable" [S9].
- In April the board rescinded the August-ballot resolution [S10].
- The Herald reported that the district will resume collecting the $0.25 levy next year whatever the vote. Brittain said EMS is about 85% of calls [S3].
- EMS calls rose from roughly 300 in 2023 to 568 in 2024 and about 580 to 590 in 2025 [S3].
- Two full-time EMS positions are funded by a federal SAFER grant that "will sunset" in 2027 [S3].
- The $0.25 levy would fund one position and medical supplies. The $0.50 levy would fund both positions, another ambulance (including staging an older unit near Sun Lakes in peak season) and equipment for intermediate life support [S3].
- Brittain said that when district crews are busy, medical calls fall to Lifeline ambulances from Ephrata or Moses Lake, "a 20 to 30 minute response time" [S3].

**Same-ballot timing.** At the June 17 board meeting, a commenter named Cliff Sears said putting the EMS levy and "the McKay bond" (Hospital District No. 4) on the same ballot "could create financial hesitation and potentially result in a hard 'no' vote" [S11].

## Arguments for

No statement for or against appears in the VoteWA record, and no committees are listed [S1]. The district asked residents to volunteer for pro and con committees by Aug. 4, 2026 [S4]. The district's case, from its own statements:
- "Our call volume is increasing every single month, EMS‑wise and fire‑wise" (Brittain) [S3].
- "In 2027 the SAFER grant will sunset. If we don't get another grant or another source of money, we'll have to decrease services" (Brittain) [S3].
- "Our people deserve better... we need to try to do both when we can" (Brittain, on handling simultaneous fire and medical calls) [S3].
- At the June 17 meeting, resident George Montgomery said the EMS levy "is necessary and not a concern for him" [S11].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed [S1]. At the June 17 meeting, Cliff Sears said he and others "support the district and the levy" but worried about two tax measures on one ballot [S11]. No organized opposition was found in news coverage [S3][S4].

## Endorsements and campaign

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote doubles the district's voter-approved EMS rate from $0.25 to $0.50 per $1,000 and extends it four years past the 2022 levy, about $250,000 a year [S1][S3][S5]. The 2022 levy had been suspended since 2024 under the 2023 lid-lift promise [S7]. Against the no-vote baseline of resuming $0.25, the measure adds $0.25 for two years and $0.50 for four more (computed). Larger tax: +2. Its legal form as a replacement levy is the case for +1.
- `spending`: the district frames the money as keeping two grant-funded EMS positions and adding an ambulance [S3]; no mapping.
