## What it does

Advisory Proposition No. 1 asks Grant County voters whether a 0.1% sales and use tax under RCW 82.14.460 "should be adopted within Grant County" [S1][S3]. The money could be used only for "chemical dependency or mental health treatment programs and services" and "therapeutic court programs and services" [S1].

**The vote does not impose a tax.** The explanatory statement says the measure "is advisory only and would not itself impose any tax; the Board may consider the results and later decide whether to adopt an ordinance imposing the tax" [S1].

- The commissioners adopted Resolution No. 26-046-CC on July 14, 2026. Commissioners Kevin R. Burgess (chair), Rob Jones (vice-chair) and Cindy Carter are each marked "Yea" [S2].
- The resolution says the board "has the statutory authority under RCW 82.14.460 to impose this sales and use tax by ordinance without obtaining voter approval," and wants voters' advisory opinion before it decides [S2]. RCW 82.14.460 lets a county legislative authority "authorize, fix, and impose" the tax, with no election required [S5].
- After the election, the board "may thereafter adopt or decline to adopt an ordinance imposing the tax" [S2].
- County Administrator Tom Gaines told the Columbia Basin Herald: "Because it's a new tax, they didn't feel like it's right to do it without hearing from voters" [S4].
- Gaines said that because the commissioners have not decided whether to impose the tax, how the money would be spent "hasn't been determined" [S4].

**What the statute allows.** Under RCW 82.14.460, the money may pay for treatment services, case management, transportation and housing that are part of a coordinated treatment program, and for therapeutic courts [S5]. It may also pay for new construction and changes to facilities for those programs [S5]. A county that adopts the tax must set up a therapeutic court component for dependency proceedings [S5].

## Cost

- The rate is one-tenth of one percent, "just one penny for every $10 spent" in the statement for [S1].
- Gaines said the tax is projected to raise about $2.7 million a year [S4].
- The Department of Revenue's Q4 2026 table lists combined rates in Grant County from 8.2% (unincorporated areas, Quincy, Warden and several towns) to 8.5% (Moses Lake) [S6]. If the board later imposed the tax, each would rise by 0.1 percentage point (computed for this dossier).

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This would be a new tax; no existing Grant County tax under RCW 82.14.460 is mentioned in the pamphlet, the resolution or the news coverage [S1][S2][S4]. Gaines called it "a new tax" [S4].

- RCW 82.14.460 says the money must pay for "new or expanded programs and services." A county with more than 25,000 people that first imposes the tax after 2011 may use up to 50% to replace existing funding for the first three years, and up to 25% in years four and five [S5].
- Grant County already runs a public behavioral-health agency, Renew. County staff told commissioners on Sept. 22, 2026 that demand for its mental-health services "continues to exceed current clinical capacity," and the board approved a part-time therapist paid for by a Carelon contract [S7]. No source ties that staffing to this measure.
- The Moses Lake public safety sales tax (Moses Lake Proposition No. 1) is on the same ballot; Moses Lake shoppers would pay both if the city measure passes and the county later adopts this tax [S3].

## Arguments for

The statement in favor (no author or committee is listed) says [S1]:
- "A small local investment can make a meaningful difference for Grant County."
- The money would support "mental health care, substance use treatment, therapeutic courts, crisis intervention, and family services."
- "Grant County is one of only 10 counties in Washington—and the largest—not using this local funding option." (This research did not verify the count.)
- The revenue "stays in Grant County" and early treatment "reduces pressure on law enforcement, emergency services, and the criminal justice system."

Gaines said: "It would be nice to have funding in place. I think there are numerous entities in Grant County that would benefit" [S4].

## Arguments against

The VoteWA record has no statement against and lists no committee against [S1]. The resolution directed the board to appoint pro and con committees by Aug. 4, 2026 [S2]. No organized opposition was found in news coverage [S4].
