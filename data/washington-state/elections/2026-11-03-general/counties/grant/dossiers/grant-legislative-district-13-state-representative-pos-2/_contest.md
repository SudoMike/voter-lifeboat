## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including the tax structure, water and agriculture policy, K-12 funding and policy, and criminal-justice statutes.

## Race dynamics
An open seat: Rep. Alex Ybarra, who held Position 2, is running for the 13th District Senate seat (S2). Two Republicans advanced from a three-Republican primary: former Moses Lake City Council member and registered nurse Deanna Martinez (37.23%) and Ellensburg City Council member and technology consultant Joshua Thompson (34.15%), with Moses Lake council member Don Myers third (26.79%), per preliminary results (S2).

The two share most positions: both oppose the 2026 income tax on income over $1 million, want lower taxes and spending restraint, support law enforcement and back the Odessa Groundwater Replacement Program (S1; deanna-martinez.md S6, S7; joshua-thompson.md S2, S3). They differ in emphasis and coalition. Martinez puts repealing the income tax and the Climate Commitment Act first and centers local control; she is endorsed by several sitting Republican legislators (deanna-martinez.md S1, S5, S7). Thompson runs as a "Constitutional Conservative" against the status quo, with endorsements from the Grant, Kittitas and Douglas County Republican parties, and his platform speaks directly to parents' rights in schools and girls' sports (joshua-thompson.md S1, S2). Thompson has raised $28.5k and Martinez $21.9k (deanna-martinez.md S11; joshua-thompson.md S6). Neither has named a position on the three statewide initiatives.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Parents and schools:** Thompson's platform says parents' rights are being "dissolved"; Martinez has no stated position.
2. **Social:** Thompson objects to boys in girls' sports and locker rooms; Martinez lists "protect girls' sports" as a one-line priority.
3. **Climate:** Martinez pledges to repeal the Climate Commitment Act; Thompson criticizes "green mandates" without a CCA pledge.
4. **Record vs. renewal:** both hold or held city office; Thompson frames himself as a challenger to "the system," while Martinez stresses working with the majority and has legislators' endorsements.
5. **Local control:** central for Martinez, also stated by Thompson.
6. **Tech:** Thompson names AI and tech industries as the district's main growth opportunity.
