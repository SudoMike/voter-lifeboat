## Background
Deanna Martinez (Prefers Republican Party) is a former Moses Lake City Council member and a registered nurse with more than 30 years of experience in the 13th District [S1][S3][S10]. She earned an associate degree in nursing through Big Bend Community College and Columbia Basin College [S1]. She grew up in Warden, where her father had been a migrant farmworker, and helped keep the books for her parents' small trucking business [S3][S9]. She was formerly a campaign assistant for Rep. Alex Ybarra, whose seat this is [S1][S10]. She lists volunteer work with the Boys and Girls Club, Columbia Basin Cancer Foundation, Soroptimists, the Grant County Fair and Kiwanis [S1]. She has not served in the Legislature [S1]. In the August 4 primary she placed first among three Republicans with 9,687 votes (37.23%) to Joshua Thompson's 8,886 (34.15%) and Don Myers's 6,970 (26.79%), per preliminary results [S9].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** her statement says her "top priority is abolishing the unconstitutional income tax the Democrat majority sold us as a 'millionaire's tax'," which she says will drive business out of the state [S1]. Listed "stop the income tax" and decreasing taxes among her top three first-session priorities [S7].
- **Spending and budget:** says lawmakers should identify waste before considering tax increases: "Let's get our spending under control first" [S9]. Wants state spending reallocated "to address our needs not our desires" [S7]; lists fiscal accountability as a priority [S4].
- **Local control:** names returning and maintaining local control as her first priority, opposing "one-size fits all mandates" from Olympia [S7]. Her pamphlet says she would help local governments retain control of their decisions [S1]. At a July forum she said "Rural Washington needs to be able to decide what we're going to focus on" [S8]. Opposes the 2026 law creating a governor-appointed board that can decertify and remove elected county sheriffs, calling it a local decision [S10].
- **Climate and energy:** says she will "work to repeal the Climate Commitment Act," which she says raises gas prices and hurts rural Washington [S1]. Has said Climate Commitment Act revenue should be used for road maintenance [S10].
- **Housing:** says the majority party has taken local control out of addressing housing; would repeal regulations that add to building costs, and names shrub-steppe policies and urban growth area restrictions as barriers [S6]. Her site describes her approach as "state goals with local flexibility" and supports removing barriers to building [S4].
- **Public safety:** lists public safety and support for law enforcement among her priorities [S4][S10].
- **Health care:** testified in Olympia for the RN multistate compact license and supports nontraditional nursing and physician training programs [S6]. Would restore state Medicaid funding, which she says the majority cut, by redirecting existing budget dollars rather than raising taxes [S6].
- **Water and agriculture:** supports further development of the Odessa Groundwater Replacement Program and water storage [S6][S8]; says she will always support and protect farmers [S1].
- **Approach:** noting Republicans are the minority in the Legislature, said "You're going to have to work with a majority" [S9].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** her priorities page says she will "protect girls' sports" [S4].
- **Parents and schools:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
