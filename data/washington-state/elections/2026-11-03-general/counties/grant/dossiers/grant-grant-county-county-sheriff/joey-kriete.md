## Background

Joey Kriete, 54, prefers the Republican Party. He grew up in Ephrata and lives in Moses Lake [S1][S5]. He was elected sheriff in 2022 and is seeking a second four-year term [S4][S5]. He spent 32 years in the sheriff's office before that [S5]. He says he has 33+ years in law enforcement and 15+ at the executive level [S1][S2]. Before becoming sheriff he was chief deputy of investigations and of corrections, a major crimes detective, a patrol and traffic sergeant, and a collision investigator [S2][S5]. He is vice president of the Washington Association of Sheriffs and Police Chiefs and its president-elect for 2026-27 [S1][S5]. He sits on the Grant County Economic Development Council and Multi Agency Communications Center boards and has coached youth and high school sports for 30+ years [S1][S5].

He placed second in the certified August primary with 5,734 votes, 105 behind Darrik Gregg [S6].

## Positions

### Public safety and enforcement
- Cites "proactive policing, reducing crime," and a Crime Reduction Team he created to target property crimes and thefts, especially in rural farming areas [S1][S5].
- Says the office "expanded mental health response services" and built partnerships "addressing behavioral health and the opioid crisis" [S1][S12]. His website says corrections works with the courts and community partners "to provide pathways for recovery" [S2].
- Said the office cooperates with federal agencies, including ICE, on criminal matters. He drew the state-law line between civil immigration violations and re-entry after deportation, which is a crime: "If it's a criminal investigation, yes, we could and there is no reason we wouldn't assist" [S7].
- Backed Flock license-plate-reading cameras at the Sept. 10 debate. The outlet's headline described him as urging caution [S13].

### Budget and resources
- Says his most likely unpopular early decision will be how to allocate limited resources in a budget year with "several funding unknowns" as the new jail opens. He would protect core services and keep operations "financially sustainable," even if that means saying no to programs, positions or equipment [S10].
- Says he has spent four years "balancing operational needs with fiscal responsibility" [S10].

### Leadership and accountability
- Calls preparing the next generation of supervisors the office's biggest leadership need: "My responsibility isn't simply to show employees I can still do their job. It's to prepare them to someday do mine" [S8].
- Defines accountability as meeting the 140+ standards of state law-enforcement accreditation, checked by outside assessors [S11].
- On the Deputy Sheriffs' Association's no-confidence vote (31 no-confidence, 6 confidence, 10 abstained), he said most concerns were management-rights or collective-bargaining issues. He said he asked for details by a deadline and received none [S7]. In July he called the vote "a significant expression of concern" and said he had met with the association's leadership [S5].
- In January he demoted Chief Deputy Josh Sainsbury to sergeant after Sainsbury announced a run against him, calling it a "difficult, painful decision" to keep "a foundation of trust" on his command team [S4]. At the debate he said it "had to do with what was best for the community" [S7].

## Record

- Grant County Sheriff since January 2023 [S1][S5].
- Says the office went from 25 employees short to hiring "dozens of new employees," many of them experienced lateral deputies, and expanded peer-support and wellness programs [S2][S12].
- Created the Crime Reduction Team and kept state accreditation [S1][S5].
- He and his staff worked on the county's $155 million sheriff's office and jail, completed in summer 2026 [S5].

## Endorsements

All three Grant County commissioners endorsed him personally, according to a campaign graphic on Facebook. Gregg supporters criticized it for using the commissioners' titles and the courthouse as a backdrop; Gregg himself did not join that criticism publicly [S14].
