## Background

Darrik Gregg, 51, of Moses Lake, prefers the Republican Party. He is a patrol sergeant with the Moses Lake Police Department [S1][S6]. He began as a Royal City patrol officer in 2003 and joined the Grant County Sheriff's Office as a deputy in May 2006. He was promoted to sergeant and, in December 2016, to chief deputy over emergency management, crisis response and special operations [S5][S6]. He resigned from the sheriff's office in 2021 [S5]. He is certified by the state Criminal Justice Training Commission and is finishing a business management degree [S1][S9]. He started the local Shop with a Cop program in 2015 and serves on the Behind the Badge Foundation's line-of-duty death response team [S1][S6]. He has not held elected office [S1].

He led the certified August primary with 5,839 votes to Sheriff Joey Kriete's 5,734; Sgt. Josh Sainsbury (4,518) was eliminated [S7].

### Lawsuit against the county
Gregg and another former chief deputy, Dustin Canfield, resigned in 2021. In a 2022 lawsuit they allege that then-Sheriff Tom Jones retaliated against them for raising concerns that his brother, Chief Deputy Ken Jones, misused leave time while working for a private security firm. Both Joneses denied any impropriety [S5][S6]. The case moved to federal court in March 2024 alleging civil-rights violations. The defendants are the sheriff's office, the county, Tom Jones and former interim Sheriff Ryan Rectenwald, and a February 2026 trial was put on hold pending Ninth Circuit review [S6]. At the Sept. 10 debate the moderator cited claims that the county has spent about $400,000 on the defense. Gregg said he would keep pursuing the case if elected: "I started it, and I'm going to finish it. It's not about me. It's about making sure this never happens to anybody else again" [S8].

## Positions

### Leadership of the sheriff's office
- Runs on "servant leadership," saying leadership "is about being present, listening to your employees" and that "Grant County needs a fresh approach to leadership" [S9]. His website pledges to rebuild "confidence in the Sheriff's Office" through open communication and clear expectations of professionalism [S2].
- In his first six months he would conduct "a comprehensive review of the Sheriff's Office, including staffing, policies, response times, spending, and communication between leadership and employees," and may change policies based on it [S11].
- Cites the deputies' association no-confidence vote as reason to "take a hard look" inside the agency. He says employees "will have a direct voice with the Sheriff" and that his faith will guide how he leads [S12]. At the debate he said the vote shows deputies "want new leadership" [S8].
- Says he is "not running simply to change things for the sake of change" but would build on what works [S13].

### Public safety and enforcement
- Says he is "tough on crime while treating people with respect and dignity" and will "protect victims and hold criminals accountable" [S1][S2].
- Pledges better staffing, training and support for deputies to reduce burnout [S2], and frames public safety as "a partnership with residents" [S2].
- Said he would work with ICE, the FBI and DEA when criminal activity is involved: "I will always support any law enforcement agency that comes into Grant County with the mission of taking violent offenders into custody" [S8].
- Backed Flock license-plate-reading cameras at the Sept. 10 debate. The outlet's headline described him as "all in" on them [S14].
