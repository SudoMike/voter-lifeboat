## Background
Tom Dent (Prefers Republican Party) is a six-term State Representative for the 13th Legislative District and a five-term chairman of the Grant County GOP [S1]. He was sworn into the House in 2015 [S2][S4]. He has owned Tom Dent Aviation since 1976, offering flight instruction and aerial application for agriculture, and runs the Flying T Ranch northeast of Moses Lake, raising grass-fed bison and hay [S1][S2]. He attended Othello High School and Big Bend Community College [S1]. He lists foster parenting, past presidency of Immanuel Lutheran Church, service as a Moses Lake Airport commissioner and membership in the Grant and Lincoln County Sheriff's Posse [S1]. He is the ranking Republican on the House agriculture and natural resources committee and serves on the Early Learning and Human Services and Transportation committees; he chairs the Aviation Caucus and co-chairs the Wildfire Caucus [S2][S4][S17]. In April 2026 he said he was third in seniority in the House Republican Caucus and that a seventh term would follow if reelected [S17]. Dent and Democrat Juan "Jerry" Garcia both qualified from the August 4 primary for the November ballot [S18].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** called ESSB 6346 "an income tax, in direct conflict with our state constitution," warned that future legislatures could lower the $1 million threshold and reach asset-rich family farms, and said "Washington does not have a revenue problem—we have a spending problem" [S5].
- **Spending and budget:** did not support the 2026 supplemental operating budget, citing a $2.3 billion increase and saying state spending had more than doubled in a decade [S6]. Voted for the House supplemental transportation budget but against the final version because of $800 million in new general obligation bonds [S6]. Says he would push for an audit of the state's day-care system over fraud allegations [S17].
- **Water and agriculture:** names water as the district's biggest issue and completing the Odessa Groundwater Replacement Program as "absolutely critical," along with more water storage [S16]. His statement says farms and working families are his first priorities [S1].
- **Wildfire and aviation:** says he fought to make wildfire aviation support funding permanent [S1]; sponsored HB 2104 on aviation assurance funding for wildland fires, signed in 2026 [S3][S6].
- **Child care and families:** says child care is "unavailable and unaffordable" and that he is working to lower costs for families and support providers [S1]; sponsored HB 1648 (2025), modifying child-care provider qualifications [S3].
- **Climate and energy:** sponsored HB 1912 (2025), the exemption for agricultural fuels under the Climate Commitment Act, signed into law [S3]. Says Washington drivers pay some of the highest gas and diesel prices in the nation "because of the carbon tax and state gas tax" [S6].
- **Public safety:** says many policies before the Legislature "do not want to hold repeat or violent offenders accountable and put victims second" [S6].
- **Mental health:** describes himself as an advocate for mental-health reforms and rural suicide prevention [S2][S4].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no explicit position found as of 2026-10-08. His 2026 session update describes IL26-638 as "protecting girls' sports" in a passage on the session's disappointments, without stating how he would vote [S6].
- **Parents and schools:** voted against ESHB 1296 (see Record) [S8]. His 2026 session update describes IL26-001 as "restoring parents' rights" and notes the majority did not act on it [S6].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted against ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal, and called it an unconstitutional income tax [S5][S7].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** no explicit position found as of 2026-10-08. His session update calls it "restoring parents' rights" and notes it will go to the ballot because the majority did not act on it [S6]. Record: voted against ESHB 1296, the 2025 amendments to the I-2081 parental-rights law [S8][S9].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** no explicit position found as of 2026-10-08. His session update calls it "protecting girls' sports" [S6].

## Record
- Voted nay on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million (2026-03-09, passed 51-46); voted yea on the failed motion to postpone it indefinitely (38-58) [S7].
- Voted nay on House final passage of ESHB 2081 (2025-04-22, passed 50-48), which raised business and occupation tax rates and created a surcharge on large companies [S10][S11].
- Voted nay on House final passage of ESSB 5813 (2025-04-23, passed 53-45), which added higher capital gains and estate tax rate tiers [S12][S13].
- Voted nay on House concurrence and final passage of ESHB 1296 as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24, passed 59-39); he was excused on the March 2025 House vote [S8]. ESHB 1296 (chapter 369, Laws of 2025) added, modified and removed rights in the I-2081 parents' list, for example changing the 10-business-day records deadline to up to 45 days under FERPA [S9].
- Voted nay on House final passage of EHB 1217 as recommended by the conference committee (2025-04-27, passed 54-44), the law limiting rent and fee increases for tenants [S14][S15].
- Sponsored bills enacted in 2025-26 include HB 1294 (pesticide application safety committee), HB 1522 (utility wildfire mitigation plans), HB 1648 (child-care provider qualifications), HB 1650 (airport projects as a use of local real estate excise tax), HB 1705 (large-animal veterinarian work group), HB 1912 (Climate Commitment Act agricultural fuel exemption) and HB 2104 (aviation assurance funding for wildland fires) [S3]. Earlier enacted bills include a foster-parent bill of rights (HB 2785, 2018) [S3].
