## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including the tax structure, water and agriculture policy, K-12 and child-care funding, and criminal-justice statutes.

## Race dynamics
The 13th Legislative District spans all or parts of Grant, Adams, Kittitas, Yakima and Lincoln counties in rural Central Washington (S2). Republican Tom Dent, in the House since 2015 and the ranking Republican on its agriculture and natural resources committee, faces Democrat Juan "Jerry" Garcia, an Othello organizer and former corrections officer making his first legislative bid (S1; S2; tom-dent.md S4).

Dent runs on seniority, water (the Odessa Groundwater Replacement Program), wildfire aviation and child-care costs; he voted against the 2026 millionaires tax (ESSB 6346) and the 2025 tax increases, against the 2026 supplemental operating budget and against ESHB 1296, the 2025 rewrite of the I-2081 parental-rights law (tom-dent.md S1, S5, S6, S7, S8, S10, S12). Garcia runs on fixing what he calls a regressive tax system, refusing corporate PAC money, ranked-choice voting, universal health care, farmworker and union protections, and also on finishing the Odessa project (juan-jerry-garcia.md S1, S2). Both name the Odessa project as a top priority (S2). Dent reports $83.8k raised; Garcia $1.3k (tom-dent.md S18; juan-jerry-garcia.md S3). Neither candidate has stated a position on the three statewide initiatives, though Dent's 2026 session update describes I-1 as "restoring parents' rights" and I-638 as "protecting girls' sports" (tom-dent.md S6).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Dent voted against ESSB 6346 and calls it an unconstitutional income tax; Garcia calls the tax system regressive and proposes taxing stock and bond wealth above about $100 million.
2. **Record vs. renewal:** six-term incumbent against a candidate who has never held office.
3. **Money in politics:** Garcia's no-corporate-PAC pledge and ranked-choice voting proposal; Dent stresses cross-aisle relationships and seniority.
4. **Health care:** Garcia names universal health care as a top priority; Dent has no comparable proposal.
5. **Spending:** Dent opposed the 2026 operating budget as unsustainable spending growth.
6. **Parents and schools:** Dent voted against ESHB 1296; Garcia has no stated position.
