## What the office does

The assessor lists and values property for taxation. State law requires all taxable real and personal property to be listed and assessed every year at its value as of January 1, and the listings are open to public inspection [S2]. The assessor values property; levy rates are set by the taxing districts and voters.

## Race dynamics

Assessor Melissa McKnight, a Republican who has held the office since 2015, did not file for re-election [S4][S5]. Clayton Johnson of Ephrata, the office's sales analyst, filed in May with no party preference and was the only name on the August primary ballot [S4][S6]. In July the county commissioners heard complaints from current and former staff about McKnight's management. The county's human resources department and its insurance pool administrator had investigated, and both reports were sent to the prosecutor's office for review; some officials questioned whether the complaints were politically motivated [S5]. The same week GCJ.news reported that Anson Bartrand, 25, a Republican precinct committee officer, had filed as a write-in candidate [S5].

Johnson received 12,282 votes in the certified primary count. Bartrand, as a declared write-in, received an estimated 3% [S6]. State law puts a primary candidate on the general ballot only with at least 1% of the votes cast for the office [S3]. County elections coordinator Rebecca Pettingill said Bartrand met that threshold and would appear on the general ballot beside Johnson [S7].

The two candidates present different profiles. Johnson cites nine years as an appraiser and sales analyst inside the office and says the office should stay free of politics. Bartrand cites work as CFO of a land investment company, a law degree in progress, and Republican Party roles, and pledges to cut unnecessary spending [S1].

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Record vs. renewal:** a nine-year staff appraiser from inside the office against a 25-year-old outsider with no assessment-office experience. Neither has held elected county office.
2. **Spending:** Bartrand pledges to "find responsible ways to reduce unnecessary spending" in the office. Johnson's statements focus on fair valuations, transparency and staff conduct, not on the office budget.
3. **Partisanship of the office:** Johnson runs without a party preference and argues assessments should be "free of politics." Bartrand runs as a "conservative Republican." This is a difference in framing, not an issue axis, and party preference is not used for scoring.
