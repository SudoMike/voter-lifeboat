## Background

Anson Bartrand, 25, of Beverly, is a Republican precinct committee officer [S4]. GCJ.news reported he has been a precinct committee officer since age 18 and has held various roles in the county Republican Party [S4]. His statement lists Beverly 002 Precinct Committee Officer and "various positions in Grant County Republican Executive Board" as his elected experience [S1]. He is Chief Financial Officer of Sentinel Shores, LLC, a land investment and management company, and has served on nonprofit committees and boards, including the Boy Scouts' Grand Columbia Council [S1]. He earned degrees in accounting and marketing management from Central Washington University (2019-2023) and lists the University of Idaho College of Law for 2023-2026 [S1]. His community service includes volunteer teaching in 2022-2023 at a children's home for refugees on the Thai-Myanmar border, and volunteer work for Scouting, Pathfinder youth groups and a Columbia Basin College family math night [S1].

### How he reached the general ballot
- He was not on the printed primary ballot. He filed as a declared Republican write-in candidate in mid-July, after Clayton Johnson had been unopposed [S4]. His PDC registration lists a declaration date of July 14, 2026 and a registration date of July 24, 2026 [S2].
- In the certified primary count he received an estimated 3% as an official write-in candidate; GCJ.news's certification story places him in Mattawa [S5]. (His PDC committee address and the July GCJ.news story place him in Beverly [S2][S4].)
- State law puts a primary candidate on the general ballot only with at least 1% of the total votes cast for the office [S3]. County elections coordinator Rebecca Pettingill said Bartrand met the 1% threshold and his name would appear on the general ballot beside Johnson's [S6].

## Positions

### Assessment practice
- Says "property owners deserve fair assessments, fair taxes, and an office that meets every statutory deadline" [S1].
- Pledges to "prioritize timely completion of required assessments and reports so that local governments receive the funding to which they are entitled" and to provide "an accessible office" [S1].
- Cites his legal education as giving him "a strong understanding of statutes, administrative procedures, and following the law" [S1].

### Spending and property rights
- Pledges to "find responsible ways to reduce unnecessary spending" [S1].
- Says "As a Republican, I believe government should respect private property rights and be a careful steward of taxpayers' money" [S1].
- Describes himself as a "conservative Republican" [S1].
