## Background

Clayton Johnson of Ephrata has worked in the Grant County Assessor's Office for nine years [S1][S4]. His statement says he spent that time as the commercial/industrial appraiser and most recently as the sales analyst [S1]. His website says he was the commercial and industrial appraiser for seven years and was promoted two years ago to appraisal analyst, focusing on real estate market analysis, and that he has completed more than 250 hours of continuing education in property assessment [S2]. His listed education is International Association of Assessing Officers (IAAO) courses 101, 102 and 300, USPAP and other continuing education [S1]. He lists no elected experience [S1]. His website says he is a military veteran, married 20 years with three sons [S2].

He filed in May with no party preference after Assessor Melissa McKnight did not seek re-election [S4][S5]. He was the only name on the primary ballot, with 9,710 votes (96.6%) in the August 7 count and 12,282 votes in the certified count [S6][S7].

## Positions

### Assessment practice and office conduct
- Says his mission is "to bring integrity, transparency and fairness to property valuations in Grant County" and to ensure valuations are "fair and equitable" [S1].
- Pledges "No back room deals or changing of values without data based backing" [S2].
- Would set and enforce tracking policies for properties owned by assessor's office staff, and bar staff from appraisals in which they have a personal or financial interest [S2].
- Wants to make valuation information easier to reach through community outreach, a modernized website and more frequent press releases, and to meet taxpayers in person rather than "sitting in an office waiting for them to come to me" [S2].
- Pledges equal treatment of taxpayers whatever their property or position, "a county commissioner, a lifelong resident or brand new citizen" [S2].
- Wants an office where "staff feel respected and have the tools necessary to best serve the public" [S1].

### Partisanship
- Says he is running as a non-partisan candidate "because I believe property assessments should remain fair, impartial, and entirely free of politics," and calls himself "a public servant, not a politician" [S2].
