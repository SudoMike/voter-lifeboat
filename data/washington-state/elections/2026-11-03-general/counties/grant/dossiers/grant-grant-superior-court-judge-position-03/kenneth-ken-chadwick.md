## Background

Ken Chadwick, 63, of Moses Lake, is a senior partner at STW Law PLLC (formerly Schultheis Tabler Wallace) in Ephrata [S1][S3]. He joined the firm in 2007 and became a partner in 2009, after starting his legal career at a Seattle firm [S1][S3]. Before law school he was a police officer and sergeant for 15 years, in patrol and detective units, and he reports owning a business beginning in the early 1980s [S1][S3]. He holds a B.A. in law and justice from Central Washington University (summa cum laude, 1998) and a J.D. from Seattle University School of Law (magna cum laude, 2002) [S1][S2]. His statement reports 23 years as a practicing attorney [S1].

## Positions

- **Judicial approach.** Says his approach to the courtroom "is grounded in a Constitutional view of the law, treating the US Constitution as fundamental to judicial decision-making and protecting individual rights" [S1]. Says every person who enters the courtroom should be treated with dignity and respect, and that his campaign centers on integrity and impartiality [S2].
- **Court administration.** Told GCJ.news he would work with the other judges to "increase judicial efficiency and lower litigation costs" through proposed changes to local court rules and by researching more courtroom space [S3].
- **The appointment.** Told GCJ.news before the primary that his campaign aimed in part at "undoing" the governor's "irregular appointment of Jennifer Richardson, who is not a Grant County attorney and was not recommended by the elected Grant County Commissioners" [S3]. His general-election statement still says he is "running to unseat Governor Bob Ferguson's recent appointee from Wenatchee," although Richardson was eliminated in the primary [S1][S4].
- **Perspective.** Says his combined experience in law enforcement, litigation, business ownership and raising a family "brings a practical and balanced perspective to the courtroom" [S1][S3].

## Record

- Has practiced in local, state and federal courts in Washington, in family, criminal and mostly civil cases, and has litigated cases against the State of Washington (self-reported) [S1][S3].
- Has served as a mediator, as an arbitrator hearing civil cases in superior court, and as a pro tem commissioner in Grant County Superior Court, in parental-rights, juvenile criminal and domestic-violence protection cases (self-reported) [S1][S3].
- Pro bono work for veterans through the Attorney General's Office and for Hands and Paws Animal Assistance; previously vice president of Grant County Animal Outreach for a decade [S1].
- Aug. 4, 2026 primary: first place with 5,533 votes [S4].
- No published rulings were found; his pro tem service is not documented beyond his own account.

## Ratings

- No bar-association rating for this race was found.

## Endorsements

His site lists retired Washington Supreme Court Chief Justice Barbara Madsen, retired Justice Richard B. Sanders, retired Grant County Superior Court Judge John Knodell, Grant County District Court Judge Nicholas L. Wallace, Kittitas County Superior Court Judges Chris Herion and James Kirkham, Mason County Superior Court Judge David Stevens, the Washington Farm Bureau PAC, the Grant County Republican Party Central Committee (a "recommendation"), and about a dozen local attorneys [S2]. Knodell's quoted statement says Chadwick has argued many cases before him and "will decide cases before him efficiently and according to the rule of law" [S2].

## Campaign finance

PDC summary (updated Oct. 7, 2026): $16,931 in contributions, no loans, $7,706 spent [S5].

## Scoring notes

- Not an incumbent. Runs on 23 years of practice plus quasi-judicial service as arbitrator, mediator and pro tem commissioner [S1][S3].
- Formation includes a 15-year law-enforcement career before law school; his law practice has been mostly civil. No stated position on bail or diversion [S1][S3].
- States a constitution-centered approach but nothing specific enough to place him on the as-written vs. pragmatic axis [S1].
