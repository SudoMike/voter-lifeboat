## What the office does

Superior courts handle felony criminal cases, civil disputes over $50,000, real estate matters, domestic relations, juvenile and probate matters, adoptions and child dependencies, and appeals from district court [S2]. Grant County has three superior court judges; the other two are Melissa Chlarson and Anna Gigliotti [S2]. The annual salary is $244,631, split between the state and the county [S2]. This seat is on the ballot for an unexpired two-year term, and the whole county votes on it [S2][S4].

## Race dynamics

- **Vacancy and appointment.** Gov. Bob Ferguson appointed Judge Tyson Hill to the Court of Appeals effective Jan. 1, 2026, and a few days later named Jennifer Richardson as Hill's interim successor [S2].
- **Primary.** In the Aug. 4 primary, private-practice attorney Ken Chadwick led with 5,533 votes. Deputy prosecuting attorney Trevor Bevier finished second, 230 votes ahead of Richardson (4,741 to 4,511), so the appointed incumbent was eliminated [S3]. The November race is between two non-incumbents.
- **Candidates.** Chadwick, 63, is a senior partner at STW Law PLLC in Ephrata who practiced law from 2003 after 15 years as a police officer and sergeant. He has served as a superior court arbitrator and pro tem commissioner [S1][S2]. Bevier, 45, spent about 14 years as an intern and associate attorney at a Moses Lake firm and joined the Grant County Prosecuting Attorney's Office as a civil deputy prosecutor in 2025 [S1][S2][S6].
- **Campaign framing.** Chadwick told GCJ.news before the primary that part of his aim was to undo what he called the governor's "irregular appointment" of Richardson, "who is not a Grant County attorney" [S2]. His general-election statement still describes the race as a run "to unseat Governor Bob Ferguson's recent appointee from Wenatchee" [S1]. Bevier stresses his Grant County roots and his experience "on nearly every side of a case" [S6].
- **Endorsements.** Chadwick lists retired Chief Justice Barbara Madsen, retired Justice Richard B. Sanders, retired Grant County Superior Court Judge John Knodell, District Court Judge Nicholas Wallace, three out-of-county superior court judges, the Washington Farm Bureau PAC and the Grant County Republican Party Central Committee [S5]. Bevier lists Prosecuting Attorney Brandon Guernsey, retired Judge Richard Fitterer, Commissioner Cindy Carter and local attorneys [S1][S2].
- **Money.** As of early October, Chadwick reported $16,931 raised and $7,706 spent [S7]; Bevier reported $15,080 raised plus $6,593 in loans and $14,952 spent [S8].
- **Bar ratings.** No bar-association rating for this race was found.

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

- **Experience (`experience`).** Neither is the incumbent. Chadwick runs on 23 years of practice, quasi-judicial work as an arbitrator, mediator and pro tem commissioner, and a prior law-enforcement career [S1][S2]. Bevier runs on about 12 years of practice since his 2014 law degree and one year as a civil deputy prosecutor, with no judicial or pro tem service reported [S1][S2][S6].
- **Professional formation (`safety`).** Chadwick spent 15 years as a police officer and sergeant before law school; his law practice has been mostly civil, with some family and criminal work, and his pro tem commissioner work included juvenile criminal and domestic-violence protection matters [S1][S2]. Bevier's practice has been civil and private, now advising the county as a civil (not criminal) deputy prosecutor [S2][S6]. Neither has stated a position on bail or diversion.
- **Judicial approach (`judicial`).** Both describe a constitutional, apply-the-law-impartially approach. Chadwick says his approach is "grounded in a Constitutional view of the law" [S1]; Bevier says he will "apply the law consistently and impartially" [S6]. Neither statement is specific enough to place them on the as-written vs. pragmatic axis.

## Not differentiating

- Both promise timely, efficient decisions and respectful treatment of litigants [S2][S6].
- Both grew their practices in Grant County courts [S1][S2].
