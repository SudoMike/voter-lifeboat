## Background

Trevor Bevier, 45, grew up in Grant County and graduated from Ephrata High School in 1999 [S1][S3]. GCJ.news lists him as living in Soap Lake [S3]. He earned an A.A. from Big Bend Community College (2001), a B.A.S. from Central Washington University (cum laude, 2011) and a J.D. from Gonzaga University School of Law (cum laude, 2014) [S1]. Before law school he spent nearly a decade with a title insurance company in Moses Lake and sold real estate [S1][S2][S3]. He worked as a Rule 9 legal intern and then associate attorney at Huberdeau Law Office in Moses Lake for about 14 years in all, handling civil litigation, contracts, construction, real estate, probate, bankruptcy, personal injury, family law and some criminal cases [S1][S2][S3]. In 2025 he joined the Grant County Prosecuting Attorney's Office as a civil deputy prosecuting attorney, where he advises the county and its elected officials, handles civil litigation and reviews county contracts [S1][S2][S3].

## Positions

- **Judicial approach.** Says his approach is to "listen carefully, treat everyone with dignity and respect, apply the law consistently and impartially, and issue timely, thoughtful decisions that explain the reasoning behind them" [S2]. States "a staunch commitment to support the Constitution of the United States and the Constitution of the state of Washington" [S1].
- **Court timeliness.** Told GCJ.news that a primary concern "is to hear cases and render decisions in a timely manner," and that he could provide "additional efficiencies" to the court [S3].
- **Community fit.** Says courts must "meet the unique, challenging, and evolving needs" of Grant County residents, and that voters "deserve a judge that represents their values, knows their struggles, listens intently" [S1].
- **Breadth of practice.** Says that having represented plaintiffs, defendants, the prosecution, the defense, private clients and public entities, he understands how important it is for people to feel heard in court [S2].

## Record

- Has appeared in Grant County District and Superior Courts, other Washington courts, the Court of Appeals, and federal and bankruptcy courts (self-reported) [S2].
- Civil deputy prosecuting attorney since 2025 [S1][S3].
- Aug. 4, 2026 primary: second place with 4,741 votes, 230 ahead of appointed Judge Jennifer Richardson [S4].
