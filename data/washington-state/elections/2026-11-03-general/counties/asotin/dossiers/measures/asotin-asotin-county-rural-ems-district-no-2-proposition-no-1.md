## What it does

Proposition 1 asks whether Asotin County Rural Emergency Medical Service District No. 2 may levy a regular property tax of "28 cents (or less) per $1,000 assessed valuation each year for six consecutive years beginning in 2027" to pay for emergency medical services (RCW 84.52.069) [S1][S2]. The county commissioners adopted Resolution No. 26-27 on July 27, 2026 to put it on the ballot. The resolution says the current levy expires on December 31, 2026 and that demand in the district is growing [S1]. The money goes to a county special revenue fund that pays for contracted EMS in the district. The county contracts with the Lewiston Fire Department, in cooperation with Asotin County Fire District #1, for emergency medical rescue, treatment and transport [S1].

- **Who votes.** The county commissioners run EMS District 2, which is "everyone in the county outside of Clarkston and Asotin County Fire District 1": Anatone, Cloverland, Heller Bar and the Snake River corridor south to Oregon [S8][S9][S10]. In the 2020 and August 2026 elections only the Anatone and Rural Asotin precincts voted on it [S3][S4]. Clarkston and Fire District 1 voters do not see it [S10][S13]. KOZE describes the levy as what keeps Lewiston medics coming to rural homes [S13].
- **Second try.** The same proposition failed in the August 4 primary, 118 yes to 146 no (44.7% yes) [S3]. The commissioners put it back on the November ballot [S8].

## Cost

- **Rate.** Up to $0.28 per $1,000 of assessed value a year for 2027-2032 [S1]. The explanatory statement says this increases the current levy "of up to 15 cents (or less) to 28 cents (or less)" [S1]. In 2025 the district's levy ("EMS Dist #1 Special" in DOR's levy detail) raised $19,924 at $0.12084 per $1,000 on $164.9 million of assessed value [S6].
- **Tax code areas.** The county's 2025 rates put the levy in tax code areas 25, 30 and 30F [S6][S7].
- **Per home.** At the full 28 cents, $28 a year per $100,000 of assessed value: $13 more than at the current 15-cent ceiling and about $16 more than at the 2025 rate (arithmetic on [S1][S6]).
- **Vote needed.** The first imposition of a six-year EMS levy needs a 60% supermajority. Approval of a later six-year levy, as here, needs a simple majority [S5]. The 2020 levy passed 262 to 174 (60.1%) [S4].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

The levy funds the county's contract with the City of Lewiston for advanced life support paramedics and ambulance service in rural Asotin County. Supporters say the contract expires December 31, 2026 [S12]. After the August failure, KOZE reported that the contract ran to the end of September and that the commissioners had asked Lewiston to extend it to year end [S9]. If the levy fails again, KOZE reports there could be no emergency medical service in the county outside Clarkston and Fire District 1 [S9][S11]. Supporters say EMS would stop on January 1, 2027 [S12].

## Arguments for

No statement for or against appears in the pamphlet or the VoteWA guide [S1][S2]. The county commissioners say they cannot legally advocate for the levy but held informational outreach [S8].
- A group of rural residents is campaigning for a yes vote. Their letter says the levy "cannot be used for other county expenses," and that without it "EMS services will cease operations on January 1, 2027" [S12].
- They say a Life Flight membership is no substitute: a medical crew or law enforcement must request the helicopter, flights depend on weather and availability, and ground paramedics often arrive first [S11][S12].
- Anatone resident Jay Holzmiller told KOZE he is "not big on taxes" but considers ambulance service a basic service he will vote for [S11].

## Arguments against

No formal opposition statement, committee or campaign was found [S1][S2][S14]. The August result (55% no in the district) is the only recorded opposition; the measure nearly doubles the maximum rate [S1][S3]. Supporters say many voters did not realize a no vote would end ambulance service [S11].

## Endorsements and campaign

No PDC committee for or against the levy appears among the 2026 Asotin County filers [S14]. The supporters held town halls with Lewiston Fire Department and Asotin County Fire District #1 officials on Oct. 6 (Anatone) and Oct. 7 (Asotin) [S11][S12].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote raises the district's regular EMS levy ceiling from 15 to 28 cents per $1,000 (the 2025 rate was about 12 cents) for six more years [S1][S6]. Direction +2.
- `spending`: the levy continues an existing contracted service rather than expanding programs [S1][S12]. Not mapped.
- `local-control`: no state mandate, annexation or regional authority is involved [S1]. Not mapped.
