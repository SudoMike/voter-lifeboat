## What the office does

Asotin County PUD is a water utility. Formed by voters in 1984, it bought the Clarkston-area water system and has run it since 1987; since 2010 it has also owned the county's sewer collection system, and it has one electric customer [S2]. A three-member board, elected to six-year terms, sets policy, approves budgets and rates, and hires the general manager [S2]. A commissioner must live in the commissioner district, but every voter in the PUD elects each commissioner at the general election [S5]. The PUD covers Clarkston and the Clarkston Heights area, not the whole county (see `COMPLETENESS.md`).

## Race dynamics

- **Open seat.** Commissioner Judy Ridge, the board's president, holds the District 1 seat; she won it in 2020, defeating Joe Louis 5,472 to 3,682 [S3][S4]. She is not on the 2026 ballot [S1].
- **Candidates.** Joe Louis, a retired PUD field foreman with about 40 years on the local water system, faces Darcy Nelly, co-owner of Nelly Broadcasting and a former Clarkston Chamber of Commerce president [S1].
- **No primary.** With two candidates the race went straight to the general. Both candidates file under the PDC mini-reporting option, which means they expect to raise and spend little [S6].
- **Context.** The PUD reports 38 consecutive clean state audits and $12.7 million of water and sewer capital improvements from 2012 to 2025 [S3].

## Differentiating issue axes

No rubric axis applies to a public utility district contest, so the race is shown without scores. The difference between the candidates is in their backgrounds. Louis offers long operating experience inside the water system and wants to keep updating mains, wells and tanks. Nelly offers business, budgeting and nonprofit-board experience and stresses transparency and accountability [S1].
