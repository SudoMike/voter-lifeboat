## Background

- **Current role.** Chief of Police of the City of Asotin for the past 11 years [S2].
- **Career.** POST-certified through North Idaho College's law enforcement program in 1993. Began as a reserve officer in Moscow, Idaho. Full-time Asotin County deputy from 1995, rising to sergeant. Then 13 years with the Lewiston Police Department [S2]. Says he has more than 31 years of full-time law enforcement in the Lewis-Clark Valley, including more than 10 as chief [S1].
- **Specialties.** Field training officer, SWAT, critical incident negotiator, K9 handler, entry team leader. A member of the Asotin County Jail Committee "from its beginning" [S1].
- **Credentials.** Executive certificate in Washington and Master Police Officer certificate in Idaho; member of the Washington Association of Sheriffs and Police Chiefs for over 10 years. Named officer of the year by the Asotin County Sheriff's Office and the Lewiston Police Department [S1].
- **Education.** BS in justice studies, summa cum laude, and an AAS in law enforcement; Command College and the FBI-LEEDA Trilogy courses [S1].
- **Community.** Clarkston Rotary, Back Country Horsemen, Clarkston EPIC, Twin River Special Olympics; youth sports coach [S1].

## Positions

**Drugs and public safety**
- Would "aggressively confront the fentanyl epidemic through strong enforcement, prevention efforts, and community education" [S1]. Would partner with the restructured Quad-Cities Drug Task Force [S2].
- Says treatment and counseling are critical, and law enforcement's role is to disrupt the supply of illegal drugs [S2].

**Jail and reentry**
- Would lead the newly opened jail and partner with local organizations to control costs and expand programs that help inmates "break the cycle of reoffending" [S1][S4].
- Supports substance abuse treatment, education, job skills training, work crews and a structured release process connecting released people with local services. Would address jail release concerns [S1][S2].

**Community policing and rights**
- Would continue "the community-oriented approach I practiced as Police Chief," building trust and solving local problems with residents [S1].
- Says he will protect "every citizen's rights ... regardless of their political views or background" [S4].
- Says SB 5974 is unconstitutional because "it's the people that remove that Sheriff" [S6].

**Management**
- Says that as chief he "managed budgets responsibly and maintained fiscal accountability" [S1].

## Record

- As Asotin police chief he set up the Blue Bridge Alliance fund, which lets officers in Asotin, Clarkston and the Sheriff's Office pay for food, gas or a night's lodging for people in crisis; it is now funded by donations [S5].
- Primary: 2,913 votes to Richards's 3,016 [S7].

## Endorsements

Former Asotin County Sheriffs John Jeffers and Wayne Weber; Jim Smith, retired commander of the Clarkston Police Department and the Asotin County Jail; Arnie Knight, retired supervisory deputy U.S. marshal; and others [S3]. Full PDC reporting: $15,464.89 raised and $8,898.23 spent [S8].
