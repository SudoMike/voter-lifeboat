## Background

- **Current role.** Asotin County undersheriff [S1]. The Sheriff's Office hired him as undersheriff on July 7, 2025, a primarily administrative position [S5].
- **Law enforcement path.** A non-certified reserve officer for the Whitman County Sheriff's Office, the Asotin County Sheriff's Office and the Asotin Police Department since 2011, called to duty in emergencies from 2019 to 2025. He completed the county's field training program [S5]. He says he took an emergency hire with the Sheriff's Office about four years ago and then worked full time as a patrol deputy [S3]. He has been on the Whitman County SWAT team since 2010 [S3]. In August 2025 Sheriff John Hilderbrand asked the state Criminal Justice Training Commission to let Richards attend the Basic Law Enforcement Equivalency Academy instead of the full academy [S5]. His pamphlet statement says he is a graduate of the Washington State Criminal Justice Training Commission Basic Law Enforcement Academy [S1]; the sources do not say whether he completed the full academy or the equivalency academy the sheriff requested.
- **Fire and EMS.** His campaign site says he has served as a full-time Pullman firefighter and paramedic since 2007 and "currently hold[s] the rank of Captain"; the August 2025 training-commission record also says he currently serves as a Pullman Fire captain [S3][S5]. Ten years as Life Flight Network's Lewiston base manager, where he says he managed personnel, budgets and "multimillion-dollar operations" [S1][S3]. He also lists paramedic program director, fire investigator and boating officer [S1].
- **Education.** Bachelor's degree in paramedicine from Central Washington University [S1].
- **Personal.** Grew up on a farm in Malden; has lived in the City of Asotin for 14 years [S3].

## Positions

**Public safety**
- Says "criminals should be in jail and needles not in our parks" [S4]. Says Washington "has spent years making excuses for criminals" [S1].
- Would work through the county's more than 1,500 outstanding arrest warrants and strengthen drug enforcement through more training to target drug dealers [S2][S4].
- Would work closely with the Prosecutor's Office "to build strong cases" and support victims [S1].
- Notes that 10 deputies cover more than 22,000 residents and that at night there may be one patrol deputy for 641 square miles [S4].

**Equipment and staffing**
- Says deputies are not issued patrol rifles and use their own; would advocate for "proper equipment, modern resources, and realistic training standards" [S2].
- Would develop certified in-house instructors, which he says could also bring in revenue for the county [S2].

**Transparency and rights**
- Promises regular community town halls and timely updates on major incidents [S2].
- Says "the Constitution still matters" and that he has "stood up" against Olympia for voters' right to choose their sheriff [S1]. SB 5974's five-year experience requirement would have excluded him; he told KOZE that voters should decide [S7].

## Record

- As undersheriff he told the county commissioners in August 2026 that he would apply for a $200,000 grant for a drone program. He said it uses artificial intelligence "for flight planning, not for camera usage. This is not a FLOCK camera" [S8].
- Led the August primary with 3,016 votes to Renzelman's 2,913 [S9].

## Endorsements

None listed on the campaign site [S2]. Full PDC reporting: $10,019.94 raised and $9,702.40 spent [S10].

## Scoring notes

- Shorter full-time law-enforcement career, reached through reserve, emergency-hire and patrol service, with long fire, paramedic and air-medical management experience. He is the current undersheriff [S1][S3][S5].
- His message is enforcement-forward: jail for criminals, clearing warrants, targeting drug dealers [S1][S2][S4].
- Emphasizes equipment and resources for deputies and town-hall transparency [S2].
