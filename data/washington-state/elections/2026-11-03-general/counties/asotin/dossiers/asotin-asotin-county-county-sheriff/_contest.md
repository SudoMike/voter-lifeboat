## What the office does

The sheriff is the county's chief executive officer and conservator of the peace: the sheriff and deputies enforce state law, arrest lawbreakers, and serve court process and warrants [S2]. In Asotin County the sheriff will also lead the newly opened county jail [S1]. The office is a four-year term; both candidates state no party preference [S1].

## Race dynamics

- **Open seat.** Sheriff John Hilderbrand, elected unopposed in 2022 [S4], is not on the ballot. The race is between his undersheriff, Blake Richards, and City of Asotin Police Chief Monte Renzelman [S1].
- **Primary.** Richards led the August primary 3,016 to 2,913 (50.7% to 49.0%), with every precinct reporting; both advance [S3].
- **Eligibility law.** SB 5974 (2026) added a requirement of five years of specific law-enforcement experience and lets a state panel decertify and remove a sheriff. A judge temporarily halted it, which let both men run; Richards does not have five qualifying years. Both candidates and Sheriff Hilderbrand oppose the law [S5][S6]. The current RCW 36.28.010 directs sheriffs to enforce state law as interpreted by the state supreme court [S2].
- **Money.** Both campaigns file full PDC reports: Renzelman reports $15,465 raised and $8,898 spent; Richards $10,020 raised and $9,702 spent [S7].

## Differentiating issue axes

- `experience`: Renzelman offers 31 years of full-time policing, including more than 10 as a police chief. Richards came to full-time law enforcement recently, after a long fire, paramedic and air-medical career, and holds the county's number-two post [S1].
- `safety`: both lead with enforcement. Richards frames the race around criminals "in jail" and clearing outstanding warrants. Renzelman pairs fentanyl enforcement with jail treatment and reentry programs and community policing [S1].
- `spending`: Renzelman cites budget management as chief; Richards cites managing multimillion-dollar operations at Life Flight [S1]. Neither makes a spending pledge.
