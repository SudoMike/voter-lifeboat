## Background

- Molly Simpson of East Wenatchee is the District 2 commissioner and board
  president of Douglas County PUD; she began serving in 2015 [S3]. She won
  the seat in 2014 over Steven E. Pauly, 5,394 to 3,798 (58.7%) [S5], and
  was re-elected unopposed in 2020 with 15,179 votes [S6].
- A fourth-generation member of a Douglas County wheat-farming family, she
  graduated from Eastmont High School and the University of Washington
  (communications) and worked eight years at the Washington Apple Commission
  [S1][S3].
- She sits on the Northwest Public Power Association board of trustees and
  is vice chair of the Washington PUD Association's Energy Committee [S1].
- Her PDC filing reports $3,526.51 raised and $5,338.38 spent as of
  2026-09-17 [S8].

## Positions

### Rates and growth
- Says county residents get "low cost, reliable power because we own Wells
  Dam" and that she will "continue to protect customer owners" as energy
  demand grows [S1].
- Cites the commission's 2024 policy requiring new loads over 1.5 megawatts
  to buy their power on the open market and pay a "power delivery fee," with
  Microsoft the first customer and large crypto and data loads moved to the
  plan by 2028: "Growth needs to pay for growth" [S1].
- Calls this a "fiscally conservative approach" [S1]; her site describes the
  PUD's tradition of "fiscally conservative, risk-adverse commissioners" and
  says she is "very protective" of Wells Dam [S2].

## Record

- Presided over the October 6, 2026 meeting that opened the 2027 budget
  hearing for the distribution system, Wells project and hydrogen facilities
  and approved a $1.82 million change order on the hydrogen fueling project
  [S4].
- Cut the ribbon with the other two commissioners on the PUD's $27 million
  hydrogen production facility in October 2025 [S7].
