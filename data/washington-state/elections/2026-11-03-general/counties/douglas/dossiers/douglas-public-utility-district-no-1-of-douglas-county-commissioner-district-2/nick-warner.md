## Background

- Nick Warner of East Wenatchee is an East Wenatchee Water District
  commissioner, a post he says he has held for nine years [S1]. He won
  Water Commissioner #1 in 2017 over John D. Sterk, 2,199 to 1,937 [S3],
  and was re-elected unopposed in 2023 with 4,684 votes [S4].
- An Eastmont High School graduate with a facilities-management degree from
  Brigham Young University, he describes an entrepreneurial business career
  [S1] and says he has worked with managers and commissioners at the Chelan,
  Douglas and Grant PUDs for 14 years [S2] (his own description; the roles
  are not specified).
- His PDC filing reports $1,488.73 raised and $3,346.81 spent as of
  2026-10-07 [S5].

## Positions

### Rates and reserves
- Says the PUD's working capital "ballooned from $60M to over $385M" while
  rates rose, and that residents paid 3% more in 2025 and schools and
  churches 8% [S2] (his figures).
- "There is no reason to increase residential power rates just to hoard
  cash"; would freeze rate increases aimed at building reserves and oppose
  planned residential, school and church increases [S2].
- Would create "Public Power Benefit" discounts and rebates for local
  customers, aiming "for a future with $0 effective power bills," using
  out-of-area power sales to offset local costs [S2].
- Would lower rates for schools, parks, churches and local governments and
  expand assistance programs for low-income customers [S2].
- Says millions earmarked for PUD offices could instead help school funding
  [S2].

### Infrastructure and services
- Would add interconnecting transmission lines, wildfire mitigation and
  gradual undergrounding of distribution lines, and expand rural broadband
  [S2].
- Promises transparency, fiscal responsibility and "strong public power
  values" with the utility kept "locally controlled" [S1].

### Energy and the dams
- Would be "a strong, unyielding advocate for Columbia River dams" and
  follow strategies of Chelan and Grant PUDs [S2].
- Would adopt solar, wind and hydro "responsibly" and maximize the value of
  green-energy attributes in wholesale markets [S2].
