## Background

- Marc Straub of Orondo is the District 3 Douglas County commissioner, a
  seat he says he has held for eight years; he has also been an elected
  Republican precinct committee officer [S1]. He was first elected in 2018,
  defeating Norman (Buck) Tupling 7,989 to 4,340 (64.8%) [S3], and
  re-elected unopposed in 2022 with 11,487 votes [S4].
- He lists 17 years as CEO of Building North Central Washington (a regional
  builders' trade association), five years as COO of Total Office
  Concepts, small-business ownership, and a finance/economics degree from
  Central Washington University [S1][S2]. He and his wife live twenty miles
  north of Orondo [S2].
- In the August 2026 district primary he received 2,583 of 3,308 votes
  [S5]. His PDC filing reports $2,241.33 raised and $1,927.20 spent as of
  2026-09-17 [S11].

## Positions

### Economy, growth and regulation
- Says he will keep a "business-friendly environment" and that the county
  "should never take a state-plus-one approach" to rules and regulations
  [S2].
- Calls responsible management of growth a priority: "Growth is healthy but
  not at all cost" [S2].
- Lists "addressing housing challenges" among the priorities he ran on
  [S1]; no specific housing policy is stated.

### Budget and taxes
- Platform planks: "Limited government scope and intrusion" and "Prioritize
  core functions & services with a balanced budget" [S2].
- Says taxpayers should expect "a respect for their hard-earned tax dollars"
  and that he has a record of "maintaining fiscal discipline, resisting
  waste" [S2] (his own claim).
- Calls maintaining roads, bridges and facilities the "chief role" of a
  commissioner [S2].

### Public safety
- "Marc prioritizes funding our Sheriff's office, emergency services, and
  the first responders" [S2].

### Local control
- Platform plank: "Decisions should be made closest to those affected" [S2].

## Record

- Chaired the board in 2022 when commissioners set up an in-house Office of
  Public Defense for Superior Court felony cases in place of contract
  attorneys; he said "it's going to be a better end product for the
  citizens" [S7].
- Voted with the unanimous board in May 2022 against an interlocal agreement
  that would have annexed about 600 homes to East Wenatchee; at the hearing
  he said the county stood to lose about $1.2 million in sales tax [S6].
- As board chair, said the Wenatchi Landing development east of East
  Wenatchee, which received $10 million in federal transportation funding in
  2026, would create hundreds of jobs [S8].
- Spoke at the March 2026 opening of the county's $1.5 million household
  hazardous waste facility near Rock Island, funded by an Ecology grant and
  a host agreement with WM [S9].
- As chair of the Chelan-Douglas Health Board in 2023, declined to take up a
  colleague's motion to change "pregnant people" wording in a staff
  presentation, saying he did not want the board "micromanaging" staff [S10].

## Endorsements

- Douglas County Republican Central Committee; retired county administrator
  James Barker; retired chief accountant Karen Goodwin; retired Sheriff Kevin
  Morris [S2].
