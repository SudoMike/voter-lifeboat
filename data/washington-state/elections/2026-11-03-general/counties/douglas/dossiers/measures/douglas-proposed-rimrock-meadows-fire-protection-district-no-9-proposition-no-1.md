## What it does

Proposition No. 1 asks voters living inside the proposed boundary whether to form a new fire protection district under chapter 52.02 RCW for the Rimrock Meadows area of Douglas County, to provide fire protection (and, per the explanatory statement, emergency medical services) [S1][S2]. If formed, it would be "financed by a property tax levy and managed by three elected fire district commissioners" [S1].

- The Douglas County Board of Commissioners adopted Resolution CE 26-19B on July 28, 2026, after a petition was filed and the Douglas County Boundary Review Board approved the proposed formation. Only "the qualified electors residing within the boundaries of the proposed Rimrock Meadows Fire Protection District" vote on it [S3].
- Resolution CE 26-25 (August 18, 2026) renamed the proposal "Rimrock Meadows Fire District No. 9" and states the boundaries are unchanged [S3].
- The formation vote and the election of the first three commissioners take place at the same election (RCW 52.02.080) [S7]. The same ballot carries the Commissioner No. 1, 2 and 3 races for the proposed district [S2].
- The Wenatchee World reported the Auditor's announcement on August 18, 2026 [S4].
- **Current coverage.** The county's address records list addresses inside the proposed boundary (Ashcroft Drive, Murcur Place, West Coyote Trail, postal city Ephrata) with fire district "000," the code its fire layer labels "No Fire District" [S5][S6]. The county's fire layer maps the proposed district at about 18,621 acres (29.1 square miles) [S5].

## Vote needed

- The explanatory statement says the district is established "if approved by a majority of those voting on the proposition" [S1].
- RCW 52.02.110 says the county declares the district organized "if three-fifths of all the votes cast at the election were cast in favor" [S8]. The two official texts differ on the threshold; this dossier records both.

## Cost

- The proposition sets no levy amount or rate [S1][S2].
- Once formed, a fire district's board may levy a regular property tax of up to 50 cents per $1,000 (RCW 52.16.130), plus further levies of up to 50 cents each under RCW 52.16.140 and 52.16.160, subject to statutory and constitutional limits [S9][S10][S11]. Levies above the general limits need a separate vote [S9].
- No district budget or planned levy rate was found.

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

The area is not in any fire district today [S5][S6], so a yes vote would create a new taxing district rather than replace one.

## Arguments for

The statement in favor (committee: Gerald Dotson, Christine Crisler, Truman Crisler) is a resident's account: he and his wife moved to Rimrock Meadows in 2017 and have "received three evacuation notices due to impending wildfires." In 2020 they evacuated for three days while a wind-driven fire advanced; "adjoining fire districts along with state and federal support" contained it "eight" miles from their home. It says "this section of the county has been overlooked for the formation of a fire district" [S1].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed. The guide says no one came forward to serve on the committee against or write a statement [S1].

## Endorsements and campaign

- `taxes`: a yes vote creates a new property-taxing district where none exists; the ballot names no rate, but the district would be "financed by a property tax levy" with statutory authority of up to 50 cents per $1,000 and more [S1][S9][S10][S11]. Because no amount is set by this vote, +1.
- `local-control`: forming a locally governed special district with elected commissioners [S1][S3] could be read as local control, but the rubric's local-control axis is about state mandates and regional authorities; no mapping.
