## What it does

Proposition No. 1 asks voters of Public Hospital District No. 1, Okanogan and Douglas Counties, to authorize up to $48,000,000 of general obligation bonds, maturing within 30 years and repaid by annual excess property taxes, to "construct, equip, remodel, renovate, and furnish improvements to Three Rivers Hospital and carry out other capital improvements" (Resolution No. 2026-10) [S1][S2].

- Three Rivers Hospital is in Brewster, Okanogan County; the district reaches into northern Douglas County (the statement in favor says it serves communities "from Mansfield to Mazama") [S1].
- The hospital says the money would improve the patient experience, strengthen emergency and surgical care, expand diagnostics, modernize technology and create safer, more efficient spaces, and that the project "builds on the plan presented to voters in 2025" [S3].
- The hospital says the final project cost depends on scope and on grants and other funding, "but the cost to the community cannot exceed $48 million" [S5].
- Bonds of this kind need a three-fifths yes vote, and turnout must be at least 40% of the district's voters at the last general election (RCW 84.52.056) [S8].

## Cost

- The explanatory statement estimates 73 cents per $1,000 of assessed value, "an estimated $18 per month for a $300,000 home" [S1].
- The hospital's cost page gives $219 a year on a $300,000 assessed value and $365 a year on $500,000 [S4].
- Up to $48,000,000 of bonds over as long as 30 years [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- This is a new excess levy for capital purposes; it does not replace an existing bond levy [S1][S5].
- The hospital separately collects an annual Emergency Room levy of $1.46 million, which the FAQ says voters approved "in August" and which pays for operations, mainly 24/7 ER staffing. The FAQ says that if the modernization is completed the hospital expects higher reimbursements and lower operating costs, which "could allow us to end the annual ER levy" [S5]. That is an expectation, not a commitment in the ballot title [S1].
- **Previous attempt.** In November 2025 the district asked for up to $45,000,000 at an estimated 78 cents per $1,000 (Resolution No. 2025-7) [S6]. It received 52.3% yes district-wide (2,357 to 2,150; 50.61% in Douglas County, 52.5% in Okanogan County) and failed the 60% requirement [S5][S7]. The 2026 request is $3 million larger at a lower estimated rate (73 cents) [S1][S6].

## Arguments for

The statement in favor (written by Michael Pruett, who chairs the hospital's board [S3]) asks voters to "keep quality healthcare close to home." It calls Three Rivers a community-owned nonprofit hospital, describes the project as a "practical investment" improving the existing hospital with updated patient spaces, technology, privacy and infrastructure, and ties local care to wildfire and emergency resilience [S1]. No rebuttal to the statement against was submitted [S1].

## Arguments against

The statement against (written by Melvin Sorensen) says voters should first have "solid county-wide information" on how best to meet Okanogan County's medical needs, and that committing $48 million and 30 years of taxes "locks in the current system." It suggests a county-wide study might favor a central facility with outlying 24-hour urgent care centers [S1]. His rebuttal says Okanogan County's three small hospitals "have few overnight patients and compete with each other," that Three Rivers is "fragile," that Medicaid reductions add to that, and that if the hospital failed residents would have "decades of increased taxes, but no hospital" [S1].

## Endorsements and campaign

- Committee for: Michael Pruett (www.threerivershospital.net/bond). Committee against: Melvin Sorensen (www.planokanoganhealth.net) [S1].
- No newspaper endorsements were found.

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote adds a new excess property tax of about 73 cents per $1,000 for up to 30 years to repay up to $48 million in bonds [S1][S4]. The FAQ's hope of ending the ER levy is not part of the measure [S5]: +2.
- `spending`: the bonds pay for capital construction at a public hospital [S1]; the opposing case is about how, not whether, to spend on health care [S1]. No separate mapping.
