## What it does

Proposition No. 1 asks Eastmont School District voters to authorize $125,000,000 of general obligation bonds, maturing within 20 years and repaid by annual excess property taxes (Resolution No. 2026-02) [S1][S2]. The money would:

- rebuild and modernize the oldest, deteriorating portions of Cascade, Kenroy and Lee elementary schools, adding permanent classrooms to replace portables, and upgrading classrooms, playfields, parking and pickup areas [S1];
- make districtwide "priority building and safety upgrades": parking and pickup areas, tennis courts, track and stadium bleachers, roofing, outdoor lighting, HVAC and interior finishes [S1].

Details from the district [S3]:

- Kenroy was built in 1958, Lee in 1957 and Cascade in 1967. The district lists them at 21%, 58% and 18% over permanent capacity, with 4, 6 and 5 portables (15 in all), and OSPI condition scores of 56.2 (poor), 58.02 (poor) and 65.58 (fair).
- It describes exterior hallways and doors (66 exterior doors at Cascade), aging roofs and HVAC that "frequently fail to keep wildfire smoke out," and congested pickup areas. The newer wings built in 2021 at the three schools would stay.
- Other projects: security entry vestibules at Clovis Point, Rock Island, Eastmont Junior High, Sterling Junior High and Eastmont High; roof, carpet, HVAC and lighting work; Sterling parking; more parking and replacement of "condemned" tennis courts at Eastmont High; metal bleacher sections at Wildcat Stadium.
- Construction would start around spring break 2028 with Kenroy, one school at a time. The district says this bond "finishes the work" and that it would not soon ask for another construction bond.
- The district expects about $25 million in state construction assistance if voters approve [S3][S6].

Bonds of this kind need a three-fifths yes vote, with turnout of at least 40% of the district's voters at the last general election (RCW 84.52.056) [S11].

## Cost

- The district estimates about $0.79 per $1,000 of assessed value over 20 years: "For a $500,000 home, this translates to $32.92 per month ($395 per year)" [S3].
- $125,000,000 in principal [S1]. Neither the guide nor the district page gives a total with interest [S1][S3].
- The 2026 page does not say whether the $0.79 is on top of the district's existing school tax rate or keeps the combined rate level. For the 2024 bond the district had said there would be "no change to the combined tax rate of $2.92" [S3][S4].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

A new bond authorization, not a renewal. It follows three failed attempts [S4][S5][S8][S9][S10]:

| Election | Proposal | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Nov. 8, 2022 | Phase 2 bond: replace Cascade, Lee, Kenroy and Rock Island elementaries, expand Sterling Junior High, a transportation facility, athletic fields [S5] | 51.26% yes, failed [S8] |
| Feb. 13, 2024 | Bonds to expand and renovate schools and improve safety | 59.56% yes (4,607-3,128), failed [S9] |
| Apr. 23, 2024 | Same title; the district's page gives $117.1 million with about $20.1 million in state funds [S4] | 59.04% yes (5,130-3,559), failed [S10] |

- NCWLIFE reports that a 39-member community facility study committee recommended another attempt in May 2026, after the two 2024 bonds "fell just under the needed 60% supermajority"; the superintendent said the district had been about 35 votes short [S7]. The district says the committee presented on May 11 and the board passed the ballot resolution on June 8, 2026 [S3].

## Arguments for

The statement in favor (committee: Alejandro C. Cruz, Dr. Gene Sharratt, Dr. Sue Kane) says Kenroy, Lee and Cascade are "aging, overcrowded, and increasingly expensive to maintain." It says the bond would replace 15 portables, create secure entrances and interior hallways, "eliminate 162 exterior classroom doors," move bus and parent pickup away from busy streets, replace aging building systems before failures, and qualify Eastmont for about $25 million in state assistance [S1].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed. The guide says no one came forward to serve on the committee against or to write a statement [S1]. No organized opposition was found.
