## What it does

"If approved by voters, this proposition would authorize Wahkiakum County to levy a regular property tax at a rate not to exceed forty cents per one thousand dollars of assessed valuation on all taxable property within the district. The levy proceeds would be used to fund operational and equipment costs as more fully described in Wahkiakum County Resolution No. 87-26 and RCW 84.52.069. The levy would be authorized for a six-year period with collection beginning in 2027." Qualifying seniors, veterans and disabled persons would be eligible for exemption under RCW 84.36.381 [S1].

- **Who put it on the ballot.** The county commissioners unanimously approved Resolution 87-26 on July 14, 2026, after the Wahkiakum County EMS and Trauma Care Council unanimously recommended it on July 2 [S2].
- **History.** The county's emergency management coordinator, Austin Smith, said the levy has been in place "for over 35 years and has remained a temporary six-year levy since that time" [S2]. Voters last renewed it in November 2020, 1,857 (63.47%) to 1,069 [S3].
- **Who votes.** Every Wahkiakum County voter; the levy is county-wide [S1].

## Cost

Up to $0.40 per $1,000 of assessed value a year, collected 2027 through 2032 [S1]. Smith said that is the same rate as six years ago [S2]. The rate actually levied in 2025 was $0.29670 per $1,000 in every tax code area of the county, against a county-wide assessed value of $889,883,494 [S4]. By arithmetic: $120 a year on a $300,000 home at the full $0.40, or about $89 at the 2025 rate; the full rate on the 2024 county value would raise about $356,000 a year.

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

The measure replaces the existing six-year county EMS levy approved in 2020 [S1][S3]. Under RCW 84.52.069, EMS levy proceeds may be used only for emergency medical care and services, including personnel, training, equipment, vehicles and structures; the first imposition of a six-year levy needs a 60% supermajority, while a later renewal needs a simple majority [S5]. The levy limitations in RCW 84.52.043 do not apply to it [S5].

The published statute also says that where a county levies an EMS tax, other taxing districts may levy only the difference up to fifty cents, and that no other district's EMS proposition may be on the same ballot as a county one [S5]. Fire Protection District No. 2 (Skamokawa) has its own EMS levy proposition on this ballot, at up to $1.00 [S1]; neither ballot title explains how the two fit together, and no explanatory statement was published.

## Arguments for

No statement for was published [S1]. County officials describe it as a continuation of a levy in place for more than 35 years, at an unchanged rate [S2].

## Arguments against

No statement against was published [S1], and no PDC committee for or against a Wahkiakum measure is registered [S6].
