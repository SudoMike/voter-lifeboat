## Background

Bianca Tse is a Bellevue attorney and a judge pro tem in King County District Court [S1]. She also sits pro tem in Eastside municipal courts and has done pro tem work since 2021 [S2][S4][S6]. She immigrated from Hong Kong as a child, grew up in Renton, and has lived in Bellevue for 25 years [S1][S6]. She spent 16 years as a King County deputy prosecuting attorney, handling misdemeanors through felonies, including homicide, assault, robbery, and domestic-violence cases [S4][S6]. In 2015 she moved to private practice. She leads Tse & Associates P.S., which offers bilingual services in civil litigation, real estate, business, and estate planning [S4][S6]. She holds a B.A. from the University of Washington and a J.D. from Tulane University School of Law [S1]. Her campaign lists service as a court-appointed guardian ad litem and as a trainer for law enforcement on search-and-seizure law [S4].

**Changes since the primary dossier [S2].** Her general-election statement was rewritten [S1][S3]:
- The primary statement named the Loren Miller Bar Association's EWQ rating and said two other associations also rated her EWQ. The general statement says only that she earned EWQ "by multiple bar associations" and names none.
- She now describes a "30-year career in trial courts" and says she has ruled in "thousands of cases, sometimes hearing more than 100 cases in a day."
- New text says the position "requires different skills than being an administrative law judge," which contrasts with her opponent's current job.
- She adds that "Judges should be non-partisan. I believe politics should play no role in our courts. I'm endorsed by people who know me by my good work, not by party affiliation."
- In the endorsement list, Newcastle Mayor Sherlock was added and the named Bellevue councilmembers were dropped.

She advanced from the primary in second place (see Record) [S7].

## Positions

Judicial approach:
- Says each case and participant matters even on high-volume calendars, and that she decides cases "with fairness and integrity" [S1].
- Campaign values are "Thoughtfulness," "Safety," and "Empathy." Under "Safety" she lists community accountability, "holistic approaches," and protection of vulnerable people [S4].
- Says politics "should play no role in our courts" [S1].

Criminal-justice formation, bail and diversion:
- Her formation is prosecution-side: 16 years as a deputy prosecutor. She told Northwest Asian Weekly that domestic-violence cases involving immigrant families shaped her [S6].
- No sourced statements on bail, pretrial release, diversion, or therapeutic courts were found. The campaign's "holistic approaches" language is not tied to specific programs [S4].

Court administration and access:
- Offers bilingual legal services in her practice [S4][S6]. No specific court-administration proposals were found [S4].

## Record

- Judge pro tem since 2021 in King County District Court and Eastside municipal courts (Mercer Island, Kirkland, and Issaquah, per her primary statement) [S2][S4]. No published rulings were located.
- **August 4, 2026 primary:** 35,074 votes (27.9%), second of three [S7].
- **Bar ratings, independently confirmed:**
  - King County Bar Association: "Qualified," dated April 1, 2025 [S8].
  - Washington Women Lawyers: "Qualified," 2026 [S9].
  - Joint Asian Judicial Evaluation Committee: "EWQ" for King County District Court, 2025 list [S10][S11]. Northwest Asian Weekly reported in April 2026 that the Asian Bar Association of Washington gave her its highest rating [S6].
- **Bar ratings, self-reported only:** her campaign lists EWQ from the Latino Bar Association and the Loren Miller Bar Association [S5]. Neither was found on a public rater page. Her pamphlet's claim of EWQ "by multiple bar associations" is thus confirmed for one association (JAJEC). The two independent ratings found for her, KCBA and WWL, are both "Qualified," and the pamphlet does not mention them [S1][S8][S9][S11].
