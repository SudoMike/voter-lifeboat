## Background

Jan Trasen is an administrative law judge in the Washington State Office of Administrative Hearings (Child & Family Care Division) and a judge pro tem for King County District Court and Edmonds Municipal Court [S1][S5]. Before becoming an ALJ she spent 26 years as a public defender and appellate attorney: 16 years with the Washington Appellate Project and 10 years with the Legal Aid Society in New York [S1][S5]. She argued criminal, juvenile, and dependency cases before the Washington Supreme Court and Court of Appeals, and her campaign says her caseload included felony, misdemeanor, and Indian Child Welfare Act matters [S1][S5]. She holds a J.D. from Boston College Law School and a B.A. from Tufts University [S1]. She taught in a Los Angeles middle school as a Teach For America corps member [S5]. She lives in Kirkland [S1].

She serves on the board of The Ruby Room, a Renton nonprofit, and on the Cardozo Society's Judicial Evaluation Committee [S5].

**Changes since the primary dossier [S2].** Her general-election statement now claims EWQ ratings from "six" local bar associations; the primary statement said "five" [S1][S3]. It describes her as "the only candidate" with those six ratings. Her endorsement list grew: "over 95 judges & elected officials" replaces "over 75 appellate/trial judges." New names include Commissioners Cho and Calkins, Claudia Balducci, Reps. Goodman and Hall, Bellevue Firefighters, the 1st and 5th LD Democrats, and Washington State Employees [S1][S3]. She advanced from the primary in first place (see Record) [S7].

## Positions

Judicial approach:
- Says she treats everyone in her courtroom with respect and makes "fair decisions in cases affecting public safety, healthcare access, and family stability" [S1].
- Says experience "across every level of the legal system" (trial, appellate, administrative, and judicial) gives her "the judgment, temperament, and independence needed to serve fairly and effectively" [S1][S4].

Criminal-justice formation, bail and diversion:
- Her formation is defense-side: 26 years of public defense and appellate advocacy [S1][S5].
- Her campaign site lists "mental-health and substance-use treatment options that reduce recidivism and make neighborhoods safer" among her priorities [S4]. This is a stated priority, not a documented record. No sourced statements on bail or pretrial release were found.

Court administration and access:
- Her campaign theme of "courts work for everyone" is general [S1]. No specific administrative proposals were found [S4][S5].

## Record

- As an ALJ she hears cases and issues orders on child-safety, family-stability, and service-access matters; as a pro tem she presides over jury trials, arraignments, civil protection orders, and small claims, per her campaign [S2][S5]. No published rulings were located.
- **August 4, 2026 primary:** 59,661 votes, 47.5% of votes cast for candidates and write-ins, finishing first of three [S7].
- **Bar ratings, independently confirmed:**
  - King County Bar Association: "Exceptionally Well Qualified," dated June 11, 2025 [S8].
  - Washington Women Lawyers: "Exceptionally Well Qualified," listed under 2025 [S9].
  - Joint Asian Judicial Evaluation Committee: "EWQ" for King County District Court, 2025 list [S10][S11].
- **Bar ratings, self-reported only:** her campaign also lists EWQ ratings from the QLaw Judicial Evaluation Committee, the Latina/o Bar Association, and the Cardozo Society Judicial Evaluation Committee, plus "Well Qualified" from the Loren Miller Bar Association [S6]. QLaw's public list showed no entries after January 2024 at access time, so its rating could not be confirmed [S12]. The Latina/o, Cardozo, and Loren Miller ratings were not found on a public rater page. She sits on the Cardozo Society's evaluation committee, per her campaign [S5].

## Scoring notes

- Defense-side formation (26 years of public defense and appeals), versus Tse's 16 years as a prosecutor [S1][S5].
- Full-time judicial experience as an ALJ, in addition to District Court pro tem service [S1][S5].
- Independently confirmed ratings: EWQ from KCBA, WWL, and JAJEC. Three of the six EWQ ratings her statement claims could not be confirmed [S6][S8][S9][S11].
- Her campaign names treatment options to reduce recidivism; there is no documented bail or therapeutic-court record [S4].
- Finished first in the primary with 47.5%. She has raised less than Tse [S7][S14].
