## Background

Noel Merfeld is a dependency attorney who represents children in the child welfare system. He is running for Southwest Position 5, a race with no incumbent on the ballot [S1][S3]. He holds a J.D. from the University of Washington School of Law and a B.A. from the University of Washington [S1]. His campaign site gives the B.A. as Interdisciplinary Social Sciences [S3].

His campaign site describes his career as follows [S3]:

- Rule 9 intern at the Skagit County Prosecutor's Office during law school.
- Prosecutor with the Okanogan County Prosecutor's Office after graduating.
- Several years as a contract public defender in Okanogan County.
- Criminal defense at Feldman & Lee in Lynnwood, practicing in Lynnwood Municipal Court and Snohomish County District Court.
- Continued Okanogan public defense during the COVID-19 pandemic while living in White Center.
- Most recently, representing children and families in dependency proceedings.

The site gives no dates for these roles. No independent source confirmed them in this research [S3]. The campaign says he was born in Seattle, raised in Bellingham, and lives in White Center [S2][S3].

## Positions

Judicial approach (self-described):

- Says judges "should listen and learn—both inside the courtroom and in the community." He argues that community engagement builds trust and gives judges better information [S1][S2][S4].
- Says "Judges are not policymakers, and there are important limits on the role judges may play in advocating for policy change" [S4].
- Says "Defendants who feel heard are more likely to accept their sentence as fair and reflect on their behavior" [S1].

Criminal-justice formation, bail and diversion:

- His formation spans prosecution (Okanogan County), public defense (Okanogan, Lynnwood, Snohomish County) and child dependency advocacy [S1][S3].
- Says judges "should be compassionate, but not permissive or naive." He says he "will impose jail when the facts of an individual case show that it is necessary to protect the public or achieve a just result" [S4].
- Supports KCDC's therapeutic courts (Community Court, Mental Health Court, Veterans Court). He says he practiced in therapeutic courts as both a prosecutor and a public defender, and that "more people who could benefit from therapeutic court programs should have access to them" [S4].
- Describes the court as a partner in the response to the fentanyl epidemic, mainly by making treatment information available and working with providers [S4].
- On protection orders, he says that when the law permits and the facts justify it, orders "should prohibit contact with children and animals in the household of the victim even when they are not the direct victim of the abuse" [S4].
- No statements specifically on pretrial bail were found [S1][S4].

Court administration and access:

- Supports more help for self-represented litigants, including "considering the use of court facilitators." He says "Filing fees and complicated procedures should not become unnecessary barriers" [S4].

## Record

- No rulings exist (he has not served as a judge). No published decisions or news coverage of his cases were located.

## Endorsements

- Pamphlet: SeaTac Mayor Mohamed Egal, Burien Councilmember Sam Mendez, "the 33rd District Democrats, and more" [S1].
- Campaign site adds State Sen. Bob Hasegawa (11th LD), Federal Way Councilmember Martin Moore, Des Moines Deputy Mayor Gene Achziger, Burien Mayor Sarah Moore and SeaTac Councilmember Caitlin Konya [S2].
- Several of these local officials also appear on his opponent's endorsement list. SeaTac Mayor Egal and Burien Mayor Moore are listed by both Position 5 candidates [S2][S12].
- He is listed as an attorney endorser of Harry Steinmetz in Southwest Position 4 [S5].
- The 34th District Democrats endorsed his opponent [S7]. The King County Democrats' 2026 list makes no endorsement in this race [S6]. His opponent's site also claims the 33rd LD Democrats, and that conflict was not resolved [S1][S12]. Party endorsements are recorded as facts only.
- Bar ratings: the King County Bar Association's 2026 page lists no rating for this race [S8], and Washington Women Lawyers' page lists none for him [S9]. He claims no bar rating [S1].
