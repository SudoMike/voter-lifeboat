## Background

Renee Walls is a prosecuting attorney and judge pro tem running for Southwest Position 5, a race with no incumbent on the ballot [S1][S3].

- Her pamphlet statement cites 26 years as a prosecuting attorney and 17 years as a judge pro tem "presiding over literally thousands of cases" [S1].
- Her campaign site lists her as prosecuting attorney "for Several Cities including Algona, Burien, Federal Way, and Tukwila" and judge pro tempore "for all levels of trial court." It also lists her as a hearing officer for the Washington State Bar Association, a past president of the South King County Bar Association, a pro bono volunteer with the Northwest Immigrant Rights Project, and a member of the Community Accountability Board for the Diversion and Partnership for Youth Justice Program [S3].
- A retired Southwest Division judge quoted on her site says she appeared before him "for at least a decade as prosecutor for City of Burien" [S5].
- None of these roles, dates or employers was confirmed by an independent source in this research. They are self-reported or attested by endorsers [S1][S3][S5].

She was born in South Korea and adopted, and was raised by a mother who immigrated from the Philippines and a father who farmed in Minnesota [S1][S3].

The education and occupation fields of her pamphlet entry are garbled in the printed pamphlet. The "Education" field reads "Lewis & Clark Law School, Portland, Juris Doctorate", and the "Occupation" field reads "University of Illinois, Urbana-Champaign, Bachelor of Arts" [S1]. Read together, they indicate a J.D. from Lewis & Clark and a B.A. from the University of Illinois, with no occupation line printed [S1].

## Positions

Judicial approach (self-described):

- Describes herself as "a conscientious and careful arbiter" with "a deep understanding of how judicial decisions affect individuals, families, and the broader community" [S2].
- Says serving in both prosecutorial and judicial roles gives her "a broad lens through which she views justice," which is "about people, families, and the extraordinary impact our courts have on our community" [S3].
- Says a judge needs "legal acumen, demonstrated judicial temperament, and relevant experience" [S3].

Criminal-justice formation, bail and diversion:

- Her formation is prosecution: 26 years, per the pamphlet, for South King County cities, per the campaign site [S1][S3].
- She lists membership on a community accountability board for a youth diversion program [S3].
- Her materials make no statements on bail, sentencing philosophy or therapeutic courts [S1][S2][S3].

Court administration and access:

- Her statement cites "fair, balanced, and efficient justice" as a pro tem [S1]. No specific court-administration proposals were found [S1][S2][S3].

## Record

- No rulings or published decisions were located. Her pro tem service is self-reported, though endorsing attorneys say they appeared before her as a pro tem, including in Superior Court [S1][S5].

## Endorsements

- Pamphlet: "over 35 judges, SW Democratic legislative districts, National Women's Political Caucus, and trusted community leaders" [S1].
- Her campaign site lists these endorsers [S4]:
  - Supreme Court: Justices Steven González and G. Helen Whitener, and retired Justice Mary Yu.
  - Superior Court: Judge Veronica Galván (Chief Judge, Clark Children and Family Justice Center), Judge Jason Poydras and others.
  - Current King County District Court: Chief Presiding Judge Rebecca Robertson, South Division Presiding Judge Brian Todd, and Judges Corinna Harn, Virginia Amato, Yvonne Chin, Laurel Gibson, Lisa O'Toole, Leah Taguba, Matthew York and others.
  - Elected officials: State Sens. Manka Dhingra, Tina Orwall, Rebecca Saldaña and Vandana Slatter, and the mayors of SeaTac, Federal Way and Burien.
  - Former Seattle Police Chief Carmen Best.
  - Organizations: the 30th, 33rd and 34th LD Democrats, Democrats for Diversity and Inclusion, and the National Women's Political Caucus.
  - About two dozen attorneys.
- The 34th District Democrats list her as their endorsee for Southwest Position 5 [S6]. The King County Democrats' 2026 list makes no endorsement in this race [S7]. Party endorsements are recorded as facts only.
- Both candidates claim the 33rd District Democrats: she lists them on her site [S4], and her opponent lists them in his pamphlet statement [S1]. The LD organization's own page could not be reached to resolve this.
- Bar ratings: the King County Bar Association's 2026 page lists no rating for this race [S8], and Washington Women Lawyers' page lists none for her [S9]. She claims no bar rating [S1].

## Campaign finance

- The PDC summary (updated Oct. 5, 2026) reports $30,838.32 in contributions, $37,533.77 in expenditures, $5,000 in loans and $4,358.41 in debts [S10].
- Itemized contributions cover 22 records through Sept. 1, 2026. About $27,099 is reported as the candidate's own in-kind contributions, including $15,000 to a communications firm and her filing fee. About $3,739 is from others, including $100 from the National Women's Political Caucus of WA [S11].
