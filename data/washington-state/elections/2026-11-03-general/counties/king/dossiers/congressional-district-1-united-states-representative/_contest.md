## What the office does

A U.S. Representative is one of 435 voting members of the House, elected to a two-year term. The House writes and votes on federal law, originates tax bills, controls federal spending through appropriations, and oversees the executive branch through committees; members also handle constituent casework with federal agencies [S2]. Washington's 1st District covers parts of King and Snohomish counties, from Eastside suburbs north through Bothell, Mill Creek, Snohomish, Monroe and Marysville; King County voters see it in the North/Eastside state pamphlet edition [S1][S3].

## Race dynamics

Incumbent Suzan DelBene (D), in office since 2012 and the 2026 chair of the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee, faces Mary Silva (R), an Everett audiologist and Republican precinct committee officer who has not held public office [S1]. In the Aug. 4 top-two primary DelBene had 53.1% and Silva 26.2% in a seven-candidate field, per the Herald's updated count [S3]. DelBene reported $3.55 million in receipts through mid-July; Silva reported $8,434 through September, most of it her own money [S4][S5].

## Issue axes that genuinely differentiate these candidates

1. **Experience:** a 14-year incumbent on Ways and Means with enacted bills versus a first-time candidate with no public office [S1].
2. **Climate:** DelBene criticizes cuts to green-energy investment and votes against rolling back California emission rules; Silva told the Herald she is "not a big believer in the climate change narrative" (see candidate dossiers).
3. **Defense and foreign policy:** both criticize the Netanyahu government, from different directions. Silva's platform calls for a Pentagon audit and her site hosts a "Netanyahu war crimes" video; DelBene says the U.S. should not subsidize Netanyahu's conduct but voted against cutting aid through the Massie amendment and against the FY2027 NDAA (see candidate dossiers).
4. **Reform vs. working the system:** DelBene runs her party's national House campaign arm and takes substantial PAC money. Silva's platform focuses on auditing agencies and contractors and on voter ID (see candidate dossiers).
5. **Taxes:** DelBene pairs working-family tax credits with IRS enforcement on high earners. Silva pairs small-business tax incentives and a lower overall burden with closing offshore tax havens (see candidate dossiers).

Neither candidate has a public position on I-645, I-1 or I-638 as of Oct. 8, 2026. `social` evidence exists for DelBene only (abortion statement, H.R. 28 vote).
