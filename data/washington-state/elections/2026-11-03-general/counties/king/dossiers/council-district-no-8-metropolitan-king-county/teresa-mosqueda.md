## Background

Teresa Mosqueda is the incumbent Metropolitan King County Councilmember for District 8, holding the seat since January 2024 after about seven years on the Seattle City Council [S2]. Her general-election pamphlet statement lists her occupation as "County Councilmember; labor champion, children's advocate, public health policy leader," and her education as a Master's in Public Administration (health-policy focus) from The Evergreen State College and a BA from the University of Washington [S1]. Before elected office she worked at the Children's Alliance and the Washington State Labor Council [S1][S2]. She chairs the King County Board of Health and sits on the Sound Transit Board [S2][S5]. District 8 covers Burien, White Center, West Seattle, South Park, the Duwamish Valley, Tukwila, and Vashon-Maury Island [S2].

## Positions

### Housing
- Pamphlet priority: "expanding affordable housing and preventing displacement"; says she has worked at the county "to increase investments in affordable and supportive housing" [S1].
- Campaign site groups housing under "Build Housing": workforce housing, supportive housing, and green housing [S3].
- Sits on the steering committee formed by County Executive Girmay Zahilay in July 2026 to evaluate a new revenue source for shelter and affordable housing, with a countywide housing levy among the options; the article reports no position she took on it [S17].

### Public safety
- Pamphlet priority: "improving community safety through behavioral health and gun violence prevention" [S1].

### Taxes and revenue
- At a Sept. 17, 2026 Board of Health hearing on federal Medicaid cuts under HR1, she called for using state-authorized property-tax tools for public health and Harborview: "We have these revenue tools at our disposal to invest in the safety net that is Harborview and our public health clinics - the time to act is now" [S5].
- Led passage of Seattle's JumpStart payroll tax while on the City Council (record kept from the primary dossier) [S2].

### Working families, health, and transit
- Pamphlet priority: "investing in working families with accessible transit, childcare, healthcare, and union job opportunities"; also cites behavioral health services, public health infrastructure, and protecting immigrant and refugee communities [S1].
- Campaign site priorities: public health, housing, and "working family stability" (healthcare, childcare, transit, living-wage jobs) [S3].
- On Vashon, she backed expanded Water Taxi service, the transfer of the Mukai facility to Friends of Mukai, and the Seattle Indian Health Board's Thunderbird Treatment Center [S16].

### Gender, LGBTQ+, and reproductive policy
- Co-sponsored Motion 2026-0264, "supporting King County's LGBTQIA2+ community and opposing Washington state ballot initiative IL26-638," and voted yes on its passage on Sept. 22, 2026 [S6][S8].
- Listed as an endorser on the NO HATE in WA State page opposing I-001 and I-638 [S12].

### Parents and schools
- Co-sponsored Motion 2026-0265, "supporting King County's LGBTQIA2+ community and opposing Washington state ballot initiative IL26-001," and voted yes on Sept. 22, 2026 [S7][S9].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- **I-645 (income-tax repeal):** Listed as "Teresa Mosqueda, King County Council" on the Vote No on Initiative 645 coalition page [S11].
- **I-1 / IL26-001 (parental rights):** Opposes. She co-sponsored Motion 2026-0265 opposing IL26-001 and voted yes on it (passed 7-2 as amended on Sept. 22, 2026) [S7][S9]. She is also on the NO HATE endorser list [S12].
- **I-638 / IL26-638 (girls' sports):** Opposes. She co-sponsored Motion 2026-0264 opposing IL26-638 and voted yes on it (passed 7-2 as amended on Sept. 22, 2026) [S6][S8][S12].

## Record

- **Since July 2026:**
  - Motions opposing IL26-638 and IL26-001: co-sponsor and yes vote; both passed 7-2 (Dunn and von Reichbauer voting no) [S6][S7][S8][S9].
  - Sponsored an ordinance on permit review performance reporting (introduced July 21, 2026; in committee as of Oct. 8) [S10].
  - Sponsored an ordinance on a master lease for an affordable housing development at 701 South Jackson Street (introduced Oct. 6, 2026) [S10].
  - Co-sponsored the ordinance for the county council to assume governance of the King County transportation district (introduced Sept. 1, 2026) [S10].
  - Sponsored a motion approving the 2025 mental illness and drug dependency (MIDD) annual report (introduced Oct. 6, 2026) [S10].
  - As Board of Health chair, convened the Sept. 17 hearing on HR1 Medicaid cuts [S5].
  - Her 2026 press releases since July cover Sound Transit's Ballard timeline, flood-reduction grants, habitat restoration in District 8, and Welcoming Week [S4].
- **Earlier record (from the primary dossier):** sponsored the county ban on rent price-fixing algorithms (September 2025); procurement labor-standards legislation (May 2025); housing impact-fee legislation (August 2025); co-sponsored Strategic Climate Action Plan updates (October 2025). On the Seattle City Council she led the JumpStart payroll tax [S2].

## Endorsements

- Pamphlet: Congresswoman Jayapal, Governor Ferguson, AG Nick Brown, "all endorsing unions including MLK Labor, SEIU locals, UFCW 3000, and Seattle Building Trades," the 34th, 33rd and 11th District Democrats, Progreso Latino Progress, Washington Conservation Action, WA Housing Alliance Action Fund, NW Women's Political Caucus, "and 200+ more" [S1].
- Campaign site also lists Seattle City Attorney Erika Evans, SEIU 6, SEIU 925 and UFW [S3].
- Vashon-Maury Island Beachcomber endorsed her in the primary [S15]. PubliCola lists her among candidates endorsed by The Stranger [S18].
- The Seattle Times editorial board endorsed her opponent in the primary [S2]. No general-election editorial endorsement for this race was found as of Oct. 8, 2026.

## Campaign finance

- PDC summary (updated Oct. 5, 2026): $140,598.29 raised, $111,140.86 spent, $1,250 in debts, $2,037.03 carried forward; $330.84 in independent expenditures for her [S13].

## Primary result

- First in the Aug. 4 primary with 39,909 votes (66.31%); Nick Duda 15,000 (24.92%); Mia Jacobson 5,090 (8.46%); write-ins 189 [S14].

## Scoring notes

- The only candidate with an elected record: three years on the County Council after seven on the Seattle City Council, with enacted housing and labor legislation [S1][S2].
- Calls for using available property-tax revenue tools to backfill federal health cuts and has a record of passing the JumpStart payroll tax [S2][S5].
- Frames public safety through behavioral health and gun-violence prevention [S1].
- Consolidated Democratic and labor endorsements; raised more than four times her opponent [S1][S13].
