## What the office does

The Metropolitan King County Council "makes laws and adopts county budget"; members are elected by council district to four-year terms, with a 2026 salary of $187,547 [C1]. The council's portfolio includes public health, behavioral health, Metro transit, the Sheriff's Office and courts for unincorporated areas, regional homelessness and housing investments, and land use in unincorporated areas [C2]. District 8 covers Burien, White Center, West Seattle, South Park, the Duwamish Valley, Tukwila, and Vashon-Maury Island [C2].

## Race dynamics

Incumbent Teresa Mosqueda and challenger Nick Duda advanced from the August primary, where Mosqueda took 66.31% and Duda 24.92% (Mia Jacobson 8.46%) [C3]. The seat is on the ballot in an even year because of a 2022 charter amendment that moved county offices to even years [C2]. Both candidates run as Democrats in a nonpartisan race: Duda calls himself "a lifelong Democrat," and Mosqueda's endorsements come from Democratic organizations and labor [C1].

Mosqueda runs on her record on housing, labor standards, and public health. As Board of Health chair she has called for using state-authorized property-tax tools to backfill federal Medicaid cuts [C1][C7]. Duda argues the region's results on homelessness, overdoses and property crime "aren't for lack of funding." He proposes audits and measurable outcomes, "treatment-required" paths off the street, and permitting reform [C1]. Since July, Mosqueda co-sponsored council motions opposing initiatives IL26-638 and IL26-001, which passed 7-2 [C6]. As of Oct. 5, Mosqueda had raised $140,598 and Duda $30,492 [C4][C5]. Duda won the Seattle Times editorial board's primary endorsement [C2].

## Issue axes that genuinely differentiate these candidates

1. **Spending (audit and restrain vs. invest and expand):** Mosqueda calls for expanding investments in housing, behavioral health, childcare and transit. Duda says spending has outpaced results and calls for audits and measurable outcomes [C1].
2. **Taxes:** Mosqueda backs using new property-tax authority for public health and passed Seattle's JumpStart payroll tax. Duda says the region has "consistently increased taxes" and that residents are "being taxed out of your home" [C1][C2][C7].
3. **Safety and homelessness:** Mosqueda: behavioral health and gun-violence prevention. Duda: treatment-required paths, escalating consequences for chronic offenders, sober housing [C1].
4. **Experience:** a three-year county incumbent with seven prior years on the Seattle City Council, against a first-time candidate with private-sector and council-staff experience [C1][C2].
5. **Housing:** both want more housing. Mosqueda emphasizes affordable and supportive housing and anti-displacement; Duda emphasizes permitting reform and density near stations. The difference is in method more than direction [C1].

Climate does not clearly separate them: neither candidate's general-election material sets out a climate-policy position beyond Mosqueda's earlier co-sponsorship of the county's Strategic Climate Action Plan update [C1][C2].
