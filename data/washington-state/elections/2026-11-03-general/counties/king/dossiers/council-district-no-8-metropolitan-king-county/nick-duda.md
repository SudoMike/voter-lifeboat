## Background

Nick Duda is a first-time candidate challenging incumbent Teresa Mosqueda [S2]. His pamphlet statement lists his occupation as "Former data analytics consultant; recent City Councilmember Policy Lead" and his education as a BA in Political Science from the University of Notre Dame [S1]. He spent 17 years in the private sector (Target, Amazon, then nearly eight years at an early-stage data analytics startup) and about 10 months as a legislative aide to Seattle City Councilmember Maritza Rivera [S2][S6]. He describes himself as "a lifelong Democrat" [S1]. He lives in West Seattle and grew up in Detroit [S2].

## Positions

### Accountability and spending
- Pamphlet: "Our leaders often measure success by how much they spend, not how effectively those dollars are spent"; says the region's results on homelessness, overdoses and property crime "aren't for lack of funding" [S1].
- Campaign site: "Good government isn't measured by how much we spend. It's measured by what we accomplish." Calls for measurable goals, published results, and stronger "audits and contract oversight before failures become crises" [S3].

### Taxes
- Pamphlet: "we've consistently increased taxes and spent more on homelessness than almost any region in America"; says government should work for "a longtime resident being taxed out of your home" [S1]. He has not stated a position on a specific tax or levy in the sources reviewed.

### Public safety
- Pamphlet: "public safety with real accountability" [S1].
- Campaign site: "Use diversion where appropriate, but escalate consequences for chronic and organized offenders"; target pimps and traffickers "while giving victims housing, services, and a path to clear their records" [S3].
- In the primary he favored more aggressive enforcement on public drug use, including a "three strikes" approach leading to mandatory treatment (Seattle Times interview, kept from the primary dossier) [S2].

### Homelessness, treatment, and recovery
- Pamphlet: "compassionate, treatment-required paths off the street for those struggling with addiction" [S1].
- Campaign site: expand public treatment capacity and use private treatment beds when necessary; create stabilization centers and fix the jail medical-intake process; "Build sober housing" [S3].
- The Beachcomber reported he proposes requiring at least half of government-funded shelters to be sober facilities and partnering with Amazon and Microsoft on homeless-services coordination [S6].

### Housing and transit
- Pamphlet: "building more housing by fixing permitting and reducing barriers to new construction, and public housing that is safe and stabilizing for our most vulnerable" [S1].
- Campaign site: "Allow substantially more density within several blocks of stations"; "Accessible fare gates paired with reduced and free fares for people who qualify" [S3].
- Economic development: "Fast-track permitting" with clear timelines and protection of industrial land [S3].

### Climate
- Pamphlet lists "clean air and water" among the things district residents want [S1]. No climate-policy position found.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- **I-645 (income-tax repeal):** No public position found as of 2026-10-08. He is not listed on the Vote No on I-645 coalition page [S7].
- **I-1 / IL26-001 (parental rights):** No public position found as of 2026-10-08. He is not listed on the NO HATE endorser page [S8].

## Record

- No elected or legislative voting record. As an aide to Councilmember Rivera he helped shepherd the legislation that put the Seattle Public Library levy on the August 2026 ballot (from the primary dossier) [S2].

## Endorsements

- The Seattle Times editorial board endorsed him in the primary; his campaign site lists that endorsement [S2][S3]. No union, party-organization, or elected-official endorsements were found. No general-election editorial endorsement was found as of Oct. 8, 2026.

## Campaign finance

- PDC summary (updated Oct. 5, 2026): $30,491.75 raised, $20,842.55 spent, no debts [S4].

## Primary result

- Second in the Aug. 4 primary with 15,000 votes (24.92%) to Mosqueda's 39,909 (66.31%) [S5].
