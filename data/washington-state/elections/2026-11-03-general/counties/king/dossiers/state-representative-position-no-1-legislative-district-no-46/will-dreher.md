## Background

Will Dreher (Prefers Democratic Party) is a first-time candidate [S1]. He is a former Assistant U.S. Attorney who prosecuted January 6th rioters, hate and violent crimes, and civil-rights violations by officers, and he clerked for U.S. Supreme Court Justice Elena Kagan [S1]. He was a 4th-grade public school teacher and a researcher at union-affiliated political organizations, and is now an attorney suing oil, tobacco and AI companies on behalf of consumers [S1]; KUOW identifies him as a partner at the law firm Keller Rohrback [S8]. He holds a BSFS from Georgetown University and a JD from Harvard Law School [S1]. His general-election pamphlet statement is materially the same as his primary statement [S1][S2].

In the August 4, 2026 primary he received 13,592 votes (about 30.7% of votes cast in the race), second to Gerry Pollet [S10].

## Positions

### AI and technology (signature issue)
- Pamphlet: "I'm the only candidate promising to aggressively regulate tech to slow relentless AI disinformation, job loss from AI/automation, and data center growth," plus social-media safeguards for kids [S1].
- Platform: "regulate or stop the construction of new data centers to preserve climate progress"; ban AI replacement of public-sector workers; liability for nonconsensual deepfakes; right-to-warn protections for AI employees; mandatory audits before internal model deployment; a statewide ban on smartphone use in school; banning addictive feeds on youth accounts; data-privacy legislation [S4].
- Told KUOW that Washington "did nothing" on AI while other states passed dozens of bills [S8].

### Taxes
- Platform: "I support the Millionaire's Income Tax, and I will fight any attempts to repeal it" [S4].
- Also proposes an AI tax, lifting the cap on the advanced computing surcharge, and replacing the B&O tax with a corporate profits tax [S4]. Pamphlet: "fix regressive taxes" [S1].

### Housing
- Platform: permitting reform, zoning density reform, transit-oriented development, supportive and transitional housing, and "rent stabilization laws that help renters from being priced out while housing supply is built" [S4].
- Told KUOW permitting delays "have added 5 to 8% to the cost of new housing construction" and proposed streamlining [S8].

### Public safety
- Platform: increase the likelihood of apprehension (deterrence), expand non-police responses to mental-health calls (Seattle's CARE model), fund a North Seattle sex-trafficking law-enforcement task force, and fund evidence-based gun-violence reduction programs [S4].
- Told KUOW the state "has done next to nothing to help our local law enforcement partners" on sex trafficking along Aurora Avenue North [S8].

### Climate
- Platform: oppose diverting Climate Commitment Act funds to general budget needs; slow data-center growth; aggressive transit investment [S4].

### Immigration
- Platform: healthcare access regardless of immigration status; divest public funds from privately operated detention facilities; support state lawsuits against federal constitutional violations [S4].

### Schools
- Pamphlet: plan to "fully fund schools and improve school performance" [S1].
- Platform: universal reading and dyslexia assessments "and parental notification of results"; reading and math specialists; curriculum revision to drop outdated literacy practices [S4].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Platform: "Support the No Hate in Washington State campaign to defeat Initiatives 1 and 638"; emergency funding for LGBTQ+ newcomer support organizations; healthcare access and safety in medical facilities and schools [S4].
- No abortion position found on his platform page [S4].

### Parents and schools
- Platform supports defeating I-1, the parental-rights initiative [S4]. His only parental-notification proposal is notifying parents of reading and dyslexia assessment results [S4].

## Endorsements

- Campaign site: The Stranger; PROTEC17; Washington State Democratic Party Stonewall Dems Caucus; College Democrats of Washington; NAACP WA PAC; Seattle City Attorney Erika Evans; Seattle City Councilmember Dionne Foster; former U.S. Attorney Tessa Gorman; former Seattle City Councilmember Andrew Lewis; others [S5]. The Stranger's endorsement was for the primary [S2].
- Pamphlet: City Attorney Erika Evans, City Councilmember Dionne Foster, College Dems [S1].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- **I-645 (IP26-645, income tax repeal):** States "I support the Millionaire's Income Tax, and I will fight any attempts to repeal it" [S4]. The platform page does not name I-645 [S4].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** Opposes; platform says he supports the No Hate in Washington State campaign "to defeat Initiatives 1 and 638" [S4].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** Opposes; same platform statement [S4].

## Campaign finance

PDC reports, as of a record updated 2026-10-04: $302,557.71 in contributions and $199,096.49 in expenditures [S9]. In June, Post Alley reported that roughly $100,000 of his early fundraising came from AI-industry donors, including employees of Anthropic and OpenAI [S6].

## Scoring notes

- First-time candidate with no elected experience; prosecutor and litigator background [S1].
- Explicit stated positions on all three statewide initiatives: will fight repeal of the millionaires' tax, and supports defeating I-1 and I-638 [S4].
- AI and tech regulation is his central issue; his platform includes stopping or regulating new data centers [S1][S4][S8].
- Housing emphasis on permitting speed and supply, paired with rent stabilization [S4][S8].
- Public-safety platform pairs deterrence and a trafficking task force with non-police crisis response [S4].
- Top fundraiser in the race [S9].
