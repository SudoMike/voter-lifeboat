# State Representative, Position 1, Legislative District 46 — contest overview

- depth: deep

## What the office does

A Washington State Representative is one of two House members for each of the 49 legislative districts, serving a two-year term in the 98-member House. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including K-12 funding, housing and land-use law, the tax code, healthcare programs, public safety and environmental policy. The Legislature is part-time; the 2026 regular session ended March 12.

## Race dynamics

- A Democrat-versus-Democrat general. Seven-term incumbent Gerry Pollet led the three-way primary with about 51.6%; former federal prosecutor Will Dreher finished second with about 30.7%, and urbanist Ron Davis (about 16.8%) was eliminated (see gerry-pollet.md, will-dreher.md).
- Dreher has outraised Pollet (about $303,000 vs. $206,000 in PDC-reported contributions); Post Alley reported in June that roughly $100,000 of Dreher's early money came from AI-industry donors.
- KUOW's September coverage framed the race around AI regulation (Dreher's centerpiece; Pollet proposes taxing AI and data-center storage with Oregon legislators), housing (Pollet emphasizes building for every income segment; Dreher emphasizes permitting speed), and Aurora Avenue trafficking (Dreher).

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Tech/AI:** both favor regulation; Dreher makes it his central issue with a long platform, including stopping or regulating new data centers.
2. **Experience:** fifteen-year incumbent and JLARC chair versus a first-time candidate.
3. **Parental rights and social:** Pollet voted for ESHB 1296 and calls himself a voice against "attacks on trans kids"; Dreher explicitly supports defeating I-1 and I-638. Both point the same direction, by different kinds of evidence (votes vs. stated positions).
4. **Taxes:** little distance. Pollet voted for and championed the millionaires' tax; Dreher says he will "fight any attempts to repeal it."
5. **Housing and safety:** differences of emphasis (government-shaped housing and rent limits vs. permitting plus rent stabilization; Dreher adds a trafficking task force and deterrence alongside non-police crisis response).
