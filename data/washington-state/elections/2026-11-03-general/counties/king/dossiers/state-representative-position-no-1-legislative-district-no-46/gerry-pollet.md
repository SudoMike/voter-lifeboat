## Background

Gerry Pollet (Prefers Democratic Party) is the incumbent State Representative for the 46th Legislative District, Position 1; his pamphlet statement describes him as "Your State Representative since 2012" [S1], and he has held the seat since 2011 [S8]. He is on the UW School of Public Health faculty, a public-interest attorney, and director of Heart of America Northwest, a citizens' group working on the cleanup of the Hanford nuclear site [S1]. He holds a law degree from the UW School of Law and serves on the Washington Coalition for Open Government board [S1]. His general-election pamphlet statement is materially the same as his primary statement [S1][S2].

In the August 4, 2026 primary he received 22,807 votes (about 51.6% of votes cast in the race), ahead of Will Dreher (13,592, about 30.7%) and Ron Davis (7,441, about 16.8%) [S14].

## Positions

### Taxes
- Says he "championed the Millionaires and capital gains taxes to make the wealthiest pay their fair share to fund schools, healthcare, and childcare"; also says he wants to cut the sales tax [S1].
- His issues page credits the capital gains tax with funding early learning and education [S4].

### Housing
- Says he is working to build affordable housing so children can afford to live in Seattle and seniors can age in place [S1].
- Supports "lowering barriers so more housing density can be built," and says "allowing rent increases of 10% per year is too high" [S4].
- Told KUOW "you have to build housing for each economic segment of our community, not just the people who are earning $200,000 or more," and argued for a government role in shaping housing development [S9].

### AI and data centers
- Lists "regulating AI and data centers" as an issue [S3]; his issues page says data centers "consume enormous amounts of water and electricity, increase air pollution, threaten fish habitat, and drive up utility costs" [S4].
- Told KUOW he is working with Oregon legislators on a tax on AI and data-center storage, with revenue for worker transition and energy, water and oversight needs [S9].

### Climate and environment
- Issues page says he worked to end the state's use of fossil fuels for electricity and pushed consideration of life-cycle methane emissions [S4].

### Immigration
- Says he passed "ICE mask bans" and protected immigrants [S1]; issues page says he "voted to force ICE agents to show their face" and protected undocumented college students from federal data sharing [S4].

### Public safety and guns
- Issues page says he co-sponsored the assault weapons ban (2023) and supports secure-storage requirements; endorsed by the Alliance for Gun Responsibility [S1][S4].

### Open government
- Long identified with legislative transparency and public-records access; as JLARC chair he says audits exposed tax breaks for data centers [S2][S4].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Says he funded abortion access [S1]; his issues page advocates full reproductive healthcare access [S4].
- Describes himself as "a strong voice against the attacks on trans kids," says he passed marriage equality and helped expand Medicaid coverage of gender-affirming care [S3][S4].

### Parents and schools
- Campaign materials focus on school funding (special-education funding cap, structured literacy) rather than parental notification or opt-outs [S4]. See his ESHB 1296 vote under Record.

## Record

- Chair of the Joint Legislative Audit and Review Committee; member of Appropriations, Postsecondary Education and Workforce, and Education committees (as of July 2026) [S5].
- Campaign credits him with helping lift the special-education funding cap and co-developing the Washington College Grant [S2][S4].
- Voted yes on ESHB 1296 (2025), which amended the parents' rights law enacted by I-2081 (House final passage 2025-04-24, 59-39) [S10].
- Voted yes on ESSB 6346 (2026), the tax on household income over $1 million (House final passage 2026-03-09, 51-46) [S11].
- No floor votes since July: the Legislature has not been in session since the 2026 regular session adjourned on March 12, 2026 [S13].

## Endorsements

- General-election pamphlet: Planned Parenthood; Washington Conservation Action; nurses; teachers; Labor Council; Alliance for Gun Responsibility; Congresswoman Pramila Jayapal; "more" [S1].
- Campaign site also lists Gov. Bob Ferguson and the Democratic Party [S3].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- **I-645 (IP26-645, income tax repeal):** No statement naming I-645 found on his campaign site or issues page [S3][S4]. Voted yes on ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S11], and his pamphlet statement says he championed it [S1].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** No public position found on his campaign site, issues page, pamphlet statement, or in the KUOW race coverage [S1][S3][S4][S9]. Voted yes on ESHB 1296, the 2025 amendments I-1 would undo [S10].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** No statement naming I-638 found [S3][S4]; his site describes him as "a strong voice against the attacks on trans kids" [S4].

## Campaign finance

PDC reports, as of a record updated 2026-10-05: $205,671.08 in contributions, $74,936.81 carried forward, and $139,012.29 in expenditures [S12]. Earlier in the cycle both primary challengers outraised him [S2][S8].

## Scoring notes

- The only candidate with a legislative record: fifteen years in the House, JLARC chair, Appropriations member [S1][S5][S8].
- Record votes on the 2026 ballot questions: yes on ESSB 6346 and on ESHB 1296 [S10][S11].
- Both candidates back progressive revenue; Pollet emphasizes building housing for each income segment and rent limits, Dreher permitting speed [S4][S9].
- On AI, Pollet proposes a data-center/AI tax with Oregon legislators; his opponent makes AI regulation his central issue [S9].
- Led the primary with about 51.6% [S14].
