## What the office does
A State Senator serves a four-year term in the 49-member Senate, voting on state budgets, taxes and all state law. The incumbent is also Senate Majority Leader and sets the majority caucus's agenda (jamie-pedersen.md [S1]).

## The district and race dynamics
The 43rd lies entirely within Seattle: Capitol Hill, the University District, Wallingford/Fremont, Eastlake, Montlake, South Lake Union and the downtown core (primary overview). The general is a Democrat-vs-Democrat contest. Majority Leader Jamie Pedersen, prime sponsor of the 2026 tax on household income over $1 million, faces first-time candidate and labor communicator Hannah Sabio-Howell, who runs to his left. KUOW reports the primary at about 66% Pedersen to 23% Sabio-Howell (jamie-pedersen.md [S5]). Gov. Ferguson and AG Nick Brown endorse Pedersen; Seattle Mayor Katie Wilson endorses Sabio-Howell (jamie-pedersen.md [S6]). Pedersen has raised about $558,000 to Sabio-Howell's $135,000 (PDC summaries in both dossiers). The I-645 repeal of Pedersen's signature tax is on the same ballot.

## Differentiating issue axes
- **Taxes:** Pedersen built the millionaires tax and frames it as restructuring an upside-down system. He has also expressed openness to estate-tax rollback and opposed a payroll-tax fund. Sabio-Howell calls the tax a first step and wants a corporate income tax and a payroll-based fund.
- **Reform:** Sabio-Howell refuses corporate PAC money and frames the race against "career politicians." Pedersen frames his approach as coalition-building.
- **Public safety:** Pedersen cites police hiring funding plus license-revocation reform. Sabio-Howell proposes non-police responders, alternatives to incarceration and restoring use-of-force standards.
- **Experience:** two decades and the Majority Leader post vs. no elected office.
- **Tech:** only Sabio-Howell addresses AI and data centers (restrictions).
- Both are on the progressive side of social policy and immigration, so these do not separate them much. Pedersen alone has a parental-rights data point: his ESHB 1296 vote. Neither has publicly stated a position on I-1 or I-638.
