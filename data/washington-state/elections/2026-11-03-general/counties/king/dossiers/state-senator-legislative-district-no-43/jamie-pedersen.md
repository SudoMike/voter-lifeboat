## Background
Jamie Pedersen (Prefers Democratic Party) is the incumbent State Senator for the 43rd District and Democratic Majority Leader (2025-present) [S1]. He served in the House 2007-2013 and has been a State Senator since 2013 [S1]. He is Executive Vice President and General Counsel at McKinstry, a Seattle engineering and construction firm (2012-present), and was a lawyer at K&L Gates (1995-2012) [S1]. He attended Puyallup High School, Yale College and Yale Law School [S1]. He was lead volunteer attorney for Lambda Legal in Washington's marriage equality case [S1]. He and his husband, a retired teacher, have four sons in public schools [S1].

In the August 4, 2026 primary, "around 66%" voted for Pedersen and "around 23%" for Hannah Sabio-Howell, per KUOW [S5]. FOX 13 reports he "garnered nearly three times as many votes" as Sabio-Howell [S6].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** his statement says he "led the effort to pass the Millionaires Tax and a capital gains tax to make our tax system fairer... while cutting taxes for working families" [S1]. He told FOX 13 that "solving key district issues like education funding requires restructuring Washington's tax code" [S6]. When the repeal effort launched he said: "Ultimately, I think the people of Washington understand how broken and upside-down our tax structure is and they will welcome this opportunity to make it better" [S7]. Per The Urbanist (cited in his primary dossier), he expressed support for rolling back the estate tax increase passed in the prior session and opposed Rep. Shaun Scott's payroll-tax "Well(th) Washington Fund" proposal as not politically feasible [S2].
- **Schools:** committed to "amply funding schools" [S1]; his site says he "championed an additional $1.4 billion investment in school districts" [S4].
- **Housing:** "I led efforts to provide $770 million in new funding for housing"; supported "rent stabilization, expand prohibited rental provisions and enacted laws aimed at streamlining housing construction" [S4].
- **Public safety:** got legislation passed "strengthening the State's authority to revoke law enforcement licenses after serious misconduct" and supported "a $100 million investment in new law enforcement hiring and training" [S4].
- **Climate:** worked to "defend the Climate Commitment Act" [S4]. His statement also cites climate action [S1].
- **Immigration:** cites "banning ICE agents hiding behind masks, stopping the use of cameras for surveillance and immigration enforcement" [S1].
- **Coalition approach:** on corporate taxation, he told KUOW that "hearing a variety of perspectives is a necessary part of coalition-building" [S5].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** "a fierce champion for equality, leading the fight for marriage equality, LGBTQ+ civil rights, and transgender privacy protections"; cites abortion rights and "restoring money Trump cut from Planned Parenthood" [S1].
- **Parents and schools:** no stated position on notification, records or opt-outs found. He voted for ESHB 1296 (see Record) [S10].

## Record
- **ESSB 6346 (2026 tax on household income over $1 million, which I-645 would repeal):** voted yea on Senate third reading (2026-02-16) and final passage as amended by the House (2026-03-11, 27-21) [S11]. He was the bill's prime sponsor and lead architect [S2][S7].
- **ESHB 1296 (2025 amendments to the parents' rights law that I-1 would undo):** voted yea on Senate final passage, 2025-04-11 (30-19) [S10].
- **EHB 1217 (2025 rent stabilization):** voted yea on Senate passage (2025-04-10) and conference-report final passage (2025-04-27) [S12].
- **ESHB 2266 (2026):** voted yea on Senate passage, 2026-03-04 (29-20) [S13]. The law requires cities to allow transitional and permanent supportive housing and indoor emergency shelters in specified urban zones [S14].
- No floor votes since July: the 2026 session "adjourned sine die on Thursday March 12, 2026" [S15], and no special session has been found.

## Endorsements

- The Stranger endorsed him in the primary, calling him "the most progressive Senate majority leader in state history" [S8]. The Seattle Times editorial board endorsed him in the primary [S2].
- Seattle Mayor Katie Wilson and the 43rd District Democrats endorsed his opponent [S2][S5].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645):** he is the prime sponsor of the tax I-645 would repeal [S7][S11]. When the repeal effort launched he said voters "will welcome this opportunity to make it better" [S7]. No statement naming I-645 directly was found on his campaign site or pamphlet [S1][S3].
- **I-1 (IL26-001):** no public position found on his campaign site, issues page or pamphlet statement, or in the KUOW and FOX 13 coverage [S1][S3][S4][S5][S6]. He voted yea on ESHB 1296, the amendments I-1 would undo [S10].
- **I-638 (IL26-638):** no public position found in the same sources [S1][S3][S4][S5][S6]. His statement cites leading on "transgender privacy protections" [S1].
