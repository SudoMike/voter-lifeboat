## Background
Chris Stearns (Prefers Democratic Party) is the incumbent State Representative for the 47th District, Position 2, Speaker Pro Tempore of the House, Vice Chair of State Government & Tribal Relations, and a member of the Capital Budget, Environment & Energy and Rules committees [S1]. He is a Navajo attorney (Cornell Law School JD, Williams College BA) and a former Auburn City Councilmember [S1]. He was appointed by President Clinton as the U.S. Energy Department's first Indian Affairs Director and formerly chaired the Washington State Gambling Commission [S1]. His community service includes the King County Affordable Housing Commission, the King County Mental Illness and Drug Dependency Committee, and the state Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women Task Force [S1]. He defeated Ted Cooke for this seat in the 2024 general election [S2].

In the August 4 primary's election-night returns he received 59.61% to Cooke's 40.19%; both advance in this rematch [S8].

## Positions

### Housing, behavioral health and workers
- His pamphlet statement cites "expanding affordable housing, strengthening worker protections, improving behavioral health services, and defending civil rights," and says he will keep "fighting for strong schools, safer communities" [S1].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- A section of his campaign site titled "Chris on Protecting Reproductive Choice" calls the Dobbs decision "unconscionable," says his work "has been recognized by Pro-Choice Washington," and says care must be accessible to Washingtonians "and those traveling from other states seeking care" [S3].
- "Healthcare – including reproductive and gender affirming care – is a human right and the legislature must protect it for everyone. Not just the wealthy" [S3]. The primary pamphlet carried the same position [S2].

### Families
- Says he will "fight for investments in families including affordable housing, childhood nutrition, and universal childcare" [S3].

### Parents and schools
- No campaign statement found [S1][S3]. His record includes a vote on ESHB 1296 (see Record).

### Energy
- Co-chairs the Legislative Nuclear Energy Caucus, per the primary dossier [S2].

## Record
- Voted yes on ESSB 6346 (2026), the tax on household income over $1 million that I-645 would repeal (House final passage 2026-03-09, 51-46) [S5].
- Voted yes on ESHB 1296 (2025), the amendments to the I-2081 parental-rights law that I-1 would undo (House third reading 2025-03-12; final passage as amended by the Senate 2025-04-24, 59-39) [S6].
- From the primary dossier (House Democrats caucus page): sponsored HB 1461 on clean energy infrastructure, signed by Governor Ferguson; a Native American apprenticeship bill signed under Governor Inslee; HB 1681 on problem-gambling funds (passed the House 80-16); and HB 1221 on data privacy and security (passed the Senate) [S2].
- No floor votes since July: the 2026 regular session adjourned sine die on March 12 [S9].

## Endorsements
- General pamphlet: Auburn Mayor Backus, Kent Mayor Ralph, Covington Mayor Smith, Congressmember Adam Smith, Governor Bob Ferguson, 47th LD Democrats, WA State Nurses Association, Aerospace Machinists 751, WA Conservation Action, WA State Labor Council [S1].
- Campaign site also lists Kent City Council President Satwinder Kaur, Sen. Claudia Kauffman, and Reps. Debra Entenman, Janice Zahn and Edwin Obras [S4].
- Washington Conservation Action [S10] and King County Democrats [S11].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, income tax repeal):** No public statement found on his campaign site or in the pamphlet [S1][S3]. He voted yes on ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S5].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** No public statement found [S1][S3]. He voted yes on ESHB 1296, the 2025 amendments I-1 would undo [S6].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** No statement naming I-638 found [S1][S3]. His site calls gender-affirming care a human right [S3].

## Campaign finance
PDC reports, as of a record updated 2026-10-05: $77,507.32 in contributions, $5,804.36 carried forward, and $50,020.86 in expenditures [S7].

## Scoring notes
- Incumbent in House leadership (Speaker Pro Tempore), with signed bills; his opponent has not held public office [S1][S2].
- Calls reproductive and gender-affirming care a human right; his opponent pledges to "uphold the biological definition of gender" [S3].
- Voted for the 2026 millionaires' tax and for ESHB 1296; his opponent would eliminate income taxes and "restore full parental rights in education" [S5][S6].
- This is a rematch of 2024, which Stearns won [S2].
