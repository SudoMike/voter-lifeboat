## What the office does

A State Representative is one of two House members elected from each legislative district to two-year terms. Representatives write and vote on state laws and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, covering taxes, housing, K-12 funding, public safety, healthcare and environmental regulation. The 47th covers the Kent, Auburn, Covington and Maple Valley area of South King County.

## Race dynamics

This is a 2024 rematch. Democrat Chris Stearns, the House Speaker Pro Tempore, faces Republican Ted Cooke, a software tester and longtime party officer who has never held public office. Only these two ran, and election-night primary returns had Stearns at 59.61% and Cooke at 40.19%. Cooke has raised very little (about $3.4K, against about $77.5K for Stearns, per PDC).

## Differentiating axes

- **taxes / spending:** Cooke would freeze new taxes, eliminate taxes on income, property and fuel, and audit every state office. Stearns voted for the 2026 millionaires' tax and runs on state investment in housing, behavioral health and childcare.
- **social:** Stearns calls reproductive and gender-affirming care "a human right" and calls Dobbs "unconscionable." Cooke pledges to "uphold the biological definition of gender."
- **parental-rights:** Cooke pledges to "restore full parental rights in education" and to post curricula for parental review. Stearns voted for ESHB 1296, the 2025 amendments I-1 would undo.
- **climate:** Cooke would end cap-and-trade and cut or eliminate the gas tax. Stearns has a clean-energy bill record and Washington Conservation Action's endorsement.
- **housing / local-control:** Cooke prefers "starter homes with yards over high-density apartments forced on us by Olympia." Stearns cites expanding affordable housing.
- **safety:** Cooke wants police to act on reasonable suspicion rather than probable cause and to keep "anti-police" members off use-of-force boards. Stearns's own public safety approach is general ("safer communities").
- **experience:** House leadership incumbent vs. a challenger who has never held public office.
