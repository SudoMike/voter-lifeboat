## Background
Jamila E. Taylor (prefers Democratic Party) is the incumbent State Representative for the 30th District, Position 1; she chairs the House Civil Rights & Judiciary Committee and the Developmental Disability Advocacy Caucus, is vice chair of the Members of Color Caucus, and sits on the Transportation and Early Learning & Human Services committees [S1]. She is an attorney and former statewide advocacy counsel who managed legal-aid attorneys serving domestic-violence survivors and crime victims, managed youth violence-reduction efforts, and runs a private law practice and nonprofit consultancy [S1]. JD, University of Oregon; BA, Virginia State University [S1]. Board member of BlackPast.org, the Federal Way Human Services Commission, Highline College Foundation, and Girl Scouts [S1]. In the August primary she received 63.95% of the vote to Republican Tiffany Bowyer's 35.93%, per final results reported by the Federal Way Mirror [S13].

## Positions
- **Public safety:** cites passing laws to reduce gun violence, improve police training and trust, and address root causes of crime [S1]; her priorities page says "excessive policing and incarceration should not be the community solution to a problem that has roots in systemic inequity and a lack of social resources" [S2].
- **Affordability and taxes:** says she delivered resources for housing and childcare, reduced small-business taxes, and expanded working-family tax credits [S1].
- **Housing and homelessness:** says she will fight so every family "can keep a roof over their head and access critical services" [S2].
- **Childcare and health care:** supports affordable and free day-care options and says the state must "care for all people" facing medication and housing costs [S2].
- **Jobs:** works with businesses, unions, and schools on apprenticeships and career pathways [S1].

## Record
- Voted yea on ESSB 6346 (9.9% tax on income over $1 million), House final passage 2026-03-09 [S4].
- Voted yea on ESHB 1296 at House passage (2025-03-12) and final passage (2025-04-24) [S5]; the bill amended the Initiative 2081 parental-rights list [S6].
- Voted yea on ESSB 5599 (2023), which lets youth shelters treat a minor's seeking gender-affirming or reproductive care as a compelling reason not to notify parents [S7][S8].
- Her campaign site lists six prime-sponsored bills that passed in 2021-2022, including HB 1773 (assisted outpatient behavioral-health treatment) and HB 2008 (ending IQ-score eligibility tests for developmental-disability services) [S2].
- No floor votes since July: the Legislature has not met in session since sine die on 2026-03-12 [S9].
- Campaign finance (PDC, data updated 2026-10-02): $86,696.76 raised and $84,938.81 spent for 2026 [S10].
