## Background

Julie Kang is a 30-year District 5 resident, first-generation immigrant, and educator; her pamphlet lists her occupation as "Seattle University, University of Washington Director & Faculty" and her education as UCLA (BA), California State University (MA) and University of Washington (PhD) [S1]. She co-founded Pacific International Bank, a community bank focused on Korean-American-owned small businesses, and serves on the King County Immigrant and Refugee Commission [S1][S2]. She calls herself "a Democrat committed to improving the lives of working families, seniors, children, and immigrants" [S1]. She was one of six finalists for the 2025 interim appointment to the District 5 seat and has not held elected office [S2].

## Positions

### Public safety and homelessness
- Campaign site: "Building back Seattle Police Department staffing levels and moving forward with a second police precinct in District 5"; "shutting down open air drug markets"; using "every tool in our tool box to bring pimps and johns to justice, including the Stay Out of Area Prostitution (SOAP) zone along Aurora Ave. N."; and "Expanding access to on-demand substance use disorder treatment" [S3].
- Pamphlet: "addressing chronic homelessness with care, treatment, housing, and accountability"; "I support police staffing, gun violence prevention, and enforcement focused on violent crime and trafficking"; coordinate providers, first responders, SPD and CARE [S1].
- Supports SOAP as "a tool worth trying out" and a mini-precinct in North Seattle [S5]. Told FOX 13: "I fully support restoring the number of officers we need. I also support we use the public safety cameras." [S6]
- Changed her position on CCTV cameras during the campaign: she initially opposed expansion over immigration-enforcement concerns, then supported it "with safety measurements in place" after reviewing local oversight rules [S5][S6][S7]. The Urbanist reported she also reversed an earlier position on SOAP [S7].

### Taxes and budget
- Campaign site: "The city must acknowledge the heavy financial burden it imposes on its own residents and local businesses through rising taxes, red tape, and inadequate basic services," citing "another regressive sales tax hike" [S3].
- At a July forum she opposed new taxes on residents and said government should budget for infrastructure needs without additional levies. She supported the city's transit measure while raising concerns about its sales-tax impact on affordability [S7].
- Told FOX 13: "We always make sure that every investment comes back with a return" [S6].

### Housing
- Pamphlet: "reduce permitting delays to build housing faster, support mixed-income and workforce housing near transit" [S1].
- Campaign site: shorten permit review; move "away from the Mandatory Housing Affordability (MHA) framework, which is a tax on desperately needed housing" and direct more Payroll Expense Tax (JumpStart) revenue to affordable housing; pair growth with sidewalks and transit; protect the tree canopy [S3].
- Backs the overall direction of the mayor's "Taller Denser Faster" proposal but says "growth needs to be paired with infrastructure" [S7].

### Small business and services
- Pamphlet: set up district offices on Aurora Avenue and in Lake City; "recruit affordable grocery stores" [S1].

### Climate
- Campaign site links housing growth to "greener, healthier, and more climate-resilient neighborhoods" through tree canopy protection [S3]. No broader climate-policy position found.

### Immigration
- Pamphlet: "I'll stand up to Trump when he threatens our communities and values"; campaign site: keep Seattle a sanctuary city [S1][S3].

### Gender, LGBTQ+, and reproductive policy
- Listed as an endorser ("Candidate for Seattle City Councilmember") on the NO HATE in WA State page opposing I-001 and I-638 [S10].

### Parents and schools
- Same NO HATE listing, which opposes I-001 [S10].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- **I-645 (income-tax repeal):** No public position found as of 2026-10-08. She is not listed on the Vote No on I-645 coalition page [S11].
- **I-1 / IL26-001 (parental rights):** Opposes; listed as an endorser on the NO HATE page against I-001 and I-638 [S10].
- **I-638 / IL26-638 (girls' sports):** Opposes; same listing [S10].

## Endorsements

- Pamphlet: "the 32nd LD Democrats, The Seattle Times, Tree PAC, LiUNA Local 242, local elected officials, community leaders, and 50+ neighborhood businesses" [S1].
- Campaign site: Senator Jesse Salomon; Representatives Cindy Ryu and Lauren Davis; Seattle Council President Joy Hollingsworth and Councilmembers Robert Kettle and Maritza Rivera; former officials including Tim Burgess, Sara Nelson and Tanya Woo; Northwest Women's Political Council; LiUNA; Moms Demand [S4].
- The only Seattle Times editorial endorsement found for this race is the primary one ("Residents who want an advocate instead of an ideologue ought to choose Julie Kang"). No general-election editorial was found as of Oct. 8, 2026 [S2][S4].

- Supports rebuilding SPD staffing, a second D5 precinct, SOAP, and public-safety cameras, paired with on-demand treatment [S1][S3][S5][S6].
- Opposes new taxes on residents and calls the city's tax burden heavy; would shift affordable-housing funding from MHA fees to JumpStart revenue [S3][S7].
- Emphasizes permitting reform and mixed-income housing near transit [S1][S3].
