## Background

Nilu Jenks is Political and Partnerships Director at FairVote Washington, a mother of two, and a Lake City resident with a BA in Economics and Spanish from Wellesley College [S1]. (The pamphlet text prints her job under "Education" and her degree under "Occupation" [S1].) She is a first-generation American whose parents immigrated from Iran [S2]. She ran for this seat in 2023, finishing third in the primary, and was one of six finalists the City Council considered in July 2025 for the interim appointment to the vacant District 5 seat [S2]. She has not held elected office [S2].

The seat is on the ballot for an unexpired one-year term, and the pamphlet lists only Jenks and Kang [S1]. Debora Juarez was appointed to the vacant seat in 2025 [S2]. Neither candidate is the incumbent.

## Positions

### Housing
- Platform: "Building 1,000 New Units of Affordable Housing" in District 5, using "public land, zoning flexibility, and partnerships with nonprofit developers" [S3].
- Pamphlet: "build more affordable housing" [S1].
- Supports Mayor Wilson's "Taller Denser Faster" proposal: "We built the transit. We should let people live next to it." [S7]
- In the primary she backed just-cause eviction, rent stabilization and a tenant right to counsel (from the primary dossier) [S2].

### Public safety
- Platform: "a fully funded CARE Department responding 24/7 to behavioral health calls, working in true partnership with SPD and Fire Response"; community violence intervention; a community court with City Attorney Erika Evans "that connects people to treatment and housing" [S3].
- Told FOX 13 "I support 1,200 police" and "We should bring alternative response teams to fill this gap"; also supports hiring detectives [S6].
- Supports opening a supplementary police precinct in North Seattle, according to KUOW [S5].
- Opposes the Stay Out of Areas of Prostitution (SOAP) law: "It didn't work. We brought it back, it didn't work before, and it's not working now." [S5]
- Opposes expanding CCTV, citing civil liberties and noting "this federal government can subpoena that info" [S5][S6].
- In the primary she opposed conditioning shelter on treatment and backed housing-first with voluntary treatment (from the primary dossier) [S2].

### Taxes and budget
- FOX 13 reports she "supports progressive revenue restructuring to shift burden from residents" [S6].
- At a July forum she proposed a tax on existing AI data centers to fund electrical-grid upgrades in underserved areas [S7].

### Transportation and climate
- Pamphlet: "move us toward meeting our climate goals" [S1]; The Urbanist describes her as a climate advocate who ties density and transit to climate goals [S7].
- Platform: expand free public transit to all Seattle students, including college students [S3].

### Democracy reform
- Pamphlet: "make sure ranked choice voting is successfully implemented in 2027" [S1][S3].

### Immigration
- Pamphlet: "protect immigrant communities from federal overreach"; platform: "robust sanctuary policies" and legal defense funds [S1][S3].

### Gender, LGBTQ+, and reproductive policy
- Listed as an endorser ("Candidate for Seattle City Councilmember") on the NO HATE in WA State page opposing I-001 and I-638 [S11].

### Parents and schools
- Same NO HATE listing, which opposes I-001 [S11]. She served as PTA Advocacy Chair at John Rogers Elementary [S2].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- **I-645 (income-tax repeal):** No public position found as of 2026-10-08. She is not listed on the Vote No on I-645 coalition page [S12].
- **I-1 / IL26-001 (parental rights):** Opposes; listed as an endorser on the NO HATE page against I-001 and I-638 [S11].
- **I-638 / IL26-638 (girls' sports):** Opposes; same listing [S11].

## Campaign finance

- PDC summary (updated Oct. 5, 2026): $180,463.14 raised, $106,012.59 spent, $3,000 in debts; $264.67 in independent expenditures for her [S10].

## Primary result

- First in the Aug. 4 primary with 16,287 votes (53.95%); Julie Kang 9,956 (32.98%); Dimitri Georgakopoulos 2,423 (8.03%); Silas James 1,451 (4.81%) [S9]. PubliCola reported she was the first D5 candidate to come out of a primary with a majority [S8].
