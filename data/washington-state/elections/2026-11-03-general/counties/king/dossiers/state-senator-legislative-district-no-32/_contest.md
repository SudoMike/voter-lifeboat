## What the office does
A Washington State Senator is one of 49 senators, elected to a four-year term. Senators write and vote on state law, pass the operating, capital and transportation budgets, set state tax policy, and confirm gubernatorial appointments. The 2026 regular session ended March 12; the winner takes office for the 2027 session.

## District and race dynamics
The 32nd District covers Shoreline, Lynnwood, Mountlake Terrace, Woodway and parts of Edmonds and north Seattle, across the King/Snohomish line (see cindy-ryu.md S7). The November 3, 2026 general is a Democrat-vs-Democrat race: 16-year House member Cindy Ryu, who says incumbent Jesse Salomon is too moderate, against Salomon, in the Senate since 2019 (cindy-ryu.md S7). Ryu led election-night primary returns 44.1% to 37.6%; the Republican, Ira McBee, was eliminated (cindy-ryu.md S7). Both campaigns have raised about a quarter million dollars (cindy-ryu.md S8; jesse-salomon.md S9). The 32nd District Democrats and WEA back Ryu; Salomon lists the Seattle Times, AWB, the Snohomish County Sheriff, Senate colleagues and many unions (cindy-ryu.md S13, S14; jesse-salomon.md S4, S14).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Both voted for the 2026 tax on income over $1 million. In an October KUOW debate Ryu said she would extend it to households near $250,000; Salomon called that "out of touch" and said more of the revenue should have gone to tax relief (cindy-ryu.md S5; jesse-salomon.md S5, S6).
2. **Spending and accountability:** Salomon's pamphlet pledges to "audit spending"; Ryu's emphasizes investment in schools and affordability programs (jesse-salomon.md S1; cindy-ryu.md S3).
3. **Housing:** Both voted for 2025 rent stabilization. Salomon stresses permitting and supply bills he wrote; Ryu lists rent caps, homebuyer programs, and social and mixed-income housing (jesse-salomon.md S3, S12; cindy-ryu.md S3, S16).
4. **Tech:** Ryu chairs the House technology committee and backs AI regulation and a privacy law; Salomon cites blocking AI rent-setting (cindy-ryu.md S16; jesse-salomon.md S1).
5. **Endorsement coalitions and experience:** Local party and WEA versus Senate caucus, business (AWB) and law enforcement; both have long legislative records.
Both state support for reproductive rights and both voted for ESHB 1296, so `social` and `parental-rights` do not separate them much.
