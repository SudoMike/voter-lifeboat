## Background
Cindy Ryu has been a State Representative for the 32nd District since 2011 and chairs the House Technology, Economic Development and Veterans Committee; she is running for the district's Senate seat against fellow Democrat and incumbent Jesse Salomon [S1]. She was Shoreline mayor and a city councilmember from 2006 to 2009, and worked as an Allstate insurance agent, a commercial property manager, and in records management and hospital admissions roles [S1]. She holds an MBA in Operations Management and a BS in Microbiology from the University of Washington [S1]. She previously chaired the House Housing Committee for five years [S16]. She has said she is challenging Salomon because she considers him too moderate [S7]. In election-night primary returns she led with 44.1% to Salomon's 37.6% [S7].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** Says she will "fight for a fairer tax system that asks the wealthiest to pay their share" with relief for working families and small businesses [S1]. In an October 2026 KUOW debate she said she was ready to expand the new income tax to household net incomes as low as $250,000, with a lower rate below $1 million [S5]. The Lynnwood Times reported she also said companies should be subject to a progressive income tax [S6].
- **Housing:** Lists supporting rent caps, first-time homebuyer programs, and speeding up construction [S3]; earlier coverage reports an emphasis on mixed-income and social housing and modular construction [S16][S17].
- **Education:** Lists fully funding public schools, smaller class sizes, early learning, and fair pay for staff [S3].
- **Workers and consumers:** Cites legislation on non-compete agreements and consumer protection against predatory practices [S1][S3].
- **Technology:** Has backed AI regulation and a comprehensive state privacy law and opposes data-center subsidies (per earlier coverage) [S16][S17].
- **Climate:** Lists advancing renewable energy and green-sector jobs [S3]; names safeguarding the environment as a Senate priority [S1].
- **Public safety:** Says she supports community policing and "both public safety and accountability in law enforcement" [S3].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** Describes herself as a "proud advocate of protecting reproductive rights," saying such decisions belong to patients and doctors [S3].
- **Parents and schools:** No campaign statement found; see the ESHB 1296 vote under Record.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** No statement naming I-645 found as of 2026-10-08. She voted for ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S9], and in October said she would expand it to lower incomes [S5].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** No public position found as of 2026-10-08. (Record: voted for ESHB 1296 in 2025, the law I-1 would undo [S10].)
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** No public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- Voted yea on House final passage of ESSB 6346 (9.9% tax on income over $1 million), 2026-03-09 [S9].
- Voted yea on House final passage of ESHB 1296 (2025 amendments to the I-2081 parental-rights list), 2025-03-12 and 2025-04-24 [S10].
- Voted yea on final passage of EHB 1217 (rent stabilization) as recommended by the conference committee, 2025-04-27 [S11].
- 2025 bills signed into law include HB 1006 (service-contract consumer protections) and HB 1205 (AI-generated forged digital likenesses) [S15].
- No floor votes since July: the Legislature has not met in session since sine die on 2026-03-12 [S12].
- PDC (data updated 2026-10-05): $238,362.46 raised, $109,552.62 spent, $15,000 in loans for 2026 [S8].

## Endorsements
- Pamphlet: 32nd and Snohomish County Democrats, SPEEA, Washington State Labor Council, Washington Federation of State Employees, WEA, WA Housing Alliance, Washington State Nurses Association, former Rep. Ruth Kagi [S1].
- WEA-PAC and the 32nd District Democrats list her for the general [S13][S14].
- Campaign site lists House Majority Leader Joe Fitzgibbon, Superintendent of Public Instruction Chris Reykdal, Insurance Commissioner Patty Kuderer, more than a dozen House Democrats, King County Councilmember Claudia Balducci, Lynnwood Mayor George Hurst, and Shoreline Councilmember Keith Scully [S4]. (Several names, including Hurst, Kagi, Rep. Lisa Parshley and Edmonds Councilmember Will Chen, also appear on Salomon's endorsement page.)

## Scoring notes
- Both candidates voted for the $1 million income tax; Ryu is the one who has said she would extend it to households around $250,000 [S5][S9].
- Carries the local party organization and WEA endorsements, against an incumbent whose list leans on Senate colleagues, the sheriff and a broad union slate [S4][S13][S14].
- Longer legislative tenure (House since 2011) than the incumbent, but no Senate service [S1].
- Tech policy (AI, privacy, data-center subsidies) is a signature focus tied to her committee chair [S1][S16].
