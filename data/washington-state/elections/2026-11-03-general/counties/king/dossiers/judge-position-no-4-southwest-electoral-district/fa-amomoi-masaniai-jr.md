## Background

Fa'amomoi Masaniai, Jr. is the incumbent King County District Court judge in Southwest Electoral District Position 4 [S1][S6]. The King County Council appointed him to the District Court in 2021, and voters elected him in 2022 [S2]. The Commission on Judicial Conduct's stipulation dates the appointment to January 2021 [S4]. In the November 2022 general he was unopposed for Southwest Position 4, receiving 59,225 votes against 1,361 write-ins [S6]. The court's roster lists him as a South Division judge assigned to the Auburn courthouse [S3].

His official court biography says he has heard criminal and civil matters at the Seattle, Maleng Regional Justice Center (Kent) and Auburn courthouses. It says he has presided over more than 50 jury trials as a judge and was assigned in 2023 to the Regional Mental Health Court and Regional Veterans Court [S2]. His pamphlet statement cites "nearly 200 jury trials" across his career, a larger figure than the court bio's count of trials as a judge [S1][S2].

His path to law did not go through law school. His campaign site says he began in 1994 as a volunteer at Tukwila Municipal Court, became a bailiff and court clerk in 1995, entered the Washington State Bar Association's APR Rule 6 Law Clerk Program in 1999, completed it in 2003, and passed the bar in 2004 [S7]. The pamphlet lists his education as Evergreen High School, Washington State University and the WSBA APR Rule 6 Program [S1]. According to the court bio, he is the first judge of Samoan heritage on a Washington court and is believed to be the first Rule 6 graduate appointed to the bench [S2].

Before his appointment, the campaign site says he was an Assistant Attorney General in American Samoa from 2006, prosecuting domestic violence and sexual assault cases. From 2009 he was an appointed Independent Prosecutor there handling corruption, white-collar and human-trafficking cases. From 2012 he practiced bankruptcy, mediation and civil litigation in Washington, and in 2014 he was a public defender for the City of Shoreline [S7]. The court bio confirms criminal defense and prosecution experience and the American Samoa independent-prosecutor role [S2]. Before his appointment he also served as a pro tem judge in several municipal courts (Seattle, Renton, SeaTac, Des Moines, Issaquah, Puyallup) and, in 2015, as a pro tem commissioner for the King County Superior Court Juvenile Division [S2][S7].

## Positions

Judicial approach (self-described):

- Says "Justice is about more than punishment. Accountability is essential, but so are opportunities for rehabilitation and redemption," and that connecting people with treatment, job training and community resources reduces recidivism [S1].
- Says he believes in "Rehabilitation versus pure punishment, but I won't hesitate in protecting the citizens if Rehabilitation fails by incarcerating those who consistently offend the law" [S8].
- Says he explains each sentence so the person understands its terms [S8].

Criminal-justice formation, bail and diversion:

- His formation includes prosecution (American Samoa), public defense (Shoreline) and civil practice [S2][S7].
- He has sat on the Regional Mental Health Court and Regional Veterans Court since 2023 [S2][S7]. His campaign lists "improving access to treatment/diversion programs" and "supporting veterans or mental health courts" among his priorities, alongside victims' rights at sentencing and backlog reduction [S8].
- No sourced bail rulings or published decisions were found.

Court administration and access:

- Credits remote (Zoom) appearances and expanded interpreter access with making court more reachable, including for people with disabilities [S8].
- Lists service on District and Municipal Court Judges' Association diversity, outreach and Law Day committees, and on the KCDC Law Day and Budget committees [S7]. He hosts school visits and mock trials through the court's Law Day program [S2][S9].

## Record

- **Judicial discipline (2026).** On April 24, 2026, the Washington State Commission on Judicial Conduct entered a Stipulation, Agreement and Order of Reprimand in CJC No. 12896-F-217 [S4][S5].
  - Stipulated facts: in 2023 he had frequent personal communications with a newly hired court clerk who clerked in his courtroom, twice offered her a neck massage, and on May 8, 2023 asked her for a hug in his chambers. The clerk described the hug as a prolonged "full body embrace" [S4].
  - His own account: in his answer he described it as a "bro hug" without significant body contact and said he did not intend anything sexual. He acknowledged that, given the power disparity, his conduct "even as he describes it, was inappropriate" [S4].
  - What happened next: he stopped contacting the clerk when she asked him to on May 15, 2023. The presiding judge reassigned him to another courthouse after the clerk went to Human Resources [S4].
  - Violations: he agreed he violated Canon 1 (Rules 1.1 and 1.2) and Canon 2 (Rules 2.3(B), harassment, and 2.8(B), courtesy to court staff) of the Code of Judicial Conduct [S4].
  - Sanction: a reprimand, which the order calls "an intermediate level of discipline." It required him to reread the Code and complete remedial training "focused on sexual harassment and gender dynamics" within a year [S4].
  - Factors weighed: the Commission called the misconduct serious but "isolated and relatively short-lived." It also noted that he had no prior discipline, cooperated, and sought help through the Judicial Assistance Program, and that he attributed the conduct in part to grief after losing eight family members since 2020 [S4].
- Uncontested 2022 election to Position 4 [S6].
- Mental Health Court and Veterans Court assignment since 2023 [S2].
