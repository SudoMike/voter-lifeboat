## What the office does

A King County District Court judge hears misdemeanor criminal cases, small claims, traffic cases and protection orders. The 2026 salary is $226,096, and judges are elected by the voters of their electoral district to four-year terms [S2]. Position 4 is one of five Southwest Electoral District seats [S3].

## Race dynamics

- Only two candidates filed for Position 4 [S14], so the race skipped the August primary and appears for the first time on the general ballot [S1][S14].
- The incumbent, Judge Fa'amomoi Masaniai, Jr., was appointed by the King County Council in 2021 and elected unopposed in 2022 [S3][S4]. The challenger, Des Moines City Councilmember Harry Steinmetz, describes a career as a BIIA Administrative Law Judge, pro tem judge, prosecutor and defense attorney [S1][S6]. He won re-election to the council in November 2025 [S8].
- On April 24, 2026, the Washington State Commission on Judicial Conduct reprimanded Masaniai under a stipulation in which he agreed he violated Code of Judicial Conduct Rules 1.1, 1.2, 2.3(B) (harassment) and 2.8(B) (courtesy). The case concerned his 2023 conduct toward a court clerk. The order required remedial training on sexual harassment and gender dynamics [S5].
  - Steinmetz's campaign site has a "My Opponent" section on the reprimand [S6].
  - His pamphlet statement stresses judicial "conduct... on and off the bench" and quotes KCDC Chief Judge Rebecca Robertson calling him "extraordinarily well qualified" [S1].
  - No source reviewed explains why the Chief Judge and other sitting KCDC judges endorsed the challenger. The reprimand is the only documented context [S5][S6].
- Masaniai's endorsements page lists three sitting KCDC judges (Harn, Amato, Green) [S7]. Steinmetz's lists eight, including the Chief Judge [S6].
- The 34th District Democrats endorsed Steinmetz [S12]. The King County Democrats made no endorsement in this race [S13].
- Money is small on both sides. The PDC summaries show $13,589 raised for Masaniai and $29,845 for Steinmetz, much of Steinmetz's from his own funds [S10][S11].
- No King County Bar Association rating exists for this general-only race [S9].

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

- **Experience (`experience`).** Masaniai has about five years as a sitting District Court judge, including the mental health and veterans therapeutic courts [S4]. Steinmetz has administrative-law-judge and pro tem service plus legislative experience on a city council, but no full-time trial-court judgeship [S1][S6].
- **Judicial conduct (not a rubric axis; recorded for voters, not scored).** Only the incumbent has a judicial-discipline record: the 2026 CJC reprimand [S5]. The challenger runs on conduct and integrity [S1][S6].
- **Professional formation (`safety`).** Both describe prosecution and defense backgrounds [S1][S4][S6]. Masaniai describes a rehabilitation and treatment-oriented approach paired with incarceration for repeat offenders [S1]. Steinmetz makes no bail, diversion or sentencing statements [S1][S6].

## Not differentiating

- Neither candidate has a bar association rating on the pages checked [S9].
- Both cite fairness and dignity for all court users in generic terms [S1].
