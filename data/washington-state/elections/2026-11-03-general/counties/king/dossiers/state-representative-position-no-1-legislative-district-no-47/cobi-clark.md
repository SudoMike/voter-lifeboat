## Background
Cobi Clark (Prefers Libertarian Party) is a housing developer and construction project manager and a South Sound Business "Top 40 Under 40" honoree; he says he helped deliver hundreds of homes across Washington, including senior living, single-family homes, market-rate apartments and middle housing [S1]. He holds an interdisciplinary bachelor's in economics, policy analysis, politics and philosophy from UW Tacoma, during which he worked as policy staff for Senator Jeff Wilson and as a business advocate for the Tacoma-Pierce Chamber of Commerce [S1]. He is a member of Auburn Rotary and lists no elected experience [S1]. He lost a November 2025 Auburn City Council race [S2].

He finished second in the four-candidate August 4 primary on election night with 30.12% to incumbent Debra Entenman's 38.54%; both advance [S13].

## Positions

### Taxes and spending
- Supports "continued opposition to a state income tax" [S1]; his platform's first repeal item is "Repeal the state income tax contemplated under SB 6346" [S3].
- "Washington doesn't have a revenue problem. It has a spending problem"; he would rein in expenditures, cut bureaucratic waste and "corporate welfare," and favors Pigouvian taxes over general revenue taxes [S6].
- Would tie spending to the revenue source ("categorical spending requirements") rather than sending about half to the general fund, and re-enact advisory votes on new taxes [S4].

### Housing
- Argues from developer experience that "excessive regulations, rising taxes, and bureaucratic delays drive up the cost of housing" [S1].
- Would cut permitting delays and "unnecessary local barriers," limit building codes to safety, and "make it easier to build more housing of all types" [S7]; proposes opportunity zones for high-density housing in underused commercial centers [S4].

### Energy
- Supports "reliable, abundant energy," investment in nuclear, geothermal and next-generation energy, and stopping "policies that punish commuters and working families" [S8]; proposes a geothermal siting study [S4].

### Public safety, policing and homelessness
- "Safe communities that respect both public safety and individual rights" [S1].
- Would end qualified immunity for state actors, "criminalize masked arrests," and end and ban Flock cameras in Washington [S3][S9].
- Would repeal SB 5974, which he says "allows bureaucrats to fire our elected sheriffs," and HB 1240 (2024), which he describes as banning "certain firearms from sale" [S3].
- On homelessness, focuses on mental health and addiction treatment as root causes, "long-term recovery, not just temporary shelter," community and faith-based recovery programs, and measurable outcomes for spending [S10].

### Government and elections
- Would establish multi-winner ranked-choice voting, a 50-year sunset for legislation unless re-enacted, and advisory votes on new taxes [S4].

### Parents and schools
- His platform says "Repeal HB 1296 which undermined the voter approved parents rights initiative 2081" [S3].
- His education page supports school choice, "respect the role of parents in education decisions," and "transparency and accountability in our schools" [S5].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No statement found. His civil-rights page says "true equality means equal rights for all – not special treatment for some" and "keep government neutral in matters of conscience and belief," without naming gender, LGBTQ+ or reproductive policy [S9]. He would repeal SB 5375, which he says compelled clergy to violate the seal of confession [S3].

### Healthcare
- Would repeal certificate-of-need requirements and require price transparency from providers [S3][S4].

## Endorsements
- Organizations: 47th District Republicans, Libertarian Party of Washington State, Association of Washington Business, Seattle King County REALTORS, Stand for Health Freedom [S11].
- Individuals include Libertarian presidential candidate Chase Oliver, Senator Jeff Wilson, former Senator Steve Johnson, former Rep. Chad Magendanz, Auburn councilmembers Lisa Stirgus, Brian Lott and Cheryl Rakes, and former mayor and Covington councilmember Jeff Wagner [S11].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, income tax repeal):** No statement naming I-645 found. His platform calls for repealing "the state income tax contemplated under SB 6346," the same tax I-645 would repeal [S3].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** No statement naming I-1 found. His platform calls for repealing HB 1296, the 2025 amendments that I-1 would also undo [S3].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** No public position found [S1][S3][S5][S9].

## Campaign finance
PDC reports, as of a record updated 2026-10-05: $52,109.91 in contributions and $46,508.44 in expenditures, with nothing carried forward [S12]. That is more raised than the incumbent this cycle [S12].

## Scoring notes
- No elected office; runs on private-sector housing development experience against an eight-year incumbent [S1].
- Would repeal the 2026 income tax and HB 1296, putting him on the opposite side from his opponent's votes on both [S3].
- Wants supply-side deregulation of housing, not public programs or tenant protections [S1][S7].
- Libertarian on policing (end qualified immunity, ban Flock cameras, criminalize masked arrests) and on firearms (repeal HB 1240; "uphold the right to keep and bear arms") [S3][S9].
- Electoral reforms (multi-winner RCV, advisory tax votes, legislative sunsets) are distinctive in this race [S4].
