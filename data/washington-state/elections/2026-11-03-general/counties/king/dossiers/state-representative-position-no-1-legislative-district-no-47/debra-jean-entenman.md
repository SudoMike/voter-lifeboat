## Background
Debra Entenman (Prefers Democratic Party) has represented the 47th District, Position 1, since 2018 and sits on the House Housing; Civil Rights & Judiciary; Transportation; and Postsecondary Education & Workforce committees [S1]. Before the Legislature she spent 12 years as a congressional staffer serving South King County, three of them as District Director [S1]. She was in the first Seattle/King County Head Start class, describes herself as a non-traditional college graduate, and holds a bachelor's in political science from Seattle University [S1]. She serves on the Washington State Board for Community & Technical Colleges, served 11 years on the Renton Technical College Board of Trustees, and is a member of the Urban League of Metropolitan Seattle and the NAACP [S1]. She was re-elected in 2024 with 56% [S2].

In the four-candidate August 4 primary she led election-night returns with 38.54%, ahead of Libertarian Cobi Clark at 30.12%; both advance [S7].

## Positions

### Safety net and public safety
- Her pamphlet statement says she has worked to "strengthen our social safety nets, improve public safety, support workers, and invest in frontline responders," and will keep working "with my colleagues across the aisle" [S1].
- Her platform calls for a police accountability standard with independent investigations, and for social workers and mental health providers alongside officers [S3].
- Platform commitments on rental assistance, food security, eviction prevention, childcare and healthcare services [S3].

### Consumer data privacy
- Wants companies to tell consumers what personal data, information and photographs they collect, with consumer control over its use [S3].

### Parents and schools
- Her platform commits to "consulting our parents and trained educators throughout the process of writing and implementing the curriculum," "providing parents with the option to have their children not participate in lessons," and "allowing school districts to create and use their own curriculum that is evidence-based and age-appropriate" [S3].
- The same page supports age-appropriate, evidence-based comprehensive sexuality education [S3]. (Undated page; it cites 2020 sources.)
- Voted yes on ESHB 1296 (see Record) [S5].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No statement found in the pamphlet or on the campaign platform page [S1][S3].

## Record
- Prime sponsor of HB 2015 (2025), public safety funding for local governments with a local option tax, signed in 2025; her pamphlet says it secured $100 million for public safety [S1][S2].
- Prime sponsor of HB 1559 (2023), the Student Basic Needs Act, which created benefits navigators for college students [S1][S2].
- Voted yes on ESSB 6346 (2026), the tax on household income over $1 million that I-645 would repeal (House final passage 2026-03-09, 51-46) [S4].
- Voted yes on ESHB 1296 (2025), the amendments to the I-2081 parental-rights law that I-1 would undo (House third reading 2025-03-12; final passage as amended by the Senate 2025-04-24, 59-39) [S5].
- No floor votes since July: the 2026 regular session adjourned sine die on March 12 [S8].

## Endorsements
- Washington Conservation Action [S9], King County Democrats [S10], and The Urbanist (primary) [S11]. The pamphlet and campaign site list none [S1][S3].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, income tax repeal):** No public statement found on her campaign site or in the pamphlet [S1][S3]. She voted yes on ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S4].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** No public statement found [S1][S3]. She voted yes on ESHB 1296, the 2025 amendments I-1 would undo [S5]. Her platform also supports parental opt-outs from specific lessons (see Parents and schools) [S3].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** No public position found [S1][S3].

PDC reports, as of a record updated 2026-10-01: $46,110.71 in contributions, $21,806.32 carried forward, and $48,370.75 in expenditures [S6].

## Scoring notes
- The only candidate in the race with a legislative record: eight years in the seat and signed bills on public safety funding and college students' basic needs [S1][S2].
- Voted for the 2026 millionaires' tax; her opponent wants to repeal it [S4].
- On parents and schools her record (a yes on ESHB 1296) and platform (curricula set by districts, comprehensive sex education, but parental opt-outs for specific lessons) point in different directions [S3][S5].
- Pairs public safety funding with police accountability measures [S1][S3].
