## What the office does

A Washington State Representative is one of two House members elected district-wide from each of the 49 legislative districts, serving two-year terms in the 98-member House. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including K-12 funding, housing and land use, the tax code, public safety and healthcare. The 47th covers the Kent, Auburn and Covington area of South King County.

## Race dynamics

Incumbent Democrat Debra Entenman (in the seat since 2018) faces Libertarian Cobi Clark, a housing developer endorsed by the 47th District Republicans, the Association of Washington Business and Seattle King County REALTORS. In the four-candidate primary, election-night returns had Entenman at 38.54%, Clark 30.12%, Jasnoor Kaur Hans 19.01% and Logan Evans 10.80%. With no Republican on the ballot, Clark is the right-of-center option. Fundraising is modest: Clark has raised about $52.1K and Entenman about $46.1K, plus $21.8K she carried forward (PDC, early October).

## Differentiating axes

- **taxes:** Entenman voted for ESSB 6346, the 2026 millionaires' tax. Clark's platform would repeal it and opposes any state income tax.
- **parental-rights:** Entenman voted for ESHB 1296 (2025). Her undated platform page supports district-set curricula and comprehensive sex education, but also parental opt-outs from specific lessons. Clark would repeal ESHB 1296, the same law I-1 targets.
- **housing:** Clark offers supply-side deregulation (cut permitting delays, limit building codes to safety). Entenman, a Housing Committee member, emphasizes rental assistance and eviction prevention.
- **spending:** Clark says Washington has "a spending problem" and wants to rein it in. Entenman emphasizes safety-net programs.
- **experience:** An eight-year incumbent with signed bills vs. a first-time legislative candidate with no elected office.
- **reform:** Clark proposes multi-winner ranked-choice voting, advisory votes on new taxes and 50-year sunsets for laws.
- **safety:** The candidates are hard to place on a single line. Entenman pairs public safety funding (HB 2015) with police accountability. Clark pairs "safe communities" with civil-liberties limits on police (ending qualified immunity, banning Flock cameras) and treatment-focused homelessness policy.
- **Not differentiating on evidence:** Neither has stated a position on gender or reproductive policy or on I-638.
