## Background

Vanessa Kritzer (Prefers Democratic Party) is a Redmond City Councilmember (2020-present), Council President 2024-2025 and Vice President 2022-2023, a former Redmond Planning Commissioner, and a Democratic precinct committee officer since 2017 [S1]. She is Executive Director of the Washington Association of Land Trusts and previously worked at Microsoft, the League of Conservation Voters and the Latin America Working Group; she began organizing on Barack Obama's 2008 campaign [S1][S5]. She holds an MPA and MBA from the University of Washington and a BA from Vassar College [S1]. Board roles include Hopelink, chair of the WRIA 8 Salmon Recovery Council, Eastside Transportation Partnership, OneRedmond and the Washington Wildlife and Recreation Coalition; she is a past University of Washington Regent [S1].

The seat is open: Rep. Larry Springer did not seek reelection [S2]. Her general-election pamphlet statement is the same text she filed for the primary [S1][S2]. In the August primary she led with 62.75% of the vote, per results as of August 10 [S7]; Republican John P Gibbons is her general-election opponent [S1].

## Positions

**Housing**
- Supports state action on landlord-tenant law and rent stabilization, and says "the state should set the floor" on housing production and affordability while cities keep flexibility on how to meet it [S5].
- Site: will "scale solutions statewide" on affordability, after local work expanding affordable housing investments, requirements and incentives [S3].

**Taxes and revenue**
- Calls Washington's tax system deeply regressive; supports progressive revenue "including recent efforts to tax high earners" and is open to broader reform such as an income tax [S5].
- Cites Redmond's business head tax as a way to make growth pay for public benefit [S5].

**Climate and environment**
- Says there is "a moral imperative to act on climate change"; local record includes Redmond's 30-year Environmental Sustainability Action Plan, a net-zero strategy for city operations, and Energy Smart Eastside and Solarize Eastside [S3].
- Cites Climate Commitment Act funding Redmond secured for electric fire engines [S5].

**Schools and childcare**
- Will work to "fully fund public education, support youth mental health, restore cut funding for childcare, parent education, and early learning" [S3].

**Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy**
- Site lists "standing up for reproductive rights after Roe v. Wade was overturned" and "passing policies to support our LGBTQIA+ community members and affirm their rights"; pledges to protect reproductive rights and access to healthcare from federal threats [S3].
- Supports strengthening state anti-discrimination protections [S5].

**Parents and schools**
- No statement on parental notification, records access or curricula opt-outs found [S1][S3][S5].

**Immigration and civil liberties**
- Secured local funding for organizations serving immigrants, refugees and asylum seekers; backed a Redmond resolution affirming constitutional protections [S3][S5].

## Record

- Redmond City Council since 2020, including Council President [S1].
- Raised Redmond's inclusionary-zoning affordable-unit requirement and lowered the income threshold served from 80% to 50% of area median income [S5].
- Helped bring a stalled permanent supportive housing project to Redmond and led creation of a municipal code chapter on renter protections [S5].
- No state legislative record.

## Endorsements

- Pamphlet: 45th Democrats, Congresswoman DelBene, Senator Dhingra, County Councilmembers Perry and Balducci, the mayors of Redmond, Kirkland, Woodinville, Duvall and Sammamish, Planned Parenthood Alliance, Washington Conservation Action, Washington Education Association, State Labor Council [S1].
- Site adds Reps. Larry Springer and Roger Goodman, Sen. Vandana Slatter, Rep. Osman Salahuddin and other legislators, Congresswoman Kim Schrier, and four Lake Washington School Board members [S4].
- The 45th District Democrats endorse her for the general [S8].
- In the primary the Seattle Times editorial board endorsed her Democratic rival Chandler Torbett, while calling Kritzer "a proven policy wonk" [S2].
