## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House, for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets: K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules for cities, health and behavioral-health funding, and criminal-justice statutes. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the two candidates who advanced from the August primary.

## District and race dynamics
The 1st Legislative District covers parts of south Snohomish County and north King County, including Brier, Bothell, Kenmore, Woodinville and Lake Forest Park (davina-duerr.md S12). Democrat Davina Duerr, appointed in 2019 and chair of the House Local Government Committee, faces Republican Maggie Wang, a former Redmond restaurant owner running for office for the first time (davina-duerr.md S1; maggie-wang.md S1). With only two candidates filed, both advanced from the primary, where Duerr took 74.7% to Wang's 25.1% in the count the Everett Herald reported (davina-duerr.md S12).

Duerr runs on her housing and permitting record and voted for the 2026 Millionaires Tax (ESSB 6346), which I-645 would repeal (davina-duerr.md S3, S6). Wang runs on stopping tax increases, auditing state spending, public safety and parental rights (maggie-wang.md S1, S2). Duerr reports $70.0k raised to Wang's $8.5k (davina-duerr.md S2; maggie-wang.md S3). Neither candidate has stated a position on the three statewide initiatives; the King County Republican Party, which endorses Wang, recommends yes on all three (maggie-wang.md S4).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Duerr voted for and praised the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million; Wang pledges to "stop new tax increases."
2. **Record vs. renewal:** seven-year incumbent and committee chair vs. a first-time candidate with a small-business background.
3. **Spending:** Wang's platform centers auditing state spending and cutting programs; Duerr's centers housing and transportation investment.
4. **Parents and schools:** Duerr voted for ESHB 1296 (2025), which amended the I-2081 parental-rights list; Wang names parental rights as one of her three priorities, without detail.
5. **Housing:** Duerr's record is state-level supply and permitting reform; Wang names housing costs as a concern without a mechanism.
