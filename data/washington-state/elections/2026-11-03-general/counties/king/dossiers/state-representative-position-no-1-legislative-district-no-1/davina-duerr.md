## Background
Davina Duerr is the incumbent Democratic State Representative for the 1st Legislative District, Position 1, appointed in 2019, and chairs the House Local Government Committee [S1]. Her 2026 committee list also includes Environment & Energy and Transportation [S6]. She served on the Bothell City Council 2016-2022, including as Deputy Mayor 2016-2020 [S1]. She describes herself as the only licensed architect in the Legislature and lists service on the Eastside Transportation Partnership, the WSDOT Executive Advisory Group, the Sound Transit Bus Rapid Transit Elected Leadership Group, the PSRC Transportation Policy Board and the Sound Cities Association board [S1]. She holds a Bachelor of Architecture from Syracuse University [S1]. In the August 4 primary she led Republican Maggie Wang 74.7% to 25.1% in the count reported by the Everett Herald; both advanced [S12].

## Positions
- **Housing:** says quality of life depends on affordable homes near jobs; lists bills that make it cheaper to build affordable housing and streamline permits for new housing [S1]. The 2026 session report credits her HB 2418 with reducing permitting delays and HB 2451 with clarifying tax increment financing [S6].
- **Taxes:** co-signed the 2026 district session report calling the Millionaires Tax "a historic step forward in fixing our upside-down tax code" that will help pay for schools, health care and higher education while reducing taxes for families and small businesses [S6].
- **Transportation:** cites $532 million secured for the district in 2025, including $464.5 million for the I-405 corridor [S1].
- **Environment:** lists bills phasing out super-pollutant refrigerants and giving the Department of Ecology enforcement over landfill regulation [S1].
- **Growth management:** lists a bill that closes a growth-management loophole [S1].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no statement of her own found; endorsed by Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates [S10].
- **Parents and schools:** names strong public education as a priority [S1]; voted for ESHB 1296 (see Record) [S4].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted for ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S3], and co-signed a session report praising it [S6].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted for ESHB 1296 (2025), the amendments to the I-2081 parental-rights list that I-1 would repeal [S4][S5].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- Pamphlet: seven of her bills became law last term, including bills to lower the cost of building affordable housing, phase out super-pollutant refrigerants, close a growth-management loophole, streamline housing permits and give Ecology landfill enforcement [S1].
- 2026 session: prime sponsor of HB 2418 (permitting delays) and HB 2451 (tax increment financing), per the district session report [S6].
- Voted yea on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million (2026-03-09, passed 51-46) [S3].
- Voted yea on House final passage of ESHB 1296 as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24, passed 59-39), which added, modified and eliminated items in the I-2081 list of parent and guardian rights [S4][S5].
- No floor votes since July: the 2026 regular session adjourned sine die on 2026-03-12 and no special session is listed [S7].
- PDC (data updated 2026-10-05): $70,020.23 in contributions and $42,385.84 in expenditures for the 2026 campaign [S2].
