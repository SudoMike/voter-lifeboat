## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members per legislative district, elected to a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, which cover school funding, taxes, housing rules, health programs and criminal law.

## District and race dynamics
This is an open seat: Rep. Cindy Ryu is running for the district's Senate seat (keith-scully.md S5). Two Democrats came out of a six-candidate primary: Shoreline councilmember and former mayor Keith Scully (26.6% on election night) and former FTC antitrust attorney Danica Noble (24.2%) (keith-scully.md S5). The 32nd District Democrats and WEA-PAC dual-endorsed both (keith-scully.md S7, S8). Scully lists the Seattle Times, The Stranger, Washington Conservation Action and Sierra Club; Noble lists the State Labor Council and Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates (keith-scully.md S4; danica-noble.md S1, S6). Noble has out-raised Scully (danica-noble.md S5; keith-scully.md S6).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience:** a decade in municipal office, including as mayor, versus a first-time candidate with federal enforcement expertise.
2. **Tech:** Noble's campaign is built on AI and tech guardrails, antitrust and surveillance-pricing bans; Scully favors "limited, responsible regulation" with specific limits (danica-noble.md S3; keith-scully.md S3).
3. **Taxes and spending:** Scully backs the millionaires tax as a start, wants regressive taxes replaced with progressive ones, and says Olympia must rein in spending; Noble has no stated tax position and talks about spending wisely with accountability (keith-scully.md S3; danica-noble.md S9).
4. **Climate:** a long Scully focus (Climate Commitment Act, clean energy, conservation endorsements) with no comparable Noble platform plank (keith-scully.md S3).
5. **Health care:** Noble proposes insurance and AI-denial regulation within the current system (danica-noble.md S3).
