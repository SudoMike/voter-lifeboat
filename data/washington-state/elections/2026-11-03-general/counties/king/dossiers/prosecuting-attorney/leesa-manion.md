## Background

Leesa Manion is the incumbent King County Prosecuting Attorney, first elected in November 2022 [S2]. Before her election she spent 15 years as the office's Chief of Staff, and her whole career has been in the King County Prosecuting Attorney's Office [S2]. She holds a JD from Seattle University School of Law and a BA from Northern Kentucky University [S1]. She is the first woman and first person of color elected King County Prosecutor [S1][S2]. She serves on the boards of the Washington Association of Prosecuting Attorneys, the national Association of Prosecuting Attorneys and the Alliance for Gun Responsibility [S2].

## Positions

Her general-election pamphlet statement:
- Says she has kept her promise "to keep our neighborhoods safe, reduce property crime, and protect survivors and the most vulnerable" [S1].
- Says the office prosecutes violent crimes, sexual assaults and property crimes daily [S1].
- Cites expanded support for survivors of domestic violence and sexual assault, "alternative sentencing for veterans, youth, and individuals facing behavioral health and addiction challenges," and juvenile justice reform [S1].
- Cites "a nearly 50% reduction in gun homicides since 2023" and says she worked to enact Extreme Risk Protection Order policies, a statewide ban on high-capacity magazines and ghost guns, and weapon-tracing tools for law enforcement (campaign claims, not independently verified here) [S1].

## Record

- Created a Gender-Based Violence and Prevention Division and an Economic Crimes & Wage Theft Division early in her term [S2].
- The office launched a digital Protection Order Portal, a juvenile "Safer Schools Strategy," and a 2023 retail-crime prosecution pilot with the Seattle City Attorney's Office, law enforcement and the Retail Industry Leaders Association [S2].
- Is leading a statewide project to redesign the Involuntary Treatment Act court system [S2].
- Co-founding partner of Choose 180, a pre-filing juvenile diversion program [S2].

## Endorsements

Pamphlet: former Governors Gary Locke and Christine Gregoire; King County Executive Girmay Zahilay; former Seattle Mayor Bruce Harrell; the Alliance for Gun Responsibility; labor unions; King County Democrat organizations; county and city leaders; retired justices; former U.S. Attorneys [S1].
