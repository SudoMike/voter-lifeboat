## What it does

Proposition No. 1 is a levy lid lift under RCW 84.55.050 [S1][S3]. The ballot title reads: "To fund public safety and land use services, and to remain an independent city, the Clyde Hill City Council passed Resolution No. 725 proposing an increase to its regular property tax levy from approximately $0.30 to $0.50 per $1,000 of assessed valuation for 2027 with 3% annual increases to the annual limit factor for 2028-2036." [S1] The explanatory statement says the money pays for "police, fire, emergency medical services, 911 dispatch, land use, and zoning services" [S1].

Resolution No. 725 defines public safety services as the costs of operating the Clyde Hill Police Department and contracts for fire protection, emergency medical services, crisis outreach and response, 911 dispatch, jail services, prosecution and indigent defense, plus land use, zoning, permitting and code enforcement [S3]. The resolution says the revenue is for "the limited purposes of providing existing levels of public safety and land use services" [S3]. The council resolved it on July 14, 2026 [S3][S4]. Exemptions apply to seniors, veterans and disabled persons who qualify under RCW 84.36.381 [S1]. Passage requires a simple majority [S1].

## Cost

- Explanatory statement: "If approved, Clyde Hill's property tax levy rate would increase from $0.29548 per $1,000 of assessed valuation in 2026 to a levy rate not to exceed $0.50 per $1,000 of assessed valuation in 2027. This equates to about a $225 increase in the city's portion of property taxes per $1,000,000 of assessed valuation." [S1]
- Resolution No. 725 states the Task Force and the city's consultant estimate "approximately $225 per million dollars of assessed valuation, or an approximate $900 total increase in the city's portion of property taxes for a $4,000,000 home in 2027" [S3]. The Task Force's July 2026 recommendation describes this as "approximately $898 or 72% increase in Clyde Hill portion of property tax bill in 2027" for a $4 million assessed value [S7].
- The consultant's analysis puts the median Clyde Hill home value at about $3,743,000 [S9].
- The city receives "less than $0.05 of every $1.00 paid in property taxes" [S1]; the consultant's report puts the city's share at about 4.5% [S9].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- **New lid lift, not a renewal, and permanent after 2036.** The city's rate was $0.91563 per $1,000 in 2001 and $0.29548 in 2026 [S3]. Under the measure, the total regular levy amount would grow by a 3% limit factor each year 2028-2036, and "The final levy in 2036 will become the base for establishing levy rates for following years." [S1][S3] Unlike a temporary lid lift, the higher base does not revert after 2036 [S1][S3].
- **Origins.** The city adopted a Financial Sustainability Plan on April 9, 2024, and created a resident-led Financial Sustainability Task Force in December 2024 after the operating deficit grew faster than projected [S4]. The Task Force's January 2026 report found the city had run "an operating deficit in 5 of the last 7 years totaling almost $1.8M," with the two non-deficit years covered by federal ARPA funds, and that "Business as usual will leave City without cash operating reserves in 2030" [S6]. It recommended "a lift of 75% of current city taxes" and coordinating timing with Yarrow Point [S6].
- **Option chosen.** The council hired the consultant Raftelis to model options [S3]. The Task Force recommended $0.50 in 2027 followed by 3% a year for 2028-2036, which it projected would extend the city's operating runway to 2038, compared with "End of 2030" under the 1% baseline [S7]. The council concurred at a special meeting on July 2, 2026 [S8].
- **If it fails.** "If this proposition is rejected, the City is projected to run out of operating funds by the end of 2030. It will be unable to sustain current service levels and may need to consider dissolution." [S1] The city FAQ says: "Without a levy lid lift, Clyde Hill will have to cut services or merge with another city. Merging with another city would cost taxpayers more because their levy rates are higher than Clyde Hill's levy rate." [S5] The Task Force listed the council's options after a failed vote as: educate residents and resubmit in a later election; implement a stormwater utility "to buy time"; "Contract police services to another jurisdiction to balance the budget"; or "Merge with another jurisdiction which will raise taxes and lessen control." [S6]
- **Contracting already in place and considered.** The city contracts with Bellevue for fire and EMS ($894,000 a year in the Task Force report); the Task Force recommended keeping that contract and joining joint renegotiation when it ends in 2027 [S6]. On police ($2.68 million a year), it rated contracting with Bellevue or Kirkland as offering significant savings but noted "Significant concern over loss of local control and responsiveness" and called for further investigation; consolidation with Medina was rated low feasibility, with "Little interest from Medina" [S6].
- **Other revenue.** The Task Force recommended raising the criminal justice sales tax from 0.1% to 0.2% and raising development fees [S6]. In May 2026 the council agreed to defer decisions on a stormwater utility, shared services and other ballot measures until the levy process is complete [S9].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, submitted by Jay Henningsen, Carl Picciotto and Tony Williams (thecampaignforclydehill.com) [S2]:

- "Rising costs and state limits on revenue growth now constrain the City's ability to deliver the services we expect." [S2]
- Clyde Hill receives "only about 4.5% of your total property tax bill," and its rate is among the lowest in the region: "Clyde Hill $0.30; Medina $0.59; Bellevue $0.96; Kirkland $0.86 per $1,000 assessed value" [S2].
- "Our budget gap cannot be closed by cuts alone. Proposition 1 is a modest revenue solution recommended by the resident-led task force." [S2]
- "Passing Proposition 1 means we keep our accredited police department and preserve land-use control that protects our neighborhood character and home values." [S2]
- Rebuttal: "Please vote 'Yes' on Prop 1 to keep Clyde Hill – Clyde Hill. It protects our home values, keeps our city tax rate relatively low, maintains our public safety, and keeps us in control of our future." [S2]

Henningsen and Picciotto were members of the Financial Sustainability Task Force [S7].

## Context / Coverage

- **How the city frames the vote.** The city's levy page says: "In order to remain an independent city with local control of its own police department and land use issues, as well as the quality fire and emergency medical services that the community has come to expect, the City needs to raise revenues." [S4] The pro committee's site contrasts YES ("Clyde Hill remains an independent, self-governing city") with NO ("We become a neighborhood in Bellevue or Kirkland"), and says NO means taxes "spike significantly later when annexed by a larger city" [S11]. These are the committee's claims; the explanatory statement says only that the city "may need to consider dissolution" [S1].
- **The Urbanist** reported on the July 2 special meeting, describing the change as "a 69% hike compared to the current city rate" and "the biggest local property tax increase in Clyde Hill in decades, and likely the city's history" [S12]. It reported that $2.8 million of the city's $6.7 million 2026 budget goes to the police department and about $900,000 to the Bellevue Fire Department contract, and that officials said the city could be forced to explore merging with "a nearby jurisdiction like Medina or Bellevue" [S12]. It also covered the city's land-use background: Clyde Hill incorporated in 1953 to control lot sizes and commercial zoning, and faces a Futurewise challenge to its growth plan [S12]. Task Force member Wayne Burns was quoted: "Unless the city did something, it was going to go bankrupt or it was going to get absorbed by another city." [S12]
- **The Seattle Times** columnist Danny Westneat wrote that the city brought in $1.37 million in property taxes the prior year, so a 1% increase is about $13,700, and that the owner of the average $4.3 million home "would see the local portion of their taxes go up by nearly $1,000" [S13]. City Administrator Heather Thomas-Murphy is quoted: "It's not a result of overspending ... It's a structural imbalance with how property tax revenue works. The 1% cap is the reason." [S13] The column also offers the writer's own opinions on the state's 1% cap [S13].
- No KING5 or Bellevue Reporter coverage of the 2026 measure was found.

## Axis notes

- **taxes:** YES raises the city's rate from $0.29548 to up to $0.50 per $1,000 in 2027 (about $225 per $1 million of assessed value; about $900 on a $4 million home), described as a 69-72% increase in the city's share, followed by 3% annual limit-factor increases for nine years and a permanent new base after 2036 [S1][S3][S7][S12]. It is a new, permanent increase, not a renewal [S1][S3].
- **spending:** The resolution limits the money to "providing existing levels of public safety and land use services" [S3]. The Task Force found the city had "already cut expenses where it can" and that staffing is lean [S3][S6]. Nothing in the record presents this as a service expansion.
- **local-control:** The ballot title states the purpose as "to remain an independent city" [S1]. The explanatory statement says that without the money the city "will not have the requisite funds to remain an independent city with local control of its police department and zoning issues" and "may need to consider dissolution" [S1]. The city FAQ names merging with another city as the alternative [S5], and the Task Force lists contracting police to another jurisdiction or merging as options after a failed vote [S6]. YES keeps police and land-use decisions with the city's own council. A NO vote does not dissolve the city by itself: the Task Force's first fallback is to resubmit the levy [S6], and the fire/EMS contract with Bellevue stays in place either way [S6].
