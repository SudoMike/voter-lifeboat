## What it does

Proposition No. 1 is a levy lid lift under chapter 84.55 RCW. In the ballot title's words, it "increases Kenmore's maximum regular property tax levy rate to $1.25/$1,000 for collection in 2027; sets the limit factor for Kenmore's regular property tax levy increases in 2028-2032 to 6%; authorizes the 2032 levy amount as the basis to calculate subsequent levies (RCW 84.55); and exempts qualifying seniors, veterans, and others (RCW 84.36)." [S1] The stated purpose is "To fund Climate & Environmental Stewardship, Affordable Housing, and Human Services." [S1] The City Council adopted Ordinance No. 26-0644 on June 15, 2026 by a 5-1 vote [S4]. Passage requires a simple majority (RCW 84.55.050) [S1]. The city says it would be Kenmore's first levy lid lift [S7][S8].

Section 2 of the ordinance defines the three purposes as follows [S3]:

- **Climate and Environmental Stewardship.** Programs on greenhouse-gas reduction, climate preparedness and preserving natural resources: energy efficiency and renewable energy, electric vehicle charging, tree planting and canopy growth, habitat and open space, waste diversion, community education, youth internships and community partnerships.
- **Affordable Housing.** City funding for A Regional Coalition for Housing (ARCH), a coalition of several East King County cities and King County, and other affordable-housing work. This includes housing policies, program administration, help for people seeking affordable rental and ownership housing, and coordinating investments in or purchases of affordable housing.
- **Human Services.** Contracts with agencies that support families, seniors, youth and other vulnerable people with food, utilities, housing, transportation, physical and behavioral health, and education.

Section 2 also provides: "The City Council may prioritize, modify, alter, make substitutions to and amend any component of the Programs and Services, and allocate levy proceeds among the various Programs and Services as it determines is in the best interests of the City." If changed conditions make it impractical to fund all or part of the programs, the city "shall not be required" to fund them [S3].

## Cost

- **Rate (explanatory statement).** Proposition 1 "would authorize the increase of the City's levy rate by approximately $0.36 per $1,000 assessed value, for a total regular property tax rate not to exceed $1.25 per $1,000 assessed value for collection in 2027, and allow the levy amount to increase by up to 6% in the succeeding five years (2028-2032)." [S1]
- **Household example (explanatory statement).** "Proposition 1 would add approximately $30 per month to the property tax bill for a home assessed at Kenmore's average value of $1,000,000." [S1] The city's FAQ gives the same figure: 36 cents per $1,000, or about $30 a month for a $1,000,000 home [S7].
- **Current rate.** Ordinance 26-0644 states: "the City's current property tax rate is $0.70 per $1,000 assessed valuation and existing maximum property tax rate is $2.66 per $1,000." [S3] The ordinance does not explain the gap between $0.70 plus $0.36 (about $1.06) and the $1.25 cap. For 2025, a city slide showed a city operating rate of $0.77 and Walkways & Waterways bond rate of $0.13, on an estimated total homeowner bill of $8,956 [S5].
- **Campaign figures.**
  - The pro statement says "It costs $3/month per $100,000 of assessed value — about $29/month for Kenmore's median home." [S2]
  - The con statement says it "will raise Kenmore's property tax rate by over 51% in 2027—costing the average household over $2,000 during the six-year levy period." [S2]
  - The con committee's site says city property tax "will increase 52-79% immediately, and double - permanently - by 2032" (campaign claim) [S10].
- **Revenue.** The council's chosen scenario (May 4 study session) was a 2026 ballot measure asking for "$3,300,000 ($0.36 per $1,000 AV)" [S5][S6].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- **New lid lift, permanent base.** The city calls this "a 6-year permanent levy lid lift" [S7]. The 6% limit factor applies to 2028-2032, and "The 2032 levy amount would be the basis to calculate subsequent levies." [S1] After 2032, the usual 1% annual limit applies again on top of that higher base [S1][S7].
- **Why now.** State law limits regular levy growth to 1% a year without voter approval. The city says this "has not kept pace with inflation" [S1], citing about 5% average annual cost growth over the past five years [S7]. The city's 2024 Financial Sustainability Plan forecasts that Kenmore will miss its 20% reserve goal by 2029 and "will have a cumulative operating deficit of approximately $20.2 million by 2032" without new revenue. Updated forecasts say the reserve goal may be missed as early as 2028 [S3][S7]. The explanatory statement says the increase, "combined with cost-cutting measures by the City, will promote financial sustainability for City operations and programs." [S1]
- **How the rate was chosen.** At the May 4, 2026 study session, staff presented three options [S5]:

  | Scenario | Annual ask | Rate per $1,000 |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1. "Reserve Goal Thru 2032" (enhanced programs and special projects) | $3,300,000 | $0.36 |
  | 2. Enhanced programs and special projects only | $2,150,000 | $0.24 |
  | 3. Enhanced programs only | $1,200,000 | $0.13 |

  The staff program table listed these costs [S5]:
  - Climate Action, $782,625 ($0.09). This covers education and outreach, Climate Action Plan programs, and urban forest plan implementation.
  - Affordable Housing, $339,988 ($0.04). This covers ARCH membership, the ARCH Housing Trust Fund and operating costs.
  - Human Services, $1,003,843 ($0.11). This covers pooled human services funding, KCRHA homelessness funding, Northshore Senior Center funding, utility assistance and a navigator.

  The council voted 5-2 to pursue Scenario 1: "a levy rate of 36 cents for a six-year permanent levy" [S6].
- **Exemption.** Section 5 of the ordinance exempts senior citizens, disabled veterans, and other people with disabilities who qualify under RCW 84.36.381 from the increase [S3].
- **No debt.** The city notes that a levy lid lift does not create municipal debt [S7][S3]. The city's FAQ says Kenmore has never put a levy lid lift to voters before, and notes that voters were asked to approve the Walkways & Waterways bond in 2016 [S7].
- **If it fails.** The pamphlet does not say what the city would cut if the measure fails [S1]. The con rebuttal says the city's commitments to these priorities "remain in place without Proposition 1" and that "The City will continue funding these priorities." [S2]

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, submitted by Tracy Banaszynski, Edouard Lassalle, Juliana Pooley (www.yeskenmore.org) [S2]:

- "Proposition 1 funds trees, greenspace, and readiness for hotter summers, wildfire smoke, and heavier storms and flooding." It funds affordable homes "through ARCH, our regional housing partner," and connects neighbors to food, utilities and health care support [S2].
- "State law limits regular property tax increases to 1% a year, but Kenmore's costs have risen about 5% a year over the past five years. This levy lid lift helps us keep services despite inflation, and without creating debt." [S2]
- "Cutting resiliency programs doesn't save money. It defers the bill." [S2]
- Rebuttal: Kenmore's spending growth reflects "population growth (1%), inflation (29%!), and necessary new resiliency services (10%)," and "State law prohibits levies from being blank checks." [S2]

## Arguments against

From the statement against, submitted by Laurie Sperry, John Hendrickson, Dave Postetter (www.AffordableKenmore.com) [S2]:

- "Keep Kenmore affordable and the City Council accountable." The statement says the tax rate rises "by over 51% in 2027" and "this increase will become permanent and the base for further tax increases." [S2]
- "Since 2020, Kenmore's population grew just 1.6%, while City spending increased more than 40% and staffing grew 36%. Now, having exhausted one-time COVID funds and other temporary revenues, the City Council is asking taxpayers to make up the difference." [S2]
- "There's no guarantee the new taxes will be spent on 'Climate and Environmental Stewardship, Affordable Housing, and Human Services.'" It quotes the ordinance's authority to "modify spending priorities and shift funding among authorized uses" and adds "Future Councils may redirect funds without voter approval." [S2] Ordinance Section 2 does give the council authority to reallocate proceeds among the listed programs [S3].
- Rebuttal: "We must demand disciplined budgeting rather than permanent tax spikes." [S2]
- **Con committee site.** The site says Prop 1 "does not increase funding or additional programs for climate and environmental stewardship and human services - it is a tax increase for the general fund." This is the committee's claim [S10].

## Context / Coverage

- **Council votes.** On May 4, 2026 the council voted 5-2 (Adman, Loutsis, Culver, Sasson, Herbig yes; Marshall, O'Cain no) to direct staff toward the 36-cent permanent lid lift [S6]. On June 15, 2026 it adopted Ordinance 26-0644 by 5-1 (O'Cain no; Marshall excused) [S4].
- **Process.** The council discussed revenue options at a May 17, 2025 retreat; at meetings on February 23, April 6 and May 4, 2026; and at a March 21-22, 2026 retreat [S7][S3]. Staff materials also mention a separate councilmanic 0.1% public safety sales tax already built into the forecast. A metropolitan park district and a public safety lid lift were listed as possible measures for 2027 or 2028 [S5].
- **Coverage.** Shoreline Area News previewed the June 15 vote [S8]. No other news coverage was found, and searches of the Bothell-Kenmore Reporter site turned up no articles on the measure.

## Axis notes

- **taxes:** A YES raises the city's regular levy by about $0.36 per $1,000, roughly half again its stated current rate of $0.70 [S1][S3]. It allows up to 6% annual levy growth for 2028-2032 and makes the 2032 amount the permanent base for later levies [S1]. The con case centers on resisting a large, permanent increase [S2].
- **spending:** A YES funds city programs in climate, housing (ARCH) and human services [S1][S3]. Staff costed these as "Enhanced + Special Projects," and the chosen $0.36 scenario also targets the city's reserve goal through 2032 [S5][S6]. The pro case frames the measure as investment in resiliency programs and keeping services despite inflation. The con case says it mainly backfills the general fund and that the council can shift funds among uses [S2][S10].
- **local-control:** The council remains the decision-maker. No governance change is involved [S1][S3].
