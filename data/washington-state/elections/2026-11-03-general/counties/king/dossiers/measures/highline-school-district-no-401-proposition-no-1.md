## What it does

Proposition 1 would authorize Highline School District to issue $595,400,000 of general obligation bonds, each series maturing within 21 years, repaid by annual excess property taxes [S1][S3]. Bond proceeds would pay for [S1][S3]:

1. Districtwide core building system and site work: plumbing, hot water, HVAC, electrical, roofing, security, fire systems and earthquake safety.
2. A new school replacing Sylvester Middle School.
3. A new school replacing Cascade Middle School.
4. A partial rebuild and modernization of Chinook Middle School.
5. Rebuilding and modernizing the former Southern Heights Elementary site as a new home for CHOICE Academy.
6. Design plans for Valley View Early Learning Center and Hilltop Elementary.

The board adopted Resolution No. 11-26 at its July 1, 2026 regular meeting [S3].

District per-project amounts, which add up to $595.4 million [S8][S9][S10][S11][S12][S13][S14][S15]:

| Project | Bond amount | District timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Sylvester Middle School replacement (current site) | $180.3M | interim site 2029-31; opens fall 2031 [S8] |
| Cascade Middle School replacement (Salmon Creek site) | $172.1M | construction projected to begin summer 2029 [S9] |
| Chinook Middle School partial rebuild and modernization | $161.3M | design from early 2029; construction from summer 2031; opens fall 2033 [S10] |
| Critical needs, districtwide | $40M | [S12] |
| CHOICE Academy at Southern Heights | $30M (of an estimated ~$45M total; rest from insurance and other non-bond sources such as FAA funding) | [S11] |
| Hilltop Elementary (critical needs plus initial design) | $5M | [S13] |
| Big Picture Schools critical needs | $3.4M | [S15] |
| Valley View Early Learning Center (critical needs plus initial design) | $3.3M | [S14] |

The district estimates rebuilding Cascade on its current site at $216.4 million, about $44 million more than at Salmon Creek. It cites wetland setbacks, a sewer easement and limited buildable area at the current site [S6][S9].

Passage requires 60% yes and turnout of at least 40% of the voters in the last general election [S1].

## Cost

- The ballot title and explanatory statement give no tax rate or household figure [S1].
- The district projects that, if both Prop. 1 and Prop. 2 pass, the combined local school tax rate goes from $4.47 to $4.89 per $1,000 of assessed value, an increase of 42 cents [S5][S6]. That combined figure covers this bond, existing bonds and the 2025 education levy [S5]. The district's household example: "On a $500,000 home, the increase for the proposed bond and technology levy is projected to be $210 annually, or $17.50 per month" [S5].
- The district's table shows total bond rates (existing plus proposed) of $2.30 in 2026 and $2.36, $2.32, $2.28 and $2.25 in 2027-2030 [S5]. The district does not publish a separate rate for this bond alone [S5][S6].
- The opposition committee says "Highline voters will pay $891.24 Million over 21 years assuming a 4% interest rate" [S26]. That is the committee's own estimate. No district repayment schedule was found.

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- This is a new bond authorization. Highline voters approved bonds in November 2016 (about $299 million, nearly 67% yes) and November 2022 ($518,397,000, more than 68% yes as of November 12, 2022) [S16][S18][S19][S20]. Before 2016, voters rejected bonds twice, in the fall 2014 general election and a special election in early 2015 [S21]. The district says voters "did not pass a bond for 10 years before the successful 2016 bond" [S17].
- The con statement says that if this bond passes, taxpayers "will have approved more than $1.4 billion in construction debt in just ten years" [S2]. Adding the 2016 (~$299M), 2022 ($518.4M) and 2026 ($595.4M) amounts gives about $1.41 billion. That sum is computed for this dossier [S19][S20][S1].
- Resolution 11-26, section 6: excess bond proceeds may go to other capital improvements or to retiring bonds. If proceeds fall short, the board funds the portion of the projects it finds "most necessary." Both require a board resolution after a public hearing under RCW 28A.530.020 [S3]. Section 8 lets the board alter project expenditures if "changed conditions or needs" arise, by the same resolution-and-hearing process [S3]. The opposition committee cites sections 6 and 8 as a "blank check" [S27].
- Bond proceeds may be used "for capital purposes only," not for equipment replacement [S3].
- The district says the state does not fully cover major school construction [S6]. The pro statement says "The state doesn't provide this funding" [S2]. Resolution 11-26 expects some state financing assistance under chapter 28A.525 RCW and does not estimate the amount [S3].
- No district statement on what happens if the measure fails was found [S4][S6].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, submitted by Tina Orwall, Dan Satterberg, Sili Savusa, www.yesforHighline.org [S2]:

- "Highline students deserve schools that are safe, healthy, and equipped for today's learning." The bond would "rebuild aging schools, address critical repairs, and make schools ADA accessible and more secure" [S2].
- It "would replace Cascade and Sylvester middle schools, rebuild Chinook Middle School, and rebuild Southern Heights for CHOICE Academy," ensuring "an equal middle school educational environment for students in every Highline neighborhood" [S2].
- "As roofs, heating, plumbing, and electrical systems age, repairs become more costly, and facilities failures disrupt student learning" [S2].
- "Local bonds are the only way to fund school construction and repairs. The state doesn't provide this funding." Highline "has a decades-long track record of completing projects on time and on budget" [S2].
- Rebuttal: "Students attending Highline's newly built schools have higher attendance and engagement"; construction costs are escalating; "Prop. 1 was shaped by a community-led advisory committee" [S2].

## Arguments against

From the statement against, submitted by Mike Bishoff, Kirsten Cooper, Katie Paulson, BetterPlanforHighline.com [S2]:

- "Vote No for Now on Highline's record-breaking $595.4 million bond proposal," which would bring approved construction debt to "more than $1.4 billion ... in just ten years" [S2].
- "Highline spends more than $25,000 per student annually, $3,000 more than the state average. Yet 67% of students test below grade level in reading, and 76% fail math and science grade level standards"; "bricks and mortar cannot fix failing academics" [S2]. (These statistics were not checked for this dossier.)
- "The district wants to rebuild three middle schools at once. Why not begin with two and return later for the third?" [S2]
- "Highline has ignored community concerns and failed to earn our trust"; "Demand a smaller, smarter bond" [S2].
- Rebuttal: "Asking taxpayers for $161.3M for Chinook's partial remodel - delayed until 2031- is not reasonable when full rebuilds at Sylvester ($180.3M) & Cascade ($172.1M) cost roughly the same" [S2]. These figures match the district's project pages. The district's Chinook timeline starts design in early 2029 and construction in summer 2031 [S8][S9][S10].
- The committee's website also questions the $40 million in "Critical Needs" without itemization and the Salmon Creek site size and traffic study, and raises possible eminent domain. It says the CHOICE Academy project would not include a library, full-size gym or functional cafeteria [S26][S27]. It also says recently revised Policy 6101 raised the superintendent's spending authority on bond and levy funds [S27]. Policy 6101, effective August 1, 2026, says board approval of the projects named in a bond or levy "constitutes the initial acceptance of all contractual commitments." After that, separate board approval is required only for change orders or amendments over $1,000,000 and for any project that exceeds its initial budget [S28].

## Context / Coverage

- **Planning committee.** The bond comes from the Capital Facilities Advisory Committee (CFAC), formed in 2015 after two failed bond measures. About half its members are chosen by lottery; the rest are appointed by cities, community groups, labor organizations and high schools [S7]. CFAC worked from fall 2024 through spring 2026 [S6]. It cut its March 30 recommendation from $615.4 million to $595.4 million after "two project changes" [S16].
- **June 3, 2026 board meeting (B-Town Blog).** CFAC members presented the recommendation [S22]. Public comment included a petition of nearly 400 Burien-area residents opposing the Salmon Creek site for Cascade, and a CFAC member questioned the committee's "50% hand-selected members" [S22]. The article notes its author is a CFAC member who worked on the recommendation [S22].
- **School tours.** The district ran tours of Cascade, Chinook and Sylvester middle schools and CHOICE Academy in October 2026, announced through the B-Town Blog [S23].
- Westside Seattle and the Seattle Times were not reached or found for this measure.

## Axis notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote authorizes $595.4 million in new bonds [S1]. The district projects that this measure and Prop. 2 together raise the combined school rate by $0.42 per $1,000 [S5]. The con case centers on the size of the debt, at more than $1.4 billion over ten years [S2].
- `spending`: the con argument is framed as accountability ("Taxpayers deserve responsible spending, transparency, and accountability"; academic results) [S2]. The pro side frames the bond as replacing aging buildings and adding career/technical learning spaces [S1][S2]. The bond mostly replaces existing buildings and does not expand programs [S1].
- `local-control`: nothing in the measure changes who governs [S1]. The CFAC composition dispute is about district process, not regional versus local control [S22].
- `parental-rights` / `social`: not addressed by the measure or the statements [S1][S2].
