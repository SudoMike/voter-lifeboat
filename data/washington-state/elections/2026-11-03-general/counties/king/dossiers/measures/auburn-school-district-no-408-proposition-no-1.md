## What it does

Proposition 1 asks Auburn School District voters to authorize up to $491,000,000 of general obligation bonds maturing within 20 years, repaid by annual excess property tax levies, to (1) build and equip a new Middle School #5, (2) build and equip a larger middle school to replace Cascade Middle School, and (3) build and equip a larger elementary school to replace Alpac Elementary School [S1]. The explanatory statement says the projects will increase capacity, reduce class sizes and portable use, improve safety and security, modernize technology and learning spaces, improve energy efficiency and improve playfields and athletic facilities for student and community use [S1]. The district expects about $36,248,500 in state construction assistance if the bonds are approved [S1].

The district's per-project estimates are Middle School #5, $184 million; Cascade Middle School, $189 million; Alpac Elementary, $118 million; the district says these include site preparation, permitting, traffic improvements, construction and inflation [S4][S5]. Middle School #5 would be built on district property on Sumner-Tapps Highway East; Cascade would be rebuilt about one mile from its current site, on district property at I St. NE and 40th St. NE; Alpac would be rebuilt on its current site [S3][S4][S7]. The district projects both middle schools opening in fall 2028 and Alpac in fall 2029 [S4][S5].

The district currently uses 43 portables at its four middle schools, housing more than 1,100 students, and expects 45 portables in 2026-27 [S4]. The district straddles King and Pierce counties; its election book reports results and validation counts for both [S5].

Passage requires a 60% yes vote and turnout of at least 40% of the voters who cast ballots in the last general election [S1]. The district puts that minimum at 7,038 voters (6,292 King, 746 Pierce) [S4][S5].

## Cost

- The ballot title and explanatory statement give no tax rate; the explanatory statement points to the district website for "the anticipated tax impact" [S1].
- The district website gives an "Average cost of $1.10 per $1,000 of assessed valuation" [S3]. Its Q&A works an example: a $600,000 home, 600 × $1.10 = $660 a year, or $55 per month [S4].
- The statement in favor uses the same $1.10 rate: "about $50.41 a month for a $550,000 home" [S2].
- The statement against says the $1.10 figure "completely fails to cover repaying the $491 million principal plus the $350+ million in interest debt" and that "true initial rates" reach "$1.93 per $1,000" [S2]. Its rebuttal works $600,000 × $1.93 into a school tax increase from $4,800 to $5,958 a year [S2].
- The district's election book projects the new bonds year by year (Table 3, three issues of $155M in 12/2026, $200M in 12/2027 and $136M in 12/2028). The projected rate for the new bonds is $0.93 in 2027, $1.09 in 2028, $1.13 in 2029, $1.08 in 2030-2031, $1.14-$1.20 in 2032-2036, $1.37-$1.58 in 2037-2039 and $1.61 in 2041-2048 [S5]. Table 4 shows the 2027 figure as $1.02 [S5]. These tables do not explain how the $1.10 "average" was calculated [S3][S5].
- In Table 3, scheduled debt service on the new bonds totals about $905.7 million from 2027 through 2048. That is the sum of the yearly amounts, computed for this dossier; the district does not publish a total [S5]. The total is roughly $414.7 million more than the $491 million principal. The con statement's "$350+ million in interest" claim is in the same range [S2][S5].
- The $1.93 figure does not appear in the district's projections for the new bonds. In the district's tables, $1.93 appears only as the actual 2024 rate for all district bonds combined [S5]. The district's documents do not respond to the con statement's $1.93 claim [S3][S4][S5].
- Combined district bond rate (existing plus new): the district projects $1.50 in 2026 (existing bonds only) rising to $2.53 in 2027-2031 and $2.65 in 2032-2039 [S5]. The combined district rate for all levies and bonds is projected at $5.81 per $1,000 in 2027-2031, up from $5.04 in 2026 [S5].
- Senior and disabled homeowners who meet income limits may qualify for exemptions [S1][S4].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- This is a new bond authorization, not a renewal. The same three schools were on the November 2024 and November 2025 ballots and failed both times [S4][S5].
  - **November 2024 (Resolution 1394):** $532,100,000, with a projected rate of 81 cents per $1,000 [S9]. The 2024 package also included capital improvements to 16 schools and 6 support buildings [S5]. Result: 55.05% yes (20,146 to 16,448), meeting the 40% validation requirement but not the 60% threshold [S5].
  - **November 2025 (Resolution 1408):** $490,000,000 at an estimated $1.04 per $1,000 [S6][S8]. The board split the 2024 package into this bond plus a separate six-year capital levy (Resolution 1409) for facility improvements, safety and replacement technology [S5]. The bond got 54.05% yes (9,296 to 7,903) [S5]. The capital levy passed with 56.85% [S5]. The Auburn Reporter put the levy at $110.8 million, an average of 77 cents per $1,000 [S8]. (In the district's 2025 bond table, the King and Pierce "Approved" columns, 8,082 and 4,999, do not add to the 9,296 total shown [S5].)
- The pro statement's "A majority voted Yes in the last election" refers to the November 2025 result (54%) [S2][S4].
- After the 2025 election the board held workshops on February 11 and April 13, 2026 and approved Resolution 1426 on April 27, 2026 [S5]. The district says the main change from 2025 is the schedule. Cascade's rebuild was moved up to open alongside Middle School #5, which the district says saves a year of inflation [S5].
- If the bond fails, the district says middle schools will keep exceeding capacity and it will keep maintaining Alpac and Cascade, "diverting resources from other uses" [S4].
- Under state law, the old Cascade building cannot be used as a K-12 school again. The district would use it as the interim site while Alpac is rebuilt in 2028-29 [S4].
- The district reports refinancing bonds six times since 2013, for about $13.9 million in taxpayer savings, and a Moody's Aa2 rating [S4].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, submitted by Cari Manry and Terri Herren [S2]:

- "Vote Yes to build a brand-new middle school and replace the aging, outdated Cascade Middle School and Alpac Elementary." More than 1,100 middle schoolers "are crowded into 45 temporary portables" [S2].
- "Clean, safe, and modern schools ignite student pride, boost teacher commitment, and uplift entire neighborhoods" [S2].
- The district "has a proven track record of delivering projects on time and under budget while keeping taxes low," at "an estimated cost of just $1.10 per $1,000 of assessed value" [S2].
- "A majority voted Yes in the last election, but we need a 60% supermajority to secure critical state matching funds" [S2].
- Rebuttal: "Misleading comparisons ignore differences in school size, site conditions, and long-term construction standards"; Auburn is "building schools designed to last a century" [S2].

## Arguments against

From the statement against, submitted by Eric Petersen [S2]:

- The $491 million package means "a staggering $163 million per individual facility," while "the neighboring Tacoma School District is successfully building eight separate schools for an average total cost of $66 million each" [S2].
- Tacoma's new Lowell and Whittier elementaries cost "under $50 million each," while Auburn "demands a whopping $118 million" for Alpac: "where exactly is the other $68 million going for this school alone?" [S2]
- The advertised $1.10 rate "completely fails to cover repaying the $491 million principal plus the $350+ million in interest debt," with "true initial rates up to $1.93 per $1,000" [S2]. (See Cost for the district's projections.)
- "Working families and honest local people are barely getting by"; the district "must bring local taxpayers a reasonable, fiscally responsible request" [S2].
- Rebuttal: "Tacoma's approved $650 million bond replaces or renovates 11 aging schools; Auburn wants $491 million for just 3. Don't be bullied, make them resubmit this with real cost" [S2].

The Tacoma cost figures in the con statement were not checked for this dossier.

## Context / Coverage

- The Auburn Reporter covered the board placing the measure on the November 3 ballot, citing a June 26 district press release on the $491 million cap, the $1.10 average estimate and the prior 55% and 54% results [S7].
- The project list comes from a 2023 Facilities Ad Hoc Committee of more than 40 community members, parents and staff. Its priority-1 recommendations were presented to the board on January 22, 2024 [S4][S5][S9].
- The district's 2016 Building for Learning bond built Bowman Creek and Willow Crest elementaries and replaced Olympic Middle School and five elementary schools [S4][S9].
- No news coverage of the opposition's cost comparisons, and no district response to them, was found apart from the pamphlet rebuttal [S2].

## Axis notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote authorizes up to $491 million in new bonds repaid by excess property taxes [S1]. This is a new debt authorization, not a renewal. The district projects its combined bond rate rising from $1.50 (2026) to $2.53 (2027-2031) and $2.65 (2032-2039) [S5]. The con argument is mainly about taxes and cost [S2].
- `spending`: the bond adds capacity, including a fifth middle school, beyond replacing existing buildings [S1][S4]. The con case is about cost per school, not about which programs are funded [S2].
- `local-control`: nothing in the measure changes who governs the schools [S1].
- `parental-rights` / `social`: neither the measure nor the statements address curriculum, parental notification or social policy [S1][S2].
