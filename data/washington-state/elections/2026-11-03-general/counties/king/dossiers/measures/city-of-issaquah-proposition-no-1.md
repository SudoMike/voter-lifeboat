## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would authorize the City of Issaquah to "issue no more than $18,000,000 of general obligation bonds maturing within 20 years; and levy annual excess property taxes to pay and retire such bonds, all as provided in Ordinance No. 3142" [S1]. The ballot title lists the projects as "sports fields and courts, new trails, trailhead improvements, restoration of Confluence Park's Ek House, playground improvements, a new dog park, and related capital improvements" [S1]. The explanatory statement adds that the trails would be on Cougar and Squak mountains, the trailhead work is for West Tiger Mountain, and the playground and dog park would be at Tibbetts Valley Park, and says "All of these projects were identified as community priorities in the adopted 2024 Park System Plan." [S1]

The council passed Ordinance No. 3142 on June 29, 2026 [S3]. Bond proceeds are "for capital purposes only (which shall not include the replacement of equipment)" [S3]. The ordinance lets the city "modify details of the Projects as it may find necessary or advisable" and, if money falls short, spend on the portions it deems "most necessary" [S3].

**Passage requirement:** "60% majority and minimum turnout of 40% of voters casting ballots in last general election (Washington Constitution, art. VII, sec. 2(b))" [S1].

### Projects

From the city's park bond page [S4]:

- **Community sports fields:** fields at Grand Ridge, Clark and Issaquah Valley elementary schools "would be renovated with multi-use turf and opened for year-round community use, outside of school hours." [S4]
- **Trailhead improvements:** the Senator Bill Ramos Memorial Trailhead (West Tiger Mountain and Grand Ridge trail systems) would get improved parking, restrooms, wayfinding signage, bike amenities, seating and a drinking fountain. [S4]
- **New trail corridors:** new trails in Harvey Manning Park on Cougar Mountain linking Central Issaquah, Tibbetts Valley Park, Talus and Cougar Mountain Regional Park; on Squak Mountain, "a new corridor through the Kelkari and Weymouth properties would provide more than 35 acres of open space." [S4]
- **Confluence Park Ek House restoration:** a full restoration to create "a gathering place for community events, arts and culture, and local history programs." [S4]
- **Tibbetts Valley Park:** a new off-leash dog park and a new playground "designed for children of all abilities." [S4]
- **Central Park pickleball courts:** new courts. [S4]

The city's per-project documents (linked from the page) could not be retrieved (HTTP 403), so per-project costs are not in this dossier.

## Cost

- Explanatory statement: "The City proposes issuing the new bonds as the prior bonds mature to keep property taxes similar, meaning the estimated cost to a $1 million homeowner would average about $80 per year." [S1]
- The city's park bond page says the renewal "would maintain the current levy rate at 8 cents per $1,000 of assessed property value and generate approximately $18 million" [S4].
- "Low-income seniors, certain veterans and others who qualify for tax exemptions from the retiring bonds would continue to be exempt." [S1]

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- **Replacement of retiring park bonds.** Voters approved $6,250,000 in park bonds in 2006 and $10,000,000 in 2013 [S3][S4]. Under the ordinance, the 2017 Bond (which refunded the 2006 Bonds and used part of the 2013 authorization) "will be fully retired in 2026," and the 2014 Bonds (also under the 2013 authorization) "will be fully retired in 2033" [S3]. The ordinance states "the levy to repay the proposed bonds is intended to not exceed the amount currently levied to pay the 2006 Bonds and the 2017 Bond," and "It is the intent of the City Council that the new bonds will replace the retiring bonds, resulting in a renewal of property taxes" [S3].
- **Prior bond uses.** The city says the 2006 bond supported "park renovations, trail development, and land acquisition," and the 2013 bond supported "repairs to the Julius Boehm Pool, development of turf fields at Central Park, neighborhood park improvements and land acquisitions" [S4].
- **Total project cost.** The ordinance estimates the projects at "approximately $24,500,000" [S3]. The city's page says the $18 million in bonds, "Combined with an anticipated $6 million in grants and other funding, ... could provide a total of $24 million" in projects [S4].
- **If it fails.** The city says "Without this funding, completing these projects could take more than 30 years" [S4]. The statement in favor says the same [S2].
- **Ordinance text.** Section 7 of the ordinance refers to submitting the question "at the November 6, 2018 election," while Sections 4 and 5 and the title set the November 3, 2026 election [S3].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, submitted by Jeffrey Newell, Robin Beukers and Donovan Richards [S2]:

- "This is a renewal—not a new tax—and an investment in the parks, trails, and gathering places that make Issaquah a great place to call home." [S2]
- Residents identified "three turf fields with sustainable cork infill at Issaquah Valley, Clark, and Grand Ridge elementary schools; a rebuilt mountain trailhead; new Cougar & Squak Mountain trail loops; a restored Ek House gathering space at Confluence Park; an all-abilities playground and dog park; and new pickleball courts. Maintenance is built in from the start." [S2]
- "Without this renewal, completing these community priorities could take more than 30 years." [S2]
- The statement lists as supporters: "Issaquah FC, Issaquah Little League, PTSA, Issaquah Alps Trails Club" [S2].
- Rebuttal: "This wasn't guesswork. Years of planning and extensive resident surveying shaped this list. ... 'Not yet' means delaying investments our community has already prioritized, potentially for decades." [S2]

## Arguments against

From the statement in opposition, submitted by Steve Pereira [S2]:

- The bond "only asked what we could do, but did not ask to prioritize projects that will achieve our stated goals and outcomes. This needs to happen before selecting $18.5M, and will show measured progress." [S2] The ballot title and ordinance authorize "no more than $18,000,000" [S1][S3]; the statement does not explain the $18.5M figure.
- "It uses 20 years of future Parks bonding capacity, but not that it best meets our citywide 20 year goals." [S2]
- The city should "Develop and implement benchmarking and Level of Service (LOS) measures that will inform and prioritize funding decisions and projects." [S2]
- "This is not an argument against specific projects." The statement asks voters to vote "'Not yet'." [S2]
- Rebuttal: the plan "should guide inform project selection priorities," and "High inflation costs necessarily need drive selection for the best Parks. Not yet." [S2]

## Axis notes

- **taxes:** YES authorizes new 20-year general obligation debt (up to $18 million) repaid by an excess levy, but the city sizes it to replace retiring park bonds and keep the levy at the current 8 cents per $1,000 (about $80 a year on a $1 million home) [S1][S3][S4]. The supporters call it "a renewal—not a new tax" [S2]. Under the ordinance, part of the 2013 authorization (the 2014 Bonds) keeps paying through 2033 [S3].
- **spending:** The bond pays for new facilities, not just upkeep: new trail corridors and more than 35 acres of open space on Squak Mountain, a new dog park and playground, new pickleball courts, and school fields converted to turf and opened for year-round community use, plus the Ek House restoration [S1][S4]. The opposition statement accepts the projects ("This is not an argument against specific projects") and objects to how they were prioritized [S2].
- **local-control / social / parental-rights:** No evidence. The measure does not change governance.
