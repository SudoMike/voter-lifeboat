## What it does

Proposition No. 1 is a levy lid lift under RCW 84.55.050 that would raise the City of Lake Forest Park's regular property tax levy to pay for "Public Safety Services" [S1][S3]. The ballot title lists "police services, safety equipment, 911 dispatch, emergency management services, jail services, and mental health crisis response" and sets "the maximum total regular levy rate at $0.88920/$1,000 for collection in 2027, with annual increases for five years based on inflation (Seattle region CPI-U)" [S1]. The explanatory statement adds prosecution and public defense costs and crisis intervention programs, and says the added levy money "would be tracked separately and could only be used to pay for the cost of Public Safety Services" [S1].

Resolution No. 26-2084 says the added revenue "shall be used exclusively for maintaining service levels of Public Safety Services," covering police staffing, training and safety equipment; 911 dispatch; emergency management; jail services; prosecution and public defense; and crisis intervention and mental health diversion programs [S3]. The council passed the resolution on June 25, 2026 [S3]. Qualifying low-income seniors, disabled veterans and other disabled persons are exempt under RCW 84.36.381 [S1][S3]. Passage requires a simple majority [S1].

## Cost

- Ballot title: maximum total regular levy rate of "$0.88920/$1,000 for collection in 2027" [S1].
- Explanatory statement: "Proposition 1 would cost property owners $0.22 per $1,000 of assessed valuation ($22 per $100,000). In 2027, for a $963,000 median value home this would be about $211.86 annually or $17.66 per month." [S1] The city's levy page gives the same $17.66 a month / $211.86 a year for "the average property (defined by King County's median property value in Lake Forest Park of $963,000)" [S4].
- The explanatory statement describes the increase as "approximately $1.2 million" a year [S1]. The resolution estimates "approximately $1,247,153" in 2027, "calculated from an assumed city-wide 2027 assessed value increase of 6-percent" [S3].
- Per the city FAQ, the city now receives 6.7 cents of each property-tax dollar, and the lid lift would add 2.4 cents [S5].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- **New lid lift, not a renewal.** The resolution states the city's current regular levy rate is "$0.66920 per $1,000 of assessed valuation; which is $1.99486 per $1,000 of assessed value less than what is statutorily authorized" [S3]. The measure adds $0.22, to a maximum of $0.88920, in 2027 [S1][S3].
- **Duration and escalator.** The resolution authorizes the added levy to be "Collected annually for six (6) consecutive years, from 2027 through 2032" and "Increased each year in January by the annual percentage growth in the June-to-June Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U)" [S3]. The ballot title phrases this as "annual increases for five years" after 2027 [S1]. The explanatory statement says each inflation increase "requires an open public process and a council vote to approve" [S1]; the city FAQ says the same [S5].
- **After expiration.** "When Proposition 1 expires, the levy will decrease to what it would have been if never approved and the City had only taken the 1% increase authorized by state law." [S1][S3]
- **Why the city says it needs it.** Under the 1% levy limit, the city's allowed increase "was $35,141 in 2026" [S3][S4]. The resolution cites cost growth for police, equipment, 911 dispatch, jail, prosecution, public defense and crisis programs, "a 24% increase in inflation over those same four years," over $455,000 in cuts to the 2025-2026 budget, and about $880,000 in one-time funding used to balance that budget [S3]. The city's levy page lists increases of $284,000 for 911 dispatch, $281,787 for jail, $122,269 for mental health crisis response and $106,488 for the prosecutor, and says the city "is now supplementing the budget with its 'savings'" [S4].
- **What the money is mapped to.** Attachment A to the resolution lists $1,881,544 in annual cost items, including 911 dispatch increases ($284,000), jail ($281,787), insurance ($193,000), patrol cars ($154,500), prosecutor ($106,488), public defender ($50,000), "Cover three (3) officer positions and cost of safety gear, equipment & training" ($689,500, citing the 2025-2026 adopted budget) and mental health mobile crisis response ($122,269, also citing the adopted budget) [S3].
- **If it fails.** The explanatory statement does not say what happens if the measure fails [S1]. The city FAQ says "Additional budget cuts would further impact city services, including the police department" [S5]. In 2024 Shoreline Area News reported the city's projection that, without cuts and new revenue, its $9,912,822 general fund would shrink to $6,819,619 by the end of 2026 [S9]. The statement in favor says city staff "project that our status quo will exhaust the general fund by 2031" [S2]; the city pages reviewed for this dossier do not state that projection [S4][S5].
- **Alternatives the city says it considered.** The FAQ says a one-tenth-of-1% sales tax would raise about $200,000 a year, and that school-zone traffic camera revenue is restricted by RCW 46.63.220(13) and cannot pay for public safety services [S5]. On contracting with the King County Sheriff, the FAQ says residents "have consistently stated they deeply value having their own police department" [S5].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, submitted by Marty Ross (www.yesonlfpprop1.com) [S2]:

- Cities face difficult choices "concocted by Tim Eyman and maintained by the State Legislature"; inflation has run 2.8% a year and "over 5% recently" while revenues are "hamstrung by a 1% annual cap on property tax increases" [S2].
- Other cities have cut back: "Brier cut police department funding. Edmonds explored selling off parks." [S2]
- "Lake Forest Park staff project that our status quo will exhaust the general fund by 2031." The city has cut costs "by closing City Hall on Fridays" [S2].
- "The City Council recognizing this crisis, unanimously voted to send this levy to us." [S2]
- "In voting down levies for decades, our beloved Lake Forest Park has been borrowing against the future." [S2]
- Rebuttal: the costs of 911, jail, legal services and policing "have risen dramatically recently, and the city has drawn down one-time general funds to fill the gap"; "Let's give city government and staff the tools to deliver these priorities and hold them accountable to it." [S2]

## Arguments against

From the statement in opposition, submitted by T.J. Fudge (nolfpprop1@gmail.com) [S2]:

- The author supports "raising taxes to increase funds for public safety," but says the city "has only committed to spend the $1M per year tax increase on public safety, with no commitment to add this additional amount to the current spending for public safety from the general fund" [S2].
- "The City exaggerates the budget struggles." The statement says unrestricted general fund revenue rose "by about $4M total, $1M per year, from 2021 to 2025, vastly more than the $35k the city implies," and that the city "has added, rather than cut, non-safety personnel" [S2]. This dossier did not independently verify these figures.
- "A 'no' vote does not threaten our public safety. It directs the city to restrain non-safety spending and commit to increasing funds for our police and public safety personnel." [S2]
- No rebuttal of the statement in favor was submitted [S2].

## Context / Coverage

- **The 2025 attempt.** In July 2025 the council placed a six-year police and public safety levy on the November 2025 ballot at $0.24 per $1,000, expected to raise about $1.4 million a year, with annual inflation adjustments "not to exceed five percent" [S7]. That measure (also Proposition No. 1) failed, 2,840 Yes (49.83%) to 2,859 No (50.17%) [S6]. The pro committee describes it as having "failed by only 19 votes" [S12]. The statement in opposition's reference to "last year's vote" is to this measure [S2][S6].
- **Differences from 2025.** The 2026 measure is $0.22 per $1,000 rather than $0.24, is projected at about $1.24 million rather than $1.4 million, and ties the annual increase to Seattle-area CPI-U rather than inflation capped at 5% [S3][S7][S10].
- **Earlier levies.** Shoreline Area News reported that a 2010 levy failed with about 78% opposed and a 2021 levy for parks and sidewalks failed with about 65% opposed, and that city voters "have never approved a property tax increase" since the 1% limit [S8][S9].
- **Council actions.** In November 2024 the council voted four to two to hire a $96,000 consultant to help the city pass a levy lid lift, during a budget process that weighed $455,612 in cuts against new taxes and fees [S9]. The 2026 resolution records that it "PASSED BY A MAJORITY VOTE" on June 25, 2026 [S3]; the statement in favor says the vote was unanimous [S2]. This dossier did not find the recorded roll call.
- **Coverage.** The 2026 coverage found in Shoreline Area News consists of republished city announcements [S10]. No independent 2026 news reporting on this measure was found.

## Axis notes

- **taxes:** YES raises the city's regular levy from $0.66920 to a maximum of $0.88920 per $1,000 in 2027 (+$0.22, about $1.25 million a year), with CPI-U increases through 2032 [S1][S3]. The levy is temporary (six years) and is a new increase, not a renewal of an expiring levy [S3]. The city's levy rate is $1.99486 below its statutory maximum [S3]. The comparable 2025 measure failed by 19 votes [S6][S12].
- **spending:** The resolution limits the money to "maintaining service levels" of existing public safety services, and Attachment A maps it to cost increases and existing budget items (including three officer positions cited from the adopted 2025-2026 budget) [S3]. The city does not present it as a service expansion [S4][S5]. The opposition argument is about how the city allocates general-fund money, not the total spent on public safety ("I support raising taxes to increase funds for public safety") [S2].
- **local-control:** The measure does not change who governs or delivers services. The city FAQ mentions keeping the city's own police department rather than contracting with the King County Sheriff, but the ballot text and explanatory statement do not frame the vote that way [S1][S5].
- **social / parental-rights:** No evidence.
