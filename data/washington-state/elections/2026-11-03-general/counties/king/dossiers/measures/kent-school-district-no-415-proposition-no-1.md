## What it does

Proposition 1 would replace Kent School District's expiring Educational Programs and Operations (EP&O) levy with a four-year levy for collection in 2028-2031 [S1]. The levy pays for day-to-day costs the state's basic education program does not fund, including:

- College and career readiness staff and programs
- Physical, social and emotional learning staff and programs, including mental health counselors
- Materials, supplies, and operating costs such as utilities and insurance
- Transportation
- Safety and security staff
- Athletics and extracurricular programs
- Electives and classroom support programs [S1]

The explanatory statement says the expiring levy "currently funds approximately 15% of the District's general operating expenses" and expires in 2027 [S1].

The board adopted Resolution 1718 [S1][S6]. The board voted 4-1 on June 24, 2026 to send the measure to voters: President Meghin Margel, Vice President Teresa Gregory, Laura Williams and Stefani Braicks voted yes, and Donald Cook voted no [S4]. Passage requires a simple majority [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

**The expiring levy.** Voters approved the current four-year replacement EP&O levy in November 2023 with 50.83% yes [S4][S5]. Late in the 2023 count it was ahead 50.78% to 49.22% [S8]. Before that election, the Kent Reporter reported the district's estimates: $79.3 million, $82.5 million and $85.8 million in levy revenue for the years labeled 2025, 2026 and 2027, at an estimated $1.72 per $1,000 [S7]. The current levy expires December 31, 2027 [S4][S5]. Kent's district website could not be retrieved, so the actual rates collected under the current levy were not confirmed.

**How much bigger the new levy is.**
- Comparing the 2023 election estimate for the last year ($85.8 million at an estimated $1.72) with the first year of the new levy ($104.7 million at $1.95): the amount rises about 22% and the rate about 23 cents, or about 13%. These comparisons are computed for this dossier [S1][S7].
- The con statement's author, Joseph Riley, made the same comparison in public comment: "In 2027, the district will collect $85 million from the existing EPO levy ... In 2028, under this new proposal, that jumps to $104 million. That is a $19 million or 22% increase" [S3][S4].
- At the June 24 meeting, Deputy Superintendent Wade Barringer recommended the $1.95 rate over a $1.76 option. He said the higher rate "will bring in about $19 million more per year" and would allow "a little bit of a surplus" and a larger fund balance: "Not necessarily grow and climb in hiring and doing a bunch more new stuff, but allows us to stabilize" [S4].
- In October, Barringer said that if the levy passes "the district will generate about $6-10 million more in revenue," and that further cuts may still be needed [S3]. The pro committee also uses the "$6-10 million more per year" figure [S13]. The articles do not reconcile the $19 million and $6-10 million figures [S3][S4].

**The state levy cap.** RCW 84.52.0531 caps enrichment (EP&O) levies at the lesser of $2.50 per $1,000 or a per-pupil limit. For districts under 40,000 students, the per-pupil limit is $2,500 adjusted for inflation, plus "inflation enhancements" of 3.33 percentage points a year from 2027 to 2030 [S12]. Riley told the board the $1.95 rate "sits just 3 cents below the absolute legal maximum lid of $1.98," down from a $2.09 maximum listed earlier [S4]. That cap figure is his and was not checked against district documents.

**Why it is on the ballot a year early.** The current levy runs through 2027 [S5]. The district had first planned a February 2027 election [S5]. Finance director Raul Parungao gave two reasons for November 2026 instead [S4][S5]:
- If it fails, the district can try again in a February special election.
- Midterm turnout is higher.

The con statement makes the same point from the other side: "this levy is running a year ahead of schedule, with no effect on funding until 2028. Voting 'No' now costs our classrooms nothing in 2027" [S2].

**If it fails.** Barringer said the loss of about $104 million in local funding "would also need to be addressed within the budget" [S3]. The district may bring a new levy proposal in the spring [S3].

**Budget situation.**
- Enrollment came in about 792 students below projection in fall 2025 [S3][S11].
- The district faced a $30.5 million shortfall for 2026-27 [S5][S9]. It announced a reduction of 229 employees to cover an estimated $32.5 million deficit [S4]. Earlier reporting described reductions of about 150 teachers expected through attrition and possible nonrenewal notices to provisional teachers [S9].
- In May 2026, employees protested cuts to library paraeducators and other staff, alongside a dispute over a superintendent buyout request [S10].
- District staff projected a further $9.5 million in cuts in each of 2027-28 and 2028-29 [S9]. In October Barringer said about $9 million more may be needed for 2027-28 [S3].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, submitted by Dana Ralph, Bill Boyce, Jeff Wagner, citizensforkentschools.com [S2]:

- "This renewal levy will help keep that investment strong. It provides critical local funding that keeps schools safe, supports student learning, provides extracurricular opportunities" [S2].
- It covers "academic support, athletics, arts, mental health resources, music, safety, and transportation" and "helps recruit and retain the outstanding teachers and staff" [S2].
- It continues local investment "while maintaining one of the lowest school levy rates among neighboring districts" [S2].
- "This levy is not a new tax" [S2].
- Rebuttal: "Kent School District has passed seven consecutive independent financial accountability audits and maintained a high credit rating"; "This renewal levy isn't a blank check" [S2].

The board appointed Ralph (Kent mayor), Boyce (Kent councilmember) and Wagner (Covington councilmember) to the pro committee on July 22, 2026 [S6].

## Arguments against

From the statement against, submitted by Joseph Riley [S2]:

- "Writing a bigger check will not fix poor financial management. The district is asking for significantly more money while class sizes grow, enrollment declines" [S2].
- "This levy comes with no binding promises to safeguard a single program or staffing group"; "this tax hike will not bring back the vital resources cut this year" [S2].
- "This levy is running a year ahead of schedule, with no effect on funding until 2028" [S2].
- Rebuttal: "The last levy promised those same things through 2027, but the district cut them this year"; "Make the district earn your trust with a community-aligned plan and new leadership before trying again next year" [S2].

Riley was the only applicant for the con committee [S6]. The Kent Reporter describes him as a parent of three Kent students and "a district watchdog." He also served on the con committee for the November 2024 capital levy [S3][S6].

## Axis notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote replaces an expiring EP&O levy [S1]. The pro side calls it "not a new tax" [S2]. The con side calls it a "tax hike," and the district's own estimates show a higher rate and amount than the expiring levy's election estimates: $1.95 vs about $1.72, and $104.7M vs $85.8M in the first year compared with the last [S1][S2][S7]. That is a renewal with a moderate increase, not a doubling.
- `spending`: district officials describe the increase as stabilizing current programs and rebuilding the fund balance, not expanding programs [S4]. The con case is about financial management, cuts and accountability [S2]. Under the measures convention this is maintenance of existing services, not expansion.
- `local-control` / `parental-rights` / `social`: not addressed by the measure or the statements [S1][S2].
