## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would authorize King County Fire Protection District No. 20 to issue up to $16,000,000 in general obligation bonds maturing within 20 years, repaid by annual excess property tax levies [S1]. The money would go to "improve emergency response support facilities and infrastructure on two sites owned by the District; renovate and modernize the existing fire station; acquire firefighting and emergency response apparatus" [S1]. The Board of Fire Commissioners adopted Resolution No. 2026-002 on May 11, 2026 [S3].

**The district.** The district calls itself the fire district for the "Skyway/West Hill community" and says it has served West Hill since 1943 [S4][S5]. Its commissioners page describes "approximately 16,000 residents in a three (3) square mile area of south King County (southeast of Seattle)" [S6]. Its community presentation gives "more than 18,344 residents" [S5]. The district reports 2,713 calls for service in 2025, including 1,867 medical emergencies and 147 fire responses [S5]. Staff are 20 career firefighters, 7 volunteers, 3 administrative staff and the fire chief [S5]. A three-member elected Board of Fire Commissioners governs the district: Craig Buckton, Gloria Hatcher-Mays and Cynthia Lamothe [S3][S6].

**Projects.** Section 2 of the resolution lists [S3]:
1. A new storage and support facility on district property, replacing "the aging building," for emergency response equipment, reserve apparatus and operational supplies.
2. Redeveloping "the existing warehouse site": demolishing the warehouse and building secure parking, more storage, and a support structure with emergency power infrastructure.
3. Renovating the existing fire station: interior renovations, modernized work and living spaces, exterior work including windows, and reconfigured upper and lower levels.
4. Buying firefighting and emergency response apparatus.

The district's presentation groups the work into four projects with addresses [S5]:
1. A new warehouse and emergency vehicle storage facility at 7621 S 124th St., replacing the building at 11619 84th Ave. S.
2. A remodel of the "44-year-old" fire station (restrooms, dorms, electrical, HVAC, kitchen, offices).
3. Demolition of 12608 Renton Ave. S. to build a secure parking lot, a utility building of about 1,000 sq. ft. and backup generator capacity for the station.
4. Replacement of a "22-year-old engine nearing end of service life."

The resolution says bond proceeds may be used "for capital purposes only, as permitted by law, which does not include the replacement of equipment" [S3]. The presentation calls apparatus "major capital assets — not routine operating expenses" [S5]. Under Section 7 of the resolution, the board may change the projects or use the proceeds on other district facilities or to retire bonds if circumstances change [S3].

Passage requires 60% yes votes and turnout of at least 40% of the number of voters who voted in the last general election (Washington Constitution, art. VII, sec. 2(b)) [S1].

## Cost

- The estimated tax rate is "approximately $0.28 per $1,000 of assessed value" [S1].
- The district's cost examples at that rate [S4][S5]:

  | Assessed value | Per year | Per month |
  |---|---|---|
  | $500,000 | $140 | $11.67 |
  | $700,000 | $196 | $16.33 |
  | $800,000 | $224 | $18.67 |

- The district says the estimate "may change depending on final bond structure, assessed values, interest rates, and market conditions" [S4].
- Some homeowners may qualify for exemptions [S1][S4].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- This is a new bond, not a renewal. The Assessor's 2026 levy table shows the district with a $1.50000 regular (expense) levy per $1,000 and no bond levy [S8].
- Levy history in the Assessor's tables checked for this dossier, per $1,000 [S8][S9][S10][S11]:

  | Year | Expense | Excess / M&O-EMS | Bond | Total |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2015 | $1.50000 | $0.67107 | none | $2.17107 |
  | 2020 | $1.13587 | $0.37390 | none | $1.50977 |
  | 2025 | $1.40702 | none | none | $1.40702 |
  | 2026 | $1.50000 | none | none | $1.50000 |

- No district bond levy appears in any of those four years, and no earlier bond measure by the district was found. Years not listed were not checked.
- Bond proceeds are restricted to capital purposes. The district says the bond "would not fund regular day-to-day operating expenses" [S4][S5].
- If approved, the district may levy the excess tax and issue short-term obligations before the bonds are issued [S3].
- If the measure fails: the district says these needs "are larger than what can reasonably be funded through the District's regular operating budget without delaying other priorities" [S4]. It does not describe a fallback plan [S4][S5].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, submitted by Terry Miller, Alesia D. Cannday and Margie Lindberg [S2]:

- "As a neighborhood committee we support this bond because it is an investment in the safety and preparedness of our community" [S2].
- "Our firefighters respond to thousands of emergency calls from a station that was built more than 40 years ago and is not seismic approved" [S2].
- The bond "will modernize Fire Station 20, replace an aging reserve engine, and improve the district's emergency operations campus by constructing nearby secure storage for emergency equipment and reserve apparatus" [S2].
- "The proposed bond is focused on long-term capital improvements that cannot be funded through the district's regular operating budget" [S2].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed. The pamphlet says: "No one in the jurisdiction contacted King County Elections to serve on the committee to write a statement against the measure" [S2]. The district had invited residents to volunteer for both committees by August 11, 2026 [S4].

- The station's name differs between sources. The pro statement calls it "Fire Station 20" [S2]. The district's event listing describes plans "to modernize Station 22" [S7]. The resolution and presentation say only "the existing fire station" [S3][S5].
- The pro statement calls the station "not seismic approved" [S2]. The district's own materials do not use that phrase; they describe "aging facilities that need modernization" and firefighter safety [S4][S5].

- `spending`: the bond pays for renovation and replacement of existing facilities and an engine, plus new secure storage and backup power [S3][S5]. It funds no new programs or staff [S4][S5].
