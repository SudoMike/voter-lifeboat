## What it does

Proposition No. 1 asks Bothell voters whether the city should "be annexed into and become a part of the Shoreline Fire Department Regional Fire Authority, effective March 1, 2027, pursuant to the plan amendment, as provided in Resolution No. 1784" [S1]. The Bothell Fire Department would stop operating as a standalone city department and become part of the larger regional fire authority (RFA) [S1]. The city's explanatory statement says annexation "will not change the delivery or the level of services to Bothell residents" [S1].

- **Who would run fire service.** Under the amended RFA Plan, the RFA governing board would grow to nine voting members: three from the Shoreline Fire Department, three from Northshore Fire Department (King County Fire Protection District No. 16), and three Bothell City Councilmembers, each appointed by its jurisdiction "in its sole discretion" (a 3-3-3 structure) [S3]. The Shoreline RFA FAQ describes the board as three commissioners for Shoreline, three for Lake Forest Park and Kenmore, and three Bothell councilmembers [S5].
- **Voting on RFA measures.** Bothell voters would be "entitled to vote on all ballot measures proposed by the RFA—including those pertaining to taxation and benefit charges" [S1].
- **Assets and staff.** On the effective date Bothell would transfer its fire-protection assets to the RFA, except Stations 42 and 45 and the assets of the Fire Marshal's office (community risk reduction and fire prevention), which Bothell keeps [S3]. Bothell would keep ownership of the two stations until its bonds for them are paid off (expected 2040), lease them to the RFA for $1 a year, and then transfer them for $1 [S3]. The city says no firefighters would lose their jobs and most personnel would move into equivalent RFA positions with the same pay and benefits [S4].
- **Snohomish County Fire District 10.** The same amended plan also annexes Snohomish County Fire District No. 10 (Sno10), subject to a separate vote in that district [S3][S5].
- **Passage.** The measure needs a simple majority (RCW 52.26.300) [S1]. Current Shoreline RFA voters do not vote on the annexation [S5].

## Cost

The ballot title and explanatory statement give no tax rate or dollar figure [S1]. The city's July 21, 2026 agenda bill gives these estimates:

- **2027.** "For 2027, property tax rates would remain the same, except for the regular 1% plus new construction increase as allowed by state law." Bothell would keep paying the fire department's direct costs in 2027 under a separate agreement with the RFA [S3].
- **2028 and after.** The RFA's property-tax levy and fire benefit charge would apply to Bothell residential and commercial property starting January 1, 2028 [S3]. The amended RFA Plan authorizes an RFA levy of up to $1.00 per $1,000 of assessed value and says the RFA "expects to levy and collect taxes at the tax levy rate of approximately $.45 per thousand" in 2028. The exact rate and the exact fire benefit charge will be set in late 2027 during the 2028 budget process [S3].
- **Household estimate (city staff).** "Under Bothell's current 2026 general levy rate, the average homeowner (assessed at $945,000) would see an increase of approximately $72 per month as a result of the new Shoreline RFA charges." The agenda bill continues: "Following annexation, the Council has stated an intention to adjust the general levy in 2028 by the fire department's share of the general fund (22 percent in 2026) ... This reduction would lower the annual property‑tax impact for an average Bothell homeowner to roughly $33 per month." [S3]
- The Shoreline RFA FAQ says Bothell "intends to reduce its general-fund property tax rate to reflect the change in fire service delivery" and that the city will provide details on the effect for Bothell taxpayers. It calls all its estimates preliminary and in 2026 dollars [S5].

The roughly $33 a month net figure depends on a stated council intention, not on anything in the ballot measure. The city's Fire Service Study FAQ describes the change as "a relative shift in where their fire services dollars are sourced from" and says the "exact financial impacts ... are still being evaluated" [S4].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- **Current funding.** Bothell now pays for fire service from its general fund [S4][S5]. City staff project that the department's net operating cost, after revenues, will rise from about $9.9 million in 2026 to $18.8 million by 2035 if it stays a standalone department, "in addition to future capital needs for a south Bothell fire station." [S3]
- **Existing levies.** Bothell would keep collecting its Safe and Secure (public safety) levy through 2030 and pass funds to the RFA to cover ten senior firefighter positions. That obligation ends when the levy sunsets [S3]. For 2027, the Bothell Urban EMS District levy would continue and be passed to the RFA. The RFA would then ask Bothell and Sno10 voters in 2027 to approve a replacement EMS levy for 2028 and beyond [S3].
- **Transfers and liabilities.** For 2027 operations, Bothell would transfer the rest of its 2027 fire budget to the RFA. It would also transfer $105,000 within 30 days of the effective date and $505,000 by April 1, 2028 for capital replacement [S3]. The RFA would take on Bothell's fire-operations liabilities, except disputed items such as the station HVAC repairs. Voter-approved bond debt stays with the jurisdiction whose voters approved it [S3].
- **Future stations.** Studies have identified a need for a new south Bothell station whatever the vote [S4]. If Bothell joins, the RFA board would decide how to fund it, through fire impact fees plus a future voter-approved capital bond shared across the RFA [S4][S5]. The RFA estimates such a bond at $0.03 to $0.058 per $1,000 of assessed value and is considering a bond measure in 2027 [S5]. If Bothell does not join, the city would plan the station through its 2027-2033 Capital Facilities Plan, with funding sources not yet determined [S4].
- **Efficiencies.** The RFA's preliminary analysis identifies about $3 million a year in potential efficiencies from combining the organizations. It estimates a possible reduction of about $0.30 per $1,000 for current Shoreline, Lake Forest Park and Kenmore taxpayers and about $0.20 per $1,000 for Sno10 taxpayers. It gives no comparable figure for Bothell [S5].
- **Plan amendments.** The RFA board may amend the plan's funding and finance section, "except when voter approval is required by statute." [S3]
- **If it fails.** "Bothell would need to reseek voter approval, negotiate a new agreement with RFA or another agency, or reconstitute the Bothell Fire Department." [S1]

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, submitted by Wesley Wang, Brandon Keith, and Mason Thompson [S2]:

- "As Bothell grows, our fire and emergency medical response needs to grow with us." The statement says the Bothell Professional Firefighters Association, IAFF Local 2099, has endorsed the measure [S2].
- "Regional fire service is the standard model in our area." Joining would let Bothell Fire "share resources, coordinate personnel, expand specialized capabilities, and improve operational efficiency" rather than duplicate administration and equipment [S2].
- Spreading the cost of future facilities and equipment "across a larger regional tax base lightens the financial impact on our city" [S2].
- "With a Yes vote, we retain a significant amount of control over the direction of the fire service in Bothell. Our city will have a substantial voice in how the RFA is run with three of the nine seats on the RFA's governing board." [S2]

## Context / Coverage

- **Background.** In May 2024 Bothell contracted with Shoreline Fire Department for executive fire administration. Shoreline Fire Chief Matt Cowan became Bothell's fire chief, while firefighters stayed city employees [S4][S6]. The Shoreline Fire Department RFA was formed May 1, 2025, after voters in the Shoreline and Northshore fire districts approved it by a 60% supermajority on February 11, 2025. Bothell says that formation created the shared border that made it eligible to annex [S3][S4].
- **Council actions.** Council actions in 2026 were:
  - Resolution No. 1736 (May 5) set up a joint meeting with the RFA board, held May 18.
  - Resolution No. 1756 (June 9) requested annexation.
  - A public hearing was held July 7.
  - The RFA board adopted Resolution 26-05, the amended plan, on July 9.
  - On July 21 the council "unanimously approved" the ballot resolution and appointed the pro committee [S3][S4].
- **Bothell is one of a few holdouts.** The city says it is "one of only eight cities in King and Snohomish Counties that still operates its own fire department." [S4]
- **News coverage.** Shoreline Area News covered the March 9, 2026 Shoreline Fire special meeting that discussed Bothell possibly joining the RFA [S6]. No news coverage of the November ballot measure itself was found. Searches of the Bothell-Kenmore Reporter site turned up no 2026 articles on the annexation.

## Axis notes

- **local-control:** A YES moves governance of Bothell's fire service from the City Council and city budget to a nine-member regional fire authority board. Bothell would appoint three of the nine seats [S1][S3]. The RFA board, not the city, would set the RFA levy and fire benefit charge within its authority, and could amend the plan's finance section without a vote except where statute requires one [S3]. The pro statement frames this as keeping "a significant amount of control" through those three seats [S2].
- **taxes:** A YES puts the RFA's property-tax levy (expected at about $0.45 per $1,000 in 2028, with authority up to $1.00) and a fire benefit charge on Bothell property starting in 2028 [S3]. City staff estimate about $72 more a month for an average $945,000 home. That would fall to about $33 a month only if the council carries out its stated plan to cut the city's general levy, so the net change is still an increase [S3]. The council's levy cut is not part of the ballot measure [S3][S4].
- **spending:** The explanatory statement and ballot title frame the measure as keeping current service levels ("Annexation will not change the delivery or the level of services"), with efficiencies and future capacity as secondary arguments [S1][S2]. That points to maintenance rather than program expansion.
