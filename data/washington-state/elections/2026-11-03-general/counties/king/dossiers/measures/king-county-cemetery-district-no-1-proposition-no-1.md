## What it does

Proposition No. 1 is a permanent levy lid lift for King County Cemetery District No. 1, which runs the Vashon Cemetery. It would raise the district's regular property tax levy "from approximately $.03 ($.02816) to $.05 per $1000 of assessed valuation beginning in 2027" [S1]. The ballot title lists five purposes: "support operational expenses, modernize and maintain burial records, acquire essential equipment, advance the development of green burial practices, and facilitate the acquisition of additional property" [S1]. The board adopted Resolution No. 295 on July 10, 2026 [S3].

The explanatory statement says the money would go to "routine maintenance, preservation of cemetery records, repair and upkeep of facilities, and protection of the site's historical character," and that the measure "does not change ownership or governance of the cemetery" [S1].

**The district.** Voters formed the district in 1976. It covers all properties on Vashon and Maury islands [S3][S5][S7]. It maintains the Vashon Cemetery and the Maury Island Cemetery [S3], and the Beachcomber reports it took over the Maury Island cemetery in 1989 [S7]. The Beachcomber also reports that it is King County's only cemetery district [S7].

Three elected commissioners govern the district [S5]:
- Catherine Sullivan, the pamphlet contact [S1][S5].
- Bridgette Webb [S5].
- Lisa Devereau, the board chair, who signed Resolution 295 [S3][S5].

Island Funeral Services operates the cemetery under contract [S5]. The Beachcomber reports that Devereau manages Island Funeral Service and has been a commissioner for more than 20 years [S7].

Passage requires a simple majority (RCW 84.55.050) [S1].

## Cost

- The rate would rise from $0.02816 to $0.05 per $1,000 of assessed value, starting in 2027 [S1][S3]. The Assessor's 2026 levy table lists the district's rate as $0.02816 [S9].
- The explanatory statement says: "An increase of two cents per $1000 of assessed valuation results in an annual property tax increase, per $100,000 of assessed property tax value, of two dollars per year," for a total of "five dollars ($5.00) per $100,000 of assessed value" [S1].
  - The exact increase from $0.02816 to $0.05 is $0.02184 per $1,000, or about $2.18 per $100,000. That arithmetic was done for this dossier [S1].
- The Beachcomber reports the increase would cost the owner of a median-value island home about $17 more in 2027, with the district's share of the tax bill going from $22 to about $39 [S7].
- The Beachcomber reports the increase would bring in "another $100,000 or so" a year, on top of the $129,000 the district now collects [S7].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- This is a levy lid lift. The district asks voters to let it levy above the limits in RCW 84.55.010, and "the dollar amount of the 2027 levy" would become the base for later years [S3]. The Beachcomber explains that the 1% annual limit on levy growth would resume in 2028 from that higher base [S7]. The ballot title calls the increase "permanent" [S1].
- The district's statutory levy limit is 11.25 cents per $1,000 [S3].
- Levy history:
  - The resolution says the district has levied "approximately three cents ($0.02762)" since 1976 [S3].
  - The Assessor's tables show $0.02762 in 2025 and $0.02816 in 2026 [S10][S9]. The two figures in the resolution match those two years.
  - The Beachcomber reports this is the first time in the district's 50-year history that it has asked voters for more tax money [S7].
- Budget, from the resolution [S3]:
  - "Every year since 2021, the property tax received fell short of expenses," and the district has covered shortfalls "only through cemetery plot sales."
  - Plot sales are "an inconsistent and unpredictable revenue source."
  - Payroll "has increased 569% in the 50 years since the district has been established." The district has two employees.
- Budget, as reported by the Beachcomber from a district budget document [S7]:
  - The district began 2026 with a $527,000 cash balance.
  - Budgeted 2026 expenses are $152,000, of which about $95,000 is wages, benefits and payroll taxes for a full-time groundskeeper and a part-time office manager.
  - First-quarter income included about $5,600 in interest and $10,600 in plot sales.
  - The commissioners told the paper the cash balance should be held for a possible land purchase.
- Planned uses [S3][S7]:
  - A truck. The district has none.
  - Replacement of aging mowers and tractors. The Beachcomber reports the tractor used to dig graves is 36 years old.
  - Electronic burial records. The Beachcomber reports the commissioners acknowledge the current register of more than 4,300 burials is inaccurate and incomplete, and the district plans aerial mapping of the cemetery.
  - More green burial capacity, and eventually more land.
- If the measure fails, the resolution's recitals suggest the district would continue covering shortfalls with plot sales [S3]. The 2024 annual report said plot sales "continue to offset the difference of tax funds collected vs. operational expenses" and that the district might someday "need to approach our community for a tax increase" [S6].

## Context / Coverage

- **Green burial.** The cemetery performed its first fully green burial in January 2023, in a section set aside for about 40 more. At the time the Beachcomber described it as the only cemetery in King County that could accommodate fully green burials [S8]. The 2026 Beachcomber story reports that all 100 plots offered in the one-acre "Greenwood Block" sold within two years, 21 people are buried there, and 15 are on a waiting list [S7]. The district's 2024 annual report said green-burial plot sales "exceeded our projections" [S6].
- **Land.** The resolution says that "Vashon Cemetery will have to add additional land to accommodate additional burial locations" [S3]. The Beachcomber reports that in 2023 the district was interested in 27 acres near Island Center Forest. A King County Parks application for $1.34 million in Parks and Conservation Futures levy money to preserve that land as a green burial site and open space was not funded, and the property was later sold to someone else [S7].
- **History.** The main cemetery began as a two-acre parcel in 1888 and is now nearly 11 acres [S3]. The Beachcomber reports the district owns 19 acres in total, not all of it usable for burials [S7].
- **Coverage.** The Vashon-Maury Island Beachcomber covered the measure on October 7, 2026 [S7].

## Axis notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote permanently raises an existing regular levy from $0.02816 to $0.05 per $1,000, about 78% higher (arithmetic for this dossier) [S1][S9]. In dollars it is small: about $2 per $100,000 of assessed value per the district, or about $17 for a median Vashon home per the Beachcomber [S1][S7].
- `spending`: the ballot title names new uses beyond current operations: developing green burial practices, acquiring more property, a truck, and modernized records [S1][S3]. The district also says tax revenue has not covered current expenses since 2021 [S3].
- `local-control`: the explanatory statement says the measure "does not change ownership or governance of the cemetery" [S1].
- `social` / `parental-rights`: not addressed [S1][S2].
