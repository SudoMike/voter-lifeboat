## What it does

Proposition No. 1 asks City of Milton voters to raise the city's sales and use tax by one-tenth of one percent (0.001) "to provide ongoing funding for public safety purposes permitted under RCW 82.14.450, such as providing adequate law enforcement staffing levels." The increase would take effect in 2027 [S1]. The explanatory statement says the measure "would maintain funding for police services and community safety" and that the city needs "additional funds to supplement existing city general fund revenue which is applied for these purposes" [S1]. Under RCW 82.14.450(7), the city must share 15% of the proceeds with the county [S1][S7].

Resolution No. 26-2005 states its purpose as "to secure additional funding for existing police and public safety services" [S4].

Passage requires a simple majority. The pamphlet cites RCW 82.14.450 [S1], and the statute says a city may impose the tax "if the proposition is approved by a majority of persons voting" [S7].

**Which county runs the election.** Milton lies in both King and Pierce counties. The VoteWA record for this measure sits under county code 27 (Pierce) and lists the counties as "King, Pierce" [S3]. Resolution 26-2005 authorizes wording changes recommended by "the Pierce County and King County Auditors" [S4]. The King County local pamphlet prints the measure in full [S1][S2]. This research did not check whether Pierce County's own pamphlet also prints it, because Pierce County's elections website refused automated requests (HTTP 403).

## Cost

- The rate is "one-tenth of one percent (0.001)" [S1], or 10 cents on a $100 taxable purchase. The pamphlet gives no per-household estimate [S1].
- The statement in favor describes it as "just one-tenth of a penny on every dollar" [S2].
- Current combined sales tax rates in Q4 2026 are 10.3% in the King County part of Milton and 10.2% in the Pierce County part [S6]. Adding 0.1% would make them 10.4% and 10.3%; that arithmetic was done for this dossier [S6].
- Revenue: "Based on 2026 sales taxes received to date, it is estimated that a Public Safety Sales Tax would generate about $200,000 annually" [S4]. The staff report does not say whether this figure is before or after the 15% county share [S4].
- State law exempts motor vehicle sales and the first 36 months of vehicle leases from this tax [S7].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- This is a new tax, not a renewal. According to the staff report, "Pierce County and King County do not currently have a Public Safety Sales Tax" [S4]. The resolution says the same about RCW 82.14.450 taxes, and adds that this leaves Milton authorized to impose up to 0.1% [S4].
- The ballot title and resolution set no expiration date. The ballot title calls the funding "ongoing" [S1][S4].
- Use of funds: under RCW 82.14.450(5), one-third of the money "must be used solely for criminal justice purposes, fire protection purposes, or both" [S7]. The resolution describes the one-third as reserved "solely for criminal justice purposes" [S4].
- City budget context, from the August 3, 2026 staff report by the Finance Director and Police Chief [S4]:
  - General Fund revenue has run above budget for several years, "primarily due to better than expected sales tax revenues and planning and building fees" from large development projects. The city "does not anticipate continued revenues at these levels" now that those projects are finishing.
  - The city expects Sound Transit light rail construction to reduce sales tax from businesses along the SR 99 corridor.
  - 2026 General Fund expenditures total $7,808,874. That includes a $4,400,000 transfer to the Criminal Justice Fund for Police Department operations, which is 56% of budgeted General Fund spending. The report expects that subsidy to grow with labor and materials costs and with renegotiation of the Milton Police Guild contract later in 2026.
  - "At current budgeted levels of revenues and expenditures, fund balance in the General Fund will fall below the targeted amount of at least 8% of budgeted operating expenditures by the end of 2028."
- If the measure fails: the July 20 council presentation says that without additional sustainable revenue, reserves "may fall below City policy," and future councils could face hiring freezes, delayed equipment replacement, reduced capital improvements, service reductions or deferred maintenance [S4]. The same presentation describes the city as "Financially Healthy Today" and says Milton is not facing "a financial crisis" [S4].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, submitted by Dave Strader and Jacki Strader (YESonMiltonProp1@gmail.com) [S2][S3]:

- "Safety is more important now than ever." The tax "will allow the Milton Police Department to maintain its current budget and high-quality services" [S2].
- "Costs for staffing, training, insurance, technology, equipment, investigations, jail services, prosecution, and municipal court operations continue to rise, while demand for public safety has increased dramatically" [S2].
- The tax "ensures we retain highly trained officers equipped with modern tools" [S2].
- A yes vote helps "preserve financial stability, ease pressure on the General Fund, and protect funding for streets, parks, and essential infrastructure" [S2].
- "Protect what matters most. Vote Yes." [S2]

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed. The pamphlet says: "No one in the jurisdiction contacted King County Elections to serve on the committee to write a statement against the measure" [S2]. At the August 3, 2026 council meeting, Mayor White told the council that two people wanted to write the "For" statement "but nobody yet for the 'Against' statement" [S5].

## Context / Coverage

- **Council action.** Finance Director Robbecke presented the measure for discussion on July 20, 2026 [S4]. On August 3, 2026 the council adopted Resolution 26-2005 6 to 0 (mover Ternes, seconder Poore; ayes Mounts, Ternes, Ceder, Poore, Hall, McNally Martinez) [S5]. A second 6-0 motion changed the ballot title to read ".001" instead of ".01%" [S5]. The council then appointed Dave and Jacki Strader to the "For" committee, also 6-0 [S5].
- In the July 20 minutes, Public Works Director Madden's report thanks the council "for the discussion on the public safety tax, it's great to be part of a proactive leadership team and council" [S4].
- **Coverage.** No news coverage was found. A search of the Federal Way Mirror archive returned articles about Federal Way's own public safety sales tax but none about Milton's measure. Seattle Times and Tacoma News Tribune searches could not be run.

## Axis notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote adds a new 0.1% sales and use tax with no expiration date [S1][S4]. Neither county currently levies this tax [S4]. The city estimates about $200,000 a year [S4].
- `spending`: the stated purpose is to maintain existing police and public safety services, not to expand them ("secure additional funding for existing police and public safety services") [S4]. The pro statement also says the measure will "maintain its current budget" [S2].
- `local-control`: the measure does not change who governs or provides police services [S1][S4].
- `social` / `parental-rights`: not addressed by the measure or the statement [S1][S2].
