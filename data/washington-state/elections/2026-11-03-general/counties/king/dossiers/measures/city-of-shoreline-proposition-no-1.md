## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would create the Shoreline Metropolitan Park District (MPD), a new taxing jurisdiction under chapter 35.61 RCW with the same boundaries as the City of Shoreline [S1]. Its purpose is "to finance and refinance the acquisition, construction, operation, maintenance, programming, renovation, and expansion of a pool facility and future recreational facilities" [S1]. A board of City Councilmembers would govern the district, and city staff would run and maintain the pool under an agreement with the MPD [S1]. On the ballot, the two choices read "For the formation of a Shoreline Metropolitan Park District to be governed by Shoreline City Councilmembers" and "Against the formation of a Shoreline Metropolitan Park District." [S3] The City Council adopted Resolution No. 562 on June 1, 2026 [S9]. Passage requires a simple majority (RCW 35.61.040) [S1].

The planned facility is about 48,000 square feet on city-owned property at 17828 Midvale Ave N. The preliminary design includes:

- an 8-lane lap pool with a diving well
- a recreation pool with a lazy river
- a warmer hydrotherapy pool
- a sauna, universal changerooms, party rooms and parking
- outdoor space connecting to the Interurban Trail [S1]

The design leaves room for a possible recreation facility addition if more funding becomes available [S1]. MPD revenue "could only be used for the pool facility and future recreational facilities, not other City needs." [S1]

## Cost

- **Rate (ballot title).** The MPD would "levy regular property taxes not to exceed $0.75/$1,000 assessed valuation (anticipated initial rate $0.55/$1,000, or as necessary to generate approximately $8.8 million annually)." [S1]
- **Household example (explanatory statement).** "Tax collection would start in 2028. A homeowner with a median value home ($831,000 in 2026) would pay approximately $421 per year, an average of $35 per month, in additional property taxes." [S1] The pamphlet does not show how it got $421. Applying $0.55 to $831,000 gives about $457.
- **Campaign figures.**
  - The con statement and the con committee's site say the MPD would raise city property taxes "by at least 33%" [S2][S11]. The site bases this on a 2026 City of Shoreline levy rate of $1.67 per $1,000 and adds that the $0.75 maximum would be a 45% increase [S11].
  - The pro rebuttal calls the 33% claim false and says the increase "would increase approximately 5.5% to 7.5%." [S2] The rebuttal does not say what that percentage is measured against.
- **Project cost.** The city's MPD Q&A, as published by Shoreline Area News, puts the total cost of the pool facility at about $100 million [S5]. That figure includes "design, permitting, construction, paying off the property purchase, and related fees and contingencies," and $16.6 million of it pays off the remaining property purchase [S5]. MPD revenue would finance $95 million of one-time costs, and a King County grant would cover the rest [S5]. The Business Journal report itemizes construction at $61.2 million, "soft costs" at $22.2 million, and the King County grant at $5 million [S4].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- **New district, not a bond or lid lift.** The MPD would be a new junior taxing district with its own regular property tax authority, starting at about $0.55 and capped at $0.75 per $1,000. The board (City Councilmembers) would set the levy within that cap. The tax would be ongoing, with no expiration date in the ballot title [S1].
- **Operations.** The city estimates operations and maintenance for the first five years at about $4.4 million a year on average. User and rental fees would bring in about $2.9 million, or 66%, and the remaining 34% (about $1.5 million) would come from the MPD property tax [S5][S4].
- **Timing.** Tax collection would start in 2028 [S1]. If voters approve, construction would begin in the third quarter of 2028 [S4].
- **What came before.** King County built the former Shoreline Pool, a 6-lane lap pool, in 1971 (the Forward Thrust bond program) [S5][S6]. The city had planned to close it in September 2021 because of age and repair needs, but closed it permanently in June 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic [S5][S8]. At that time the pool's operating costs exceeded program revenue by about $800,000 to $850,000 a year, paid from the general fund [S8]. The old pool was later demolished, and a picnic shelter now stands on the site [S6][S9]. Shoreline currently has no public pool [S9].
- **Exemptions.** The ballot title and explanatory statement do not mention a senior or disabled-person exemption [S1]. The con committee's site says that, because the MPD is a junior taxing district, the low-income senior and disabled exemptions in RCW 84.36.381 would not apply, unlike the 2019 bond. This is the committee's claim [S11].
- **If it fails.** The pamphlet does not describe an alternative plan if the measure fails [S1].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, submitted by Krishnakant Nammi, Susie McDowell, and Buddy Smith (letshorelineswim.org) [S2]:

- "It's Time Shoreline! Let's invest in our future." The facility would serve swim lessons, high school swim teams, lap swimmers and seniors' water exercise [S2].
- "Studies show that formal swimming lessons reduce drowning risk in young children by 88%," while aquatic exercise improves strength, mobility and fall prevention in older adults [S2].
- "Shoreline residents shouldn't have to leave town or pay higher non-resident fees for pool access." The MPD "establishes stable, dedicated local funding to build, operate, and maintain an affordable, year-round facility" [S2].
- Rebuttal: "The Con statement is fundamentally inaccurate." It says the MPD's authority "is limited between 55 and 75 cents per $1,000 assessed" and that the measure "simply replaces the pool we enjoyed from 1971 to 2020." [S2]

## Arguments against

From the statement against, submitted by Sandra Cribbs, Jeff Hays, and Joseph Smith (www.shorelineprop1.com) [S2]:

- "A Permanent Tax Hike for a $100 Million Luxury Pool?" The statement says the MPD can "permanently raise local/city property taxes by at least 33%" and is "not a temporary bond" [S2].
- It says the measure "hands the newly created Metropolitan Park District sweeping authority to fund future projects without ever coming back to voters for approval." [S2]
- It says landlords will pass costs to renters, and that the plan is "just a luxury pool facility – no recreation center, no fitness center, no senior center, no commercial kitchen." [S2]
- Rebuttal: "This is no time to add $100M in debt for a pool facility." It cites "extremely uncertain economic times with high interest rates and rising inflation" and other expected tax increases [S2].
- **Con committee site.** The site argues the 2019 bond would have cost the median household $244 a year for about 20 years for a larger 75,000-square-foot facility, compared with $421 a year with no end date now. It also says the $100 million estimate rests on designs about 10% complete. These are the committee's claims [S11]. The site calls itself "not anti-pool" [S11].

## Context / Coverage

- **Council vote.** The City Council "unanimously passed the resolution" placing the measure on the ballot (Monday, June 1, 2026), according to the Puget Sound Business Journal report republished by Shoreline Area News [S4][S9].
- **Prior attempt.** In November 2019, Shoreline Proposition 1 was a bond for the Shoreline Aquatics, Recreation, and Community Center plus park improvements. It received 54% approval but failed the 60% bond requirement [S7][S6]. That proposal also covered the programs at the Spartan Recreation Center. The current plan keeps Spartan as a separate recreation site [S6]. Councilmember Chris Roberts said at the June 2026 meeting: "There's a lot of disappointment that the previous bond did not pass" [S4].

## Axis notes

- **taxes:** A YES creates a new junior taxing district with permanent regular property-tax authority. The initial rate is about $0.55 per $1,000 (about $8.8 million a year), and the cap is $0.75 [S1]. The city estimates $421 a year for the median home [S1]. The con case centers on resisting what it calls a permanent tax hike of "at least 33%" of the city levy [S2][S11].
- **spending:** A YES funds a new roughly $100 million aquatic facility and ongoing operating subsidies (34% of O&M). It also enables future recreation facilities [S1][S5]. Shoreline has had no public pool since 2020, so this restores and expands a public service [S5][S9]. The pro case frames it as public investment in health and safety. The con case argues the cost is too high for "too few" [S2].
- **local-control:** The district would have the same boundaries as the city, and the sitting City Councilmembers would be its board [S1]. Who decides does not change; the measure creates separate taxing capacity for the existing city government. The con statement's concern is that the new district could fund future projects without returning to voters [S2].
