## Background
Shelley Kloba is the incumbent Democratic State Representative for the 1st Legislative District, Position 2, serving since 2017 [S1]. She is Vice-Chair of the House Technology, Economic Development, and Veterans Committee, sits on the Consumer Protection & Business and Environment & Energy committees, is an ex-officio member of the Gambling Commission, serves on an NCSL task force on AI, cybersecurity and data privacy, and founded the House Technology caucus [S1]. She was a Kirkland City Councilmember 2013-2016 and earlier a Lake Washington Schools Foundation trustee, Kirkland Park Board member and WA State PTA Legislative Director [S1]. She has been a licensed health care provider for over 20 years and holds a B.S. in Kinesiology from the University of Illinois [S1]. On primary night she led a four-candidate field with 48.5%, with Republican Cliff Moon second at 23.1%, per the Everett Herald [S14].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** pledges to "fix our regressive tax code" [S1]. Her site says the Legislature "passed the Millionaires Tax" and that this, "along with the capital gains tax, is the start of correcting our state's upside down tax code. I will continue to support bills that create fair tax systems" [S2]. She co-signed the district session report calling the Millionaires Tax "a historic step forward" [S8].
- **Technology and privacy:** cites work to improve data privacy, rein in data brokers and reduce AI harms to consumers [S1]. Prime sponsor of the People's Privacy Act (HB 1671) and of HB 2483, a data-broker registry bill that did not pass in 2026 [S8]. Her site says AI "should serve people, not exploit them for profit" and that data-center benefits "must be weighed against the potential environmental harms" [S2].
- **Health care:** "Access to healthcare should never be tied to your employment status. It is a right, not a privilege," and calls for "getting rid of the middleman to bring costs down" [S2]; pamphlet pledges to increase access to behavioral health care [S1].
- **Public safety:** her site describes the district's regional crisis response system, which pairs mental-health professionals with police calls, and crisis treatment centers law enforcement can use "instead of jail or the emergency room" [S2].
- **Climate and environment:** supports reducing dependence on fossil fuels, renewable-energy investment and green jobs [S1][S2].
- **Education:** supports fully funding K-12 [S1]; would close tax loopholes to fund schools [S2].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no statement of her own found beyond a pledge to "protect civil rights" [S1]; endorsed by Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates and Washington Stonewall Democrats [S3][S13].
- **Parents and schools:** an original sponsor of HB 1296 (2025), which amended the I-2081 parental-rights list [S7]; voted for it (see Record) [S6].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted for ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S5], and defends it on her campaign site [S2].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** no public position on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. Record: original sponsor of HB 1296 and voted for ESHB 1296, the amendments I-1 would repeal [S6][S7].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- Voted yea on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million (2026-03-09, passed 51-46) [S5].
- Original sponsor of HB 1296 [S7]; voted yea on House final passage of ESHB 1296 as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24, passed 59-39) [S6]. The final bill report says the bill made "additions, modifications, and eliminations" to the I-2081 list of parent and guardian rights and that those rights create no private right of action [S7].
- 2026 session: HB 2152 ("Ryan's Law"), allowing terminally ill patients in hospitals, nursing homes and hospice to use medically authorized cannabis, passed [S8].
- Passed 2019 legislation creating a voluntary gambling self-exclusion program, per her caucus bio [S10].
- No floor votes since July: the 2026 regular session adjourned sine die on 2026-03-12 and no special session is listed [S9].
- PDC (data updated 2026-10-06): $68,483.94 in contributions and $43,690.01 in expenditures for the 2026 campaign [S4].
