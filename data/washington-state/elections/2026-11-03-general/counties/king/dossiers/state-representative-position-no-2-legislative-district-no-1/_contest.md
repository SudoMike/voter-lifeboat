## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House, for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets: K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules for cities, health and behavioral-health funding, and criminal-justice statutes. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the two candidates who advanced from the August primary.

## District and race dynamics
The 1st Legislative District covers parts of south Snohomish County and north King County, including Brier, Bothell, Kenmore, Woodinville and Lake Forest Park (shelley-kloba.md S14). Four candidates ran in the primary. Democratic incumbent Shelley Kloba, in the House since 2017, led on primary night with 48.5%; Republican Cliff Moon edged Democrat Jenne Alderks for the second slot with 23.1%; Libertarian Jeff Lyon had withdrawn but remained on the ballot (shelley-kloba.md S14; cliff-moon.md S5). The intra-party Kloba-Alderks contest that defined the primary is over, and Kloba's campaign now lists the 1st Legislative District Democrats among her endorsers (shelley-kloba.md S3).

The general is a conventional D-vs-R contrast. Kloba runs on her technology and privacy record, defends the 2026 Millionaires Tax and was an original sponsor of HB 1296, the 2025 rewrite of the I-2081 parental-rights list that I-1 would repeal (shelley-kloba.md S2, S5, S7). Moon, a construction-business owner, runs on property-tax relief, police funding, spending audits, curriculum transparency and opposition to state land-use mandates (cliff-moon.md S1, S2). Kloba reports $68.5k raised; Moon reports no contributions (shelley-kloba.md S4; cliff-moon.md S3). Neither has stated a position on the three statewide initiatives.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Kloba defends the Millionaires Tax and capital gains tax as correcting an "upside down" tax code; Moon wants property-tax caps and lower B&O taxes and resists raising taxes.
2. **Record vs. renewal:** nine-year incumbent vs. first-time candidate.
3. **Public safety:** Kloba emphasizes crisis-response teams and treatment centers as alternatives to jail; Moon emphasizes fully funding police, with crisis response as "a complement to — not replacement for" police.
4. **Local control:** Moon opposes "one-size-fits-all mandates from Olympia" on housing; Kloba supports state-level action.
5. **Parents and schools:** Kloba sponsored ESHB 1296; Moon supports "transparency in curriculum" and says parents should raise their children.
6. **Tech:** Kloba's record centers AI and data-privacy regulation; Moon has no stated tech position.
