## Background

- Republican-preference candidate; no prior elected office [S1].
- Small business owner and restaurateur in Leavenworth; the Herald reports he is 37, owns three restaurants there and lives in Cashmere [S1][S5]. He told Seattle Red he opened his first Leavenworth restaurant in 2018 and employs about 100 people [S6].
- Completed coursework at Edmonds Community College [S1]. Husband and father of four; his site says faith "is central" to his family [S3].

## Positions

**Taxes and small business**
- Platform: tax incentives for small businesses that invest and hire; "Simplify the Tax Code" and "eliminate special-interest loopholes"; a competitive tax and regulatory environment to bring production home; more flexibility on quarterly estimated taxes for struggling small businesses [S4].
- Told the Herald he would seek a seat on the House Small Business Committee [S5].

**Spending and government**
- Platform: "enforceable restraints on deficit growth and a serious commitment to reducing waste, duplication, and unnecessary federal spending"; plain-English, searchable federal spending data [S4].
- Platform: supports congressional term limits, single-subject bills ("No more hiding controversial provisions inside thousand-page bills") and a ban on members trading individual stocks [S4].

**Housing and cost of living**
- Platform: reduce regulatory barriers to home building "while respecting state and local authority over land use"; first-time homebuyer tax relief; capital-gains changes to help seniors downsize; limit institutional investors' purchases of starter homes; a pilot for portable mortgages starting with VA borrowers [S4][S6].
- Told Seattle Red housing is "probably the number one issue" he hears across the district [S6].

**Energy, climate and public lands**
- Platform: "All-of-the-Above American Energy," including "hydropower, nuclear power, renewables, natural gas, and domestic oil production," to keep utility and transportation costs manageable; "Protect Hydropower" [S4].
- Told the Herald the country should invest in "cheaper energy alternatives," naming "innovation, solar, fusion" [S5].
- Platform and Herald interview: active forest management, thinning, prescribed burns and reopening forest roads to reduce wildfire risk [S4][S5].

**Immigration**
- Pamphlet: "keeping our border secure" [S1]. Told the Herald "We shouldn't allow illegal entry at all," while saying he is "100% open" to bipartisan reform of legal pathways [S5].

**Health care**
- Told the Herald the country should rely on the private sector; supports price transparency and making premiums tax-deductible [S5].
- Platform: real price transparency, more insurer and provider competition including interstate insurance reforms, support for independent practices against consolidation [S4].

**Technology and AI**
- Platform: "Government shouldn't try to stop innovation, but neither should America enter this transition without safeguards"; "strong federal privacy protections"; enforce antitrust law against dominant tech firms while avoiding barriers for startups; transparency and avenues to challenge consequential automated decisions; keep the U.S. the world leader in AI [S4].

**Public safety**
- Pamphlet lists "protecting our communities" as a priority [S1]; no specific policing proposals found.

**Gender and reproductive policy**
- No statement on abortion, gender-affirming care, sports eligibility or anti-discrimination law found.

**Parents and schools**
- Site: "Spencer supports parental rights with parents playing a meaningful role in education" [S3]. Platform: "Parents should have meaningful access to information about what their children are being taught," "consistent with applicable law and legitimate student protections," and curriculum under "state and local control" [S4].
- Told Seattle Red that Schrier's vote against the PROTECT Kids Act was the "cleanest rebuttal" to her moderate image, describing the bill as "preventing schools from socially transitioning kids ... without parental consent" [S6].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- **I-645 (income-tax repeal):** No public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **I-1 / IL26-001 (parental rights):** No public position on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. His general parental-rights statements are listed above [S3][S4].
- **I-638 / IL26-638 (girls' sports):** No public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- The Washington State Republican Party, which endorsed him, urges yes votes on all three; that is the party's position, not a statement by Meline [S8].

## Campaign finance

- FEC, 2026 cycle through July 15, 2026: $126,517 in receipts, of which $85,000 is a loan from the candidate and $41,517 individual contributions; $36,655 spent; $89,862 cash on hand [S9].

## Primary result

- Second with 15.67% districtwide in the Aug. 6 count (Schrier 52.93%), 619 votes ahead of Republican Trinh Ha [S7]. The state party described him as finishing second in a six-candidate primary [S8].
