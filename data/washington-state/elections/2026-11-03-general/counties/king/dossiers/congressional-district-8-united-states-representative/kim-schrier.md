## Background

- Incumbent U.S. Representative for Washington's 8th Congressional District since 2019, seeking a fifth term; lives in Sammamish [S1][S8].
- Issaquah pediatrician for 17 years before Congress; pediatric residency at Stanford (2000), M.D. from UC Davis (1997), B.A. in astrophysics from UC Berkeley (1991) [S1].
- Serves on the House Energy and Commerce Committee (subcommittees on Health; Energy; and Commerce, Manufacturing, and Trade) [S1].

## Positions

**Cost of living and taxes**
- Pamphlet: introduced legislation "to immediately suspend the gas tax through the end of the year" [S1]. Told the Herald she would also introduce a bill barring oil and gas companies from price gouging during an energy emergency [S8].
- Pamphlet: "my Republican colleagues slashed your healthcare to give a tax break to billionaires" [S1].
- Opposed the Albertsons-Kroger merger and says she is "taking on big business monopolies" [S1][S3].

**Health care**
- Pamphlet: says she passed bills to cap insulin costs, let Medicare negotiate prescription prices and cap seniors' out-of-pocket costs; wants to "crack down on insurance company abuses" [S1].
- Campaign site: undo Republican cuts to Medicaid (Apple Health) and restore the premium tax credits; "doctors and patients make healthcare decisions, not insurance companies or the government" [S3].
- Told the Herald she wants to fund more community health centers and school-based health clinics, and extend $35 insulin to everyone and do the same for inhalers and EpiPens [S8].

**Spending and district projects**
- Pamphlet: secured over $60 million in Community Project funding, including a school-based health clinic in Graham, veterans' housing in Ellensburg, Highway 2 work, food banks, wildfire resiliency, and body cameras and a rapid DNA system for King County law enforcement [S1].
- Campaign site supports universal free pre-K and expanded school meals (per the primary dossier's campaign-site source) [S2][S3].

**Housing**
- Pamphlet: introduced legislation to "create more affordable housing" [S1]; sponsored the Permanent Housing Affordability Act (H.R. 8127) [S2].

**Climate and energy**
- Campaign site: "Climate change is real and I know we must take immediate action"; will "fight efforts to reduce or eliminate clean air and clean water protections" [S7].
- Told the Herald she supports clean energy technology, "especially nuclear fusion," and partnering with the Forest Service on wildfire risk [S8].
- Her Hydropower Licensing Transparency Act, written with Rep. Russ Fulcher (R-Idaho), was signed into law in September 2026; it requires FERC to report annually to Congress on each pending hydropower license application [S9].
- Voted no on H.J.Res. 210 and H.J.Res. 213 (Sept. 15-16, 2026), resolutions disapproving EPA decisions on California's nonroad engine pollution standards for ships at berth and harbor craft; both passed [S15][S16].

**Immigration**
- Campaign site: secure the border; pursue comprehensive bipartisan reform "with a path to citizenship"; expand worker visas; codify DACA and the DREAM Act [S5].
- Told the Herald the administration is "terrorizing immigrant communities" and that a new system should respect "the laws, borders and the dignity and humanity of immigrants" [S8].

**Public safety**
- Campaign site: "I will always fight back against any efforts to defund the police"; supports body-worn cameras and mental-health professionals working with police; cosponsored the Invest to Protect Act for departments with fewer than 200 officers [S6].

**Technology and data**
- Campaign site says she "led the bill to protect your kids' data from Big Tech" [S3]. Told the Herald: "we need regulations to protect people and their data and how that data is used" [S8].

**Foreign policy and defense**
- Voted yes on H.R. 4795 (Sept. 3, 2026), which bars colleges receiving federal student aid from boycotting Israel; 33 Democrats voted yes and 167 no [S12][S13].
- Voted no on Massie Amendment No. 8 to the State/foreign-operations appropriations bill (failed 104-314) [S22].
- Voted no on final passage of the FY2027 NDAA (passed 216-212) [S14].

**Gender and reproductive policy**
- Campaign site: "As the only pro-choice woman doctor in all of Congress, I will always support a woman's right to choose"; says she voted twice for the Women's Health Protection Act to codify Roe v. Wade [S4].
- Voted no on H.R. 28, the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act (Jan. 14, 2025) [S17].

**Parents and schools**
- Voted no on H.R. 2616, the PROTECT Kids Act (May 20, 2026; passed 217-198), which would require parental consent before elementary and middle schools change a minor student's gender markers, pronouns or preferred name on school forms, or sex-based accommodations [S18][S19]. Her opponent cites this vote against her (see Meline dossier).

## Record

- Sponsored 104 bills since 2019 per congress.gov as of July 2026 [S2]; first standalone enactment found: the Hydropower Licensing Transparency Act (September 2026) [S9].
- Votes since July 2026 where she split from most House Democrats: yes on H.R. 6955, the Main Street Act, which eases bank regulations (passed 270-155; DelBene voted no) [S20]; yes on H.R. 4795 (above) [S12]. She voted "Present" on the Sept. 15 motion to table an impeachment resolution against President Trump [S21].
- Campaign finance (FEC, 2026 cycle through July 15, 2026): $3,016,894 in receipts, $1,329,765 in disbursements, $3,508,751 cash on hand; $2,035,067 from individuals and $900,550 from other political committees [S10].
- Primary (Aug. 4, 2026): 52.93% districtwide in the Aug. 6 count; Spencer Meline second with 15.67% [S11].

## Endorsements

- Pamphlet: Planned Parenthood Action Fund, Washington State Labor Council, League of Conservation Voters, and mayors including Mike Poirier (Wenatchee), Rich Elliott (Ellensburg), Dan Rankin (Darrington) and Nancy Backus (Auburn) [S1].
- Herald (July): also the Snohomish County Labor Council, Washington State Building and Construction Trades Council and Washington Farm Bureau [S8].

## Scoring notes

- Four-term incumbent; her opponent has never held office [S1].
- Health care is her signature issue: drug-price caps and Medicare negotiation within the existing system [S1][S3].
- Votes with the Republican majority on some bills (Main Street Act, H.R. 4795) and describes herself as a bipartisan legislator [S3][S12][S20].
- Combines climate rhetoric with a gas-tax suspension proposal and support for hydropower and nuclear fusion [S1][S7][S8][S9].
