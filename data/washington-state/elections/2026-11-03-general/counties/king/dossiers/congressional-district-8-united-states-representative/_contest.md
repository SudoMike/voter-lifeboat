## What the office does

A U.S. Representative is one of 435 voting members of the House, elected to a two-year term. The House writes and votes on federal law, originates tax bills, controls federal spending through appropriations, and oversees the executive branch; members also handle constituent casework and secure district project funding [S2]. Washington's 8th District covers all of Chelan and Kittitas counties and much of eastern King, Pierce and Snohomish counties outside the largest cities, and a small part of Douglas County [S3][S4].

## Race dynamics

Incumbent Kim Schrier (D), a pediatrician first elected in 2018, seeks a fifth term against Spencer Meline (R), a Leavenworth restaurant owner running for office for the first time [S1][S3]. In the primary Schrier had 52.93% districtwide and Meline 15.67%, edging fellow Republican Trinh Ha for the second general-election spot (Aug. 6 count) [S4]. Through mid-July Schrier reported $3.02 million in receipts and $3.51 million cash on hand; Meline reported $126,517, including an $85,000 loan from himself [S5][S6]. Meline's campaign stresses housing costs and small business. His main line of attack on Schrier is her vote against the federal PROTECT Kids Act [S7].

## Issue axes that genuinely differentiate these candidates

1. **Experience:** a four-term incumbent with committee work and a newly enacted bill, against a first-time candidate [S1].
2. **Spending and taxes:** Schrier highlights $60 million in district projects and wants Medicaid and ACA credits restored. Meline calls for enforceable deficit restraints, cuts to waste, and small-business and homebuyer tax relief (see candidate dossiers).
3. **Climate and energy:** Schrier says climate action is urgent and voted against rolling back California emission rules, while also backing hydropower and a gas-tax suspension. Meline backs "all-of-the-above" energy including oil and gas, framed around cost (see candidate dossiers).
4. **Immigration:** both say the border should be secure. Schrier supports a path to citizenship and codifying DACA; Meline says "We shouldn't allow illegal entry at all" but is open to reforming legal pathways (see candidate dossiers).
5. **Government reform:** Meline backs term limits, single-subject bills and a congressional stock-trading ban. Schrier emphasizes bipartisanship and takes PAC contributions (see candidate dossiers).

Parents' role in schools is a stated contrast (Meline's parental-rights platform vs. Schrier's PROTECT Kids Act vote), but `parental-rights` is not a Federal axis in this rubric. Neither candidate has a public position on I-645, I-1 or I-638 as of Oct. 8, 2026.
