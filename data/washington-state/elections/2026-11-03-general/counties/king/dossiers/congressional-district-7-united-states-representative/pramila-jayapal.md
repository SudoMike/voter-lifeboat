## Background

Pramila Jayapal is the incumbent U.S. Representative for Washington's 7th District, serving since 2017; she was a Washington State Senator from 2015 to 2017 [S1]. Before elected office she founded and directed OneAmerica and directed the PATH Fund for Technology Transfer [S1]. She holds a BA in English and Economics from Georgetown and an MBA from Northwestern [S1]. Her pamphlet lists the House Judiciary, Foreign Affairs and Budget committees; Ranking Member of the Immigration Subcommittee; Chair Emerita and executive board member of the Congressional Progressive Caucus; co-chair of the Monopoly Busters Caucus and of the Trans Equality Task Force; and membership in the Congressional Equality Caucus and the Pro-Choice Caucus [S1]. She is seeking re-election against Nirav Sheth (R) [S12].

## Positions

**Economy, taxes and spending**
- General-election pamphlet: says she leads legislation to make higher education tuition-free, guarantee housing as a human right, expand workers' rights, raise the federal minimum wage, and "establish an ultra-millionaire wealth tax" [S1]. Her primary-era record includes sponsoring the Ultra-Millionaire Tax Act of 2026 (H.R. 8085) and the Housing Is a Human Right Act of 2025 (H.R. 4457) [S2].
- Says she founded the Monopoly Busters Caucus "to fight consolidation, corporate greed, and price-gouging" [S1].

**Healthcare**
- "I'm leading the fight for the Medicare for All Act to ensure that universal healthcare is a right, not a privilege" [S1].

**Immigration**
- Pamphlet: says she is "implement[ing] humane immigration reform" and has been "on the frontlines of holding Trump accountable, including around the lawless ICE and CBP actions that have kidnapped and disappeared our neighbors" [S1].
- As Immigration Subcommittee ranking member she issued a Sept. 26, 2026 statement opposing the administration's pocket rescissions, which her office describes as targeted primarily at immigration services [S10]. Earlier sponsored bills include the ICE Out of Our Faces Act (H.R. 7363) and the Dignity for Detained Immigrants Act (H.R. 6397) [S2].

**Public safety**
- Sponsored the Housing not Handcuffs Act of 2025 (H.R. 4182) [S2]. In September 2026 she reintroduced a moratorium on government use of facial recognition and other biometric technology [S9].

**Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy**
- Pamphlet: says she has worked to "protect abortion rights"; lists co-chairing the Trans Equality Task Force and membership in the Pro-Choice Caucus and Congressional Equality Caucus [S1]. She sponsored a resolution supporting a Transgender Bill of Rights (H.Res. 1058) [S2]. Planned Parenthood endorses her [S1].

**Parents and schools**
- No statement found on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs.

**Technology and surveillance**
- On Oct. 1, 2026 she released a "National AI Charter Act" framework that would require every AI company to hold a federal charter, with pre-release government testing, a "government-controlled kill switch," strict liability for companies and executives, and separation of AI companies from Big Tech platforms [S8].
- In September 2026 she reintroduced legislation to halt government use of facial recognition and other biometric technology [S9]. Pamphlet: leads bipartisan bills to "protect Americans from government spying" and to ban stock trading by members of Congress [S1].

**Foreign policy and defense**
- Pamphlet: "I have authored a War Powers Resolution to end Trump's illegal war in Iran" [S1]. That resolution, H.Con.Res. 89, passed the House 214-208 on July 23, 2026 [S6]; her office says the Senate blocked it 49-50 [S7].

**Climate**
- Pamphlet says she has worked to "fight climate change" [S1]; the dossier records no specific 2026 climate bill.

**Campaign finance and reform**
- "I have never taken corporate PAC money"; says she is "doing everything possible to get money out of politics and reform the Supreme Court" [S1].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- **I-645 (IP26-645, income-tax repeal):** Seattle Red quotes a Sept. 26, 2026 post by Jayapal: "Now, millionaires and billionaires are trying to pass I-645, a ballot initiative that would give them a $300 billion tax break by cutting social programs in K-12 schools. Make it make sense." The column disputes the $300 billion figure [S13]. Read as opposition to I-645; the underlying post was not reviewed directly. The No on 645 campaign lists her ("U.S. Representative, CD 7") among its elected leaders [S14].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** Listed as "Member of Congress, Chair Congressional Progressive Caucus" on the NO HATE in WA State page of elected officials and candidates opposing I-001 and I-638 [S15]. No statement of her own found as of 2026-10-08.
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' sports):** Listed as opposing I-638 on the same NO HATE in WA State page [S15]. No statement of her own found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record

House votes since the primary research (July 2026 onward), from House Clerk roll calls:
- Voted Aye on Massie Amendment No. 8 to the FY2027 National Security and State appropriations bill (H.R. 8595), July 15, 2026; the amendment failed 104-314 [S4]. (Rep. Smith's office describes this amendment as stopping $3.3 billion in military assistance to Israel; see the CD 9 dossier. Jayapal's own statement on it was not found.)
- Voted Nay on passage of the FY2027 National Defense Authorization Act (H.R. 8800), which passed 216-212 on July 22, 2026 [S5].
- Voted Yea on her own Iran War Powers resolution (H.Con.Res. 89), which passed 214-208 on July 23, 2026 [S6][S7].
- Earlier, a Daily UW column noted she voted for Iron Dome funding despite a generally pro-Palestinian stance [S2].
- Constituent-service claims in the pamphlet: over 130 town halls, over $25 million in federal benefits recovered, over $70 million in federal investments (campaign statement, not independently verified) [S1].
