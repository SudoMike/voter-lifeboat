## Background
Sharon Tomiko Santos (Prefers Democratic Party) has represented the 37th District in the House since 1999 and chairs the House Education Committee; she also sits on the Finance and Consumer Protection & Business committees [S1][S2][S5]. She is the state's longest-serving House member [S5]. Her background is in nonprofit governance and management (she created three nonprofits) and local-government policy; she is a Franklin High School graduate with a BA from The Evergreen State College and an MA from Northeastern University [S1]. Her community service includes founding board roles at Hate Free Zone (OneAmerica) and KUOW and founding membership in the Washington Tax Fairness Coalition and an NCSL Task Force on Immigration and the States [S1]. In the August primary she finished second with 47.9% (18,517 votes) to Kelabe Tewolde's 51.5% (19,902) [S12].

## Positions

### Cost of living and taxes
- Pamphlet: focused on lowering the cost of living "while protecting the essential services upon which we all rely" and "ensuring tax fairness for working families" [S1].

### Housing
- Pamphlet and site: increase housing supply, expand affordable housing near transit, prevent displacement [S1][S3].
- Defends her 2025 vote against the rent-increase cap as protecting smaller landlords struggling with rising costs; preferred "a fine-edged scalpel" approach and said rent control "has not stemmed the cost of housing" [S5][S6][S7].
- Told the International Examiner the real cost drivers are tech salaries and land-cost inflation under the Growth Management Act [S7].

### Education
- Says Seattle schools are not failing and advocates "competency-based education" [S7].
- Opposes a universal school cellphone ban, preferring that local school boards decide [S5].

### Public safety
- Site: community-led violence prevention and behavioral-health response services [S3].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No statement found in her pamphlet, site or coverage [S1][S3][S5][S7].

### Parents and schools
- As Education Committee chair she signed the majority "do pass" report on substitute HB 1296 (2025), which rewrote the I-2081 parents' rights list (for example replacing the right to examine curriculum with classroom-observation access, lengthening the records-access window from 10 to 45 days, and eliminating prior notification of offered medical services) and added a statement of student rights [S11].
- Voted yes on ESHB 1296's House final passage [S10].

## Record
- Voted no on EHB 1217, the 2025 residential rent-increase limit, on House final passage; she was the only Seattle-area legislator to do so [S6][S7][S8].
- Voted yes on ESSB 6346, the 2026 millionaires' tax, on House final passage [S5][S9].
- Chaired the committee that reported HB 1296 and voted for it on the floor [S10][S11].
- No floor votes since July: the 2026 regular session ran January 12 to March 12, 2026, and the Legislature's session-documents page lists no 2026 special session [S14].

## Endorsements
Her site lists Speaker Laurie Jinkins, Rep. Chipalo Street, former Gov. Gary Locke, former Seattle Mayor Bruce Harrell, former Superintendent Randy Dorn, King County Councilmember Teresa Mosqueda, Seattle Councilmembers Debora Juarez and Joy Hollingsworth, several Renton councilmembers, and labor and advocacy organizations including AFSCME, ILWU, Moms Demand Action and the Alliance for Gun Responsibility [S4]. The Seattle Times editorial board endorsed her opponent and criticized her for a lack of vision on education [S6].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (income tax repeal):** No public statement on I-645 found (campaign site, pamphlet, news search) [S1][S3][S5]. She voted for ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S9].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** No public statement on I-1 found [S1][S3][S5]. She chaired the committee that advanced ESHB 1296, the 2025 law I-1 would undo, and voted for it [S10][S11].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** No public position found (campaign site, pamphlet, news search) [S1][S3][S5].

## Campaign finance
PDC data as of 2026-10-06: $178,505.14 in contributions, $38,623.38 carried forward, $89,329.42 in expenditures [S13]. The Washington State Standard reported her campaign was defrauded of $20,000 in 2025 [S5].

- Prefers local school-board control over a statewide cellphone ban [S5].
