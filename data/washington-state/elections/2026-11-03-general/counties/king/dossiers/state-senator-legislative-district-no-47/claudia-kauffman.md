## Background
Claudia Kauffman (Prefers Democratic Party) is the incumbent State Senator for the 47th Legislative District, serving since 2022 [S1]. She previously held the same Senate seat from 2007 to 2011 and describes herself as the first Native American woman elected to the Washington State Senate [S2]. Her professional experience includes serving as Intergovernmental Affairs Liaison for the Muckleshoot Indian Tribe and co-founding Native Action Network [S1]. She holds an associate's degree from Northwest Indian College and a certificate in project management from the UW School of Business, and formerly chaired the Green River College Board of Trustees [S1]. At the primary research date she chaired the Senate Business, Trade & Economic Development Committee, was vice chair of State Government, Tribal Affairs & Elections, and sat on Ways & Means [S2].

In the August 4 primary's election-night returns she received 58.72% to Kristina Soltys's 41.10%; both advance [S8].

## Positions

### Taxes and spending
- Her platform says Washington "ranks 50 out of 50" on tax fairness and that "those in the bottom 20% of the wealth spectrum pay SEVEN times more of their income in taxes than billionaires"; it proposes reducing property and sales taxes, reforming the B&O tax, and "adopting better, more fair options" [S4].
- Her general-election pamphlet statement says she has worked for property tax relief for seniors and expanded childcare access, and that the social safety net must be "robust enough to catch those falling behind" [S1].

### Housing
- Says the state must tackle the housing crisis "by increasing supply and protecting renters from predatory price gouging," and that she will keep pushing for middle-income housing [S1].

### Other stated priorities
- Her platform lists public safety, early learning, affordable housing, the environment ("a plan that will help our environment for generations to come"), and reviewing policies and budgets for their impact on a diverse community [S4].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No statement found in the pamphlet or on the campaign platform page [S1][S4].

### Parents and schools
- No campaign statement found [S1][S4]. Her record includes a vote on ESHB 1296 (see Record).

## Record
- Voted yes on ESSB 6346 (2026), the tax on household income over $1 million that I-645 would repeal: Senate third reading 2026-02-16 and final passage as amended by the House 2026-03-11 (27-21) [S5].
- Voted yes on ESHB 1296 (2025), which amended the parental-rights law enacted by I-2081 and which I-1 would undo: Senate third reading and final passage as amended, 2025-04-11 (30-19) [S6].
- 2026 session items she cited to the Auburn Reporter: over $41 million in capital projects for flood damage, more than $2 million for the Signature Pointe Levee in Kent, landlord flood-zone disclosure rules, a $7,500 property tax deduction for low-income, disabled and retired military members, $15 million in crosswalk safety grants, and Covington Aquatic Center repairs [S9].
- Campaign-cited bills from the primary dossier (self-reported, not re-verified): SB 5683 (state-Tribal foster care coordination), SB 5811 (caregiver eligibility), SB 5342 (transit bus purchasing), SB 5494 (lead-based paint removal) [S2].
- No floor votes since July: the 2026 regular session adjourned sine die on March 12 [S10].

## Endorsements

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, income tax repeal):** No public statement on I-645 found on her campaign site or in the pamphlet [S1][S3][S4]. She voted yes on ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S5].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** No public statement found [S1][S4]. She voted yes on ESHB 1296, the 2025 amendments I-1 would undo [S6].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** No public position found [S1][S4].

- Incumbent with two Senate stints and committee leadership, against a city councilmember [S1][S2].
- Voted for the 2026 millionaires' tax and writes that low-income residents pay a far larger share of income in taxes than billionaires; her opponent opposes any income tax [S4][S5].
- Pairs housing supply with renter protections; her opponent favors permitting reform and opposes state mandates [S1].
- Voted for ESHB 1296; she has made no campaign statement on parental rights or the initiatives [S6].
