## Background
Kristina Soltys (Prefers Republican Party) has served on the Covington City Council since 2019, when she defeated a fourteen-year incumbent [S1]. She serves on the Sound Cities Association Public Issues Committee and the South King Housing and Homelessness Partners Executive Board [S1]. She came to the United States from Ukraine at age five [S1]. She has worked as a diagnostic medical sonographer, loan originator, and choir and piano teacher, and is a mother of three; she holds an AA from Bellevue College and RDMS certification [S1]. Her community service includes the Covington Chamber of Commerce, the Covington Storehouse Food Bank, organizing Ukraine medical supply relief, and PTA service [S1]. She was re-elected to the council unopposed in 2023 [S2].

In the August 4 primary's election-night returns she received 41.10% to incumbent Claudia Kauffman's 58.72%; both advance [S7].

## Positions

### Taxes and spending
- Opposes a state income tax: "Washington voters have rejected an income tax ten times," and voters deserve a say on taxation [S4].
- "Olympia does not have a revenue problem. It has a spending problem"; she opposes the cumulative effect of recent tax increases, including B&O and gas taxes [S3][S4].
- Calls the B&O tax "one of the most burdensome in the country" and wants it reformed [S4].
- In a July 31 radio interview she said "people don't mind paying taxes, but what they want to see is results" [S5].

### Housing
- "Local communities know their neighborhoods. State government should empower them"; she opposes top-down state mandates and favors permitting reform and market incentives [S3][S4].

### Public safety and homelessness
- Pairs law enforcement funding and more officers with mental health and addiction treatment resources [S3][S4].
- Her pamphlet record on the Covington council: fully funding police, including an additional officer; a community resource officer program for residents facing homelessness or mental health crises; the "Clean Team" public-spaces program [S1].
- On homelessness: "We need to help them for real and not just take them off the streets" [S5].

### Transportation
- Wants state funding for the WSDOT-designed SR-516 roundabout improvements and less congestion [S1][S4].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No statement found in the pamphlet, on the campaign site, or in news coverage reviewed [S1][S3][S4][S5].

### Parents and schools
- No statement found beyond her past PTA and COVID-era school volunteering [S1][S3][S4].

## Record
- City-level only (Covington City Council, 2019 to present); items above are self-reported [S1]. Her campaign's claim that crime in Covington fell 24% during her tenure was not independently verified [S2].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, income tax repeal):** In the July 31 interview, discussing the income tax repeal, she said "it is easily a 99.5% rejection of it" among people she had talked to, and that because voters have rejected an income tax "10 times... it doesn't belong here" [S5]. That is opposition to an income tax; she did not state in that piece, or on her site, how she will vote on I-645 [S3][S4][S5].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** No public position found [S1][S3][S4].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** No public position found [S1][S3][S4].
