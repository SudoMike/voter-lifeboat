# State Senator, Legislative District 46 — contest overview

- depth: light

## What the office does

A Washington State Senator is one of 49 members of the state Senate, serving a four-year term. Senators write and vote on state laws and adopt the operating, capital and transportation budgets, setting statewide policy on taxes, housing, public safety, education, healthcare and elections. The 46th District covers northeast Seattle; all of its primary votes were cast in King County (see the candidate dossiers' results sources).

## Race dynamics

- The same two candidates as in the primary. Incumbent Democrat Javier Valdez, Senate State Government, Tribal Affairs and Elections chair, won about 90.6% of the primary vote; Republican Sandra Stephens, a retired nurse running her first campaign, won about 9.2% (see javier-valdez.md, sandra-stephens.md).
- Evidence is asymmetric: Valdez has a documented legislative record (SB 5855 mask ban, yes votes on ESSB 6346 and ESHB 1296), while Stephens's file is her pamphlet statement plus campaign-finance and results records. Stephens reports about $1,000 raised; Valdez about $133,000.

## Differentiating issue axes

- **Taxes:** Valdez voted for ESSB 6346, the millionaires' tax that I-645 would repeal, and campaigns on it; Stephens blames rising taxes on corruption and unaudited nonprofits without a specific tax proposal.
- **Social (reproductive policy):** Valdez cites backfilling Planned Parenthood funding; Stephens states belief in "the sacredness of life" and was an officer of a pro-life nurses' association.
- **Immigration:** Valdez sponsored the law-enforcement mask ban aimed at ICE; Stephens does not address immigration.
- **Experience:** incumbent committee chair since 2017 in the Legislature versus a first-time candidate.
- **Parental rights:** only Valdez has evidence (his yes vote on ESHB 1296); Stephens has no stated position. Neither candidate has stated a position on I-1 or I-638.
