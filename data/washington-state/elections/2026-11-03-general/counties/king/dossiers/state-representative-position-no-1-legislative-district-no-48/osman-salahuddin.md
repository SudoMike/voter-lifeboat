## Background

Osman Salahuddin (Prefers Democratic Party) is the incumbent State Representative for the 48th LD, Position 1, and a former Redmond City Councilmember [S1]. He was appointed in January 2025 to replace Vandana Slatter after her move to the Senate [S2], and won the November 2025 election for the seat with 70.08% against Republican Dennis Ellis [S6]. He is a Redmond native and son of immigrants, with a BS in Neurobiology from the University of Washington, where he was student body president [S1][S3]. Prior work includes cancer research at Fred Hutchinson, medical device consulting, community engagement for King County, and owning a coffee shop [S1]. Board service includes Hopelink, Transportation Choices Coalition and the OneRedmond Foundation [S1].

His general-election pamphlet statement is the same text he filed for the primary [S1][S2].

## Positions

**Listed priorities**
- Housing and transit infrastructure ("We simply need more housing"), education and workforce development, healthcare and behavioral health ("Healthcare is a human right, and I will fight for access"), public safety and gun violence prevention, environment and climate, small business and workers [S3].
- Pamphlet emphasis: public safety, healthcare access, strong schools, "common-sense solutions and economic growth" [S1].

**Public safety**
- Says a safe community means "a comprehensive community response that aligns with that of our first responders" and "prioritizing prevention" [S3].

**Climate**
- Calls for "a just transition to clean energy while we prioritize environmental justice in our reduction of carbon emissions" [S3].

**Budget and revenue**
- His 2025 session recap says the Legislature closed a projected shortfall with "responsible spending adjustments and equitable revenue measures to protect working families" [S4].

**Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy**
- No campaign statement found; Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates endorses him [S1][S5].

**Parents and schools**
- No campaign statement on parental notification, records access or opt-outs found [S1][S3]. His vote on ESHB 1296 is listed under Record.

## Record

- Says he "passed more laws than any legislator this year," citing bills on affordable housing, gun violence prevention and immigrant protections (candidate's characterization) [S1].
- 2025 bills he lists as passed: HB 1484 (pregnancy resulting from rape as an aggravating sentencing factor), HB 1875 (paid sick leave usable for immigration proceedings), HB 1811 (behavioral-health "co-response" teams, which his site says aim "to reduce reliance on law enforcement and emergency rooms"), HB 1486 (student member on the community and technical college board), and companion bills on recognizing Eid holidays and extending a workforce training program [S4].
- Voted yes on ESHB 1296 (2025), the bill amending the parents' rights law enacted by I-2081, on House passage (2025-03-12) and final passage as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24) [S7].
- Voted yes on ESSB 6346 (2026), the 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million, on House final passage (2026-03-09) [S8].
- No floor votes since July: the Legislature has not been in session since the 2026 regular session adjourned sine die on March 12, 2026 [S9].

## Endorsements

- Pamphlet: Congresswoman DelBene, Governor Ferguson, County Councilmember Balducci, the mayors of Redmond, Bellevue, Kirkland and Medina, teachers, nurses, Conservation Action, State Labor Council, 48th Democrats, Planned Parenthood Alliance [S1].
- Site lists organizations including the Washington State Building & Construction Trades, LiUNA 242, Washington State Nurses Association, Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates and One America Votes; a separate "2025 Endorsements" list includes the Master Builders Affordable Housing Council, Association of Washington Business, Seattle King County Realtors, Washington Housing Alliance Action Fund, Alliance for Gun Responsibility, Fuse WA, The Stranger and The Seattle Times [S5].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- **I-645 (IP26-645, income tax repeal):** No public statement on I-645 found on his site, in the pamphlet or in news searches [S1][S3]. He voted yes on ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S8].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights in schools):** No public statement found [S1][S3]. He voted yes on ESHB 1296, the 2025 amendments I-1 would undo [S7].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports eligibility):** No public position found [S1][S3].
