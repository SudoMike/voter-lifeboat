## Background
Nicole Macri (Prefers Democratic Party) has been the State Representative for LD 43, Position 1, since 2017 [S1]. She is Deputy Director at DESC (Downtown Emergency Service Center), helping oversee a $150 million budget for supportive housing, integrated healthcare and social services for people who have experienced homelessness [S1]. She is a past president of the Washington Low Income Housing Alliance [S1]. Education: MPA, UW Evans School; BA in sociology, Rutgers [S1]. Her campaign site says she is vice chair of the House Appropriations Committee [S3].

## Positions
- **Statewide initiatives:** "I'm voting no on all statewide initiatives and hope our community unites against these harmful measures" [S1].
- **Housing:** "championed landmark laws to prevent rent gouging" [S1]; site: "Creating stability and fairness for renters, funding supportive housing and expanding affordable options" [S3].
- **Taxes:** "enact historic tax fairness to fund schools and childcare, lower costs for working families and seniors, and cut small business taxes" [S1].
- **Climate:** "Aggressively tackling climate change by modernizing our energy grid, cutting building emissions" [S3].
- **Healthcare:** making "housing and healthcare affordable" [S1]; site: access to "mental health support, substance use resources, and life-saving care" [S3].
- **Public safety:** cites work "to reduce gun violence, address the fentanyl crisis" [S1]; site: "common-sense gun safety laws and community-led violence intervention" [S3].
- **Immigration:** "protect immigrant and LGBTQ+ neighbors from hateful federal overreach" [S1].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** "led the fight to protect abortion access" [S1]. Her site commits to "Guaranteeing that Washington remains a safe haven for reproductive freedom by expanding, protecting, and fully funding access to abortion" and "Standing unapologetically with queer and trans people, ensuring Washington is a sanctuary state for gender-affirming care" [S3]. The Stranger noted her work on "trans healthcare policy" [S4].
- **Parents and schools:** no specific statement found. Her no-on-all-initiatives statement covers I-1 [S1], and she voted for ESHB 1296 (see Record) [S6].

## Record
- **ESSB 6346 (2026 tax on household income over $1 million, which I-645 would repeal):** voted yea on House final passage, 2026-03-09 (51-46) [S7].
- **ESHB 1296 (2025 amendments to the parents' rights law that I-1 would undo):** voted yea on House third reading (2025-03-12) and final passage as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24, 59-39) [S6].
- **EHB 1217 (2025 rent stabilization):** voted yea on House conference-report final passage, 2025-04-27 [S8]. Primary coverage described her as the lead campaigner for the bill [S2].
- **ESHB 2266 (2026):** listed among the original sponsors (Peterson, Macri, ...) [S10]; voted yea on House passage and final passage [S9]. The law requires cities to allow transitional and permanent supportive housing and indoor emergency shelters in specified urban zones [S10].
- No floor votes since July: the 2026 session "adjourned sine die on Thursday March 12, 2026" [S11], and no special session has been found.

## Endorsements
- The Stranger endorsed her in the primary as "an intelligent, competent legislator with a proven track record of progressive policymaking" [S4]. Her site displays a Stranger endorsement graphic [S3].
- No other endorsement list was found on her site [S3].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645):** opposes. Her pamphlet statement says she is "voting no on all statewide initiatives" [S1]. She voted yea on ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S7].
- **I-1 (IL26-001):** opposes, by the same statement [S1]. She voted yea on ESHB 1296 [S6].
- **I-638 (IL26-638):** opposes, by the same statement [S1].

- Explicit positions on abortion access and gender-affirming care [S3].
