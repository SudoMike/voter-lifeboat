## Background

Bardi D. Martin is the incumbent King County District Court judge for West Electoral District Position 1 [S1][S5]. The Metropolitan King County Council appointed him by Motion 16944, introduced Jan. 20, 2026 and passed Jan. 27, 2026 on a 9–0 vote, to fill the vacancy left by Judge Lisa Paglisotti's departure [S2][S3]. Under state law, the appointee serves the rest of Paglisotti's term, which ends in December 2026 [S3]. Two eligible applicants went through the council's merit-selection process: Martin and Andrew Repanich [S3]. The court roster lists him at the Shoreline courthouse [S6].

His court bio says he served two years as a judge pro tempore before his appointment, presiding over more than 200 calendars at all eight District Court locations. Those calendars covered protection orders, infractions, impound hearings, name changes, small claims and criminal arraignment, pretrial, motion, disposition, review and in-custody calendars [S5]. The council staff report dates his pro tem service from 2023 [S3]. His bio describes 18 years in private practice as a criminal defense attorney, criminal and civil appellate attorney, civil litigator and public defender [S5]. The staff report says he spent three years as a public defender at several King County firms and was managing member of Boyle Martin Thoeny, PLLC, a civil and criminal litigation practice [S3]. He holds a JD from Seattle University School of Law and a BA from the University of Washington [S1][S5]. His statement says he was adopted at birth, has Puerto Rican heritage and was a professional musician before law [S1].

## Positions

Judicial approach:
- His court bio says every person in court should be treated "with dignity, patience, and respect" and that justice should be "delivered fairly, equitably, and compassionately" [S5].
- His campaign stresses "fairness, transparency, and accountability" and says "safe communities require courts that work, courts that listen, and courts that people can trust" [S8].

Criminal-justice formation and bail/diversion:
- Formation is defense-side: he started as a public defender, then moved to private criminal-defense and civil practice [S1][S3][S5].
- Therapeutic court: his statement says he presides over Shoreline and Kenmore's Community Court, which connects people charged with low-level offenses to housing, mental-health and substance-abuse resources, "promoting accountability while reducing repeat offenses" [S1]. The District Court's community-courts page confirms that Shoreline/Kenmore Community Court exists, but unlike the Bellevue and Redmond entries it does not name the presiding judge. His assignment to the Shoreline courthouse is consistent with his claim [S6][S7]. That claim is partly corroborated.
- No statement on bail or pretrial release was found in his pamphlet statement, court bio or campaign site [S1][S5][S8].

## Record

- Sitting District Court judge since his January 2026 council appointment. He presides over civil and criminal matters [S1][S2][S5]. No published rulings or news coverage of his rulings were found. District Court decisions are generally not published.
- Bar ratings at appointment, from a council record attachment dated Oct. 24, 2025: King County Bar Association "Well Qualified," QLaw "Well Qualified" and the Joint Asian Judicial Evaluation Committee "Qualified." The table lists no Latina/o Bar, WWL, Cardozo or Loren Miller rating for him [S4]. KCBA ratings are valid for three years [S2]. KCBA's 2026 election ratings page (last updated Aug. 4, 2026) does not list this race [S12], and WWL's ratings page does not list him [S13]. His pamphlet statement and campaign site cite no bar ratings [S1][S8].

## Endorsements

- King County Democrats (confirmed on the organization's 2026 list) [S10].
- The Stranger endorsed Martin in its July 2026 endorsements issue. It contrasted his public-defense background with his opponent's prosecution background [S11].
- His campaign site lists about 54 judges and commissioners, including District Court Chief Presiding Judge Rebecca Robertson [S9]. His pamphlet statement says "over 50 judges" [S1].
- His site groups the 32nd, 36th and 43rd Legislative District Democrats under the King County Democrats [S9]. His opponent's statement also claims the 32nd and 36th District Democrats [S1]. These district-level claims were not independently checked.

## Campaign finance

- PDC summary (updated Oct. 5, 2026): $14,935.96 raised, $6,386.75 spent, no debt [S14]. That includes $5,260.96 in in-kind contributions from the candidate: the King County Elections filing fee ($2,260.96) and campaign consulting ($3,000) [S15]. His opponent has raised more than three times as much [S14].

## Scoring notes

- Incumbent by appointment: unanimously appointed by the King County Council in January 2026 after merit-selection review. He has about nine months on the bench, preceded by about two years as a pro tem [S2][S3][S5].
- Formation: public defender, then private criminal-defense and civil practice. His opponent spent six years as a Seattle city prosecutor [S3][S5].
- Therapeutic court: self-reports presiding over the Shoreline/Kenmore Community Court, which is consistent with his Shoreline assignment. The court's own page does not name the judge [S1][S6][S7]. No documented bail posture.
- Bar ratings on the public record (KCBA WQ, QLaw WQ, JAJEC Q, from the appointment file) are lower than the ratings his opponent self-reports. Neither candidate has a current KCBA rating on KCBA's 2026 election page [S4][S12].
