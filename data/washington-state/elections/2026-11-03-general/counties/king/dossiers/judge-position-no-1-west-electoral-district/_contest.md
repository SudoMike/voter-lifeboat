## What the office does

King County District Court judges hear misdemeanor criminal cases, small claims, traffic cases and protection orders. They are elected by voters in their electoral district to four-year terms, with a 2026 salary of $226,096 [S1]. The court handles about 250,000 filings a year across four divisions [S3]. It also runs community courts, including the Shoreline/Kenmore Community Court, that link people charged with low-level offenses to services [S1][S14]. In practice, a judge's discretion here centers on pretrial release, protection orders and therapeutic-court supervision.

## Race dynamics

- Incumbent by appointment. The King County Council appointed Bardi Martin 9–0 on Jan. 27, 2026 (Motion 16944) to replace Judge Lisa Paglisotti, who had departed. The appointment runs through the December 2026 end of her term, so the seat is on the November ballot [S2][S3]. Two applicants were considered, Martin and Andrew Repanich. Rose-Akins was not one of them [S3].
- No primary. Two candidates filed [S1], and the August 2026 primary results list Northeast Position 1 as the only District Court race on that ballot, so this contest goes straight to the general [S15].
- Professional split: Martin was a public defender and later a private criminal-defense and civil lawyer, with pro tem service from 2023 [S3][S5]. Rose-Akins was a Seattle city prosecutor (2010–2016) and civil city attorney (2016–2023) and is now a pro tem judge and commissioner [S6]. The Stranger put it this way: "He was a public defender, she was a prosecutor" [S9].
- Endorsements: the King County Democrats and The Stranger back Martin [S9][S10]. Rose-Akins lists five legislative-district Democratic organizations, three Supreme Court justices, the Attorney General and former Mayor Bruce Harrell [S1]. Martin's site lists the 32nd, 36th and 43rd District Democrats, and Rose-Akins's statement also claims the 32nd and 36th. Neither claim was independently checked [S1][S13].
- Bar ratings: KCBA's 2026 election page lists no ratings for this race [S7]. The council's appointment file shows Martin rated KCBA "Well Qualified," QLaw "Well Qualified" and JAJEC "Qualified" [S4]. Rose-Akins self-reports six "Exceptionally Well-Qualified" ratings, including KCBA [S1][S6]. WWL, the only independent rating found for her, rated her "Well Qualified" [S8].
- Fundraising (PDC, Oct. 5, 2026): Rose-Akins about $50,200 raised and $23,900 spent; Martin about $14,900 raised and $6,400 spent [S11][S12].

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

- **Professional formation (`safety`).** Martin comes from defense: public defender, then a private criminal-defense practice [S3][S5]. Rose-Akins comes from prosecution: six years as a Seattle misdemeanor prosecutor, then a civil city attorney advising SPD [S6]. Neither has a documented bail position.
- **Therapeutic courts (`safety`).** Martin says he presides over the Shoreline/Kenmore Community Court. That is consistent with his Shoreline assignment but not named on the court's page [S1][S14]. Rose-Akins reports working with Seattle Mental Health Court and Community Court participants as a prosecutor [S6].
- **Experience (`experience`).** Martin is a sitting judge, about nine months on the bench after about two years pro tem, selected through the council's merit-selection process [S2][S3][S5]. Rose-Akins has pro tem judge and commissioner service (self-reported, 2+ years) and 13 years in the Seattle City Attorney's Office [S6].
- **Judicial approach (`judicial`).** Both statements are general. Martin stresses "steady judgment" and dignity [S1][S5]. Rose-Akins's motto is "listen first, decide last" [S1]. No differentiating philosophy beyond that was found.

## Not differentiating

- Both emphasize dignity, fairness and public safety [S1].
- Party endorsements are recorded as facts only. This is a nonpartisan judicial office, so they are not evidence of judicial philosophy.
