## Background

Nyjat Rose-Akins is a King County court pro tem judge and a former assistant city attorney [S1]. Her campaign site identifies the employer as the Seattle City Attorney's Office. She was an Assistant City Prosecutor in its Criminal Division from 2010 to 2016, then a Precinct Liaison Attorney-Supervisor in its Civil Division from 2016 to 2023 [S2]. In the civil role she says she advised the Seattle Police Department, the Mayor's Office and other departments on police accountability, homelessness, nuisance properties and liquor and marijuana licensing [S1][S2]. During law school she was a Rule 9 intern at the Pierce County Prosecuting Attorney's Office [S2]. She says she has served for more than two years as a pro tem judge in King County and currently serves as a Court Commissioner pro tem. The site does not name the specific court [S2]. She was born in Kingston, Jamaica, immigrated at age seven, worked as an investment analyst at GE Capital before law school, and lives in West Seattle [S2]. She holds a BBA in international business and finance from Howard University and a JD from Seattle University School of Law (2008) [S1][S2]. She lists board service with Columbia Legal Services, the Seattle University Law Alumni Board and the Loren Miller Bar Association executive board [S2].

## Positions

Judicial approach:
- "Listen first, decide last" [S1]. Her site adds "never let ego interfere with fulfilling judicial responsibilities" and describes a belief in "individualized justice" [S2].
- Says her experience prepared her "to make impartial decisions that protect public safety while ensuring fairness" [S1].

Criminal-justice formation and bail/diversion:
- Formation is prosecution-side and city-government: six years prosecuting misdemeanors in Seattle Municipal Court (over 60 jury trials, self-reported), then seven years as a civil city attorney [S2].
- As a prosecutor she says she worked with participants in Seattle's Mental Health Court and Community Court "to compassionately address non-violent criminal behavior" [S2].
- As a pro tem she reports making probable-cause determinations, "setting pre-trial release conditions, and imposing sentences." As a commissioner pro tem she reports hearing civil protection-order and contested Involuntary Treatment Act hearings [S2]. The site does not describe how she approaches release decisions, and no specific bail or diversion position was found [S1][S2].

## Record

- No elected or appointed judicial office. Pro tem judge and commissioner service is self-reported [S1][S2]. She was not among the applicants for the council's January 2026 appointment to this seat [S8].
- Bar ratings, independently confirmed: Washington Women Lawyers rated her "Well Qualified" for King County Courts of Limited Jurisdiction in 2026 and in 2022. In 2026 it also rated her "Qualified" for King County Superior Court [S3].
- Bar ratings, self-reported: her pamphlet statement lists "Exceptionally Well-Qualified" from the Cardozo Society, the Joint Asian Judicial Committee, "King County," Latino Bar and Loren Miller Bar associations [S1]. Her site lists the King County Bar Association and QLaw among six "Exceptionally Well-Qualified" ratings [S2]. KCBA's 2026 ratings page (last updated Aug. 4, 2026) does not list this race or her, so the KCBA rating is not confirmed there [S4]. The other four were not independently checked.
