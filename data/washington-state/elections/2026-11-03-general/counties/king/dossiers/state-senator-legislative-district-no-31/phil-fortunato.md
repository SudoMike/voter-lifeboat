## Background
Phil Fortunato (prefers Republican Party) has been the 31st District State Senator since 2017; he was elected 31st District State Representative in 2016 and served as a 47th District State Representative from 1998 to 2000 [S1][S10]. His professional background is environmental training and consulting, international trade, and a small contracting business; he was founding director and first president of the PNW Erosion Control Association [S1]. He studied landscape architecture at Rutgers University's Cook College of Agriculture [S1]. He lists membership in the Knights of Columbus (4th Degree), Project Rachel, the Fraternal Order of Eagles, Sons & Daughters of Italy, and the Liberian Association of WA [S1]. In the August primary he had 53.86% to Democrat Tamara Stramel's 46.02% in the election-night results from King County Elections reported by the Auburn Reporter [S14].

## Positions
- **Taxes and spending:** says the state's financial problems "stem from excessive spending and the unchecked growth of government programs" and that people are "being crushed by rising taxes" [S1]; his Blueprint proposes zero-based budgeting, rolling back "unsustainable budget increases," bonuses for state employees who find savings, and funding transportation from the existing sales tax on motor vehicles "without raising taxes" [S3].
- **Housing:** attributes a "self-imposed housing crisis" to regulations, permit costs and fees, and land-use rules, and proposes "an aggressive program of opening up our state to build more housing of all kinds" [S3].
- **Homelessness and public safety:** says people cannot be allowed to camp on sidewalks; homeless people who commit crimes, or who have mental illness or addiction, should be "removed from the streets and provided treatment" [S3]; proposes two-year transitional housing with escalating rents [S3]. His site also cites "real solutions to crime and homelessness" [S2].
- **Schools and local control:** proposes letting school districts obtain waivers from state mandates he says cost money without reaching classrooms [S3].
- **Gun rights:** will "stand firmly against efforts to erode our Second Amendment rights" [S1].
- **Parents and schools:** his campaign home page cites "defending parental rights" among his priorities [S2].
- **Decision framework:** "God first, the district second, and personal preference last" [S1].

- Voted nay on ESHB 1296 in the Senate (2025-04-11) [S5]; the bill amended the Initiative 2081 parental-rights list [S6].
- Voted nay on ESSB 5599 (2023) at Senate passage and final passage; that bill lets youth shelters treat a minor's seeking gender-affirming or reproductive care as a compelling reason not to notify parents [S7][S8].
