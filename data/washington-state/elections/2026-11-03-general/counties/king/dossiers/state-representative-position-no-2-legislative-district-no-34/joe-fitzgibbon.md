## Background
Joe Fitzgibbon (Prefers Democratic Party) has been State Representative for the 34th Legislative District since 2010 and House Majority Leader since 2022; he chaired the House Environment & Energy Committee from 2014 to 2022 [S1]. He previously worked as a legislative assistant and environmental policy consultant, holds a BA in History and Political Science from Principia College, and served on the boards of the Democratic Legislative Campaign Committee (2019-2025) and Futurewise (2016-2019) [S1]. His general-election statement describes him as "a new parent of two young children" [S1]. The primary dossier records that he was raised in Burien in a Navy family, lives in West Seattle and served on the Burien Planning Commission [S2]. He took 74.5% of the King County primary vote (30,680 votes) to Mary Anito's 24.3% (9,999) [S10].

## Positions

### Taxes and budget
- Says he "led passage of landmark tax reform — a millionaires' tax paired with tax cuts for working families and small businesses" and helped stabilize the state budget [S1].
- Calls for "fair taxes" and "better school funding" [S1].
- Says he "secured investments in education, healthcare, transit, public safety, and the environment" [S1].

### Climate and energy
- Says he passed "the nation's strongest climate laws" [S1]; his site pledges to protect and strengthen the Climate Commitment Act and expand clean-energy and efficiency investment, and credits him with leading passage of the Climate Commitment Act and Clean Fuel Standard [S3].

### Housing and transportation
- Supports expanding affordable housing and homeownership, building more housing near transit and jobs, and protecting renters against displacement [S3].
- Supports expanded transit and ferry service and safer streets for pedestrians and bicyclists [S3].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- His site commits to "protecting reproductive freedom and healthcare access" against federal attacks [S3].

### Parents and schools
- Co-sponsored HB 1296 (2025), the bill that rewrote the parents' rights list enacted by Initiative 2081 (changing curriculum-examination and records-access rights and eliminating some medical-service notification rights) and added a statement of student rights [S6].
- Voted yes on ESHB 1296's House final passage as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24) [S5].

## Record
- House Majority Leader since 2022; chaired House Environment & Energy 2014-2022 [S1].
- Voted yes on ESSB 6346, the 2026 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million, on House final passage (2026-03-09) [S7].
- Voted yes on EHB 1217, the 2025 residential rent-increase limit, on House final passage as recommended by the conference committee (2025-04-27) [S8].
- Co-sponsored and voted for ESHB 1296 (2025) [S5][S6].
- No floor votes since July: the Legislature's 2026 regular session ran January 12 to March 12, 2026, and the Legislature's session-documents page lists no 2026 special session [S12].

## Endorsements
His campaign lists Sen. Emily Alvarado, Rep. Brianna Thomas, the 34th District Democrats, WEA-PAC, the Washington State Labor Council, SEIU Healthcare 1199NW, SEIU 775, SEIU 925, the Washington State Building and Construction Trades Council, Teamsters Local 117, WA Realtors, Washington Conservation Action and the Sierra Club, among others [S4].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% high-earner income tax, ban on income taxes):** Opposes. Told KOMO in July that "most Washingtonians want to see the kind of tax reform that this initiative would threaten" and that without the new tax "there's no way to balance the budget without really devastating cuts to education and likely other tax increases" [S9]. He also voted for ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S7].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** No public statement on I-1 found (campaign site, pamphlet, news search) [S1][S3]. He co-sponsored and voted for ESHB 1296, the 2025 law I-1 would undo [S5][S6].
- **I-638 (sex-based eligibility for girls' school sports):** No public position found (campaign site, pamphlet, news search) [S1][S3].
