## Background

- Incumbent U.S. Representative for WA-9 since 1997; Washington State Senator 1991-1996 [S1].
- Seattle city prosecutor 1993-1995; J.D., University of Washington (1990); B.A., Fordham (1987) [S1].
- Grew up in SeaTac in a union household [S1]. Past chairman and current top Democrat (ranking member) on the House Armed Services Committee [S1][S6].
- Faces Republican Doug Basler; the two have met in the 2014, 2016, 2018, 2020 and 2022 general elections, with Smith winning each time [S19].

## Positions

**Housing and economy**
- Pamphlet: "We must bring down the cost of housing and make homeownership achievable again"; "grow from the middle out," protect unions, expand job training [S1]. Campaign priorities: "Lower Your Rent & Build More Housing," "Put People Before Profits," "Build Pathways to High Paying Jobs" [S3].
- Co-introduced the Protecting American Homes from Hedge Funds Act, which his office says would "permanently drive hedge funds out of the single-family housing market" [S10].

**Taxes**
- On July 31, 2026 introduced a resolution calling for wage income and capital gains to be taxed the same way ("a dollar earned should be a dollar taxed"), arguing the "ultra-wealthy" pay too little and the tax system "isn't bringing in enough money" [S8].

**Healthcare**
- Pamphlet: "Our healthcare system needs to be restructured to create a path to a single-payer plan like Medicare for All" [S1]; repeated to Seattle Weekly in October [S17].
- Introduced a bipartisan bill to bolster the 988 mental-health crisis line (2026) [S10]; earlier sponsored the Behavioral Health Crisis Care Centers Act of 2025 [S2].

**Immigration**
- On Oct. 1, 2026 co-introduced the Immigrants in Nursing and Allied Health Act and the Internationally Trained Physicians Assistance Act "to help immigrants enter the U.S. health care workforce" [S10].

**Defense and foreign policy**
- Voted against final passage of the FY2027 NDAA (H.R. 8800) on July 22, 2026; it passed 216-212 [S13]. His statement: "The problem is the $1.6 trillion price tag—a 60 percent increase in the defense budget at a time when our country is running a nearly $2 trillion deficit... I will not support making massive cuts to critically important domestic programs and slashing taxes to meet those needs." He also objected to floor amendments he described as a "rightwing culture war assault on the LGBTQ+ community," and said the bill included Democratic priorities he supported [S6].
- Voted Aye on Massie Amendment No. 8 to the FY2027 State/foreign operations appropriations bill (failed 104-314) [S14]. His statement: "My vote to stop $3.3 billion in military assistance to Israel is my effort to" get the Netanyahu government's attention; he also wrote, "I still support Israel. I recognize the threats they face," and called Israel's West Bank actions "indefensible" [S7].
- Voted Yea on the Iran War Powers resolution (H.Con.Res. 89), which passed 214-208 on July 23, 2026 [S15]. In September he called the Iran conflict "Trump's disastrous war" and said it "must end now" [S12].
- Pamphlet: as Armed Services ranking member he has "prioritized making sure that all of our service members and their families, no matter who they are, receive the support they need" [S1].

**Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy**
- Campaign priority: "Protect Our Right to Abortion" [S3]. Endorsed by Planned Parenthood Action Fund, Human Rights Campaign PAC and Washington State Stonewall Democrats [S4].
- Co-signed a September 2026 letter to the Secretary of Defense stating, "we strongly oppose the underlying transgender military ban," and demanding timely separation pay for discharged transgender service members [S11].

**Parents and schools**
- No statement found on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs.

**Climate and environment**
- Campaign priority: "Protect Puget Sound for Future Generations" [S3]. Voted Nay on H.J.Res. 210 (Sept. 15, 2026), a resolution disapproving an EPA decision on California's at-berth emission standards for ocean-going vessels; it passed 216-211 [S16].

## Record

- Primary sponsor of 7 bills enacted into law over his career, including the FY2021 NDAA and the Promoting Affordable Housing Near Transit Act (GovTrack, as of July 2026) [S2].
- Missed 6.4% of roll-call votes from 1997 to June 2026 versus a 2.1% median (GovTrack, as of July 2026) [S2]. He was recorded as not voting on most House roll calls from Aug. 31 to Sept. 16, 2026; his office said he was "dealing with some medical issues" and a postponed procedure [S9].
- Other 2026 bills: the Safety Starts at the Top Act (Aug. 7), requiring worker representatives and safety experts on large aerospace manufacturers' boards [S10].

- Incumbent since 1997 and Armed Services ranking member; the only candidate with a legislative record [S1].
- 2026 defense record is mixed: voted against the FY2027 NDAA citing its cost, voted to block $3.3 billion in Israel military aid while stating continued support for Israel, and backed the Iran War Powers resolution [S6][S7][S13][S14][S15].
- Calls for equal taxation of capital gains and wages and a "path to" single-payer healthcare [S1][S8].
- Accepts PAC contributions ($563,000 from other committees this cycle) [S5].
- Social: abortion-rights priority and opposition to the transgender military ban [S3][S11].
