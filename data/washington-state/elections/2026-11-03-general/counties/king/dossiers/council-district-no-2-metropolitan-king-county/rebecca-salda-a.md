## Background

Rebecca Saldaña has represented the 37th Legislative District in the state Senate since 2016 [S1][S7]. She chairs the Senate Labor & Commerce Committee and sits on Agriculture & Natural Resources, Rules, and Ways & Means [S2][S8]. She was executive director of Puget Sound Sage from 2014 to 2016 and earlier organized with farmworker unions and SEIU Local 6 [S1][S2]. She holds a B.A. in humanities and theology from Seattle University and is the daughter of a formerly undocumented Mexican farmworker [S1]. She is not running for re-election to the Senate in 2026 [S2].

District 2 is open. Former Councilmember Girmay Zahilay became King County Executive in November 2025, and the council appointed Rhonda Lewis as interim councilmember until the November 2026 result is certified [S7]. A Metropolitan King County Councilmember "makes laws and adopts county budget," serves four years, and is paid $187,547 (2026) [S1].

**Primary result.** First in the August 4 primary with 35,247 votes (55.8%). Toshiko Grace Hasegawa had 22,120 (35.0%) and Miriam Mboya 5,525 (8.7%) [S13].

## Positions

**Taxes and revenue**
- Pamphlet: "In the state senate, I championed and passed taxes on the ultra-wealthy, including the Capital Gains Tax and Millionaires Tax, to fund the essential services we need" [S1].
- Campaign site: running "to continue her fight to tax the ultra-wealthy so that those most disadvantaged don't continue to bankroll billionaires and corporations"; says she "co-sponsored the Capital Gains Tax" and "sponsored a tax on massive corporations" [S3].
- Her Urbanist questionnaire, quoted in that outlet's endorsement: "King County must continue leading on progressive revenue so working families are not paying a larger share of their income in taxes than the wealthiest among us" [S9].
- At an October 3 debate she spoke of "raising the cap on property taxes" in the context of keeping long-time homeowners in their communities, as reported by Northwest Asian Weekly; the article does not say what cap change she means [S8].
- At a June forum she was reluctant to back a county property-tax levy for social housing and preferred progressive revenue and state-level approaches [S2].

**Housing**
- Pamphlet: "build more housing that is affordable at any income" [S1].
- At the July 23 forum she said the county must defend "Housing First" models [S7].
- At the October 3 debate she cited cannabis-revenue community reinvestment and home-ownership programs as anti-displacement tools [S8].

**Public safety, behavioral health and jails**
- Pamphlet: will "expand behavioral health and crisis response" [S1].
- On gun violence she said she would prioritize community-based crime prevention, behavioral-health investment and victim resources, citing Rainier Beach programs such as Community Passageways [S7].
- She co-chaired the state's Joint Legislative Task Force on Jail Standards and said the county has fallen short on oversight recommendations after 11 deaths in county jails in 2025 [S7].

**Immigration**
- Pamphlet: "I will never stop fighting to protect our communities from masked ICE agents terrorizing our neighbors" [S1].
- Said the county must ensure "not a single penny is being used to aid or support ICE," citing the Keep Washington Working Act she sponsored [S7].

**Transit, climate and labor**
- Campaign site: wants public transit "reliable, fast and free"; credits her with the Climate Commitment Act, the Clean Fuel Standard and the Move Ahead WA transportation package (campaign characterization of shared legislative work) [S3].
- Told The Urbanist in December, "In the last 10 years, we have put a price on carbon" [S9].
- Pamphlet: will "raise wages, improve transit, and make universal child care a reality"; cites Paid Family and Medical Leave and the Domestic Workers Bill of Rights [S1].

## Record

- Prime-sponsored the HEAL Act (environmental justice) and the Washington Voting Rights Act, among other laws [S2][S8].
- **ESSB 6346 (2026 tax on income over $1 million):** listed as a sponsor of SB 6346 [S5]. Voted yea on Senate final passage on February 16, 2026 (27-22) and yea on final passage as amended by the House on March 11, 2026 (27-21) [S4]. Voted nay on three Republican floor amendments on February 16 [S4]. This is the law I-645 would repeal.
- **ESHB 1296 (2025 amendments to the parents' rights statute and student-rights provisions):** voted yea on Senate final passage as amended on April 11, 2025 (30-19), and nay on a floor amendment by Sen. Gildon the same day [S6]. I-1 (IL26-001) would repeal the part of ESHB 1296 that rewrote RCW 28A.605.005.
- The 2026 legislative session ended before the primary; no Senate floor votes since July were found [S4].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- **I-645 (repeal of the income tax on income over $1 million):** At the July 23 forum, asked about the county's $150 million general-fund shortfall, she "said she would push to defeat ballot initiatives she said would undercut a wealth tax" (Northwest Asian Weekly's paraphrase; the article does not name the initiatives) [S7]. She was a sponsor of and voted for the tax I-645 would repeal [S4][S5]. No statement naming I-645 found as of 2026-10-08.
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights in schools):** No public position found as of 2026-10-08. She voted for ESHB 1296, the 2025 law whose rewrite of the parents' rights statute I-1 would repeal [S6].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' sports eligibility):** No public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Scoring notes

- Ten years in the state Senate with a committee chair and a record of passed laws, including votes for and sponsorship of the 2026 tax on income over $1 million [S1][S4][S5].
- Both candidates call for taxing the wealthy; Saldaña's has a legislative record behind it, and she prefers progressive revenue to a county property-tax levy for social housing [S1][S2][S4].
- Northwest Asian Weekly's October debate report found the two candidates gave similar answers and "supported the same progressive measures" [S8].
- Holds the King County Democrats' sole endorsement and a service-sector and public-employee union base [S1].
