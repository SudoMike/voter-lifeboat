## Background

Toshiko Grace Hasegawa is a Port of Seattle Commissioner [S1]. She has served on the commission since 2022 and was its president in 2025 [S2][S3]. PDC records show she was re-elected to Port Commission Position 4 in November 2025 [S13]. She is a fourth-generation Japanese American raising her family in the Beacon Hill home her grandparents bought after their release from World War II incarceration [S1]. Before the Port she was executive director of the Washington State Commission on Asian Pacific American Affairs, in Gov. Jay Inslee's cabinet for six years, and earlier was communications and outreach manager for King County's Office of Law Enforcement Oversight (OLEO) [S6][S7][S5]. She holds bachelor's and master's degrees in criminal justice and a bachelor's in Spanish from Seattle University and lists herself as a small business owner [S1]. She served on transition teams for Governor Ferguson, Executive Zahilay and Seattle Mayor Wilson [S1].

District 2 is open after Girmay Zahilay became County Executive; interim Councilmember Rhonda Lewis holds the seat until the November result is certified [S7]. A Metropolitan King County Councilmember "makes laws and adopts county budget" and serves four years [S1].

**Primary result.** Second in the August 4 primary with 22,120 votes (35.0%), behind Rebecca Saldaña's 35,247 (55.8%) [S12].

## Positions

**Taxes and revenue**
- Platform: "I will aggressively lobby the state legislature to lift the 1% property tax cap which strangles our ability to fund basic services" [S4].
- Platform: will "explore every avenue to implement high-earner excise taxes where legally permissible to fund housing and homelessness prevention," and will advocate for state authority for county progressive taxes such as a modified B&O tax on large corporations' profits, adding that "any tax on our businesses does not land on the backs of our small businesses community" [S4].
- Platform: supports Executive Zahilay's proposed countywide housing levy ("A housing levy is a great start") and "maximizing our use of progressive levies," naming the Crisis Care Centers levy [S4].
- Platform: proposes exploring a King County public bank to expand financing for housing, childcare and electrification [S4].
- Platform also proposes a temporary property-tax cap for the first five years of homeownership and pooling utility taxation across unincorporated areas to lower bills in places such as Skyway [S4].
- At the July 23 forum she raised a potential property-tax exemption for legacy homeowners facing new levies [S7].

**Housing**
- Platform: scale social housing countywide, working with Seattle's Social Housing Public Development Authority and dedicating county-owned land; land-bank sites near future transit stations for "100% social and affordable housing"; require review of surplus county land for housing before sale; expand community land trusts; speed permitting for affordable and social housing [S4].
- Platform and forum: a "Stay Housed Guarantee" of eviction prevention, rental assistance and mediation, legal counsel for renters, and a "right to return" policy for displaced residents [S4][S7].
- At the June Urbanist forum she backed a county social-housing levy "with urgency" [S2].

**Public safety and the justice system**
- Pamphlet: "upstream investments that get better outcomes for health and public safety," including "fully funding mental health and community-led gun violence prevention strategies, like after school and mentorship programs" [S1].
- Platform: expand Law Enforcement Assisted Diversion (LEAD) and pre-trial diversion; "Establish a Goal of Zero Youth Incarceration"; a "Traffic Safety for All" ordinance directing the Sheriff's Office to stop pulling drivers over for non-moving violations; random OLEO audits of body-camera footage; a "Zero-Profit" civil-forfeiture rule [S4].
- Platform and forum: give OLEO "full subpoena power," including to audit the Sheriff's Office for compliance with the Keep Washington Working Act [S4][S7].
- Seattle Times (July): supports a coordinated regional approach to homelessness but wants changes at the King County Regional Homelessness Authority; proposed Functional Family Probation and Parole for juvenile offenders [S6].
- July forum: called for continued investment in the Regional Office of Gun Violence Intervention and independent investigations of jail deaths modeled on Initiative 940 standards [S7].

**Budget and accountability**
- Platform: supports the executive's new in-house audit function and wants "oversight with teeth, like a suspension trigger when there are ethics issues or underperformance" [S4].
- At the July forum she named "accountability first," housing and child care as budget priorities for the county's $150 million general-fund shortfall [S7].
- Supports participatory budgeting, which she says she used at the Port [S4][S7][S8].

**Transit and environment**
- Platform: opposes adding general-purpose lanes on Rainier Ave S. ("HARD NO"); supports lidding I-5, which she says will combat air pollution; seeks grade separation or safety gates at Rainier Valley light-rail crossings [S4].
- Former Gov. Inslee's endorsement credits her with "advancing the 'Teal New Deal'" at the Port [S3].
- At the June forum she said she would prioritize keeping bus service over fleet electrification if forced to choose [S2].

**Child care**
- Pamphlet and platform: affordable child care, including a 10% of household income cap on child-care costs for low- and moderate-income families through Best Starts for Kids subsidies [S1][S4].

## Record

## Scoring notes

- Executive and port-district experience (state commission director, port commissioner) against her opponent's decade of legislative work; she has also held elected office, so the contrast is the kind of record rather than insider versus outsider [S1][S6].
- Her platform is the more detailed of the two on county-level revenue: lifting the 1% cap, a countywide housing levy, high-earner excise taxes and a public bank [S4].
- Her public-safety platform centers on diversion, oversight (OLEO subpoena power) and a zero youth incarceration goal [S4].
- Northwest Asian Weekly's October debate report found few differences between the two candidates [S8].
