## Background

Gabe (Gabriel) Rothstein has been a public defender since 2005 and still does that work full time. He also serves as a judge pro tempore in municipal and district courts [S1]. The Seattle Times identifies him as a Snohomish County public defender [S9]. His defense roles have included misdemeanor and felony defense, drug treatment court, juvenile court unit lead and civil commitment unit work [S3]. He has tried more than 70 cases to verdict [S1][S3]. Before becoming a public defender he volunteered with the Housing Justice Project and the Unemployment Law Project [S1]. He chairs the Washington State Bar Association Character and Fitness Board and serves on the board of the Holman Recovery Center, an inpatient substance-abuse treatment facility. He also takes evening and weekend calls on the Youth Access to Counsel Line [S1]. He holds a BA in sociology from Emory University and a JD from UCLA School of Law [S1].

## Positions

Judicial approach:
- Calls Seattle Municipal Court "the people's court." He says every person in the courtroom, "victims, defendants, and those who cannot afford an attorney," deserves dignity, respect and fairness and should be heard [S1].
- Emphasizes judicial patience, listening and clearly explaining outcomes [S3].

Criminal-justice formation and bail/diversion:
- Bail and release: his site says release decisions "should turn on... whether somebody is likely to come back to court, and whether they pose a danger. Not whether they have sufficient resources to afford bail." It also says that people held because they cannot afford bail often "plead guilty just to go home" [S3]. This re-verifies the position recorded in the primary dossier [S2].
- Treatment and diversion: says he believes in "treatment, the accountability that comes with recovery, redemption, and second chances" [S1]. His site advocates a treatment-centered approach to substance-abuse and mental-health cases [S3].
- This is a self-described position from campaign materials. No rulings documenting how he has set release conditions as a pro tem judge were found.

## Record

- Judge pro tempore since 2024 in Seattle, Burien, Redmond, Kent and Shoreline courts and Everett Municipal Court. He has handled small-claims trials, vehicle-impound hearings, contested hearings, and criminal and jail calendars (self-reported) [S3].
- August 4, 2026 primary: first place with 105,878 votes (54.6%), ahead of Garmon Newsom at 53,988 (27.9%) and Lindsay Calkins at 33,291 (17.2%) [S6].
- Bar ratings, independently confirmed: King County Bar Association "Well Qualified" (July 14, 2026) [S4]; Washington Women Lawyers "Well Qualified" for King County Superior Court and for King County Courts of Limited Jurisdiction (2024 list) [S5].
- Bar ratings, self-reported: his site lists "Exceptionally Well Qualified" from QLaw, the Latino Bar Association, the Joint Asian Bar Association and the Cardozo Society [S3]. In July the site listed the Cardozo and Joint Asian ratings as pending [S2]. These were not independently verified.

## Campaign finance

- PDC summary (updated Oct. 1, 2026): $39,180.99 raised, $17,166.29 spent, no debt [S10]. That is up from about $35,300 raised and $15,000 spent in mid-July [S2]. His opponent has raised about three times as much [S10].

## Scoring notes

- Formation: a 20-year public defender (felony, misdemeanor, drug court, juvenile, civil commitment), against an opponent from prosecution and city-attorney work [S1][S3].
- The only candidate in the race with a documented, specific bail position: release should depend on flight risk and danger, not ability to pay [S3].
- The only candidate with a published KCBA rating ("Well Qualified") [S4]. WWL also rated him "Well Qualified" [S5].
- Judicial experience is pro tem only, since 2024, across six limited-jurisdiction courts [S3].
- Won a 54.6% majority in the three-way primary [S6].
