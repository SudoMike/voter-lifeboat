## Background

Garmon Newsom II is a Seattle Municipal Court pro tem judge and a former city attorney [S1]. His statement describes 34 years as a felony and misdemeanor prosecutor, municipal attorney, civil litigator, King County Ethics Board member and judge pro tem [S1]. His campaign site lists the Bronx County District Attorney's office, the New York City Special Narcotics Prosecutor's office and a New York City agency general counsel's office (1999–2005). It then lists the Renton City Attorney's office from 2005 (prosecutor, Chief Prosecutor, senior assistant city attorney), assistant corporation counsel for West Hartford, Conn. (from 2015), and Burien City Attorney from 2020 until April 2026 [S4]. The Seattle Times reports that he also served as a King County District Court pro tem judge from 2013 to 2015 [S7]. According to the Seattle Times, he was removed as Burien City Attorney earlier in 2026 by then-City Manager Adolfo Bailon, whom the council then placed on administrative leave. Newsom told the editorial board he had advised the council on the legal process for separating from the city manager [S7]. He was raised in Detroit and holds a BA in political science from Morehouse College (1988) and a JD from George Washington University Law School (1992) [S1][S4].

## Positions

Judicial approach:
- Says the court needs judges "who are not focused on politics and ideology" and that he will deliver "fair, pragmatic, and equitable outcomes that build trust and public safety" [S1].
- Describes "impartial application of the law as written, grounded in precedent yet mindful of real-world impacts." He says judges apply law rather than make policy, and that he will not "allow agendas, ideologies, or external factors" to influence decisions [S5].
- Told the Seattle Times: "I don't think Seattle needs a prosecutor or a public defender or a defense attorney of any sort on the bench" [S7].

Criminal-justice formation and bail/diversion:
- His career is in prosecution and municipal-government law. His site lists no defense practice [S4].
- Says he is "focused on protecting the community while providing defendants with the opportunity for diversion, services, treatment, and support" [S1]. His primary-era statement framed diversion as "evidence-based, effective, and accountable" [S2].
- No statement on bail or pretrial release was found in his pamphlet statement or on his site [S1][S3][S5].
- As Burien City Attorney he advised the council during disputes over homeless encampments [S7]. The Stranger reported that at a January 2025 Burien City Council meeting he said threatening homeless people with arrest would give them an "incentive to actually accept the offer of shelter, treatment, and housing." That characterization comes from an outlet endorsing his opponent [S8].

## Record

- Pro tem service: King County District Court 2013–2015 [S7]; currently Seattle Municipal Court [S1].
- As Burien City Attorney he drafted ordinances and briefs, including the city's grocery-worker hazard-pay ordinance, which survived a federal constitutional challenge [S7].
- Over 75 civil and criminal trials (self-reported) [S3].
- August 4, 2026 primary: second place with 53,988 votes (27.9%), behind Gabe Rothstein at 105,878 (54.6%) [S10].
- Bar ratings: the King County Bar Association lists him as N/A, not rated within the last three years [S9]. He self-reports "Exceptionally Well-Qualified" from the Cardozo Society, the Latina/o Bar Association and the Joint Asian Bar Associations [S1]. His site adds QLaw [S3]. The Seattle Times repeats the Latina/o, Joint Asian and Cardozo ratings [S7]. His statement says he is "the only candidate in this race" with that rating, but his opponent's site claims the same three ratings [S1].

## Endorsements

- Seattle Times editorial board (primary recommendation, June 9, 2026) [S7]. No separate general-election editorial for this race was found on the Times' editorial index at access time.
- Per his general-election pamphlet statement: Supreme Court Justices Sal Mungia, Mary Yu (ret.) and Barbara Madsen (ret.); King County Superior Court Presiding Judge Rothrock and Assistant Presiding Judge Thorp; former Presiding Judge Shah; Judges Galvan, Amato, Hawkins and Gipe; Chief Presiding Judge York; retired Judges McCullough, Kondo and McKenna; Seattle Municipal Court Presiding Judge Anita Crawford-Willis, former Presiding Judge Faye Chess and "retiring Judge Willie Gregory"; the Seattle Times editorial board; and the 32nd LD Democrats [S1]. His opponent also lists the 32nd District Democrats [S1].

- Changes since the primary: the general statement adds Justices Mungia and Madsen, Presiding Judges Rothrock and Crawford-Willis, and others [S1][S2]. Judge Gregory's court profile shows a term through January 2027 but does not mention retirement [S13].

## Campaign finance

- PDC summary (updated Oct. 5, 2026): $115,644.45 raised, $70,710.50 spent, $1,500 in debt [S11]. That is up from about $76,700 raised in mid-July [S2].
- Includes $27,500 of the candidate's personal funds: $2,500 on Sept. 18, 2026 and $25,000 on Oct. 2, 2026 [S12].

## Scoring notes

- Formation: prosecution (Bronx DA, NYC Special Narcotics, Renton Chief Prosecutor) and city-attorney work, against an opponent's 20-year public-defense career [S1][S4].
- No documented bail or pretrial-release position. Diversion is framed as part of public safety and community protection [S1].
- Longer overall legal career (34 years) and earlier pro tem service (KCDC 2013–2015, now SMC). No KCBA rating is published [S1][S7][S9].
- Holds the Seattle Times recommendation and the retiring incumbent's endorsement. Trailed by about 27 points in the primary [S1][S7][S10].
- Out-raising his opponent roughly three to one, including $27,500 self-funded since mid-September [S11][S12].
