## Background

Amy Walen (Prefers Democratic Party) has been State Representative for the 48th LD since 2019; she was Kirkland Mayor 2014-2018 and a Kirkland Councilmember 2009-2019 [S1]. She chairs the House Consumer Protection & Business Committee [S3]. She has 20+ years as an owner and CFO in family businesses (her site mentions managing a car dealership) and holds a law degree and a BA in political science from the University of Queensland [S1][S3]. In November 2025 she challenged Sen. Vandana Slatter for the LD 48 Senate seat and lost, 40.7% to 57.97% [S7].

Her general-election pamphlet statement is the same text she filed for the primary [S1][S2]. In the August primary her Democratic challenger Jessica Forsythe "narrowly edged Walen ... by 17 votes," per The Urbanist [S8].

## Positions

**Taxes**
- Pamphlet: "I voted no on the income tax," citing small-business and tech jobs [S1].
- Site: "I believe in the promise of a high-earners income tax," but she voted against the 2026 millionaires' tax "because it was unconstitutional, not progressive enough, and raised the possibility of broad middle class tax hikes in the near future" [S3].
- Site says she offered amendments to apply the $1 million deduction per individual instead of per household, to return most of the projected revenue by cutting the state sales tax rate from 6.5% to 5.75%, and proposed a constitutional amendment authorizing an income tax [S3].
- Supports replacing the B&O tax with a corporate income tax and calls B&O reform her "top tax priority next session" [S3].

**Budget and spending**
- Describes herself as championing "balanced budgets and fiscal responsibility" [S1].
- Site cites concern over the state's credit-rating downgrade and rainy-day fund drawdown, and "$16 million going missing" at the Regional Homelessness Authority [S3].
- Proposes "robust state funding for childcare" and says on infrastructure "this is a time to forgo our usual conservatism around local borrowing ... Borrow now and put people to work" [S3].

**Housing**
- Emphasizes supply: prime sponsor of bills enabling conversion of commercial buildings to housing (HB 1757, HB 1042), limiting "historic designation" to buildings at least 40 years old with owner consent (HB 1576), and barring occupancy limits in some housing types (HB 1054) [S3].
- Prime sponsor of HB 1075 and HB 1046 expanding public housing authorities' financing and "mixed middle" construction [S3].
- Says she has "consistently supported missing middle and transit-oriented development, carrying concerns from the cities I represent" [S3]. The Urbanist reports progressives criticized her for voting against "statewide middle housing reform" [S8].

**Public safety and guns**
- Says she has passed an assault-rifle ban, a high-capacity magazine ban and permit-to-purchase "while continuing to be endorsed by our local police departments," and favors "well-trained, community-focused law enforcement while also expanding mental health and crisis response services" [S3].
- Says she supported all of the Legislature's police accountability work and expanded Law Enforcement Academy slots [S3].

**Climate and environment**
- Voted for the Climate Commitment Act in 2021 and supports low-carbon fuel standards; sponsored HB 1033 on compostable food-service materials [S3].
- Supports incentives for hydrogen, nuclear, solar, wind and geothermal energy [S3].

**Immigration**
- Pamphlet: "banning ICE from wearing masks" [S1]. Site: supported HB 2173 barring ICE face coverings, voted to keep ICE out of schools, hospitals and daycares without a judicial warrant, and was prime sponsor of HB 1889 removing citizenship requirements from most professional licenses [S3].

**Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy**
- Site: "Reproductive options and gender affirming care are health care," which "must be affordable, accessible, and covered by all kinds of health insurance"; says people must be able "to determine our own gender identity" [S3].
- Pamphlet cites "backfilling money cut from Planned Parenthood" [S1].

**Parents and schools**
- Site: "Our school system has an essential role in providing comprehensive sexual health education, and all of our systems must provide a safety net to protect children from unhealthy or abusive families" [S3]. Her vote on ESHB 1296 is listed under Record.

**Technology**
- Calls for "AI-resilient" job growth and training incentives; no position on AI regulation found [S3].

## Record

- State Representative since 2019; chair of Consumer Protection & Business [S1][S3].
- Voted no on ESSB 6346 (2026), the 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million, on House final passage (2026-03-09) [S4]. The Urbanist reports the vote "drew the ire of progressives" [S8].
- Voted yes on ESHB 1296 (2025), the bill amending the parents' rights law enacted by I-2081, on House passage (2025-03-12) and final passage as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24) [S5].
- Sponsor of consumer-protection bills, including HB 1730 on "zombie debt" collection [S3].
- No floor votes since July: the Legislature has not been in session since the 2026 regular session adjourned sine die on March 12, 2026 [S6].

## Endorsements

- Pamphlet: Bellevue Police Guild, Alliance for Gun Responsibility, Governor Gregoire, Congressman Smith, Councilmember Claudia Balducci [S1].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- **I-645 (IP26-645, income tax repeal):** No statement naming I-645 found on her site, in the pamphlet or in news searches [S1][S3]. She voted no on ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal, while saying she supports a high-earners income tax in a different form [S3][S4].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights in schools):** No public statement found [S1][S3]. She voted yes on ESHB 1296, the 2025 amendments I-1 would undo [S5].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports eligibility):** No statement naming I-638 found [S1][S3]. Her site says people must be able "to determine our own gender identity" [S3].

## Scoring notes

- Seven-year incumbent, committee chair and former mayor, against a city councilmember [S1][S3].
- The main contrast with her opponent is her no vote on the millionaires' tax, which she frames as a disagreement over design and constitutionality [S1][S3][S4].
- Supply-side housing record, with reported opposition to the statewide middle-housing mandate [S3][S8].
- Strongly progressive on reproductive and gender policy and on constraining ICE [S1][S3].
