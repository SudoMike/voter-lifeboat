## Background
Noel C. Frame (Prefers Democratic Party) has been State Senator for the 36th District since 2023 after serving as its State Representative from 2016 to 2023 [S1]. She is Vice Chair of Finance on the Ways & Means Committee, Vice Chair of the Human Services Committee, a member of the Juvenile Justice Partnership Council, and past co-chair of the Children & Youth Behavioral Health Work Group [S1]. She is managing partner of the consulting firm Uncommon Bridges, a housing provider since 2025, and a former nonprofit director; she holds bachelor's and master's degrees from George Washington University [S1]. Her pamphlet lists current and past PTA membership while she was a foster parent and caregiver to extended family [S1]. She took 87.7% of the King County primary vote (42,548) to Jillian England's 12.1% (5,876) [S9].

## Positions

### Taxes and budget
- Describes her work as "rebalancing the state's regressive tax code"; says she "helped pass the Millionaires Tax to fund our public schools, healthcare, human services and early learning" [S1].
- Cites expansion of the Working Families Tax Credit (460,000 new households) and the senior property tax exemption (50,000 new households), a low-income energy assistance program, and sales-tax exemptions on key household items [S1].
- Her site says she "led on the groundbreaking Millionaires Tax" and, as House Finance chair, "passed the Capital Gains Tax"; it calls for "ensuring Washington's wealthiest pay their fair share" [S3].

### Other platform areas
- Her site lists platform pages on housing and homelessness, education, public safety, healthcare access, child welfare, environment, racial and social equity, and opposing the Trump administration [S3].
- Pamphlet cites "historic investments in affordable housing" [S1].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No statement on these topics found in the pamphlet or campaign site [S1][S3].

### Parents and schools
- Voted yes on ESHB 1296 (2025), the amendments to the I-2081 parents' rights list that I-1 would undo, on Senate final passage [S7].

## Record
- Co-sponsored SB 6346 and voted yes on Senate final passage of ESSB 6346, the 2026 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million [S5][S6].
- Prime sponsor of wealth-tax bills since 2021 (SB 5486 in 2023, SB 5797 in 2025); the Washington State Standard called her the Legislature's "revenue queen" [S2][S4].
- Voted yes on EHB 1217, the 2025 residential rent-increase limit, on Senate final passage [S8].
- No floor votes since July: the 2026 regular session ran January 12 to March 12, 2026, and the Legislature's session-documents page lists no 2026 special session [S11].

## Endorsements
Pamphlet lists the 36th District Democrats, Working Families Party, Washington State Labor Council AFL-CIO and Washington Conservation Action [S1]. Her site's endorsement page shows organization logos without text names [S3].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (income tax repeal):** No public statement on I-645 found (campaign site, pamphlet, news search) [S1][S3]. She co-sponsored and voted for ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal, and her pamphlet promotes it [S1][S5][S6].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** No public statement found (campaign site, pamphlet, news search) [S1][S3]. She voted for ESHB 1296, the 2025 law I-1 would undo [S7].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** No public position found (campaign site, pamphlet, news search) [S1][S3].
