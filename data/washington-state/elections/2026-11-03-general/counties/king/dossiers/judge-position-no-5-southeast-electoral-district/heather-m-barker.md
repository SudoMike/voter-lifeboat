## Background

Heather M. Barker has been a judge pro tem in King County District Court since 2022, presiding over civil and criminal matters including Mental Health and Veterans Court calendars [S1][S3]. She founded and owns Northwest Community Law, PLLC, a civil practice her campaign describes as serving local families, neighbors, and small businesses, with a property-law focus. Her statement says she has owned a civil-litigation firm "for over a decade" and has been an attorney for twelve years [S1][S2][S3]. She holds a J.D. from Seattle University School of Law (2013, per her campaign) and a B.A. with honors in anthropology from California State University, Long Beach (2005) [S1][S3]. Before law school she owned a coffee shop and worked about three years in residential real estate development [S3]. She was born and raised in Kent, graduated from Kentwood High School, and lives in Renton [S1][S3]. She says she has drafted free estate plans for first responders through a First Responder Will Clinic for over a decade, and that she volunteers with the YMCA Mock Trial program [S1][S3].

No primary dossier exists: with only two candidates, the race did not appear on the August ballot.

## Positions

Judicial approach:
- Says "fair, thoughtful justice builds stronger, safer communities" and lists listening carefully, treating everyone with dignity, and applying the law "fairly and thoughtfully" [S1][S2].
- Says she has "earned the trust of defense and prosecuting attorneys alike" [S1].

Criminal-justice formation, bail and diversion:
- Her formation is civil practice; no prosecution or public-defense employment appears in her pamphlet or campaign materials [S1][S3].
- Says she is "committed to protecting public safety from the bench by carefully balancing accountability with rehabilitation" [S1]. Her stated pro tem work includes the court's Mental Health and Veterans Court calendars [S1][S3]. That is self-reported; no court record of her therapeutic-court rulings was found. No sourced statements on bail or pretrial release were found.

Court administration and access:
- Her pro tem docket includes misdemeanors (theft, trespass, assault), protection orders, infractions, and small claims, per her campaign [S2]. No court-administration proposals were found [S2][S3].
