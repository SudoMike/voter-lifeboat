## Background

Rodney 'Star' Thornley (Prefers Independent Party) is a first-time candidate with no elected experience [S1]. He lists 35 years with the Lake Washington Education Association and two terms as a state WEA representative, along with long service in sports-officiating and water-ski organizations (past two-term president and current secretary of the PNBOA officials association; president of the Pacific Northwest Water Ski Association and Lake Washington Ski Club) [S1]. He attended Seattle Central Community College, Seattle Pacific College, and the University of Washington [S1]. Community service includes first-aid volunteering for Husky athletics, First Aid/CPR/AED instruction, and coordinating an inner-city youth water-ski program and "Seattle Night Out" in the University District [S1]. His general-election pamphlet statement is materially the same as his primary statement [S1][S2].

In the August 4, 2026 primary he received 4,386 votes (about 10.0% of votes cast in the race) [S6].

## Positions

### Governing approach
- Running as an independent because "Olympia needs a culture change. Too many elected officials fall in line with party leadership instead of focusing on the people they serve" [S1]. His site says "a legislator's first obligation should be to the constituents" [S3].
- Platform proposes a "50% assessment on political contributions," with the revenue going to a "Washington Voter Information Fund" [S4].

### Immigration
- Pamphlet priority: "Washington State citizenship for undocumented residents" [S1]. Platform describes a voluntary Washington Resident Registration Program at the Department of Licensing that would give qualifying undocumented residents a state credential, exclude new arrivals and people with felony convictions, and bar transfer of participants to ICE without a judicial warrant [S4].

### Taxes and spending
- Pamphlet priorities: "fiscal accountability, and a government that listens to citizens before it spends their money" [S1].
- Platform: gradually reduce the B&O tax on small businesses (with an option to tax profits instead of gross receipts); says the capital gains tax should be "reconsidered by voters" and any replacement needs constitutional protection against expansion [S4].

### Housing
- Platform: support "modifying House Bill 1110" (multifamily housing in single-family zones) to preserve single-family neighborhoods with on-site parking; streamline permitting; "lower unnecessary fees and regulations"; encourage higher density near transit; incentives for landlords of ten or more units to set aside an income-limited unit; cap rent at 28% of income in mixed-income housing [S4].

### Public safety
- Platform: "enforce existing misdemeanor laws while also providing a path forward"; "cities need to prosecute misdemeanor crimes, especially when repeat offenders are involved"; on prostitution and trafficking, "enforcement alone is not enough, but neither are social services without accountability"; homelessness approach combining accountability, addiction treatment, mental-health treatment, housing and jobs [S4].
- Says Flock surveillance cameras raise "legitimate privacy concerns" and need clear access rules [S4].

### Education
- Pamphlet priority: a three-year college path [S1]. Platform: reduce bachelor's degree requirements from 180 to 135 credits [S4].
- Platform: "School districts should have more authority to set seat-time requirements and graduation standards"; reduce central district administration; supports the federal school-choice scholarship tax credit; says schools "should be fully funded" [S4].
