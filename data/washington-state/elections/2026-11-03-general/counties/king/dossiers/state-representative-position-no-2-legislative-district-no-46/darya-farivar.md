## Background

Darya Farivar (Prefers Democratic Party) is the incumbent State Representative for the 46th Legislative District, Position 2, and describes herself as the first Middle Eastern woman elected to the Washington State Legislature [S1]. She is an ADA and Disability Equity Specialist with the King County Executive's Office [S1]. She holds a B.A. from the University of Redlands [S1]. Her community service includes co-chairing the Seattle Women's Commission, chairing policy for the State Special Education Advisory Council, and board service with Peyvand and the Washington Coalition for Police Accountability [S1]. Her general-election pamphlet statement is materially the same as her primary statement [S1][S2].

In the August 4, 2026 primary she received 39,373 votes (about 89.4% of votes cast in the race) to Rodney 'Star' Thornley's 4,386 (about 10.0%) [S11].

## Positions

### Food access and consumer protection
- Says she passed legislation preventing stores from blocking new grocers and pharmacies, after Lake City lost a major grocery store [S1][S4].

### Health care and caregiving
- Says she passed legislation expanding the caregiver workforce [S1][S5]. Her issues page says her vision includes "universal coverage, prioritizing patients over profits," and cites the My Health My Data act [S6].

### Taxes
- Issues page says she supports a wealth tax (HB 1406) and a modified real estate excise tax (HB 1628) to fund affordable housing [S6].

### Housing
- Issues page calls rent stabilization "the most important legislation to address housing instability and affordability" and lists support for missing-middle housing legislation (HB 1782) and funding for CoLead Northgate transitional housing [S6].

### Public safety
- Issues page cites funding for behavioral-health response teams, a bill pairing accountability with treatment access, and HB 1445 to "strengthen the Attorney General's power to investigate patterns of police misconduct" [S6]. Board member of the Washington Coalition for Police Accountability [S1].
- Supports "comprehensive bans on assault rifles" and limits on bulk firearms purchases [S6].

### Climate and transit
- Issues page supports light rail expansion and "policies that expand clean, efficient transportation to tackle the climate crisis" [S6].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Issues page cites support for the state "Shield Law" protecting people from out-of-state legal action over reproductive care [S6]. Endorsed by Pro-Choice Washington [S1].

### Parents and schools
- Issues page cites work on bills for students with disabilities and language barriers and on teacher pay [S6]. No statement on parental notification or opt-outs found. See her ESHB 1296 vote under Record.

## Record

- Chair, Joint Administrative Rules Review Committee; Vice Chair, Civil Rights and Judiciary; member of Community Safety, State Government and Tribal Relations, Rules, and Crisis Response Improvement Strategy committees [S3].
- Prime sponsor of HB 2294 (2026), barring restrictive covenants that block new grocery stores or pharmacies; passed the House and advanced in the Senate [S4].
- Sponsor of HB 1395 on barriers for qualified caregivers, plus 2026 bills on disability facility names and Residential Habilitation Center transparency [S5].
- Voted yes on ESHB 1296 (2025), which amended the parents' rights law enacted by I-2081 (House final passage 2025-04-24, 59-39) [S7].
- Voted yes on ESSB 6346 (2026), the tax on household income over $1 million (House final passage 2026-03-09, 51-46) [S8].
- No floor votes since July: the Legislature has not been in session since the 2026 regular session adjourned on March 12, 2026 [S10].

## Endorsements

Per her general-election pamphlet statement: 46th District Dems, Pro-Choice Washington, Washington State Labor Council, Attorney General Brown, and Congresswoman Jayapal [S1].
