## Background

Roger Goodman (Prefers Democratic Party) is the incumbent State Representative for the 45th Legislative District, Position 1, serving since 2007 [S1][S4]. He chairs the House Community Safety Committee and serves on the Statute Law Committee, Civil Rights & Judiciary, and Early Learning & Human Services [S1][S4]. Before the Legislature he practiced law, worked at the King County Bar Association (2000-08), directed the Washington State Sentencing Guidelines Commission (1998-2000), and was a congressional chief of staff and legislative director (1988-1995) [S1]. He holds an AB from Dartmouth College, a JD from George Washington University and an MPA from Harvard University [S1]. Community roles include the Hopelink board, president of Kirkland's Lakeview Neighborhood Association, Rotary and the King County Behavioral Health Advisory Board [S1].

His general-election pamphlet statement is the same text he filed for the primary [S1][S2]. In the August primary he led with 72.87% against JoAnn Tolentino, per results as of August 10 [S9].

## Positions

**Public safety and behavioral health**
- Says his priorities are improving public safety, reducing gun violence and helping people in crisis [S1].
- His campaign site describes police-accountability reform as a focus: "restricting the needless use of force, holding police accountable for misconduct and ensuring the equitable treatment by law enforcement of all communities" [S3].
- Describes strengthening the response to mental illness as a priority [S1][S3].

**Taxes, budget and services**
- Says he "voted to reduce the tax burden on working people and small businesses and to provide free school meals and more affordable childcare" [S1].
- Pledges to keep championing K-12 funding and affordable housing investments [S1].

**Climate**
- Pledges to champion climate action [S1]; his primary-era site material cited legislation to cap carbon emissions [S2].

**Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy**
- Pledges to champion reproductive rights [S1].

**Parents and schools**
- No campaign statement on parental notification, records access or opt-outs found [S1][S3]. His vote on ESHB 1296 is listed under Record.

**Federal government and immigration**
- His campaign site criticizes the federal administration and says "masked ICE agents snatch people out of their vehicles and off the streets", which it says has eroded trust in local law enforcement [S3].

## Record

- State Representative since 2007; chair of the House Community Safety Committee [S1][S4].
- Says he passed laws to protect survivors of crime, expand drug and mental health services, and improve law enforcement accountability and training [S1].
- Voted yes on ESHB 1296 (2025), the bill amending the parents' rights law enacted by I-2081, on House passage (2025-03-12) and on final passage as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24) [S5].
- Voted yes on ESSB 6346 (2026), the 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million, on House final passage (2026-03-09) [S6].
- No floor votes since July: the Legislature has not been in session since the 2026 regular session adjourned sine die on March 12, 2026 [S7].

## Endorsements

- The 45th District Democrats endorse him for the general [S10].
- His campaign endorsements page quotes a Seattle Times editorial on his public-safety committee experience and lists individual supporters' quotes; no current organizational list was found there [S3].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- **I-645 (IP26-645, income tax repeal):** No public statement on I-645 found on his campaign site or in the pamphlet [S1][S3]. He voted yes on ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S6].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights in schools):** No public statement on I-1 found [S1][S3]. He voted yes on ESHB 1296, the 2025 amendments I-1 would undo [S5].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports eligibility):** No public position found on his campaign site, in the pamphlet or in news searches [S1][S3].
