## What the office does
One of two State Representatives for the 31st Legislative District, elected to a two-year term in the 98-member House, voting on state law and the operating, capital, and transportation budgets. LD31 spans southeast King County and northeast Pierce County (see the primary overview for state-senator-legislative-district-no-31), so Pierce County voters also vote in this contest.

## District and race dynamics
November 3, 2026 general election, two candidates: first-term Republican incumbent Joshua Penner, a former Orting mayor and Marine veteran, and Democrat John Bielka, a civil engineer and public works director who raises cattle near Orting (see joshua-penner.md S1; john-bielka.md S1). They were also the only two candidates on the primary ballot.

Both campaign on accountability and practical results. Penner stresses oversight of state agencies (child welfare, disability care, Sound Transit) and voted against ESSB 6346 and ESHB 1296 (joshua-penner.md S2, S4, S5). Bielka, unusually for a Democrat, says he will not support tax increases until the Legislature prioritizes spending, and backs the Second Amendment; he is endorsed by the LD31 and Pierce County Democrats, WEA-PAC, and the Alliance for Gun Responsibility Victory Fund (john-bielka.md S3, S4, S6). Penner has raised about $56k against Bielka's about $15k (PDC; joshua-penner.md S9, john-bielka.md S5).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** a first-term legislator and former eight-year mayor, against a first-time candidate with a long public-works career.
2. **Taxes:** narrower than the party labels suggest: Penner voted against the 2026 income tax, and Bielka will not support new taxes until spending is prioritized.
3. **Parental rights:** Penner voted against ESHB 1296; Bielka states no position.
4. **Local control:** Penner opposed letting Sound Transit sidestep local zoning; Bielka emphasizes faster permitting and more housing types without addressing state preemption.
5. **Spending:** both run on fiscal accountability; neither proposes major new programs.
