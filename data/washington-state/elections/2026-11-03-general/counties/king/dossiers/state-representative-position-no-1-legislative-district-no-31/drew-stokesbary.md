## Background
Drew Stokesbary (prefers Republican Party) is the incumbent and current House Minority Leader; he previously chaired the Select Committee on Pension Policy and served as Ranking Member on House Appropriations and as House Minority Floor Leader [S1]. House Republicans chose him as leader in 2023 [S12]. His campaign site says he was first elected in 2014 [S2]. He is a partner at Chalmers, Adams, Backer & Wallen, advising startups, small businesses and investors on corporate and securities law [S1]. J.D. cum laude, Notre Dame Law School; B.S. in economics, Duke University [S1]. He co-founded the Citizen Action Defense Fund, which provides pro bono counsel to churches and nonprofits on constitutional-rights matters, and is a volunteer tax preparer [S1]. In the August primary he had 57.88% to Democrat Stephen Szczurko-Walton's 42% in the election-night results from King County Elections reported by the Auburn Reporter [S15].

## Positions
- **Taxes and economy:** says "taxes keep increasing, by billions each year" and pledges "a stronger and more affordable economy" [S1]. The House Republican caucus page summarizes a Wall Street Journal report in which he called the 2026 income tax on earnings over $1 million a "betrayal" that threatens to drive away businesses, and posts video of him speaking against the bill at final passage [S4]. His (undated) issues page says he helped defeat new taxes on income and other items as a Finance Committee member, and that the Legislature should prioritize spending instead of raising taxes [S2].
- **Spending:** his issues page says he has worked "to identify and eliminate waste, fraud and abuse" as House Republicans' chief budget writer and calls for budget-process reform [S2].
- **Public safety:** wants "safer neighborhoods where laws are enforced and criminals are apprehended" [S1]; his site cites fighting for more law-enforcement and firefighting funding and sponsoring HB 1761 to prevent "heroin injection sites" [S2].
- **Education:** cites falling test scores, attendance, and college enrollment and pledges "better schools that prepare all students for college or career" [S1]; cites work on the 2017 school-funding reform [S2].

## Record
- Voted nay on ESSB 6346 at House final passage (2026-03-09) [S5]; per his caucus page, House Republicans fought the bill for more than 24 consecutive hours before passage [S4].
- Voted nay on ESHB 1296 at House passage (2025-03-12) and final passage (2025-04-24) [S6]; the bill amended the Initiative 2081 parental-rights list [S7].
- Voted nay on ESSB 5599 (2023) at House final passage; that bill lets youth shelters treat a minor's seeking gender-affirming or reproductive care as a compelling reason not to notify parents [S8][S9].
- His campaign site lists five prime-sponsored bills signed into law, including HB 1467 (fire-service efficiencies) and HB 1940 (flood-control district funding) [S2].
- No floor votes since July: the Legislature has not met in session since sine die on 2026-03-12 [S10].
- Campaign finance (PDC, data updated 2026-10-05): $387,429.61 raised and $247,055.53 spent for 2026 [S11].
