## Background

Rob Foxcurran is Senior Appraiser for the City of Seattle, a Certified General Real Estate Appraiser and a former Hearing Examiner on the King County Board of Appeals and Equalization [S1]. He holds a B.A. from the University of Washington and graduated from Seattle Public Schools [S1]. He describes himself as a fifth-generation Seattleite whose career has included commercial valuation in the private sector [S1]. He says he has lived in income-restricted housing and is a PROTEC17 shop steward [S1]. The Urbanist describes a private-sector appraisal career beginning over a decade ago before his City of Seattle role [S9].

The Assessor "sets property values for county property taxes"; the office is elected countywide to a four-year term, with a 2026 salary of $241,096 [S1 page 14]. The seat is open: incumbent John Wilson did not seek another term [S2].

**Primary result.** Foxcurran finished first in the August 4 primary with 231,854 votes (47.1% of votes cast in the race). Dominique Scarimbolo was second with 94,725 (19.2%), ahead of Christopher Roberts with 91,770 (18.6%) and Al Dams with 72,783 (14.8%) [S7].

## Positions

**Property taxes and affordability**
- General-election pamphlet: "In Olympia, I'll push to establish homestead protections for primary residences to help homeowners manage rising costs, and create a renter's tax credit to give tenants relief from rising housing costs" [S1].
- Campaign site: will push "for a universal homestead exemption, expanding relief programs, and making payments more flexible so families can pay monthly instead of in large lump sums," and for "a system that ties property taxes to income so no one is taxed out of their neighborhood" [S3].
- Says residential homeowners carry most of the property-tax burden while "the largest corporate landowners hire teams of experts and walk away with tax breaks that shift the burden to everyone else"; says he will "ensure the wealthiest corporations pay their fair share" [S3]. The pamphlet makes the same point from his Board of Equalization experience [S1].
- His primary-season pamphlet statement also called for lifting the state's 1% levy cap [S2]. The general-election statement does not repeat that item [S1].
- The Urbanist reports that he favors a land value tax, a homestead exemption with renter credits and circuit-breaker programs, and that he argues under-assessing surface parking lots and vacant parcels in transit-connected neighborhoods subsidizes car-dependent land use [S9].

**Administration and transparency**
- Pamphlet: wants "an accurate, fair, and transparent Assessor's office"; describes himself as "the only candidate in this race who has actually done the valuation work this office depends on" [S1].
- The Urbanist lists his priorities as accuracy, transparency and affordability: continuous assessments, plain-language explanations and tools, and more outreach about existing exemption programs [S9].
- Campaign site: will assess small-business personal property "accurately and fairly" [S3].

**Housing**
- Campaign site: will use the office's authority to identify surplus land and coordinate across agencies to support affordable-housing development [S3].
- The Urbanist reports he wants legislators to explore funded inclusionary zoning as a long-term alternative to the Multi-Family Tax Exemption, and that he says golf-course tax treatment should be addressed by the Legislature [S9].

**Statements after the primary**
- Told KUOW by email that the result showed voters "want an Assessor with real appraisal experience who understands both sides of the property tax system" [S8].

## Campaign finance

- PDC summary as of the October 5, 2026 update: $70,926.00 raised, $56,450.49 spent, $2,500.00 in debts [S5].
- Of that, $61,026 came from 185 individual contributions and $9,900 from 10 organizations. The largest organizational gifts were PROTEC17 PAC ($2,400) and $1,200 each from BOMA PAC of Washington State, Laborers Local 242, the Master Builders Association of King & Snohomish Counties Affordable Housing Council, ATU COPE and the Washington Association of Realtors PAC [S6].
- KUOW reported he was the primary's fundraising leader [S8].

## Scoring notes

- He is the only candidate in the general with professional appraisal and assessment-appeals (Board of Equalization) experience; his opponent's experience is as a taxpayer and appellant [S1].
- His affordability platform relies on state-level changes (homestead exemption, renter's credit, income-tied relief) plus shifting assessment burden toward large commercial owners; he does not call for lower overall property-tax collections [S1][S3].
- He carries the Democratic party-organization slate, labor and the county executive, and in the general also the endorsement of Al Dams, the chief deputy assessor he ran against in the primary [S1][S4].
- He has not held elected office; his record is as a city employee and quasi-judicial hearing examiner [S1].
