## Background

Dominique M Scarimbolo is a small family business owner and President of the King County chapter of the Citizens Alliance for Property Rights (CAPR), with a B.A. in Criminal Justice from Washington State University [S1]. The campaign site says she lives in rural unincorporated King County and describes her as "not a career politician" [S3]. Her primary dossier records earlier work at Alaska Airlines, including service as an IAM Local 2202 shop steward, and family business ventures including Four Horsemen Brewery [S2]. KUOW describes her as a brewery owner [S6].

Under the same PDC filer ID she ran for King County Council in 2021 as Dominique Torgerson; that committee advanced to the general election and lost [S2][S4].

The Assessor "sets property values for county property taxes" and serves a four-year term [S1 page 14].

**Primary result.** She finished second in the August 4 primary with 94,725 votes (19.2%), 2,955 votes ahead of Christopher Roberts (91,770, 18.6%). Rob Foxcurran led with 231,854 (47.1%) [S5].

## Positions

**Property taxes**
- Pamphlet: the campaign is "about pursuing meaningful, system-wide reforms that protect taxpayers, strengthen property rights, ensures due process and helps ensure people aren't taxed out of their homes" [S1].
- KUOW, which calls her an "anti-tax candidate," reported that in a post-primary phone interview she "went on to express her opposition to property taxes, despite seeking the role of assessor." Her quoted words: "That makes me a better candidate. I'm not out to screw the taxpayer," and "Nobody can say that we don't have enough funding, if we didn't blow it on all this fraud that we don't want to account for. It's so frustrating to see how overtaxed we are" [S6].
- Pamphlet: "Government works best being fully transparent and accountable, and we demand fiscal responsibility" [S1].
- Her published taxpayer guide includes chapters titled "Why Washington's B&O Tax Is So Regressive," "The Truth About the 1% Limit" and "The Levy Lid Lift Loophole" [S3].
- One of her four audit requests to the King County Auditor asks whether "overlapping levies, repeated lid lifts, and bond levies that never seem to expire have created a workaround to the constitutional 1% limit" [S3].

**Assessment uniformity and appeals**
- Pamphlet: the system "is required to produce fair and uniform assessments, but if that system does not produce the results required by law, we have a responsibility to examine it honestly, measure its performance, and improve it" [S1].
- Pamphlet: property owners "deserve parcel-specific breakdowns on their individual property valuations—not simply a final number," plus "due process, timely appeals and logical evidentiary standards," and should never need to "pay for professional help, simply to challenge their assessment" [S1].
- Her audit requests ask the County Auditor to examine ratio studies and whether rural and unincorporated areas are "disproportionately overvalued," whether valuation methods meet the "true and fair value" standard, and whether the appeals process gives owners a meaningful opportunity to contest values [S3].
- Campaign site: the Assessor "does not set tax rates, create budgets, or determine how much money taxing districts collect" [S3].

**Transparency**
- Campaign site: the office should operate "with an open-books philosophy," with easy access to valuation methods, exemption programs and appeal processes [S3].

## Scoring notes

- Runs as a taxpayer-advocate outsider with no elected or assessment-office experience; her stated experience is studying the system as an appellant, records requester and audit-request author [S1][S3].
- Her stated priorities are uniformity, transparency, due process in appeals and limits on overall property-tax growth (levy stacking, lid lifts) [S1][S3][S6].
- Very low-budget campaign (mini reporting, about $250 raised as of August) against an opponent with roughly $71,000 raised [S4][S6].
- Republican Party "Best Choice" rating in a nonpartisan race; no other organizational endorsement found [S7].
