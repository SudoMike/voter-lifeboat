## What the office does

The King County Assessor "sets property values for county property taxes." The office is elected countywide to a four-year term; the 2026 salary is $241,096 [S1]. The Assessor values all taxable real and personal property, maintains parcel records and administers exemption programs (seniors, people with disabilities). The Assessor does not set tax rates, approve levies or decide how much taxing districts collect; values determine how a district's levy is shared among property owners [S2][S4]. Changes to exemptions, the 1% levy-growth limit or a homestead exemption require action by the Legislature.

## Race dynamics

The seat is open; incumbent John Wilson did not run [S2]. In the August 4 primary Rob Foxcurran, a City of Seattle senior appraiser and former Board of Equalization hearing examiner, led with 47.1%. Dominique Scarimbolo, a small business owner and president of the King County chapter of the Citizens Alliance for Property Rights, took second with 19.2%, 2,955 votes ahead of Shoreline Councilmember Christopher Roberts (18.6%). Chief Deputy Assessor Al Dams was fourth (14.8%) [S3].

The two finalists differ in background, resources and endorsements. Foxcurran has professional valuation and appeals-hearing experience, about $71,000 raised, and the support of the King County Democrats, labor, the county executive, three newspapers in the primary and, in the general, Al Dams. Scarimbolo has no elected or assessment-office experience, uses PDC mini reporting (about $250 raised as of August per KUOW), and is rated "Best Choice" by the King County Republican Party. KUOW described her as an "anti-tax candidate" who opposes property taxes [S4]. Fundraising and endorsement details are cited in the candidate dossiers.

## Issue axes that genuinely differentiate these candidates

1. **Experience (`experience`).** Foxcurran is a working appraiser and former assessment-appeals hearing examiner; Scarimbolo runs explicitly as "not a politician" with a taxpayer and appellant's perspective. Neither has held elected office.
2. **Taxes (`taxes`).** Both talk about affordability, but in opposite directions. Foxcurran wants relief targeted by state law (homestead exemption, renter's credit, income-tied relief) and more of the burden on large commercial owners; he does not seek lower overall collections. Scarimbolo says the county is "overtaxed," opposes property taxes, and has asked the County Auditor to examine levy stacking and lid lifts as a "workaround" to the 1% limit.
3. **Spending and oversight (`spending`).** Scarimbolo's platform centers on audits, "open-books" government and "fiscal responsibility"; Foxcurran's centers on accuracy, transparency tools and relief programs.
4. **Housing (`housing`).** Foxcurran proposes using the office's data to identify surplus public land for affordable housing; Scarimbolo's platform does not address housing supply.

Axes that do not differentiate or are out of the office's reach: climate, public safety and immigration. Neither candidate has a public position on the three statewide initiatives (I-645, I-1, I-638).
