13 Official Ballot Drop Box FAQ We recommend you track your ballot at kce.wiki/BallotAlerts to watch its progress as it moves through the system. You can also sign up for ballot alerts to receive automatic text or email alerts as your ballot is processed and counted. Track your ballot with Ballot Alerts! Is there an official ballot drop box near me? Yes! You can find the 3 official ballot drop boxes nearest to you on the insert that comes with your ballot. In King County, more than 97% of active voters are within 3 miles of an official ballot drop box. A full list of all official ballot drop boxes is available at kce.wiki/DropBox . Who can deliver ballots to an official ballot drop box? Under Washington state law, anyone can deliver ballots to a drop box! We recommend delivering your own ballot, or only allowing someone you trust with your vote to deliver it. How will I know my ballot is safe and will be counted? King County’s official ballot drop boxes weigh 1,000 pounds and are secured in concrete with heavy gauge steel screws. The boxes have no pry points and the ballot return slot is specifically designed to fit a ballot and not much else. Boxes are equipped with fire suppressant systems and monitoring systems that provide real-time data if a drop box is accessed or tampered with. Drop boxes are regularly emptied by trained staff during the voting period, in teams of two. On Election Night, trained teams of Elections staff in branded safety vests and aprons are on site to assist voters and to close and lock the drop boxes promptly at 8:00 p.m. sharp. When are official ballot drop boxes open? Official ballot drop boxes open the day after ballots are mailed, and remain open 24 hours a day until 8:00 p.m. sharp on Election Night. Pre-register to vote If you are at least 16, you can pre-register to vote online or by mail at any time. We’ll hold on to your registration and will automatically send you a ballot once you’re eligible to vote. Audits and Quality Control Our team conducts several tests before, during, and after each election to ensure all votes are being counted accurately. Logic & Accuracy Test Conducted about three weeks before the election, the Logic & Accuracy test uses test ballots and a set of expected results to ensure the tabulation equipment is reading votes correctly. Random Batch Audit On the Thursday after Election Day, six batches of ballots are selected at random and hand counted to ensure the tabulation equipment continues to read votes as it should. Risk Limiting Audit Conducted between Election Day and certification, this auditing system checks a random sample of ballots to reach statistical confidence in the results of the election. 81 Journey of your ballot King County Elections mails ballots to all registered voters around three weeks from Election Day. You can return your ballot to an official ballot drop box, 24/7, until Election Day at 8 p.m. sharp. If returning your ballot by mail, USPS recommends mailing it at least one week before Election Day. What happens after we receive your ballot back at Elections Headquarters? Your ballot envelope is sorted. Our mail sorting machine scans your ballot envelope and sends an image of your signature to be verified. 1 2 4 5 Your signature is verified. The signature on your voter registration record is compared to the image of the signature from your ballot envelope. Right after 8 p.m. on Election Night, we tabulate results and post them on our website for all to see. Results are updated every weekday at 4 p.m. until the election is certified. Your ballot is scanned. Your ballot is scanned and an image is stored on a secure and closed system until 8 p.m. on Election Night. Your ballot is counted. If the signatures match, your ballot moves on to be opened. If your signature is missing or doesn’t match your registration record, the ballot will be challenged. We will contact you to resolve the issue before the ballot is opened. Track your ballot each step of the way! Opt in at kce.wiki/BallotAlerts to receive texts or emails as your ballot is processed and counted. 3 Your ballot is opened. Your ballot is removed from the envelope and security sleeve by trained staff. This is where your votes are separated from your identity. The ballot is then visually inspected to make sure it is able to be scanned.

Truth Matters Between partisan arguments, online rumors, and AI-generated fakes, figuring out what’s real can be challenging. Here are some tips for finding your way to the facts. T R U T H M A T T E R S FACTS Pause, then question the source. Whether you see something confusing online or hear it from a friend, family member, or colleague, take a moment to find out where the information came from. Learning whether the source is known and trusted can help you figure out whether the information is true. Think critically. It can be helpful - especially when we feel strongly - to pause and be curious. Consider a few simple questions: Who is sharing this information? Why? When was it originally published?
