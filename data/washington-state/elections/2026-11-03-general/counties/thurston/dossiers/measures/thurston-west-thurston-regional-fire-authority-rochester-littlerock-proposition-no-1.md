## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would let the West Thurston Regional Fire Authority (WTRFA) levy an excess property tax of $1,547,228 for collection in 2027, at an approximate rate of $0.36 per $1,000 of assessed value, "without regard to the dollar rate and percentage limitations imposed by Chapter 84.52 RCW" [S1][S2]. The ballot title says the money is "to be used for maintenance and operations and to maintain the current level of fire services and emergency medical services" [S1][S2]. The board adopted Resolution No. 2026-005 [S1][S2]. It is a one-year levy [S1].

- **The authority.** WTRFA serves about 24,000 residents from five fire stations across a 162-square-mile area from the Tumwater city limits to the Lewis County line, including Rochester, Littlerock, Grand Mound, Maytown and Scott Lake [S6]. It is a combination department of about 85 responders and support staff (31 career and 40 volunteer firefighter/EMTs) that answers more than 3,000 calls a year [S6]. The measure appears on ballots in both of its former fire districts, Rochester and Littlerock [S2].
- **Why, per the explanatory statement:** "apparatus failures, facility water system replacement, and an aging fleet," plus rising daily costs of service. The levy "will enable the Authority to address facility, fleet and equipment failures, and maintain current service levels" [S1].
- **How the money would be used, per the statement for:** "All levy revenue will be dedicated to apparatus replacement and facility maintenance under a long-term capital improvement plan, with regular public reporting" [S1]. That commitment is narrower than the ballot title, which authorizes maintenance and operations generally [S1][S2].
- **Vote threshold.** Excess levies for a fire authority need 60% approval [S6][S7]. The Chronicle reports a turnout requirement of at least 40% of the last election's turnout [S7].

## Cost

- $1,547,228 in 2027, about $0.36 per $1,000 of assessed value [S1][S2].
- The explanatory statement says the "maximum cost increase to a home with an assessed value of $500,000 would be $15.00 monthly" [S1], or $180 a year (computed for this dossier).
- One year only; any levy for 2028 would need another vote [S1][S6].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

The authority's regular fire levy is capped at $1.50 per $1,000 with 1% annual revenue growth, so it relies on voter-approved measures to keep up [S6]. Property tax levies make up about 70% of its revenue [S6]. WTRFA's plan says a regional fire authority can run only a single-year M&O levy, unless the two underlying fire districts (Fire Districts 1 and 11) each run a multi-year levy, which risks one passing and one failing [S6].

**Recent levy history:**
- February 2016: levy lid lift passed, 73.11% yes (combined FD 1 and FD 11) [S3].
- November 2022: M&O levies received 59.38% yes in Rochester and 51.41% in Littlerock [S5]. WTRFA's plan says voters did not approve the 2022 M&O levy, which caused a 37% drop in 2023 tax revenue and required "significant budget cuts" [S6]. Its table shows tax revenue falling from $6,106,171 in 2022 to $3,795,961 in 2023 [S6].
- August 2023: levy lid lift passed, 63% yes, restoring the fire levy to $1.50 per $1,000 [S3][S6].
- November 2024: M&O levy failed with 54% combined yes [S3] (55.3% in Rochester, 53.38% in Littlerock [S4]).
- August 2026 primary: WTRFA ran four-year M&O levies at $0.38 per $1,000 as two separate measures, one for each district [S7]. On election night the Rochester measure had 51.6% yes and the Littlerock measure 59.16%, both under the 60% needed [S8]. This research did not retrieve the certified August results; the Auditor's past-results list for this ballot does not include the August 2026 measures [S3].

**The authority's stated needs.**
- Its strategic plan lists "multiple M&O levy failure (2022 and 2024)," apparatus failure and higher apparatus and equipment costs among its challenges, and says it is "reliant on excess levies to fund apparatus replacement and other critical needs" [S6].
- It says many engines "are aging beyond recommended NFPA and WSRB standards" and sets a goal to replace or order at least two engines by 2030 under a 20-year replacement schedule [S6].
- Chief Nathan Drake said of the August levy: "Our main goal with this levy is to support our vehicle purchases and maintenance of our facilities that is not supported by our standard budget" [S8].

## Arguments for

The statement for (Tyler Mason and Cathe Linn) says [S1]:
- "Many frontline emergency vehicles are nearing or have exceeded their recommended service life," and delay means higher repair costs and a greater risk that apparatus is unavailable in an emergency.
- The levy would fund station maintenance, including "aging HVAC systems, roofing, electrical and plumbing repairs."
- "Replacing equipment before it fails and maintaining facilities before serious problems develop is generally more cost-effective than waiting for emergencies."

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed: "After recruitment attempts, no person in the jurisdiction contacted the Auditor to write a statement against the ballot measure" [S1]. No organized opposition was found. The 2022, 2024 and August 2026 results show that a majority, short of 60%, supported the earlier levies [S3][S4][S5][S8].
