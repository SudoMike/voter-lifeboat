## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would let Lacey Fire District 3 (LFD3) issue up to $98,300,000 of general obligation bonds maturing within 20 years, repaid by annual excess property taxes [S1][S2]. The money would pay to construct a new community fire station (Britton Pkwy), replace Station 32 (Yelm Hwy), build a regional live fire training facility and a logistics warehouse, acquire property and make "other capital improvements and apparatus acquisitions" [S1][S2]. The board adopted Resolution No. 903-06-26 [S1][S2].

- **The district.** The explanatory statement says LFD3 provides fire, rescue and emergency medical services to "over 110,000 people living in Lacey and other areas of Thurston County" [S1]. Chief Jennifer Schmidt said the service area had about 100,000 residents in 2020 and about 110,000 in 2025, and cited regional projections of nearly 130,000 by 2045 [S7].
- **Four deficiencies, per the district:** an inadequate existing fire station, a response coverage gap north of Interstate 5, inadequate live fire training capabilities, and a lack of adequate storage [S1][S5][S6].
- **The new station.** The district's bond page calls it Station 36, proposed for the growing communities north of I-5 [S4]. Only one of the district's five stations is north of I-5 [S1][S7]. Schmidt said an earthquake that damaged the I-5 overpasses could isolate Hawks Prairie from the rest of the district's resources [S7].
- **Station 32.** The statement for says the bond would replace Station 32's "outdated 1970s apparatus bay and double-wide living quarters" with a permanent station sited to improve response times in the southeast part of the district [S1]. The fact sheet describes it as a relocation "to a more optimal site on the Yelm Highway corridor" [S5].
- **If it fails, per the district:** "slower response times as demand increases and continued reliance on aging, inefficient facilities" [S5].
- Bond proceeds cannot be used for salaries or routine operations [S1][S8].

**Pamphlet layout note.** PDF page 28 of the local voters' pamphlet carries a stray heading and ballot title for "Thurston County Fire Protection District 12 Proposition No. 1, Authorizing Restoration of Previous Property Tax Levy" (Resolution No. 2025-01) above Lacey Fire District 3's explanatory statement, contacts and argument for [S1]. Fire Protection District 12 has no measure on this ballot: neither the sample ballot nor the Auditor's past-results list for this election includes one [S2][S3]. A Fire Protection District 12 Proposition No. 1 with the same title was on the November 2025 ballot (approved, 59.2% yes) [S11]. The Lacey ballot title itself is printed lower on the same page [S1]. This research treats the FD 12 text as a layout leftover from that earlier pamphlet.

## Cost

- Up to $98.3 million in bonds over at most 20 years [S1][S2].
- Estimated rate and cost, by source:
  - Explanatory statement: about $0.23 per $1,000 of assessed value, or $9.60 a month on a $500,000 home [S1].
  - Statement for, and the district fact sheet: about $8 to $11 a month on a $500,000 home [S1][S5]; the fact sheet gives a range of $0.22 to $0.25 per $1,000 [S5][S6].
  - The district's online calculator uses $0.24 per $1,000 [S4].
- At $0.23 per $1,000, a $500,000 home would pay about $115 a year (computed for this dossier from [S1]).
- JOLT reported that city council documents for Lacey's support resolution did not list an estimated levy rate or property-owner cost [S9].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This is a new bond authorization, not a renewal. LFD3 funds day-to-day operations through a regular fire levy and a Medic One EMS levy [S8]. The regular levy is capped at $1.50 per $1,000 and its revenue growth is limited to 1% a year plus new construction [S5][S6][S8]. The district says this cap makes it hard to pay for capital projects from the operating budget, and that saving up cash reserves would not meet immediate needs while construction costs rise [S5][S8]. Schmidt compared the levy to a household checking account and the bond to a mortgage [S6].

**Earlier measures (Auditor's list):**
- November 2017 bond: passed, 74.96% yes [S3].
- November 2020 levy lid lift: passed, 63.07% yes [S3].
- August 2023 levy lid lift: passed, 68.62% yes [S3]. JOLT reports the 2023 lid lift has eased pressure on routine expenses but cannot finance major capital projects [S8].

This research did not find how much of the 2017 bond remains outstanding or when it retires, so the district's total bond rate after this measure is not known here.

**Why now, per the district.**
- Calls: more than 18,000 a year, the highest in Thurston County [S1][S5][S6]. Schmidt said calls rose from about 12,000 a year a decade ago to about 18,000 in 2025 [S7]; the statement for says calls are up 43% in ten years [S1]. (JOLT's June article describes 43% as the growth of the service area [S6].)
- Growth near Hawks Prairie, which Schmidt described as becoming a commercial hub [S7].
- Schmidt said the projects are sized for 20 years of growth "to avoid asking for smaller amounts multiple times" [S6][S7].
- Building sooner avoids construction cost inflation, the district says [S5][S6].

## Arguments for

The statement for (Steve Brooks and Tom Carroll; peopleforpublicsafety.com) says [S1]:
- LFD3 "now responds to more than 18,000 emergencies each year, the highest in Thurston County," with calls up 43% in ten years and only one of five stations north of I-5.
- A disaster could damage freeway access "and leave a large part of our community isolated."
- The bond builds a station north of I-5, replaces Station 32 and gives firefighters and paramedics "the facilities, equipment, and supplies they need."
- Bond funds "cannot be used for salaries or routine operations," and "Building now will avoid higher construction costs later."

Schmidt: "We're asking for $98.3 million, and that is a lot of money, but please, again, understand that this is not just one quick fix" [S6].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed: "After recruitment attempts, no person in the jurisdiction contacted the Auditor to write a statement against the ballot measure" [S1]. No organized opposition was found.

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote adds a new excess property tax of about $0.23 per $1,000 (about $9.60 a month on a $500,000 home) for up to 20 years to repay $98.3 million in bonds [S1]. It is a capital bond, not an operating levy, and the district's earlier bond and lid lifts passed comfortably [S3]. A new tax of moderate size: +1.
- `spending`: the measure funds new capital (a new station, a replacement station, a training facility and a warehouse) rather than programs [S1]. No opponent case was filed [S1]. Not mapped.
- `local-control`: the measure does not change governance or regional structure [S1]. The proposed facility is described as a "regional" training facility [S1][S5], but that refers to its use, not to a change in who governs. Not mapped.
- `social` / `parental-rights`: nothing in the sources bears on these. Not mapped.
