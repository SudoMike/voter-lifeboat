## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would "restore the District's regular property tax levy rate for library services, operations and maintenance from $0.22 to $0.35 per $1,000 of assessed valuation for both 2027 and 2028, subject to applicable limitations" [S1][S2]. The 2028 levy amount becomes the base for computing later levy limits under chapter 84.55 RCW [S1][S2]. The Board of Trustees adopted Resolution No. 26-002 [S1].

- **The district.** Timberland Regional Library (TRL) serves Grays Harbor, Lewis, Mason, Pacific and Thurston counties through 29 libraries [S4][S6]. The pro statement says Thurston County has eight branches plus the traveling Anywhere Library [S1]. The measure is on every Thurston ballot (a countywide measure) [S2].
- **Vote threshold.** The measure is decided by the combined vote of all five counties and passes with more than 50% yes; it does not need to pass in each county [S4][S7][S9].
- **What the money would pay for.** The explanatory statement says approval "would provide funding necessary to maintain Library services, staffing, collections, and open hours" [S1]. The interim executive director lists the priority order as more open hours and staffing, more collections spending, more programs (especially for school-age youth), then facilities projects with the community [S5]. JOLT reports it would also rebuild cash reserves [S11], and Heisel told the Tumwater council the money would also go to building maintenance [S10].
- **If it fails.** The explanatory statement says reductions in staffing, materials, hours, online services, bookmobile service and programming "will begin in 2027," and maintenance could be delayed [S1]. TRL lists significantly fewer staffed hours, reduced Anywhere Library mobile service, smaller collections and card-access-only unstaffed hours at branches with Expanded Access [S4]. Board President Brian Mittge said some small libraries "might be unstaffed" with keycard access only [S9].

## Cost

- Rate: from about $0.22 (the explanatory statement gives $0.228924) to $0.35 per $1,000 of assessed value in 2027 and 2028 [S1]. State law allows library districts up to $0.50 [S1][S4].
- Per-home estimates differ by source and home value:
  - Explanatory statement: about $40.44 a year ($3.37 a month) on a $334,000 home [S1].
  - Statement for: about $69 a year ($5.75 a month) on a median Thurston home of $530,959 [S1].
  - TRL's levy page: $59 a year ($4.92 a month) on a 2025 Thurston median of $457,400, revised Aug. 20, 2026 from Department of Revenue data [S4].
  - Interim Executive Director Andrea Heisel told the Tumwater council about $53 a year for the average Thurston homeowner [S10].
- Revenue: about $14 million more in 2027 than without a lid lift [S4][S7]. JOLT cites TRL data of $26,844,125 projected 2027 revenue without the lid lift and $40,784,249 with it [S7].
- After 2028 the rate is expected to decline again as the 1% limit applies; Heisel said "it starts slowly declining again over time" [S10]. The Nisqually Valley News describes the levy as growing a maximum of 1% a year after 2028 [S8].
- TRL says no other taxing district would lose money through prorationing, because all five counties have room under the $5.90 per $1,000 cap [S4].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This is a levy lid lift, not a new levy: TRL's regular levy already exists, and the measure raises its rate [S4]. About 96% of TRL's budget comes from the levy [S1][S4]; the Nisqually Valley News cites budget documents putting it at 93.7% in 2026 [S8]. TRL says the 1% annual limit on property-tax revenue growth has lowered its rate from $0.50 to $0.22 [S4][S5].

**Levy history.**
- In February 2009 a TRL levy lid lift failed with 45.14% yes in Thurston County [S3]. TRL says that resolution was not adopted [S1] and that it failed "by a relatively small margin" [S5].
- TRL's FAQ says the last voter-approved levy lid lift was in 1987 [S4]. TRL and the statement for describe the levy as not having increased, or not having been at a full rate, for 25 years [S1][S4][S6]. Heisel referred to "the 2001 levy lid lift" at the Tumwater meeting [S10]. This research did not resolve the difference between the 1987 and 2001 dates.

**2026 budget crisis.**
- TRL faced a shortfall reported as $3.8 million [S8][S9][S10] (JOLT reported $3.5 million [S7]). Former Executive Director Cheryl Heywood announced it with a proposal to cut 20% of staff, and resigned after public pushback [S9][S10].
- TRL reports budget adjustments of about $3.69 million: lower collection spending, unfilled positions, voluntary and involuntary layoffs, an administrative restructure and salary review, and furloughs [S5][S9]. Mittge said these cuts are "not a particularly sustainable response for years ahead" [S9].
- Staffing fell from about 251 FTE in 2016 to 182 FTE as of August 2026 [S4]. Heisel wrote that TRL eliminated 100 positions from 2021 to 2026 and that "reductions to staffing and services in recent years should not have been implemented without also pursuing a restoration of the Library's levy rate" [S5].
- New policies require multi-year budget forecasts and a fund balance of at least 30%, and TRL now posts monthly financial reports [S9]. TRL adopted a Financial Transparency Policy [S5].

**How the rate was chosen.** On July 22, 2026 the board voted unanimously to put the measure on the ballot [S6][S8]. Staff had presented four options: no lid lift, $0.30, $0.35 or $0.40 [S8]. Staff said $0.35 would prevent further cuts and rebuild reserves to the required threshold within a year [S8]. Trustee Mary Beth Harrington (Thurston) argued for $0.40 and then agreed to $0.35 [S8].

## Arguments for

The statement for (TimberStrong Libraries Committee: Kylie McQuarrie, Jan Morris and Laura Cameron) says [S1]:
- About 96% of TRL's revenue comes from a levy that "hasn't increased in 25 years," and its value has been eroded by higher prices.
- State law caps growth at 1% a year "until voters lift the lid."
- Approval would let TRL "restore hours and staffing" and expand book, ebook, music and movie collections and programs. It calls the increase "a long overdue cost-of-living raise."
- Its rebuttal says libraries "have endured a 25-year financial drought" and serve all ages, including job-seekers and seniors on fixed incomes.

Trustee Dustin Loup (Grays Harbor) said: "We need to allow our communities to make this decision. If we don't put it on the ballot, they don't get to make that decision" [S6][S7].

## Arguments against

The statement against (Sean Swope, Citizens for Accountable TR Libraries) says [S1]:
- A no vote "is a vote for fiscal responsibility, taxpayer accountability, and careful budgeting," at a time when property owners face rising bills from many levies.
- Before asking for more revenue the library should show it has cut costs, improved efficiency, sought grants and partnerships and managed existing resources better.
- The tax "could put future investments in schools and emergency services at risk."
- Its rebuttal says many library services are "delivered through nonprofits and community organizations more efficiently and effectively," and that "Voting 'No' is not a vote against libraries or literacy."

## Endorsements and campaign

- The Tumwater City Council unanimously passed a resolution supporting Proposition No. 1 on Sept. 15, 2026 [S10].
- The Olympia City Council scheduled a resolution in support for Oct. 6, 2026, with a staff recommendation to support it [S11]. This research did not confirm the outcome.
- The against author, Sean Swope, is a Lewis County commissioner [S8]. The Nisqually Valley News reports he had said he would "oppose any levy lid lift" if TRL opposed Board President Mittge's vision for the library system (his "desired outcomes" list) and "refuses to return to its core mission." It also reports that in 2023 he supported a proposed TRL book-rating system [S8]. Mittge, who had said he would oppose a lid lift if TRL did not commit to pursuing that list, said in July that he supported taking the proposal to voters, and the board vote was unanimous [S8].
- PDC: a committee named "Yes For Libraries" (Olympia; jurisdiction Thurston County; a levy measure, ballot number "TBD", stance "for") reports $46,855.18 raised and $10,586.28 spent [S12]. Its filing does not name TRL, so this research did not confirm it is the TRL committee. No 2026 committee named for TimberStrong, TRL or "Citizens for Accountable TR Libraries" was found [S12][S13].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote raises TRL's regular levy rate from about $0.22 (exactly $0.228924) to $0.35 per $1,000, adding about $14 million in 2027 [S1][S4][S7]. The rubric's pole_b names "levy-cap lifts" explicitly. Direction +2.
- `spending`: the measure would restore and expand open hours, staffing and collections [S1][S5], and the against side argues for audit and restraint first [S1]. Following the Spokane convention for single-district operating levies, this research does not map `spending`.
- `social` / `parental-rights`: the measure itself does not change library collection, content or minors' access policy [S1][S2]. The 2023 book-rating debate tied to the against author [S8] is context, not something the measure does. Not mapped.
- `local-control`: TRL is a five-county regional district and the vote is decided across all five counties [S4]. But the measure changes a tax rate, not the regional structure. Not mapped.
