## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would authorize Yelm Community Schools to levy excess property taxes for two years "to maintain essential educational programs, extracurricular activities, and operations not funded by the State," listing teachers, arts, nurses, counselors, classified staff, paraeducators, safety, graduation readiness, technology, athletics, facilities and curriculum [S1][S2]. The board adopted Resolution No. 09-25-26 [S1]. The explanatory statement says it would keep the programs in place in 2025-26 running through 2026-27 and 2027-28 [S1].

- **The district** covers parts of Thurston and Pierce counties [S4][S7].
- **What it would pay for, per the district:** high school varsity and JV athletics, current class sizes and offerings (including AP and electives), extracurriculars such as FFA, CTE, band and choir, safety-related maintenance, basic technology, and current nurse, health room, security and school resource officer staffing [S4]. It says a "limited amount" would let the board bring back some counselors, behavior support, safety services and previously cut activities [S4].
- **If it fails, per the district:** it cannot run again in 2026, and the schools would get no local levy money in 2027 [S4]. The district says OSPI has told it the state would move YCS from "Binding Conditions" into "Financial Oversight." A state committee would then have authority up to "dissolving or 'breaking up' our school district" [S4]. The district projects another 70-90+ staff cut, class sizes up 5-10 students, and athletics, clubs and after-school activities eliminated [S4].

## Cost

- Up to $11,194,449 collected in 2027 and $12,278,072 in 2028, at an estimated $1.50 per $1,000 of assessed value [S1][S2].
- The district says the rate is estimated; levies are set as dollar amounts, so the rate falls if total assessed value rises [S4].
- Why $1.50: the district says it is the lowest rate that still qualifies for full state levy equalization (Local Effort Assistance), which requires a levy of at least $1.50 per $1,000. The legal maximum is $2.50 [S4].
- Seniors (61 or older) and people with disabilities who meet income limits may qualify for exemptions [S4].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

There is no Yelm EP&O levy in place now. Superintendent Chris Woods said after the November 2025 failure: "We will have no levy dollars for 2025 and 2026, so two calendar years" [S7]. The district says its schools "have functioned without a local levy for the last two years" [S4]. A yes vote therefore restores a local levy that is not being collected now.

**Four failed attempts (combined Thurston and Pierce results):**
- Feb. 2024: 46.28% yes [S3]
- April 2024: 48.47% yes [S3]
- Feb. 2025: 45.24% yes [S3]
- Nov. 2025: 47.63% yes [S3]. The Thurston-only share was 49.27% (3,777 to 3,889) [S11].
- The first three asked $2.50 per $1,000; the November 2025 measure asked $1.30 [S8].

**Budget since then:**
- In February 2026 the board moved $1.1 million in accumulated interest from the capital fund to the general fund. Woods called it "a one-time help" against a deficit of up to $4 million that year and $8-10 million the next [S9].
- In March 2026 the board unanimously adopted a reduced education plan to identify positions to cut. Woods said there was still a gap of more than $3 million to the board's 5% ending-fund-balance policy [S10].
- The district says it has cut 163 positions since 2024 and has spent reserves to below 1% [S4].

**Board vote.** On Aug. 3, 2026 the board voted 4-1 to put the levy on the November ballot [S5].
- Board President Casey Shaw said $1.50 "is the minimum that we can collect" and that the district would still need "a couple million (dollars) in cuts to stay solvent" [S5].
- Director Ryan Jewell voted no, saying the levy "doesn't add back programs that we've already cut, so offering less and asking more comes across tone deaf" [S5].
- Director Denise Hendrickson called it "our last chance to try to keep our local control," because without it the state would start financial oversight [S5].

## Arguments for

The statement for (Yelm Kids First: Judson Willis, Debbie Edwards and Kathryn Cullum) says [S1]:
- After "multiple levy failures and ongoing funding shortfalls" the district "has already made difficult cuts to staffing, programs, athletics, and student opportunities."
- The state "does not fully fund" many services, and the levy supports teachers, paraeducators, counselors, nurses, social workers, safety staff, technology, athletics and career readiness.
- Its rebuttal agrees "Olympia should be held accountable" but says "rejecting this levy will not fix that failure."

## Arguments against

The statement against (Martin Miller and Frank Vance) says [S1]:
- "Four times Yelm voters have said No." The district restores "none of the programs already cut," which means "Asking citizens to pay more for less."
- The district "used one-time capital resources to support recurring operating expenses while approving raises and maintaining compensation costs above state funding allocations."
- Basic education is the state's "paramount duty": "Stop making neighbors tax neighbors to compensate for a funding problem created in Olympia."
- Its rebuttal cites a budget of $99.7 million in spending against $90.3 million in revenue, with 84.7% of spending on compensation, and says "Four No votes require change, not another request."

In 2025 Woods said Miller, who also wrote that year's against statement, objected over taxes and being on a fixed income [S8].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: no Yelm EP&O levy is being collected in 2025 or 2026 [S4][S7]. A yes vote brings back an excess levy of an estimated $1.50 per $1,000, about $11.2 million in 2027 and $12.3 million in 2028 [S1]. Relative to current tax bills this is a new tax, not a like-for-like renewal. Direction +2.
- `spending`: the measure keeps current programs and adds a limited restoration [S4]; opponents frame their case as accountability first [S1]. Following the Spokane convention for district operating levies, `spending` is not mapped.
- `local-control`: both sides invoke local control. The district and board say a yes vote avoids state financial oversight [S4][S5]. Opponents call for "meaningful local authority over education" by pressing the state [S1]. The guide puts school governance outside `local-control`, and the two sides use the term in opposite directions. Not mapped.
- `parental-rights`: the levy funds staff, programs and operations [S1]. Nothing in the sources concerns parental notification, record access or curriculum opt-outs. Not mapped.
