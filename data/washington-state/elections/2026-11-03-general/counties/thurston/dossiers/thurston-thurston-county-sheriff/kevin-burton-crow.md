## Background

- Kevin Burton-Crow (Prefers Democratic Party) is a special victims detective with the Thurston County Sheriff's Office (2024-present), previously a patrol deputy (2021-2024) and volunteer reserve deputy (2014-2021); he has not held elected office [S1].
- He served 20 years in the U.S. Army (2001-2021), retiring as a Sergeant First Class, with deployments to Afghanistan, Iraq and the United Arab Emirates; in the Army he was a sexual harassment/assault response victim advocate and unit substance-use prevention leader [S1][S3].
- At the Sheriff's Office he also lists roles as Marine Services Unit and Search and Rescue coordinator and as one of two instructors for the Active Bystandership for Law Enforcement (ABLE) peer-intervention program [S3].
- He is president of the Thurston County Deputy Sheriff's Association (WA FOP Local 38), the deputies' union [S1][S3][S6]; one passage on his campaign biography page lists him as vice president, while its "Fast Facts" and the pamphlet say president [S1][S3].
- Education: M.S. in criminal justice (Troy University) and Master of Public Administration (The Evergreen State College) [S1][S3]. He was American Legion Law Enforcement Officer of the Year in 2023 [S1][S6].
- In the August 4, 2026 primary he received 24,254 votes (28.99%) to Sheriff Derek Sanders' 59,143 (70.69%) [S7].

## Positions

### Public safety and policing
- Says he will seek outside accreditation through the Washington Association of Sheriffs and Police Chiefs, as Mason County, Grays Harbor County and the Lacey Police Department have, with a full policy review, independent assessments and internal audits [S1][S4][S6].
- Pledges to publish the office's policies and train deputies in "victim-centered approaches and harm reduction" [S1].
- Says deputy performance should be judged "by their impact on the community, not just by how many stops or arrests they make," and that "Public safety success should be measured not only by arrests, but by prevention, recovery, and community outcomes" [S4].
- Would explore moving from the Field Training Officer model toward a Police Training Officer model that emphasizes community engagement and problem-solving, and keep initial training at or above peer agencies [S4].
- Proposes a county abatement and compliance team (Sheriff's Office, Public Health, planning, environmental health, code enforcement) for problem properties, describing the goal as "early intervention and prevention," not punishment, and distinguishing these properties from people "struggling with housing and living in RVs" [S4].
- Proposes regional multi-agency disaster exercises every other year, citizen academies and rotating community advisory groups [S4][S6].
- Says he wants to "move past just reacting to crime" and use "practical, evidence-based solutions" [S2].

### Budget and spending
- Pledges "clear annual budget disclosures so taxpayers never have to dig to find out how their money is spent" and to "spend your tax dollars wisely, with your input" [S1].
- Says every major program should show how it improves safety, spending should be linked to measurable outcomes, and the office should publish annual program reports; argues accreditation and risk management reduce litigation and liability costs [S4].

### Partisanship
- Says he runs as a Democrat "because I believe being upfront and honest with voters matters," and lists values including support for labor unions and collective bargaining, access to health care, public education, fiscal responsibility and protecting constitutional rights; says the office should apply the law in a non-partisan way [S2].
