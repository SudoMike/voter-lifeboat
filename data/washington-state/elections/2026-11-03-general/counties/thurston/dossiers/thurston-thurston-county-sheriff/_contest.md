## What the office does

The sheriff is in charge of county law enforcement, the jail, civil and legal process and emergency operations, for a four-year term [S2]. Under state law the sheriff is the county's chief executive officer and conservator of the peace: the sheriff and deputies enforce state law, arrest people who break it, defend the county against threats to public peace and carry out court process and orders [S3]. In Thurston County the office patrols the unincorporated county and runs the county corrections facility (see candidate dossiers).

## Race dynamics

Sheriff Derek Sanders (Prefers Independent Party), elected in 2022, seeks a second term against Kevin Burton-Crow (Prefers Democratic Party), a special victims detective in the same office, a retired Army sergeant first class and president of the deputies' union, the Thurston County Deputy Sheriff's Association [S1][S4]. In the two-candidate August 4 primary Sanders received 59,143 votes (70.69%) to Burton-Crow's 24,254 (28.99%), the largest margin among the county's contested primary races [S5].

Sanders runs on his first-term record: staffing up about 30 percent, new equipment, vehicles, pay and benefits, training and de-escalation, and a proposed mental-health wing at the jail; he lists endorsements from officials across parties and jurisdictions (derek-sanders.md S1, S2, S3; [S4]). Burton-Crow runs on outside accreditation, published policies, victim-centered and harm-reduction training, annual budget disclosures and judging deputies by community outcomes rather than stops and arrests; he is endorsed by the county Democrats, labor groups and the sheriff's captains' association (kevin-burton-crow.md S1, S4, S5; [S4]). Sanders has raised more ($90.0k vs. $51.1k) (derek-sanders.md S8; kevin-burton-crow.md S8). Neither candidate has a public position on I-645, I-1 or I-638.

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Experience and renewal:** a one-term incumbent sheriff with 12 years in law enforcement versus a first-time candidate who is a detective and union president inside the office.
2. **Reform and accountability:** Burton-Crow's platform centers outside accreditation, published policies and outcome reporting; Sanders cites body-camera review after use of force and points to the existing record.
3. **Public safety approach:** Sanders emphasizes staffing, vehicle pursuits and jail capacity (with a mental-health wing); Burton-Crow frames success as prevention, recovery and community outcomes, with harm-reduction training.
4. **Spending:** both pledge careful use of tax dollars; Sanders cites investments in pay, equipment and facilities and freezing his own salary, Burton-Crow ties spending to measurable outcomes and annual disclosures.
5. **Partisanship:** Sanders runs as a nonpartisan independent; Burton-Crow runs as a Democrat and says voters should know his values.
