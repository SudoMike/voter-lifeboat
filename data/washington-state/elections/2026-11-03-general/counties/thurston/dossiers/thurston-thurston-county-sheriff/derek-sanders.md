## Background

- Derek Sanders (Prefers Independent Party) was elected Thurston County Sheriff in 2022 and took office in January 2023 [S1][S3][S4].
- He has 12 years of law enforcement experience: a Lacey Police Department community service officer from 2014, a Thurston County deputy from 2016, then sheriff [S1][S4]. He received the Medal of Valor and two Life Saving Awards [S1].
- Training listed: Basic Law Enforcement Academy, 21st Century Policing, and LEEDA supervisor, command and executive leadership institutes [S1].
- He says he is running as a "nonpartisan independent" because impartiality is central to effective law enforcement [S3].
- In the August 4, 2026 primary he received 59,143 votes (70.69%) to Kevin Burton-Crow's 24,254 (28.99%), the largest margin among contested races on the county's primary ballot [S5].

## Positions

### Public safety and policing
- Plan: "practical, data-informed strategies to reduce crime," recruiting and retaining deputies through competitive pay and training, and accountability measures such as mandatory supervisor review of body-worn camera footage after use-of-force incidents [S2].
- Describes the office as "a statewide leader in public communication, training standards, and proven de-escalation tactics," and says deputies "tactically leverage time, distance, and cover to save lives" [S1][S2].
- Wants a mental health wing at the county jail, "still secured and ... still part of the jail, but ... operated by mental health professionals instead of corrections deputies," and says the jail has a space crisis [S4].
- In 2025 he defended vehicle pursuits after a complaint about his tactics, saying a gang-member arrest "was only made possible by the fact that pursuits are now authorized in Washington State" and rejecting the view that pursuits cause deaths: "The people who are killing people in these car chases ... are sitting in jail right now" [S7]. The complaint alleged he regularly patrols at night himself looking for pursuits [S7].
- Says the sheriff should lead "from the front lines" rather than "from behind a desk" [S1].

### Budget and spending
- Pledges to invest in employees, facilities, equipment and long-term infrastructure "while being a responsible steward of taxpayer dollars" [S2].
- Asked the county Salary Commission in 2026 to freeze his own salary, writing that a raise "would be highly inappropriate at this time" given cost-of-living pressures; he says he made a similar request, granted, in 2025 because of the county budget deficit [S6].

## Record

- His campaign says that when he took office the agency was "the second-worst staffed sheriff's office in the state," with 33 working patrol deputies and eight detectives, and that it now has 55 patrol deputies and 18 detectives after equipment, vehicle, training and pay improvements made with county leadership [S3]. The JOLT reported staffing up around 30 percent in his first term [S4].
- His campaign cites implementation of body-worn cameras, planning for a new sheriff's headquarters and improved corrections training facilities [S3].
- Salary freeze requests in 2025 and 2026 [S6].
