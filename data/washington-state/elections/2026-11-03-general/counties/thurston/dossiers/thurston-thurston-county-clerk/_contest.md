## What the office does

The County Clerk preserves the records of the opinions, decisions and judgments of the Superior Court; the term is four years [S2]. The office is where the public files protection orders, family-law and other court matters and seeks court records [S1]. One candidate puts its caseload at about 350,000 active cases and 7.5 million documents, handled by just over 30 employees [S4].

## Race dynamics

- Open seat: incumbent Clerk Linda Myhre Enlow is not on the ballot and endorses Garrett Cady [S1][S4].
- Nicole Miller (Prefers Democratic Party) is a child-welfare consultant and former Thurston County Racial Equity Program Manager with nearly 30 years in public service; Garrett Cady (Prefers Independent Party) is a Clerk's Office proceedings specialist and former juvenile court assistant [S1].
- The backdrop is a county budget cut: Cady says the commissioners' 3-2 vote on the 2026-2027 budget eliminated Clerk's Office positions and closed the office to the public, and that positions have since been restored [S4]. Miller says limited in-person counter service resumed on September 21, 2026 after months without it [S5]. Both candidates pledge to keep in-person service open [S4][S5].
- The certified August 4 primary was close: Miller 40,864 votes (50.72%), Cady 39,251 (48.72%) [S3].

## Differentiating issue axes

- **experience:** Cady runs on hands-on work inside the Clerk's Office and the support of the incumbent clerk, office staff and 21 other county clerks [S4]; Miller runs on decades of management and leadership outside the office and a pledge to change how it serves the public [S1][S5].
- **reform:** Cady runs as an independent and argues the clerk should be free of political influence, like nonpartisan judges [S1][S4]. No statement by Miller on party politics in the office was found; her endorsements are mainly current local and 22nd LD elected officials, including three county commissioners and the 22nd LD's state senator and representatives [S6].
- **spending:** both want restored in-person service after the budget cuts; Cady criticizes the cut as disproportionate [S4], while Miller says the office's budget challenges are real but closing the doors to the public should not be the answer [S5].
