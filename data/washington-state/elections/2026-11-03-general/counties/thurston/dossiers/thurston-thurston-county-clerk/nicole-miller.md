## Background

- Nicole Miller (Prefers Democratic Party) is an independent child-welfare and community-wellbeing consultant and a former Thurston County Racial Equity Program Manager, with nearly 30 years of public service at the local, state and national levels [S1]. She has no prior elected experience [S1].
- She describes herself as a lifelong Thurston County resident [S3]. Her career began at the state Department of Social and Health Services in 1995; roles include social worker, Indian Child Welfare program consultant, juvenile rehabilitation clinical consultant and trainer, and, at the county, helping develop Thurston County's Diverse Business Inclusion Plan [S3].
- She holds an M.A. in counseling psychology and a B.A. in psychology from Saint Martin's University and is a Certified Diversity Executive [S1][S3]. Community roles: League of Women Voters board member (including chair of its Diversity, Equity, Inclusion and Justice Committee), Olympia Family Theater board, former NOVA Middle School board [S1][S3].
- In the certified August 4 primary she received 40,864 votes (50.72%) to Garrett Cady's 39,251 (48.72%) [S8].

## Positions

- **Public access:** says she is running to "restore public trust, reopen meaningful access to the office," and restore helpful in-person service [S1]. After limited counter service resumed on September 21, 2026, she said "part-time access is not enough" and pledged "reliable, full-time in-person access" [S2][S4].
- **Budget:** says that as Clerk she would participate fully in county budget meetings, communicate clearly so "there should be no surprises," keep the front counter open, and "examine opportunities to increase revenue and reduce costs" while protecting staff resources [S5]. Says closing the doors "should not be our default response" to budget challenges [S5].
- **Service and management:** would address backlogs, improve customer service and workplace culture, and work with staff, labor, the courts and attorneys [S1]. Says access to court services should not depend on income, disability, language or ability to afford an attorney [S1]. Cites a Clerk's Office email reply to an attorney's office as an example of customer-service and training problems [S4].
- **Experience argument:** says the Clerk is an elected executive and that "familiarity with existing procedures is not the same as experience leading an organization"; disputes her opponent's use of the "Deputy Clerk" title, saying he has spent three years in the Clerk's Office and his position is Clerk's Judicial Proceedings Specialist, not Chief Deputy Clerk [S2][S6].
- **Taxes, housing, public safety, climate:** no positions found.
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no position found.
- **Parents and schools:** no position found on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs.
