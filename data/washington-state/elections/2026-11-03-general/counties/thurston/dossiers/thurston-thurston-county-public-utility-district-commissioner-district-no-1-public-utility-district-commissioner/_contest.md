## What the office does

Thurston PUD is a water utility: it owns and operates 271 water systems serving about 25,000 people in Thurston County and also runs systems in Pierce, Lewis, King and Grays Harbor counties [S3]. A three-member elected board governs it [S3][S4]. Commissioners set policy, adopt rules by resolution, pass the annual budget, approve rates, bonds and other financing, and appoint or remove the general manager [S3]. The county pamphlet adds that the PUD plans water supply, sets customer rates, and can levy property taxes within the district [S8]. Full terms are six years [S2][S8].

## Who votes

Only voters in Commissioner District 1 vote in its primary. Voters of the entire PUD vote in the general election for each commissioner district [S2]. The PUD is countywide, so all Thurston County voters see this race in November; the PDC lists the jurisdiction at 213,297 voters [S6].

## Race dynamics

- **Appointed incumbent.** Commissioner Linda Oosterman resigned as of July 31, 2025. The board said the appointee would serve until the November 2026 results are certified [S3]. On Sept. 2, 2025 the two remaining commissioners interviewed six applicants and advanced Jim Campbell, then the PUD's director of field operations (jim-campbell.md S7, S8, S9) [S5]. The seat on the ballot is the four-year unexpired remainder of the term [S1].
- **Primary.** Campbell, Troy Kirby and Bruce D. Wilkinson Jr. were on the August primary ballot (primary `_contest.md`). The PDC lists both Campbell and Kirby as "Qualified for general" [S6][S7]. Vote totals were not retrieved: the Secretary of State results site did not return 2026 primary data to automated requests, and no news story with the totals was found.
- **Insider vs. critic of the board.** Campbell, a 22-year water-industry worker and former PUD field operations director, runs on technical experience and preventive maintenance [S1]. He is endorsed by the other two sitting commissioners and by his predecessor [S11]. No endorsements for Kirby were found (troy-kirby.md). Kirby, a former Lacey parks commissioner and state agency communications staffer, runs on affordability [S1]. His campaign fact sheet faults "the current Board" on fixed fees, a PFAS surcharge, emergency bid waivers, connection charges and acquisitions [S10]. The 2026 rates, fees and surcharges were adopted on Sept. 30, 2025 by Commissioners Olsen and Stearns, before Campbell joined the board (jim-campbell.md S10, S11) [S9].
- **Rates.** In July 2026 Campbell recommended a 1.9 percent rate increase for the 2027 budget, to be revisited later in the year [S12]. Kirby would pay for PFAS cleanup and major projects with grants, infrastructure funding and polluter settlements [S1].
- **Money.** Campbell reports $18,645.67 raised and $24,198.30 spent, including $7,500 in loans (PDC, updated 2026-10-04) [S6]. Kirby registered under the PDC "mini" option and had no reports on file [S7].

## What differentiates the candidates

- **Record vs. renewal.** An appointed incumbent who joined the PUD staff in 2017 (jim-campbell.md S4) faces a challenger whose public experience is a city parks board and two unsuccessful campaigns (troy-kirby.md S5, S6) [S1]. The `experience` axis does not apply to PublicUtility contests in this rubric, so this is shown descriptively only.
- **View of the PUD's current direction.** Campbell runs to "retain" his seat with the board's backing [S11]. Kirby's campaign is built on a critique of the board's rates, surcharges and bidding practices [S10].
