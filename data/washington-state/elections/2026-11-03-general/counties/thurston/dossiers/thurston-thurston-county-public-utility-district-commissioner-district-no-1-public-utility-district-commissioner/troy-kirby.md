## Background

Troy Kirby lists seven years as a Lacey Parks Commissioner, overseeing parks and civic infrastructure projects [S1][S2]. He leads community outreach, media projects and public communications for a state agency, and is a former small business owner [S1][S2]. He is a Timberline High School graduate, attended Eastern Washington University and Seattle University, and taught business classes at Saint Martin's University [S1][S2]. He received the Lacey South Sound Chamber's 2019 Entrepreneur of the Year award [S1][S2]. He is a Gateway Rotarian and a founding board member of the Gold Star Family Memorial and Purple Heart Monument of Lacey [S1][S2].

**Prior campaigns.** He ran for Lacey City Council in 2019 and lost in the primary [S1][S5]. In 2023 he ran for Port of Olympia Commissioner, District 5, and lost the general election to Sarah Tonge, 46.77% to 52.8% [S1][S6].

## Positions

- **Rates and affordability.** Says "families deserve reliable, quality water service at a fair price" and that people "pay more in taxes and utility costs but feel like they receive less service in return" [S1][S2]. Says water costs affect housing costs [S1].
- **Paying for PFAS cleanup and big projects.** Would pursue state grants, infrastructure funding and polluter settlements to pay for PFAS cleanup and other major projects [S1]. His fact sheet calls the PUD's $1.78 monthly PFAS ("Emerging Contaminants") surcharge "a tax on the victims, not the polluters." It says the PUD has secured over $10 million in grants and loan forgiveness plus settlement money from 3M and DuPont [S3].
- **Fixed charges.** His fact sheet says base rates and fixed surcharges (a $48.69 base rate, $16 capital surcharge and $1.78 PFAS surcharge in a Group A system) add up to $66.47 a month "before a single drop of water leaves your tap," and that this structure "punishes" conservation [S3].
- **Maintenance and bidding.** Wants to "fix problems before they become emergencies," improve aging infrastructure, hold contractors accountable and expand backup power for storms [S1]. His fact sheet criticizes a budget policy of running most assets "to failure" and the board's repeated emergency waivers of competitive bidding. It lists ten waivers declared from 2021 through December 2025 [S3].
- **Connection fees and acquisitions.** His fact sheet says the general facility charge to connect a new home rose from $4,500 in 2022 to $8,000 in 2026. It also says neighborhoods the PUD acquires carry large loan-repayment surcharges, citing $134.01 a month in Dana Passage [S3].
- **Customer assistance.** Supports "stronger assistance programs" so families do not lose service in hard times [S1]. His fact sheet criticizes the 2024 end of the equal payment plan and the board's hiring of a lobbyist (Resolution 25-39) [S3]. The minutes confirm the lobbyist contract was adopted unanimously on Nov. 4, 2025 [S8].
- **Aquifers and land use.** Would protect aquifers and water rights through "smart regional planning and cooperation" [S1]. His fact sheet opposes siting lithium battery energy storage systems over South County aquifers in Rochester and Tenino [S3].
- **Open government.** Pledges to support open public meetings and listen to residents [S1].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no statements found.
- **Parents and schools:** no statements found.

The fact sheet's figures are the campaign's own. Two checks against the minutes: the 2026 rates, fees and surcharges were adopted on Sept. 30, 2025 by Commissioners Olsen and Stearns [S7], and Jim Campbell first sat as a commissioner on Oct. 28, 2025 [S9].
