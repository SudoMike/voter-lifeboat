## Background

Jim Campbell is the appointed Thurston PUD commissioner for District 1 [S1][S5]. He is a lifelong Thurston County resident and Olympia High School graduate [S1][S4]. He worked 20 years at the Olympia Brewery as a foreman and was a member of Teamsters Local 378 [S1][S2]. After the brewery closed in 2003 he retrained under the federal Trade Adjustment Act and went into the water industry, first at "The Water Company" and, from 2017, at Thurston PUD [S1][S4]. He was the PUD's Director of Field Operations, overseeing about 270 water systems [S1][S4]. He lists certifications as a Water Distribution Manager II, water treatment plant operator, cross-connection control specialist, electrician, plumber and electrical administrator [S1]. He reports receiving the state Department of Health's Commitment to Excellence Award (his site dates it 2024) [S1][S2].

**Appointment.** Commissioner Linda Oosterman resigned as of July 31, 2025, and the board sought applicants for a roughly 14-month appointment lasting until the November 2026 results are certified [S6]. Six applicants were interviewed on Sept. 2, 2025, including Campbell and Bruce Wilkinson Jr. [S7][S8]. Commissioner Olsen's nomination of James Cooper and Commissioner Stearns's nomination of Wilkinson each failed for lack of a second. Olsen then nominated Campbell, Stearns seconded, and the board directed the general manager to advance his appointment [S8]. The board voted unanimously on Sept. 9, 2025 to appoint him, effective when he took the oath [S9]. He was still listed as Director of Field Operations at the Oct. 14, 2025 meeting [S11]. He first sat as a commissioner on Oct. 28, 2025 [S12].

## Positions

- **Infrastructure and maintenance.** Describes utility management as "infrastructure first, cost second, and safety always" and says preventive maintenance and planning "help stabilize rates" [S1][S2].
- **Costs and oversight.** Says his field experience lets him "ask tough questions, evaluate costs realistically" and judge what a contractor's project "should cost and how long it should take" [S1][S2].
- **Workforce.** Supports "a certified, well-trained workforce" and presents himself as a voice for field staff [S1][S2].
- **Rates.** As commissioner, recommended a 1.9 percent rate increase for the 2027 budget, to be re-evaluated when more fiscal information is available (has done) [S14].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no statements found.
- **Parents and schools:** no statements found.

## Record

- **Before he joined the board.** The 2026 operating and capital budgets and the 2026 rates, fees, charges and surcharges (Resolutions 25-24, 25-25, 25-26) were adopted on Sept. 30, 2025 by Commissioners Olsen and Stearns, the only two commissioners present [S10].
- **Votes as commissioner.** Seconded the motion adopting Resolution 25-39, a professional services agreement for a lobbyist, and the 2025/2026 budget changes requested by the general manager, both adopted unanimously on Nov. 4, 2025 [S13]. Seconded Resolution 25-38, an amendment to the Cedar Shores 677 generator surcharge, on Oct. 28, 2025 [S12]. Moved Resolution 26-02, which standardizes the terms of new surcharges for water-system improvements that state or local health agencies do not require; it passed unanimously on Jan. 27, 2026 [S15].
- **2027 budget.** Proposed the 1.9 percent rate increase for 2027. Commissioner Olsen supported it but asked for a set date to revisit it [S14].
