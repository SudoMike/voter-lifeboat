## What the office does

Thurston PUD is a water utility: it owns and operates 271 water systems serving about 25,000 people in Thurston County and also runs systems in neighboring counties [S9]. A three-member elected board sets policy, passes the budget, approves rates, bonds and other financing, and appoints or removes the general manager [S9]. The county pamphlet describes the office as providing utility services and water planning and setting customer rates, and notes the PUD can levy property taxes within the district [S6]. Commissioners serve six-year terms [S2][S6].

## Who votes

Voters of the entire PUD vote in the general election for each commissioner district [S2]. The PUD is countywide; the PDC lists the jurisdiction at 213,297 voters [S4][S5]. With only two candidates filed, the race was not on the August primary ballot [S4][S5].

## Race dynamics

- **Open seat.** The current District 3 commissioner, Chris Stearns, is not on the ballot [S1][S3]. He and the other two sitting commissioners, Jim Campbell and Russ Olsen, endorse Chris Pettit [S7].
- **Candidates.** Pettit is the state Department of Health's Drinking Water State Revolving Fund program manager and a former executive director of the Washington State Conservation Commission, with a law degree and earlier water-policy posts in Florida [S1]. Jeff Curry is a small business owner whose utility experience is a board seat for the Scott Lake community water district [S1]; PDC records show he ran for county treasurer (2022), Tumwater school board (2023, 2025) and governor (2024) without winning [S8].
- **Platforms.** Both lead with affordability [S1]. Curry adds solar generation and a public water-quality phone app [S1]; Pettit cites a 25% rise in rates over five years and proposes affordability measures from a legislative report, more transparency on rate-setting and stronger conservation and climate resilience (chris-pettit.md S1, S7).
- **Money.** Pettit reports $7,961.60 raised and $3,643.83 spent, including $4,280 in loans [S5]. Curry registered under the PDC "mini" option and had no reports on file [S4].
- Neither candidate has a public position on I-645, I-1 or I-638.

## What differentiates the candidates

- **Professional water-policy background vs. a community water-board seat:** Pettit's career is in state and Florida water agencies; Curry's utility experience is the Scott Lake water district board [S1].
- **Relationship to the current board:** Pettit has the endorsement of all three sitting commissioners [S7]; Curry lists no endorsements (jeff-curry.md S1, S2).
- **Priorities:** Pettit emphasizes transparency, assistance programs and conservation; Curry emphasizes rates, solar and a consumer water-quality app [S1].
