## Background

- Chris Pettit is program manager of the Drinking Water State Revolving Fund (DWSRF) at the state Department of Health; he has not held elected office [S1][S4].
- Earlier positions he lists: executive director of the Washington State Conservation Commission; director of water policy at the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services; environmental policy manager for Palm Beach County; and attorney for the Southwest and South Florida water management districts [S1].
- Education: B.A. in political economy (New College), J.D. (University of Florida) and Master of Laws (University of Cape Town) [S1].
- Community and civic roles he lists include the PARC Foundation, the Thurston County Fair Board, chair of the CIFA State/EPA Workgroup, the Thurston County Chamber of Commerce and Leadership Thurston County; he also lists membership in the Thurston County Democrats and Thurston County Democratic Women and serving as a delegate for the LD 35 Democrats [S1]. The office is nonpartisan [S1].
- His campaign says he has supported public water systems across the state through infrastructure funding, technical support, policy development and long-term planning [S2].
- **Contact with the PUD as a state program manager.** The PUD borrows from the DWSRF: on Dec. 9, 2025 the board approved DWSRF loan contracts for PFAS remediation projects [S6]. At the Feb. 10, 2026 board meeting Pettit, introduced in the minutes as DWSRF program manager, spoke about the PUD's acquisition of the Dana Passage water system, answered commissioners' questions about PFAS remediation bidding and funding, and addressed the board on the state Public Works Trust Fund [S4].

## Positions

- **Affordability.** Says utility rates "have risen over 25% in the past five years" and pledges to "examine every opportunity to increase affordability and reduce the cost of service," including solutions from a recent legislative report on utility affordability [S1]. His website says he facilitated that report, which called for low-income assistance programs and lowering the cost of service through state and federal subsidies [S7]. He attributes the rate increases to COVID, regulatory requirements and federal funding in the market, and expects tariffs and state and federal budget pressures to add to them [S7].
- **System planning and acquisitions.** Says the PUD should continue long-term system planning, asset management and fiscal planning, including "a long term plan to acquire additional systems and to divest in those systems that are far afield and increase overall costs" [S7].
- **Transparency and public input.** Pledges to explain how rates are structured and projects prioritized [S1]; would strengthen the Utility Rate Advisory Committee, expand education and outreach, form additional water and environmental advisory groups, and make rate and budget documents easier to find and understand [S7].
- **Environment and climate.** Says the PUD must "modernize infrastructure with care, strengthen resilience" as "climate change and human activity put pressure on our water resources" [S1]. Wants the PUD to expand conservation programs, protect water sources, practice "holistic watershed management" and adapt to climate change [S7].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no statements found.
- **Parents and schools:** no statements found.
