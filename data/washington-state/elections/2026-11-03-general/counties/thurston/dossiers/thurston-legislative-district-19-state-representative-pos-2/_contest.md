## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term [S1]. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the two candidates who advanced from the August primary.

## Race dynamics
The 19th District covers Pacific and Wahkiakum counties and parts of Grays Harbor, Lewis, Cowlitz and Thurston counties [S2]. Rep. Joel McEntire (R-Cathlamet), first elected in 2020, faces a rematch with Terry Carlson (D-Longview), president of the Cowlitz-Wahkiakum Central Labor Council, whom he beat with 62% in 2024 [S2][S5]. Carlson placed first in the four-way primary with 43.23% (18,354 votes) to McEntire's 41.62% (17,670) [S3]; two other Republicans took about 15% combined, so the November race remains competitive [S4].

McEntire runs on fiscal restraint and opposition to recent tax increases, including the 2026 income tax, and on timber and shellfish interests (joel-mcentire.md S1, S6, S8). Carlson runs on affordability, infrastructure, rural healthcare and labor, and openly supports the millionaires' tax (terry-carlson.md S4). In September 2026 the Legislative Ethics Board fined McEntire and a district court issued a protection order against him over Facebook posts to constituents; Carlson has made conduct toward constituents a campaign theme (joel-mcentire.md S14, S15; terry-carlson.md S9). Neither candidate has stated a position on I-645, I-1 or I-638.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Carlson supports ESSB 6346; McEntire opposes it and the 2025-26 tax increases.
2. **Record vs. renewal:** three-term incumbent with two 2025 enacted bills versus a labor leader without public office.
3. **Parents and schools:** McEntire says parents have a right to know what is taught and backs school choice, and voted against the 2025 parents' rights rollback; Carlson stresses school funding, career and technical education, and school-based health services.
4. **Healthcare and spending:** Carlson would have the state backfill federal Medicare and Medicaid cuts and invest in rural infrastructure; McEntire calls for living within means.
5. **Conduct in office:** McEntire's ethics fine and protection order are a campaign issue.
