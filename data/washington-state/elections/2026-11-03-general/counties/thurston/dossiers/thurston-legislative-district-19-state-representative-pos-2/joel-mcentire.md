## Background
Joel McEntire (R-Cathlamet) is the incumbent in Position 2, elected in 2020, 2022 and 2024, and is seeking a fourth term [S1][S11]. He was chair of the Wahkiakum County Republican Party from 2018 [S1]. He served 15 years in the U.S. Marine Corps with three overseas deployments and has worked as a public-school and university educator [S1]; his campaign site says he taught at John C. Thomas Middle School and holds a BS in evolutionary ecology from Central Washington University and a master's in mathematics education [S1][S3]. His House committees are Education, Labor & Workplace Standards, and Postsecondary Education & Workforce (assistant ranking minority member), plus several joint committees including the Washington Marine Resources Advisory Council [S5].

In the August 4 primary he received 17,670 votes (41.62%), second to Democrat Terry Carlson's 18,354 (43.23%) [S12]; two other Republicans took about 15% combined [S13]. He beat Carlson in 2024 with 62% of the vote [S11].

In September 2026 the Legislative Ethics Board fined him $500 ($250 suspended) after finding that a Facebook comment to a constituent suggested he could use his office to look up her confidential benefits information [S14]. Later that month a Cowlitz County District Court judge granted a different constituent a one-year protection order over two of his Facebook posts, finding them outside First Amendment protection; McEntire argued the posts were protected speech [S15]. The Daily World reports he has received more than 20 ethics complaints [S15].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** his pamphlet statement says recent years have brought "the sharpest tax increases" with "very limited returns" [S1]. He called ESSB 6346 "blatantly unconstitutional" and said "your income is theirs" under the bill [S6]. He opposed 2026 bills on local option taxes (HB 2442), insurance taxes, a mortgage B&O tax and prescription-drug B&O rates, and says "we do not need new or increased taxes to balance our state budget" [S7].
- **Spending:** calls for "living within our means" [S1] and "right-sizing our government" [S7].
- **Public safety:** his caucus site says House Republicans prioritize public safety, supporting law enforcement, preventing crime and helping victims [S4].
- **Climate, forestry and natural resources:** opposed the Forest Practices Board's 2025 stream-buffer rule, saying it takes about 200,000 acres of timberland out of production and that "environmental activism has put enormous pressure on lawmakers" [S8]. His campaign site says Seattle politicians have "waged war against our fishing, forestry, and farms" [S2].
- **Local control:** says "a one-size-fits-all approach does not work in Washington state" [S4]. Co-signed the district session report opposing SB 5974's state removal of elected sheriffs [S9].
- **Immigration:** the district session report he co-signed criticizes SB 5974 for pushing sheriffs toward non-cooperation with federal immigration authorities [S9]. He voted no on SSB 5855 on law enforcement face coverings [S24].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no position found in the sources reviewed.
- **Parents and schools:** says "parents deserve a say in their children's education and have a right to know what is being taught in the classroom" and supports school choice [S4]. Wants to help economically disadvantaged districts build new schools and address pandemic learning loss [S4]. The session report says he authored amendments on parents' rights and education in 2026 [S9].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no explicit statement on I-645 found as of 2026-10-08. He opposes ESSB 6346, the law I-645 would repeal, and says he will "continue to fight against" it [S6].
- **I-1 (parental rights):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- **ESSB 6346 (2026 income tax):** the roll call records him as excused on the final-passage vote (51-46) and on the motion to postpone; he voted yes on a block of Republican amendments the same day [S16]. His caucus release describes a statement "on his 'no' vote" [S6]; the official roll call does not show a vote from him on final passage [S16].
- **ESHB 1296 (2025 parents' rights amendments):** voted no on third reading (56-37) and on final passage as amended by the Senate (59-39); voted yes on all twelve Republican floor amendments [S17].
- **Other tax votes (2025):** no on ESSB 5813 (capital gains and estate tax rates) [S18], ESSB 5814 (excise taxes on services) [S19] and ESHB 2081 (business and occupation tax increases) [S20].
- **Budget:** no on the 2025-27 operating budget, ESSB 5167 [S26].
- **Housing:** no on EHB 1217 (2025 rent and fee increase limits) [S21] and on third reading of ESHB 2266 (2026 shelter and supportive housing siting); excused on its final vote [S25].
- **Climate:** no on 2SHB 1409 (2025 clean fuels program) [S22].
- **Public safety:** no on 2SSB 5974 final passage after voting yes on Republican amendments [S23].
- **Bills enacted:** SHB 1309 (2025) creates a Department of Agriculture research program on burrowing shrimp that damage oyster beds, and EHB 1393 (2025) requires schools to let students wear items of cultural significance at graduation [S10].
- **Labor scorecard:** Northwest Labor Press reports an 18% lifetime rating on the Washington State Labor Council scorecard [S13].
