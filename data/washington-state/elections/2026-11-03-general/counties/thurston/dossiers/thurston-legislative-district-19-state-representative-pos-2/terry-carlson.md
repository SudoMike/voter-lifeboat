## Background
Terry Carlson (D-Longview) is president of the Cowlitz-Wahkiakum Central Labor Council and secretary of IAM Woodworkers Local W536 [S1]. He grew up in Granite Falls, holds a psychology degree from Washington State University, and spent 25 years in the timber industry [S1][S3]. Northwest Labor Press reports he worked as a non-union logger before joining Weyerhaeuser in Longview as a processor operator, and that after a multiple sclerosis diagnosis he took a job with American Income Life, represented by OPEIU Local 277 [S5]. His campaign site calls him a fourth-generation logger and says "culture wars occupy our leaders and keep them too busy to bother with our concerns" [S2]. His pamphlet statement lists only union posts under elected experience [S1].

He ran against McEntire in 2024 and lost with McEntire taking 62% [S7]. In the August 4, 2026 primary he placed first with 18,354 votes (43.23%) to McEntire's 17,670 (41.62%) [S8]; two other Republicans took about 15% combined [S6].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** supports ESSB 6346: "I'm not going to lose sleep over a tax on somebody making more than a million dollars a year," says the law expands the Working Families Tax Credit and cuts small-business taxes, and estimates 50 to 100 district households would pay it [S4]. Also says "I will always oppose taxes on working families" and that taxes, fees and regulations should be judged by their cost to ordinary families [S4]. Platform: "Make the wealthy and big corporations pay their share" [S4].
- **Spending and infrastructure:** calls for investment in energy generation, roads and bridges, rural broadband, ports and industrial infrastructure, and says rural communities should get "their fair share of state investment" [S4].
- **Housing:** "Build more housing. Cut unnecessary costs and delays that make homes more expensive to build" [S4].
- **Climate and energy:** "Protect our environment without making working families carry the cost at the gas pump"; calls for more power generation to attract employers [S4].
- **Healthcare:** pledges to protect rural hospitals and maternity services, expand behavioral health and addiction treatment, and have the state fill gaps left by federal Medicare and Medicaid cuts [S4].
- **Labor:** pledges to protect collective bargaining, pensions and apprenticeships [S4]. Says "Organized labor is the purest form of social justice we have" [S5].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no position found in the sources reviewed. His platform cites the higher maternal mortality rate for rural women as a healthcare problem [S4].
- **Parents and schools:** supports more public-school funding "while giving local communities a stronger voice," expanded career and technical education, and expanded school-based health and mental health services [S4]. No position on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs found.
- **Conduct in office:** says representatives should not engage in "online fights, insults, or harassing the people I'm supposed to represent" [S4]; after McEntire's protection order he said people should be able to disagree with elected officials "without being intimidated or harassed" [S9].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

## Record
No public office record. As labor council president he joined unionists pressing the Port of Longview over a non-union contractor's safety record and set up a dedicated fund for families affected by the Nippon Dynawave mill accident [S5].
