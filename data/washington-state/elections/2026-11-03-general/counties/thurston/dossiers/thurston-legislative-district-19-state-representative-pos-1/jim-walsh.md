## Background
Jim Walsh (R-Aberdeen) is the incumbent in Position 1, first elected in 2016 and seeking a sixth term; he has chaired the Washington State Republican Party since 2023 [S5]. He is owner and publisher of Silver Lake Publishing (since 1998), holds a BA from Amherst College, and lists volunteer work as a Grays Harbor YMCA youth basketball coach [S1]. In the House he is ranking minority member of the Civil Rights & Judiciary Committee, assistant ranking minority member of State Government & Tribal Relations, and a member of Capital Budget [S4]. His caucus site lists his priorities as family-wage jobs, school funding, and protecting taxpayers, property rights and gun rights [S3].

In the August 4 primary Walsh received 24,113 votes (55.5%) to Kevin Moynihan's 19,272 (44.35%) across the district [S6].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** opposed ESSB 6346, calling it illegal and unconstitutional under Article VII's uniformity rule, and describes himself as the author of I-2111, the 2024 initiative barring a state tax on personal income [S22]. State filing records list him as the filer of the "No Taxes Based on Personal Income" initiatives to the Legislature in 2023, including IL-2111, and of 2023 initiatives to repeal the capital gains tax [S26]. In April 2026 he filed two initiatives to the people titled "Repeal the Unconstitutional WA State Income Tax" (IP26-211, IP26-563); neither is the measure on the ballot [S28].
- **Economy and schools:** his campaign site says he "helped pass an historic reform that added more funding for our local schools and made our property taxes fairer," calls for a diversified economy with family-wage jobs, and describes him as "a voice for the Coast, not Seattle" [S2].
- **Spending and accountability:** says state spending has doubled in ten years and calls for full audits and transparency after reported losses at the King County Regional Homelessness Authority and in DSHS and DCYF payments [S21]. The district's 2026 session report attributes the income tax to spending growth and projects a possible $10 billion deficit in 2027-29 [S19]. He filed 2023 initiatives titled "Limit State Govt Spending Growth" [S26].
- **Public safety:** led House floor opposition to SB 5974, which lets a state commission remove elected sheriffs, arguing that voters who elect a sheriff should be the ones to remove one [S20]. He filed 2023 initiatives to "Restore Police Pursuit" and "Make Hard Drugs Illegal Again" [S26]. He prefiled a 2025 bill to allow tougher sentences for knowing delivery of fentanyl [S32].
- **Local control:** the session report says he opposed HB 2266 because it limits cities' and counties' ability to decide where homeless housing and shelters are located [S19]. His pamphlet statement pledges to advocate "against unlawful mandates from Olympia" [S1].
- **Housing:** voted no on final passage of EHB 1217 (2025), which limits residential rent and fee increases [S13], and no on ESHB 2266 (2026) on siting supportive and emergency housing [S17].
- **Climate and energy:** voted no on 2SHB 1409 (2025) on the clean fuels program [S14]. He filed 2023 initiatives titled "Repeal the Cap and Trade Tax" [S26].
- **Immigration:** voted no on SSB 5855 (2026) on face coverings worn by law enforcement officers [S16]. The session report criticizes SB 5974 for pushing sheriffs toward "non-cooperation with federal immigration authorities" [S19].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** his 2016 campaign site lists an endorsement by Human Life of Washington PAC [S29]. He took part in the Feb. 3, 2026 House listening session on IL26-638 (girls' sports) and IL26-001 after the majority declined to hold hearings [S19]. No direct statement of his own on transgender athletes or abortion was found in the sources reviewed.
- **Parents and schools:** state records list him as the filer of the 2023 "Washington Parents' Bill of Rights" initiatives, including IL-2081 [S26]. As party chair he said the parents' bill of rights "DOES give parents and guardians the right to be notified about school and health information involving their minor children" and opposed HB 1296's changes to it [S24]. He offered an amendment to ESHB 1296 to restore parents' right to notification when medical services given to their child could affect their insurance payments; it was not adopted [S9].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** supports repeal. As WAGOP chair he is quoted in an Aug. 3, 2026 party release announcing an amicus brief that states the party "supports enactment of IP26-645" [S23]. He voted no on ESSB 6346 [S7] and filed his own income-tax repeal initiatives in April 2026 [S28]. The certified IP26-645 was filed by Brian Heywood, not Walsh [S28].
- **I-1 (parental rights, IL26-001):** spoke at a Feb. 3, 2026 Capitol rally organized in support of IL26-001 and IL26-638, urging the crowd to defend the initiative process [S25], and took part in a House listening session on both measures [S19]. He filed IL-2081, the 2023 parents' bill of rights initiative [S26]. IL26-001 itself was filed by Larry Jensen [S27]. No explicit yes-or-no recommendation from Walsh on I-1 was found as of 2026-10-08.
- **I-638 (girls' school sports, IL26-638):** appeared at the same rally and listening session [S25][S19]. IL26-638 was filed by Brian Heywood [S27]. No explicit yes-or-no recommendation from Walsh on I-638 was found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- **ESSB 6346 (2026 income tax):** voted yes on the failed motion to postpone the bill indefinitely (38-58) and no on final passage, which carried 51-46; offered three floor amendments, all rejected [S7]. The session report says his amendments sought voter approval and protection against future expansion [S19].
- **ESHB 1296 (2025 parents' rights amendments):** voted no on third reading (56-37) and on final passage as amended by the Senate (59-39); voted yes on all twelve Republican floor amendments, including his own amendment 396 [S8][S9].
- **Other tax votes (2025):** no on ESSB 5813 (capital gains and estate tax rates, 53-45) [S10], ESSB 5814 (excise taxes on services, 50-47) [S11] and ESHB 2081 (business and occupation tax increases, 50-48) [S12].
- **Budget:** no on the 2025-27 operating budget, ESSB 5167, on conference-report final passage (52-45) [S18].
- **Public safety:** no on 2SSB 5974 final passage (54-42) after voting yes on Republican amendments, two of them his own [S15].
- **Initiatives filed:** state records show six 2023 initiatives filed by Walsh certified to the Legislature: IL-2081 (parents' bill of rights), IL-2109 (repeal capital gains tax), IL-2111 (no taxes based on personal income), IL-2113 (restore police pursuit), IL-2117 (repeal cap and trade) and IL-2124 (long-term care opt-out) [S26].
- **Bills:** none of the 13 bills he introduced in 2025 became law [S31]. A family burial ground bill he filed passed the House in 2023 and 2024 but not the Senate [S32].

## Endorsements
- His 2016 campaign site lists endorsements from the Grays Harbor Republican Party, Human Life of Washington PAC, Gun Owners Action League of Washington, Hunters Heritage Council and the Washington Realtors PAC [S29]. No 2026 endorsement list was found.

## Campaign finance
PDC (updated 2026-10-02): $60,684.94 in contributions, $15,520.38 carried forward, $81,049.91 spent and $27,562.08 in debts [S30].

## Scoring notes
- Five-term incumbent and sitting state Republican Party chair [S5].
- Filed the 2023 initiatives on income taxes, parents' rights, police pursuits, capital gains, long-term care and cap-and-trade repeal; six were certified to the Legislature [S26].
- Voted against ESSB 6346 and the 2025 tax package, and makes opposition to the income tax a central theme [S7][S10][S22].
- Opposes state oversight of elected sheriffs and state limits on local siting decisions [S19][S20].
