## What the office does

The Assessor determines the market value of all real and personal property in the county, which is used to calculate property taxes; the term is four years [S2]. The office oversees more than 120,000 parcels with $66 billion in assessed value and the calculation of more than $650 million in property taxes a year [S1].

## Race dynamics

- Two candidates who both state a preference for the Democratic Party are running for an open seat [S1]. Incumbent Assessor Steven Drew is not on the ballot and endorses JJ Olson [S1][S4].
- JJ Olson is a ten-year employee of the Assessor's Office (field appraiser, senior appraiser, now Business Applications Administrator) and the only accredited appraiser in the race [S1]. Lynda Nashed Zeman worked in executive administration in the Assessor's Office and now in the County Commissioners' Office, and was an appointed Lacey City Councilmember in 2019 [S1].
- In the certified August 4 primary, Olson received 40,570 votes (55.10%) and Zeman 30,960 (42.05%); both advance [S3].

## Differentiating issue axes

- **experience:** both candidates come from inside county government. Olson runs on technical appraisal experience and continuity with the current office, endorsed by the incumbent and 20 current and former office staff including the chief deputy assessor [S1][S4]; Zeman runs on executive administration, budget and county-wide experience [S1].
- Neither candidate has published positions on property-tax levels, housing, public safety or climate; the assessor does not set tax rates, and the race turns on administration (technology transition, staffing, communication with property owners) [S1].
