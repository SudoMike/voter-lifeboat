## Background

- Emily Clouse (Prefers Democratic Party) is the incumbent Thurston County Commissioner for District 5 and is running for a second term [S1][S2][S3].
- She is a U.S. Army veteran who served as a military intelligence analyst, then managed two family businesses, and was a supervisor in the Department of Social and Health Services' Office of Forensic Mental Health Services and a WFSE Local 443 member before taking office [S1][S2].
- In 2021 she founded the Collaborative Association for Reintegration and Education (CARE Olympia), a nonprofit for justice-impacted youth and families, and she served on the community advisory board for CYS Juvenile Diversion from 2015 to 2022 [S1][S2].
- Education: AA from South Puget Sound Community College; B.A. in psychology (cum laude) from UW Tacoma [S1].
- County board assignments she lists include chair of the Thurston Climate Mitigation Collaborative, chair of the Solid Waste Advisory Committee, vice chair of the Regional Housing Council, the Olympic Region Clean Air Agency, the Disability Board and the Joint Animal Services Commission, plus the Board of Health and the Law and Justice Council [S1]. Her website also lists the NACo Justice and Public Safety Steering Committee and the Thurston-Mason Behavioral Health Administrative Services Organization [S2].
- In the August 4, 2026 primary (District 5 voters only) she placed second with 5,119 votes (34.68%) behind Nicolas Martinez-Dunning's 5,662 (38.36%); Michelle Gipson was third with 3,722 (25.22%) [S4].
- An independent workplace investigation released in late 2024 dismissed harassment and retaliation claims involving a former aide but found her actions violated county ethics policies; the board of commissioners censured her, and the former aide's lawsuit against the county settled for $300,000 [S5]. A recall petition was dismissed by a visiting superior court judge in January 2025, and the Washington Supreme Court affirmed the dismissal on February 26, 2026, holding the charges legally or factually insufficient [S5].

## Positions

### Housing and homelessness
- Says that as Regional Housing Council chair she oversaw approval of $25 million for affordable housing, including 186 new supportive units, and says she will keep prioritizing affordability at all income levels [S1].
- Says in a second term she will expand affordable and supportive housing and strengthen regional partnerships [S2][S3].

### Climate and environment
- Cites discounted heat-pump upgrades (the Energize Thurston program) and $7.6 million for Deschutes Estuary restoration [S1][S2].
- Says she stood up to protect Capitol Forest from harmful logging practices and advanced air-quality monitoring on the ORCAA board [S2].
- Says in a second term she will put the county Comprehensive Plan's climate commitments into action, protect forests and farmland, and expand access to clean, affordable energy [S2].

### Budget and spending
- Says government must be "transparent, fiscally responsible, and accountable," and that she voted against a county budget process that "bypassed community input while cutting staff and domestic violence navigation services" [S1].
- Says she protected animal services funding from proposed cuts, opposed "unnecessary" salary increases for elected officials and pushed for budget transparency; pledges clear budget processes and "priority-based spending focused on services that improve people's lives" [S2].

### Public safety
- Cites "data-driven public safety" as part of her record [S1]; says her forensic mental health work informs her approach to public safety, behavioral health and systems accountability [S2].

### Governance and politics
- Describes herself as a "pro-democracy" candidate and says local government should work for residents, "not special interests" [S2][S3].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No stated position found. She lists the LGBTQ+ Victory Fund and the National Women's Political Caucus of Washington among her endorsers [S2].

### Parents and schools
