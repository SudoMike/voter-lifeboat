## What the office does

A county commissioner serves as the local government and legislative authority outside city limits, for a four-year term [S2]. Thurston County has a five-member board: each commissioner lives in and is nominated by a separate district in the primary, then is elected by voters of the entire county in the general (RCW 36.32.0556) [S3]. The board adopts the county budget and sets policy for county services such as the sheriff's and jail budgets, land-use permitting and regional housing and homelessness funding (see candidate dossiers). The seat pays $147,936 a year [S5].

## Race dynamics

Incumbent Emily Clouse (Prefers Democratic Party), seeking a second term, faces former Lacey City Councilmember Nicolas Martinez-Dunning (Prefers Moderate Democrat Party) [S1][S5]. In the August 4 District 5 primary Martinez-Dunning led with 5,662 votes (38.36%) to Clouse's 5,119 (34.68%), with Michelle Gipson third at 3,722 (25.22%) [S4]. The general is decided county-wide [S3].

Clouse runs on her first-term record chairing the Regional Housing Council ($25 million for affordable housing, 186 supportive units), climate programs (heat pumps, Deschutes Estuary) and opposition to a budget process that cut staff and domestic-violence services; she is endorsed by the county Democrats, labor and environmental groups (emily-clouse.md S1, S2). Her 2024 censure and the $300,000 settlement with a former aide, and the recall petition the state Supreme Court dismissed in February 2026, are part of the race's background (emily-clouse.md S5). Martinez-Dunning runs on budget integrity, rebuilding trust after "Commissioner scandals," protecting deputy and jail funding first, recovery-based housing and freezing permit fees; he refuses PAC money and is endorsed by Sheriff Derek Sanders and the Lacey mayor (nicolas-martinez-dunning.md S1, S2). He resigned from the Lacey council in August 2026 while running (nicolas-martinez-dunning.md S5). Clouse reports more money raised ($49.4k vs. $33.4k) (emily-clouse.md S6; nicolas-martinez-dunning.md S6). Neither candidate has a public position on I-645, I-1 or I-638.

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Public safety:** Martinez-Dunning puts deputy and jail funding first and is endorsed by the sheriff; Clouse cites data-driven safety, behavioral health and gun-safety group endorsements.
2. **Housing:** Clouse emphasizes public funding for affordable and supportive housing; Martinez-Dunning emphasizes recovery-based housing and lower permit fees to encourage building.
3. **Spending and accountability:** both use transparency language; Martinez-Dunning frames the race around deficits and budget integrity, Clouse around protecting services from cuts and clearer budget processes.
4. **Climate:** Clouse has a detailed climate record and plan; Martinez-Dunning has no stated climate platform.
5. **Money in politics / record vs. renewal:** Martinez-Dunning refuses PAC money and runs as a challenger; Clouse is the one-term incumbent with party and labor backing.
