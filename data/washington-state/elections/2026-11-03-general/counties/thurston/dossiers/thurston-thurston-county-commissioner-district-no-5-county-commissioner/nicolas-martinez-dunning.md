## Background

- Nicolas Martinez-Dunning (Prefers Moderate Democrat Party), who campaigns as Nic Dunning, owns West Coast Fitness, a 24/7 gym in Lacey named 2025 South Sound Business of the Year and Best Place to Work, and founded the nonprofit We Heart Olympia [S1][S2].
- He served 10 years in the U.S. Marine Corps and Washington National Guard, deploying to Iraq in 2007 as a Marine infantryman and later to Afghanistan with the Guard; he describes himself as a combat-disabled veteran [S1][S2][S5].
- Education: University of Washington, politics, philosophy and economics, with environmental studies [S1][S5].
- He was elected to Lacey City Council Position 3 in November 2023, sworn in January 2, 2024, and resigned effective August 18, 2026, while running for commissioner; Lacey Mayor Andy Ryder said he had told him he was uncertain he could keep devoting the time the council position required [S5]. Regional assignments he lists: Regional Housing Council, Economic Development Council (alternate), LEOFF pension committee, Olympic Region Clean Air Agency and Nisqually River Council [S1][S2].
- He finished first in the August 4, 2026 District 5 primary with 5,662 votes (38.36%) to incumbent Emily Clouse's 5,119 (34.68%) [S4].

## Positions

### Budget and spending
- Says "$20–$30 million deficits are forcing painful cuts to essential services" and that "Commissioner scandals broke public trust" [S1].
- Lists "budget integrity" first among his priorities [S2].
- Says he will report results on homelessness "back to you in plain numbers" and tie any future permit-fee increase to measured permit turnaround times [S2].

### Public safety
- Cites Washington's low ratio of officers per resident, the Sheriff's Office's 113 deputies for about 149,000 unincorporated residents, a 22% rise in aggravated assaults in the sheriff's area in 2025, a roughly $2.5 million cut to the 2026 sheriff's budget and a jail the sheriff says needs 40 to 80 more cells [S2].
- Says he "will protect deputy and jail funding first when budgets get tight" and will work with Sheriff Sanders and the courts to keep response times, the jail and the justice system funded [S2].

### Housing and homelessness
- Cites the 2026 point-in-time count of 1,058 people homeless in the county, up from 883, and says "The system we have is not working" [S2].
- Says he will back "recovery-based housing, where people get a stable place to live along with treatment and support to stay sober" [S2].
- Says he wants the county to welcome people who want to build; pledges to freeze the permit fees that rose on January 1, 2026 and tie any future increase to faster, more predictable permit times [S2].
- A Lacey council colleague, Maren Turner, said she and Martinez-Dunning had policy disagreements, particularly on housing [S5].

### Governance and money in politics
- Says his campaign has "rejected all funding from special interest groups and political action committees" and is endorsed by more than 600 individuals and businesses "representing every party" [S1]; the website describes the campaign as grassroots-funded with an all-volunteer staff [S2].
- Pledges to "move beyond political fighting" and work across party lines on safety, affordability, opportunity and transparency [S1][S3].
