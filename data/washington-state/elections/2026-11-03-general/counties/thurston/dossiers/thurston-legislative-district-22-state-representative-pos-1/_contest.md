## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term [S1]. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the two candidates who advanced from the August primary.

## Race dynamics
Incumbent Beth Doglio, who has held the seat for eight years and chairs the House Environment & Energy Committee, faces Republican Don Hewett, an Air Force veteran and engineer running for office for the first time [S1][S2]. Doglio runs on her record on climate, labor, housing and gun legislation and lists a long slate of labor, environmental and Democratic endorsements (beth-doglio.md S1, S3). Hewett's statement offers no policy platform beyond describing himself as "not red, not blue" and a non-politician, and his PDC record shows no money raised (don-hewett.md S1, S2). Doglio reports about $120,000 raised (beth-doglio.md S17). August primary results were not retrieved for this dossier.

Doglio opposes I-1 and I-638 and voted for ESSB 6346, the income tax I-645 would repeal (beth-doglio.md S5, S16); Hewett has stated no position on the initiatives.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** an eight-year incumbent committee chair against a first-time candidate who stresses he is not a politician.
2. **Taxes, climate, parental rights, immigration:** Doglio has a documented voting and sponsorship record on each; Hewett has no stated positions, so these axes show contrast only on her side.
