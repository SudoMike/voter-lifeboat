## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term [S1]. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the two candidates who advanced from the August primary.

## Race dynamics
Both finalists prefer the Democratic Party [S1]. Rep. Lisa Parshley, a veterinary oncologist and former Olympia councilmember first elected to the House in 2024, is challenged from the left by Jamie Keenan-deVargas, a retail worker, renter and immigrant-rights organizer running for office for the first time [S1][S2]. Keenan-deVargas's pamphlet statement attacks Parshley's corporate PAC contributions and her endorsements of Reps. Marie Gluesenkamp Perez and Marilyn Strickland [S1]. The Urbanist declined to endorse, saying Keenan-deVargas showed high alignment on housing and transit but had not shown that Parshley "is the problem," and noting a large gap in fundraising and endorsements [S2]. Parshley reports about $110,600 raised to Keenan-deVargas's $5,300 (lisa-parshley.md S21; jamie-keenan-devargas.md S8). August primary results were not retrieved for this dossier.

Both oppose I-1 and I-638 (lisa-parshley.md S20; jamie-keenan-devargas.md S7). Parshley voted for and defends ESSB 6346; Keenan-deVargas calls it "performative" and wants broader wealth taxes (lisa-parshley.md S3, S6; jamie-keenan-devargas.md S5).

## Differentiating issue axes
Because both are Democrats who agree on direction on several issues, the contrast is mostly degree and approach:
1. **Reform / campaign money:** Keenan-deVargas refuses corporate and PAC money and makes campaign-finance restrictions his top priority; Parshley accepts contributions of up to $2,400 each from health, energy and business donors and PACs.
2. **Record vs. renewal:** one-term House incumbent with six years on the Olympia council vs. a first-time candidate.
3. **Health care:** both back the Washington Health Trust (Parshley is its prime sponsor); Keenan-deVargas argues it is stalled in Parshley's committee.
4. **Housing:** Parshley pairs supply-first language with renter protections and voted for 2025 rent stabilization; Keenan-deVargas wants inflation-indexed rent caps with no exemptions, social housing and much broader state preemption of local zoning.
5. **Public safety:** Keenan-deVargas proposes drug decriminalization, removing school resource officers and divesting from prisons; Parshley's record shows no comparable stance.
6. **Taxes and spending:** both favor progressive revenue; Keenan-deVargas proposes larger new wealth taxes and universal programs (free childcare, single-payer, fare-free transit).
