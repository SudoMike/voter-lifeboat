## Background
Lisa Parshley served on the Olympia City Council from 2018 to 2024 and was elected to the State House in 2024 [S1][S3]. She is a veterinary oncologist (DVM, Colorado State; PhD in biochemistry, Oregon Health Sciences University) who co-owned Olympia Veterinary Specialists for more than a decade [S1][S3]. On the council she worked on city finances, public safety and climate planning and served as president of the LOTT Clean Water Alliance board [S3]. In the House she is vice chair of the Capital Budget Committee and serves on Health Care & Wellness, Local Government and the Universal Health Care Commission [S3][S5].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** says she "fought and voted for the Millionaires' Tax" in 2026 to fund free school meals, sales-tax exemptions for over-the-counter medications and hygiene products, and expansion of the Working Families Tax Credit [S3]. Voted yes on ESSB 6346 on final passage [S6], on ESSB 5813 (2025 capital gains/estate tax) [S8] and on ESHB 2081 (2025 B&O surcharges) [S9]. Is prime sponsor of HB 2626, which would raise the insurance premium tax on certain health insurers [S16].
- **Spending:** website pledges "expanded childcare subsidies" and says the Legislature "must do more" on affordability [S2]; says she championed "dedicated funding for affordable housing" [S3]. Voted for the 2025-27 operating budget (ESSB 5167) [S24].
- **Housing:** supports "building more homes of every type" on the view that "more supply means more options" and "lower costs," and pushes for "stronger renter protections" [S2]. Voted for EHB 1217 (2025 rent stabilization) on final passage of the conference report [S10]. Is prime sponsor of HB 1763 (state funding for affordable housing programs) [S19]. The Urbanist called her "a reliable vote on housing and transit issues" [S23].
- **Climate:** voted for 2SHB 1409 (clean fuels program) [S11]; endorsed by Washington Conservation Action and the Washington State Sierra Club [S4]. Her pamphlet statement lists "the environment" among her issues [S1].
- **Health care:** is prime sponsor of HB 1445, the Washington health trust bill to universalize eligibility for comprehensive public coverage for all Washington residents under a unified financing system; it remains in the House Health Care & Wellness Committee [S15]. Website says she has championed "a stronger universal health care system" and pushes for "healthcare that doesn't bankrupt people" [S2][S3]. Sponsored HB 1186, now law, allowing hospitals to send patients home with a full course of medication [S3].
- **Immigration:** voted for 2SHB 2105 (immigrant worker protections) and SSB 5855 (limits on law-enforcement face coverings) [S13][S14].
- **Tech and AI:** is prime sponsor of HB 1622, allowing public-employee bargaining over the use of artificial intelligence [S18]; voted for E2SHB 2515 (large energy use facilities such as data centers) [S12].
- **Labor:** website supports "policies that lift wages and protect workers from exploitation" [S2].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** is prime sponsor of HB 2657, establishing an abortion savings program [S17]; endorsed by Planned Parenthood Votes NW [S4]; The Urbanist describes her as "a champion for reproductive rights" [S23]. Listed as opposing I-638 [S20].
- **Parents and schools:** voted for ESHB 1296 (2025 parental-rights amendments) on House passage and final passage, and against Republican floor amendments to it [S7]. Listed as opposing I-1 [S20]. Endorsed by Olympia School Board directors Renee Fullerton and Gil Lamont [S4].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no direct statement on I-645 found as of 2026-10-08; she says she fought and voted for the tax I-645 would repeal [S3][S6].
- **I-1 (parental rights):** opposes; listed among elected officials opposing I-1 and I-638 by the NO HATE in WA State campaign [S20].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** opposes, per the same list [S20].

## Record
- ESSB 6346 (2026 income tax over $1M): yes on final passage (51-46) [S6].
- ESHB 1296 (2025 parental-rights amendments): yes on third reading (56-37) and final passage as amended by the Senate (59-39) [S7].
- ESSB 5813 and ESHB 2081 (2025 tax bills): yes [S8][S9].
- EHB 1217 (rent stabilization): yes on conference report final passage (54-44) [S10].
- 2SHB 2105 and SSB 5855 (2026 immigration-related laws): yes [S13][S14].
- Prime sponsor: HB 1445 (Washington health trust), HB 2626 (health insurer premium tax), HB 2657 (abortion savings program), HB 1622 (AI bargaining), HB 1763 (affordable housing funding) [S15][S16][S17][S18][S19]; HB 1186 (full course of discharge medication) is law [S3].
