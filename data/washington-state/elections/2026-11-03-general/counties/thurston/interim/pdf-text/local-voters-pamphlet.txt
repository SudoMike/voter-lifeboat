49 Dear Voter, Soon, it will be time to vote in the November General Election. This election is your opportunity to shape the future of our local, state, and national leadership. My goal is to make sure you have the tools to vote with confidence. Getting Your Ballot to Us If you plan to mail your ballot, please do so early. Due to changes in U.S. Postal Service policy, mail can sit for up to a week or more before receiving a postmark. Because of these delays, we cannot guarantee your ballot will have a valid postmark if it is mailed close to Election Day. The best and most direct way to return your ballot is to use an official ballot drop box. You can drop off your ballot in any drop box in the state (from Neah Bay to Newport) or use one of our 31 strategically placed drop boxes right here in Thurston County. A full list of local locations is available at ThurstonWAVotes.gov or page 50 in this pamphlet. Teams of two election workers collect these ballots in secured, sealed ballot containers and bring them straight to our processing center, keeping a close eye on your ballot every step of the way. How We Protect Your Vote When your ballot arrives, we check your signature against the one in your voter registration record. Every single signature is reviewed by trained staff members. Once verified, no one else can vote a ballot in your name, and you can track your ballot's progress at VoteWA.gov . To give you even greater insight into how your vote is safeguarded, we have established a new observer corridor at our ballot processing center. If you want to watch elections in action, we invite you to come by and visit. We have installed explanatory video boards that guide you through every phase of the process. Reflections on a Career of Public Service This is my last election serving as your County Auditor. Being able to serve you as an election administrator has been the highest honor of my professional life. Election administrators serve as the gatekeepers of democracy. It is a responsibility that demands never bending to pressure and always standing firmly by the law. I am immensely proud of our team, who show up every day committed to these principles to make sure your voice is heard. Together, we have led efforts to improve security, expand voter access, and ensure our elections remain transparent and fair for all. Thank you for the trust and confidence you have placed in me over the years. Respectfully, Mary Hall Mary Hall Thurston County Auditor, CERA Message from Thurston County Auditor Mary Hall Local Voters’ Pamphlet

Your signature is needed to make sure your ballot gets counted. Your ballot will only be counted if your signature is verified. If you registered to vote online or through Department of Licensing, the signature from your driver's license, permit, or state ID will be on your voter registration record. The signature on your return envelope is compared to the signature on your voter registration record. Trained election officials are responsible for verifying each and every signature. lf your signature is missing or does not match your voter registration record, the county elections office will contact you by mail before results are certified. They may call, text, or email if you gave them this information. Your ballot will only be counted if your signature is verified. Argument Against A "No" vote on the Timberland Regional Library levy lid lift is a vote for fiscal responsibility, taxpayer accountability, and careful budgeting. Property owners throughout Washington have experienced rising tax bills due to increasing property values and voter-approved levies for schools, fire districts, parks, and other local governments. At a time when many families, seniors on fixed incomes, and small businesses are struggling with higher costs for housing, groceries, insurance, and utilities, another property tax increase deserves scrutiny. Before asking taxpayers for additional revenue, the library should demonstrate that it has fully explored opportunities to reduce costs, improve operational efficiency, prioritize essential services, seek grants and private partnerships, and better manage existing resources. This is about priorities, and a property tax could put future investments in schools and emergency services at risk. Libraries provide important educational, cultural, and community services, but supporting libraries does not require supporting every request for higher taxes. Taxpayers have a reasonable expectation that existing dollars will be spent efficiently before additional burdens are placed on homeowners and businesses. A "No" vote encourages the library to focus on its core mission, evaluate programs and staffing levels, and identify long-term needs. Voting "No" is not a vote against libraries or literacy. It is a vote for responsible financial stewardship and accountability. Everyone's budget is tight, voters must prioritize investments, and the library should first demonstrate prudent management of existing resources before asking voters to approve higher property taxes, especially when households are already facing significant financial pressures. Rebuttal of Against Argument Washington communities fund key services through property taxes, and libraries have endured a 25- year financial drought. Voting yes restores stable funding after decades of erosion. Libraries offer safe, welcoming “third spaces” outside home and work or school. They offer valuable programs for all ages, from families with small kids, to community college students and job-seekers, even seniors on fixed incomes. As costs rise, libraries remain an affordable source of knowledge, connection and enjoyment for everyone. Argument For Thurston County has eight free public libraries, plus the traveling Anywhere Library that visits places like community centers, churches and assisted living facilities countywide. Part of the Timberland Regional Library system (TRL), these branches are community crossroads of knowledge and fun. Great children’s sections keep kids learning year-round. Teen Spaces provide safe places for studying, crafting and making friends. Free branch programs support job-hunters and retirees. Patrons can check out books, movies, games, nature backpacks, assistive devices and more. However, TRL is now at a crossroads of a different kind: funding and costs. About 96% of its revenue comes from a property tax levy that hasn’t increased in 25 years. The value of that income has dwindled, nibbled away by higher prices for materials, maintenance, salaries, even electricity. State law caps the amount libraries can collect on increasing property values at 1% annually until voters lift the lid. This measure would increase TRL’s levy from $0.22 to $0.35 per $1,000 of property value. Tax on a median- priced Thurston County home of $530,959 would be about $69 a year or $5.75 a month. After a quarter- century, it’s a long overdue cost-of-living raise. With your help, TRL can restore hours and staffing. It can expand local book, ebook, music and movie collections, and add community programs that are the heart of any thriving local library. Please vote yes to lift the levy lid and bring library funding closer to today’s reality. Rebuttal of For Argument Libraries matter, but raising property taxes without accountability is wrong.
