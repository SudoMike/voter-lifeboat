## What the office does

The sheriff is the county's chief executive law-enforcement officer: the sheriff and deputies keep the peace, enforce state law, make arrests and serve court orders and warrants [S2]. In Kitsap the Sheriff's Office is the county's largest department [S4]. The winner of this election serves a four-year term [S3].

## Race dynamics

This is an open seat. Sheriff John Gese, elected as a Democrat in 2022, had planned to retire at the end of his term but resigned in June 2026 along with four members of his leadership team [S4]. The commissioners appointed retired KCSO lieutenant Ken Dickinson as interim sheriff until the November result is certified [S5]. Gese promoted Myers to patrol chief before he left, and the Kitsap Democrats nominated Myers as one of three interim candidates; the commissioners chose Dickinson [S4][S5]. Kuss criticized the promotion as "succession politics" [S4]. As a two-candidate race, both candidates advanced automatically from the August primary [S4].

Brandon L. Myers (Prefers Democratic Party) has spent 23 years at the Kitsap County Sheriff's Office and is now its patrol chief [S3]. Rick Kuss (Prefers Republican Party) is a retired Navy officer who has been a King County deputy sheriff since 2023 [S3]. He lost to Gese in the 2022 sheriff race [S4]. The race is taking place during a county budget squeeze. The county projects a $9.8 million shortfall in 2027, driven partly by sheriff's-office overtime, and is weighing a one-tenth of 1% sales-tax increase, layoffs or both [S6]. Kuss says he will not seek new taxes and will cut waste in the office budget [S3]. Myers says the office must be "properly staffed, supported, and equipped" [S1]. Myers did not answer the Kitsap Daily News general-election questionnaire [S3].

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Record vs. renewal:** Myers is the inside candidate, with a 23-year career in the office he would lead and endorsements from former and current sheriffs. Kuss argues that "change needs to come from the outside."
2. **Spending and taxes:** Kuss pledges no new taxes and an office budget review to cut waste and control overtime. Myers talks about proper staffing and funding and about working with partners on unfunded mandates and budget shortfalls.
3. **Public safety approach:** Kuss leads with enforcement: restoring a regional drug task force, going after fentanyl traffickers and using incarceration when mandatory treatment fails. Myers stresses staffing, transparency, community trust and his crisis-intervention background.
