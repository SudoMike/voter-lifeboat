## Background

- Rick Kuss (Prefers Republican Party) is a deputy sheriff and trained mental-health co-responder [S1]. Kitsap Daily News reports that he has been a deputy with the King County Sheriff's Office since 2023 [S3][S4]. He and his wife raised their children in Kitsap County [S1][S3], and his campaign committee is based in Seabeck [S6].
- He is a retired Navy officer who rose from the enlisted ranks. After 24 years he retired as a lieutenant commander, with responsibilities that included strategic weapons, security, training and personnel readiness [S1][S3]. He first came to Kitsap through the Navy in 1999 [S1].
- He holds an M.S. in criminal justice from Michigan State University and is a doctoral student in criminal justice leadership [S1][S2].
- Community roles: co-director of a HOPE Worldwide chapter, partner with New Day Ministry (meals for homeless people in Bremerton), board member of Fraternal Order of Police Evergreen Lodge #1, member of Poulsbo Rotary, VFW, American Legion and U.S. Submarine Veterans, and a former Scarlet Road mentor working with people leaving sexual exploitation [S1][S3].
- He ran for Kitsap County sheriff in 2022 and lost to Democrat John Gese by 17 points. He then helped raise money for a citizen-requested hand recount meant to test the county's tabulators, which confirmed the result [S5].
- Both candidates advanced to the general without a contested primary [S4].

## Positions

### Public safety and the Sheriff's Office
- Calls protecting children his top priority: investigations into child abuse, sexual exploitation, trafficking and internet crimes against children, working with regional partners and the prosecutor [S3][S2][S1].
- Would "restore the regional drug task force" to fight fentanyl brought in by cartels. He pledges not to seek reelection if he does not restore it during his term [S3]. His site focuses the task force on "especially South Kitsap" [S2].
- Supports "evidence-based policing with accountable diversion programs that will work to treat addiction," adding: "If mandatory treatment fails, accountability through incarceration will result" [S3].
- Would set deputy patrol times and locations from an analysis of crime rates, and would put "more deputies in our neighborhoods" [S3][S2].
- Prioritizes hiring and retaining deputies and corrections officers to reduce mandatory overtime and burnout [S3].
- Pledges to "defend individual liberties, protect property rights, support victims" and keep the office "fully accountable to the people we serve" [S1].

### Budget, spending and taxes
- "I will not seek new taxes to solve problems caused by wasteful spending and misplaced priorities. Taxation should be a last resort, not a Band-Aid for fiscal irresponsibility" [S3].
- Would "thoroughly review the Sheriff's Office budget, cut waste, increase transparency, control overtime through responsible hiring" and redirect money to frontline personnel, child-protection investigations, training and equipment [S3].

### Leadership and outside perspective
- Argues that the office needs someone with executive leadership experience. "Working as a front-line supervisor for a long time does not mean you are qualified to be the chief executive officer of that agency" [S3].
- "Change needs to come from the outside. I am unburdened by its past, willing to challenge status quo policies" [S3].
- Says he has testified alongside sheriffs on criminal-justice legislation in Olympia [S3].
- Called Myers's promotion to patrol chief before the interim appointment a case of "succession politics" being put ahead of public safety [S4].

## Scoring notes

- He runs as the outsider: "Change needs to come from the outside," set against Myers's 23 years inside KCSO [S3].
- He pledges no new taxes and a budget review to cut waste and control overtime, while the county weighs a sales-tax increase [S3].
