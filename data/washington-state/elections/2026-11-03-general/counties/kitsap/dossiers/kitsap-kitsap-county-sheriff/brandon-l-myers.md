## Background

- Brandon L. Myers (Prefers Democratic Party) grew up in Kitsap County and attended Central Kitsap schools and Olympic College [S1][S4].
- He started at the Kitsap County Sheriff's Office at 17 as an Explorer Cadet. He then worked as a 9-1-1 dispatcher, reserve deputy, patrol deputy, patrol sergeant, support services sergeant and detective sergeant [S1][S4]. He was a founding member of the Crisis Intervention Team, a field training officer and FTO coordinator, and a statewide instructor [S1].
- He is a lead investigator on the Independent Investigations Team for officer-involved incidents [S4]. As detective sergeant he supervised major-crime and child-abuse investigations [S1][S6].
- Training includes the FBI-LEEDA Leadership Trilogy and the West Point leadership program. He received the IACP "40 Under 40" award [S1][S4].
- Community roles: board president of Central Stage Theatre of County Kitsap, eight years on the appointed Central Kitsap Community Council (as chair and vice chair), and a seat on the Leadership Kitsap board [S4].
- In June 2026 outgoing Sheriff John Gese said Myers would be promoted to patrol chief [S7]. Kitsap Daily News now describes him as KCSO patrol chief, with 23 years at the office [S6][S9].
- In 2021 the Kitsap Democrats picked him as their top choice to fill a sheriff vacancy. The commissioners appointed John Gese instead [S10]. In July 2026 the Kitsap Democrats again nominated him as one of three interim-sheriff candidates, and the commissioners again chose someone else (Ken Dickinson) [S8][S9].
- Both candidates advanced to the general without a contested primary [S7].

## Positions

### Public safety and the Sheriff's Office
- Says the office "must be properly staffed, supported, and equipped" and names staffing shortages and rising demands as its main challenges [S1].
- Priorities: supporting employees, recruitment and retention, community partnerships, and the core mission of "professionalism, fairness, teamwork, and integrity" [S1].
- Supports "modern policing, clear expectations, and open communication with the public." He says public safety starts with trust, and trust requires transparency [S3].
- Calls for investing in leadership training, deputy wellness and fair, consistent supervision [S3].
- Says the sheriff should be "visible, accessible, and accountable to the community not driven by party politics" [S3].

### Budget and spending
- His announcement said his experience would help restore "trust, accountability, and proper funding for the Sheriff's Office" and called for "responsible stewardship of resources" [S5].
- Supports "transparent stewardship of taxpayer dollars" and working with local and state partners "to address unfunded mandates and budget shortfalls" [S3].
- No position found on the county's proposed sales-tax increase as of 2026-10-08. He did not answer the Kitsap Daily News general-election questionnaire [S6].
