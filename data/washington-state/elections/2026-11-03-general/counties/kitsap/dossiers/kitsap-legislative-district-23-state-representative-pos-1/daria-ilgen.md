## Background
Daria Ilgen is a Democratic challenger for Position 1 in the 23rd Legislative District, running against fellow Democrat Rep. Tarra Simmons [S1][S2]. He lives in Poulsbo and describes himself as a second-generation immigrant whose mother's family fled Iran during the revolution [S12][S5]. He reports about 20 years as a strategist for Amazon, Zillow and Expedia before stepping back to support his wife's Kitsap small business and raise their children on a family farm [S1][S5]. He holds a BA in political science from the University of Colorado and an MBA from Hult International Business School [S1].

He has not held public office. He is an elected Democratic precinct committee officer and 23rd District Democrats state committeeman, sits on the executive boards of the 23rd District and Kitsap County Democrats, and says state party leaders picked him as one of ten members to help redraft the party's 2026 platform [S1][S5][S2]. He serves on the boards of the Kitsap Economic Development Alliance, Kitsap Community Foundation and Olympic College Foundation, advises the Kitsap intra-county ferry board, and led the 2024 North Kitsap School District levy campaign [S1][S5][S2].

In the August 4 primary he received 16,753 votes to Simmons' 24,583 (about 39% to 57%) [S3]. In December 2025, when Simmons learned he was considering the race, she texted him asking him to challenge "one of the men instead" and asked several Democratic officials to talk with him; the Legislative Ethics Board dismissed a complaint about those contacts filed by the 23rd District Democrats' chair, finding no ethics violation [S11]. Ilgen said he did not regret "being transparent about conduct I found alarming" [S11].

## Positions
- **Priorities:** "fair taxation, honest budgets, reliable ferries, affordable homes and childcare, strong schools, and safer communities," and ending "insurance and retail price gouging"; "It's time for new leadership" [S1]. His L.I.V.E. platform stands for Leadership, Income Support, Value in Healthcare, and Environmental Protections & Inclusive Communities [S6][S2].
- **Taxes:** his Taxpayer Accountability Standard says any new or higher tax must answer five questions (problem, expected result, who pays, tradeoffs, how success is measured) before earning his vote: "I am not promising to oppose every tax. I am promising never to vote for one without asking the questions taxpayers deserve to have answered" [S8]. Says lawmakers should examine existing spending before seeking new revenue [S8]. Platform calls for "advancing tax equity and targeted tax relief" and "rebalancing the tax code" to help small businesses [S6]. No statement on the 2026 tax on income over $1 million found.
- **Spending:** platform pledges "fiscal discipline," "stronger audits, performance measures, and accountability when programs are not delivering," and ending ineffective programs, while protecting essential services [S6][S2].
- **Housing:** "Expand housing people can afford by cutting red tape, reducing permitting delays, encouraging responsible density, and supporting greater stability for cost-stressed families and first-time homebuyers"; supports missing-middle housing near ferries and transit [S6].
- **Public safety:** pledges to give "law enforcement the staffing and tools they need," improve coordination on trafficking, violence and hate crimes, and keep guns from people who may harm themselves or others [S6][S2]. Endorsed by the Washington Council of Police & Sheriffs and the Poulsbo Police Officers Association [S7].
- **Health care:** hold insurers accountable for hidden fees and denials, prevent surprise bills, expand provider capacity including Olympic College's planned Allied Health Center in Poulsbo, and protect mental health and addiction funding [S6][S2].
- **Environment and climate:** protect Puget Sound, shorelines and forests in collaboration with tribes; focus growth near ferries and transit to reduce "auto dependence and carbon emissions"; preserve forests and farmland [S6]. No position on the Climate Commitment Act found.
- **Technology and consumer pricing:** would support legislation requiring businesses to disclose when personal data or automated systems influence prices and banning use of sensitive information to price essential goods, plus tighter price-gouging rules [S9][S6].
- **Campaign finance and ethics:** proposes a "TRUST Act" requiring due diligence and conflict disclosure for charitable transfers of surplus campaign funds over $5,000, a carry-forward limit on surplus balances, and a 90-day disposal deadline [S10]. Cites Simmons' Legislative Ethics Board penalty and says officials must "never use public office for self-enrichment" [S2].
- **Immigration:** pledges to "stand up for immigrant families so they can live in community without fear" [S6].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** pledges to protect "LGBTQ+ neighbors" and reject "hate, injustice, and intolerance" [S6]. No statements on sports eligibility, gender-affirming care or abortion found.
- **Parents and schools:** platform supports "outcome-focused reforms" that support teachers, improve test scores and grade-level readiness, and reduce classroom cell phone use [S6]. No position on parental notification, record access or opt-outs found.
- **Ferries:** pledges to be "a productive seatmate to Rep. Greg Nance" on ferry reliability, vessel construction and workforce [S6].
