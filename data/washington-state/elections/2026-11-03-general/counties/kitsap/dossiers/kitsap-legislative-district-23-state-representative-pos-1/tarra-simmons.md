## Background
Tarra Simmons is the Democratic incumbent in Position 1 of the 23rd Legislative District, which covers north Kitsap County, Bremerton and Bainbridge Island, and has served in the House since 2021 [S1][S14]. She is Deputy Speaker Pro Tempore and vice chair of the Community Safety committee, and sits on the Technology, Economic Development & Veterans committee [S4][S1]. She is an attorney and former registered nurse (Harrison Hospital, Martha & Mary) and grew up in Bremerton; she holds an AAS from Olympic College, a BSN from Pacific Lutheran University and a JD from Seattle University [S1][S2]. She served 20 months in prison from 2011 to 2013 on drug and theft convictions, which were vacated in 2023, and was the first Washington legislator with felony convictions [S2]. She was elected in 2020, 2022 and 2024 and is seeking a fourth term [S2][S11].

In the August 4 primary, a two-candidate race, she received 24,583 votes to Daria Ilgen's 16,753, with 1,608 write-ins (about 57% to 39%) [S3][S11].

Ethics: On Aug. 14, 2026, the Legislative Ethics Board found three violations of conflict-of-interest and special-privilege law, tied to a $1 million budget proviso she helped secure in 2024 that funded a nonprofit where she was employed, her intervention in a dispute between two nonprofits, and an attempt to get a nonprofit leader to rein in the person who filed the complaint; it dismissed three other allegations and levied the maximum $5,000 per violation [S11]. The board's Aug. 28 final order added $37,457.98 in costs, about $52,000 in total [S12][S2]. Simmons denies wrongdoing, called the fine unfair and excessive, and says she will appeal in Thurston County Superior Court [S11][S12]. In a separate case, the board dismissed a complaint over her efforts in December 2025 to dissuade Ilgen from running, finding no ethics violation [S13]. The Washington State Standard reported in May 2026 that the Public Disclosure Commission had dismissed a complaint against her [S14].

## Positions
- **Priorities:** says affordability is the top issue she hears from voters, naming housing, health care, child care and transportation costs [S2]. Describes herself as "the progressive leader" who will "ensure opportunity isn't reserved for the ultra-wealthy" [S1].
- **Taxes:** campaign platform calls for "a fairer tax system that provides relief to working families while asking more from those who can afford it" [S5]. Lists helping pass the "Millionaires Tax" (SB 6346) as a 2026 accomplishment, along with an expanded Working Families Tax Credit and B&O tax relief for small businesses [S6][S2].
- **Spending and services:** calls for increased state investment in affordable housing, homelessness prevention and supportive housing; fully funding schools including counselors, nurses and special education; and expanded child care [S5]. In 2025 she supported cutting state funding for legal aid tied to vacating drug convictions under State v. Blake, writing to colleagues that the funding was "wasteful" during a budget shortfall; she later said the budget forced difficult decisions [S16].
- **Housing:** platform calls for more state investment in affordable and supportive housing [S5]. In 2024 she said "we need to stabilize rent prices" and streamline permitting, and proposed incentives for builders and relaxed zoning for multi-family housing and ADUs [S17]. Lists support for social housing authorities (EHB 1687, 2026) and tenant protections (EHB 1217, 2025) [S6].
- **Public safety:** platform centers behavioral health treatment, community-based interventions, reentry and law-enforcement accountability and transparency, alongside "address the root causes of crime while keeping communities safe" [S5]. In 2024 she said she wanted to increase law-enforcement staffing and support and that housing and behavioral health investment would let police focus on violent crime [S17]. Introduced a 2026 body-worn camera bill (HB 2644) that did not pass [S6].
- **Climate and environment:** supported the Climate Commitment Act; platform calls for holding major polluters accountable and expanding clean-energy incentives [S6][S5]. Lists ferry electrification and land conservation investments [S5][S6].
- **Health care:** authored prior-authorization reform, the 2022 charity-care expansion and a 2025 mental health parity law; platform calls for lower prescription drug costs and protection from insurance barriers [S6][S5][S2].
- **Immigration:** lead sponsor of HB 2641 (2026, the "ICE Out Act"), which would have barred Washington law enforcement from hiring officers who joined ICE after Jan. 20, 2025; it died in committee [S15][S6]. Platform calls for immigrant communities to have a meaningful voice in government [S5].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** platform pledges to "defend access to healthcare and gender-affirming care," protect LGBTQIA+ people from discrimination and "expand access to reproductive healthcare" [S5]. Lists championing EHB 1851 (2022) protecting abortion care [S6]. Endorsed by Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates [S1][S8].
- **Parents and schools:** voted against Initiative 2081 (the 2024 parents' bill of rights) when the House adopted it, 82-15 [S26]. Co-sponsored HB 1296 (2025), which changed the I-2081 list of parental rights, added a statement of student rights and required school policies to prioritize student safety and privacy, and voted for it on final passage [S28][S27]. Platform pledges support for "safer, more inclusive schools" for LGBTQIA+ youth [S5].
- **Tribal relations:** calls for strengthened government-to-government relationships and Tribal participation in state decisions [S5].

## Record
- **ESSB 6346 (2026, tax on income over $1 million):** voted yes on House final passage, 51-46, on March 9, 2026, and no on the motion to postpone it indefinitely; she voted no on every floor amendment that failed and yes on the six that were adopted [S18][S19].
- **Other revenue votes (2025):** voted yes on ESHB 2081 (B&O tax surcharges and rates), 50-48, and ESSB 5813 (more progressive capital gains and estate tax rates), 53-45 [S20][S21][S22][S23].
- **Initiative 2111 (2024, prohibiting state and local personal income taxes):** voted yes when the House adopted it, 76-21 [S24][S25].
- **Initiative 2113 (2024, police vehicular pursuits):** voted no; the House adopted it 77-20 [S33][S34].
- **EHB 1217 (2025, limits on rent and fee increases):** voted yes on final passage, 54-44 [S29][S30].
- **ESHB 1589 (2024, clean energy transition):** voted yes on final passage, 50-45 [S31][S32].
- **Parents' rights:** no on I-2081 (2024); co-sponsor of HB 1296 and yes on final passage (2025) [S26][S28][S27].
- **Authored laws (per her campaign):** voting rights restored on release from incarceration (ESHB 1078, 2021), legal financial obligations reform (2022, 2023), criminal-justice addiction-treatment account (HB 1983, 2024), prior authorization reform (2023, 2025), charity care (SHB 1616, 2022), mental health parity (E2SHB 1432, 2025) [S6].
- **Ethics:** Legislative Ethics Board finding of three violations, $15,000 penalty plus costs (about $52,000 total), under appeal [S11][S12].
- **PDC (updated 2026-10-05):** $196,651.21 raised, $158,536.70 spent, $8,950 in debts [S10].
