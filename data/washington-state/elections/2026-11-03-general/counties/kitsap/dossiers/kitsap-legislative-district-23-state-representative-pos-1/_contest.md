## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House, for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets: K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding, ferries and criminal-justice statutes. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the top two finishers from the August primary.

## Race dynamics
The 23rd District covers north Kitsap County and Bainbridge Island, including Hansville, Kingston, Poulsbo and Bremerton [S4][S5]. This is a Democrat-versus-Democrat race. Rep. Tarra Simmons, first elected in 2020 and now Deputy Speaker Pro Tempore, is seeking a fourth term against Daria Ilgen, a former tech-industry strategist, Poulsbo farm owner and 23rd District Democrats state committeeman who has not held public office [S1][S2]. In the two-candidate primary Simmons received 24,583 votes, Ilgen 16,753 and write-ins 1,608 [S3].

Ethics is a central issue. In August the Legislative Ethics Board found Simmons committed three conflict-of-interest and special-privilege violations; with costs, the penalty is about $52,000, and she says she will appeal [S5][S2]. Ilgen cites the case directly in his pitch [S2]. A separate complaint over Simmons' December 2025 efforts to talk Ilgen out of running was dismissed [S6].

On policy, Simmons runs on her record (health care and criminal-justice laws, the 2026 tax on income over $1 million, rent stabilization) and on progressive priorities including gender-affirming care and immigrant protections (tarra-simmons.md S5, S6, S18, S29). Ilgen runs on fiscal discipline, audits and a five-question test before any new tax, permitting-focused housing supply, police staffing and consumer-pricing protections, with endorsements from business, builder, realtor and police groups (daria-ilgen.md S6, S7, S8, S9). Simmons' endorsements come mostly from labor, Democratic legislators and progressive organizations (tarra-simmons.md S1, S7, S8). Poulsbo Mayor Ed Stern and Bremerton School Board director Shannon Turner appear on both candidates' endorsement lists (tarra-simmons.md S7; daria-ilgen.md S7). Simmons has raised about $197,000 and Ilgen about $105,000 (tarra-simmons.md S10; daria-ilgen.md S13).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** three-term incumbent with an authored-law record (and an ethics penalty under appeal) vs. a first-time candidate pitching new leadership and accountability.
2. **Taxes:** Simmons voted for the tax on income over $1 million and other 2025 revenue bills; Ilgen sets conditions on any new tax and has not stated a position on that tax.
3. **Spending:** Simmons emphasizes state investment in housing, schools and child care; Ilgen emphasizes audits, performance measures and ending ineffective programs.
4. **Public safety:** treatment, reentry and police accountability (Simmons voted against I-2113) vs. law-enforcement staffing with police-union endorsements.
5. **Housing:** rent stabilization and public investment vs. permitting speed and density.
6. **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy / parents and schools:** Simmons voted against I-2081 and co-sponsored its 2025 rewrite and pledges to defend gender-affirming care; Ilgen states general support for LGBTQ+ neighbors and has no position on parental-rights questions.
7. **Immigration:** Simmons sponsored the ICE Out Act; Ilgen pledges general support for immigrant families.
8. **Reform / tech:** Ilgen proposes surplus campaign-fund rules and limits on surveillance pricing.
