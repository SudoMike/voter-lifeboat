## Background
Lance Byrd is the Republican candidate for Position 2 in the 23rd Legislative District [S1]. He is a longtime Bremerton resident and a senior technical program manager with more than 26 years in IT, after eight years as a restaurant cook and kitchen lead [S1][S2][S3]. He holds an associate degree in electrical engineering technology from North Seattle College and AWS and Microsoft certifications, and has described work in CPU chip manufacturing at Intel and messaging systems at Amazon Web Services [S1][S6]. He volunteers at Refuge Church in Bremerton and helps plan events and strategy for the local party [S1][S3].

His statement lists no elected experience, and his campaign site says he "is not a career politician" [S1][S2]. He ran for Bremerton School Board (Director Position 1) in 2023 against incumbent Karen Bolton [S6] and lost the 2024 race for 23rd District State Senate to Sen. Drew Hansen [S3][S8]. In the August 4, 2026 primary he received 14,202 votes (about 30%), second to Rep. Greg Nance's 27,915 (about 58%) [S4].

## Positions
- **Pitch:** asks moderate Democrats and independents to vote for "balance" against "near-total one-party control" [S2][S3]. Pamphlet priorities: housing affordability, reducing crime, supporting law enforcement, lowering the cost of living, balancing the budget, and "real help and shelter for the mentally ill" [S1].
- **Taxes:** "I will oppose new state income taxes" [S3]. His campaign's first priority is "Respect the Voters — No State Income Tax"; he notes the Legislature adopted Initiative 2111's income-tax ban in 2024 and passed ESSB 6346 two years later, and says both 23rd District House incumbents voted for it [S2]. Pamphlet: will protect education funding and essential services "while respecting Washington voters' clear, repeated decision against a state income tax" [S1]. In 2024 he called for lowering the state gas tax [S5].
- **Spending:** says the two-year operating budget has grown to about $80 billion and that budgeting based on population and inflation growth would put it near $51–56 billion; cites former Govs. Christine Gregoire and Gary Locke saying the state has a spending problem [S2][S3]. "Government must live within its means" [S1].
- **Climate and energy:** says the Climate Commitment Act and Clean Energy Transformation Act are raising utility bills and pump prices "before the grid is ready"; would restore the natural gas protections of Initiative 2066 and use natural gas as a bridge until nuclear power is available [S2][S3]. Says Washington "cannot solve global emissions alone" [S2]. In 2024 he called ferry electrification "ill-considered" and favored bringing diesel boats online [S5].
- **Housing:** "State mandates under the Growth Management Act limit supply and drive up costs"; will advocate reforms "that empower local communities to build more housing without one-size-fits-all Olympia rules" [S3][S2].
- **Public safety:** pledges to stand with local law enforcement [S2][S1]. In 2024 he said more officers are needed, laws that "hamstring" officers should be reformed (citing the requirement to obtain medical clearance before booking), and suggested a citizen review board that includes officers [S5].
- **Parents and schools:** "Parents are the primary decision makers for their children"; supports rights to review instructional materials, timely notice and "meaningful involvement" [S3]. Calls parental rights "one of the main reasons I am running"; criticizes HB 1296 (2025) for narrowing Initiative 2081's records access (to education records on a 45-day timeline) and removing its advance-notice rules for school-arranged medical services and immediate notice of campus removal, and says he would restore those rights [S2]. As a 2023 school board candidate he named "the erosion of parental rights" his first issue, citing cases where a student with gender dysphoria could receive medical procedures without parents' knowledge or consent [S6].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no statements on sports eligibility, gender-affirming care as such, or abortion policy found; his statements on minors' care are framed as parental notification (above) [S2][S6].
- **Medical mandates:** in 2024 favored a state constitutional amendment barring lawmakers from mandating medical interventions, and called for rehiring ferry workers who left over the COVID-19 vaccine mandate [S5].
