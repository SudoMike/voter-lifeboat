## Background
Greg Nance is the Democratic incumbent in Position 2 of the 23rd Legislative District and has served in the House since 2023 [S1]. He was appointed to the seat, succeeding Drew Hansen, and faced Republican Jamie Miles in the 2024 general election [S7]; he is seeking re-election [S1][S2]. He is vice chair of the House Postsecondary Education & Workforce Committee, sits on the Transportation and Agriculture & Natural Resources committees, and is vice chair of the Maritime Caucus [S4][S1]. He grew up on Bainbridge Island, attended Kitsap public schools, and studied at the University of Chicago (Truman Scholar) and Cambridge University (Gates Scholar, master's in management and innovation) [S1][S6]. He co-founded Moneythink, a student-debt nonprofit, led Dyad Mentorship, and ran 3,156 miles across the country for youth mental health; he is a former board member of Kitsap Mental Health Services [S1][S6][S2].

In the August 4 primary he received 27,915 votes (about 58%) to Republican Lance Byrd's 14,202 (about 30%) and Democrat Kristin Lillegard's 5,882 (about 12%) [S3]. Lillegard had been endorsed by a local Democratic Socialist group [S10].

## Positions
- **Priorities:** "fixing our ferries, protecting Puget Sound, and reducing the cost of living" [S2]. Says the Legislature secured $895 million for ferry construction, maintenance and workforce, and that he helped secure $42 million to modernize West Sound Tech and protect 680 acres of Kitsap forest from clearcutting [S1][S2].
- **Taxes:** no campaign statement on taxes found; see Record for his votes on the 2026 tax on income over $1 million and on 2025 revenue bills.
- **Spending:** campaign site pledges to "fight for the investments our Public Schools need," including fully funding special education, school meals, buses and counseling, and calls for investment in urgent care, walk-in clinics, senior housing and first-responder mental-health resources [S5].
- **Housing:** calls housing "a human right" and says he will "work for permit and zoning reform, community partnerships, and aligning private sector incentives to fast track construction" [S5].
- **Public safety and mental health:** proposes expanding the Poulsbo Fire CARES program of in-home and mobile services for people with mental illness and substance use disorders, and Mental Health First Aid training [S5]. Sponsored HB 2492 on mental health and substance use in the trades [S2].
- **Climate and environment:** in 2024 said, "The Climate Commitment Act holds Washington's biggest polluters accountable and makes them pay a fair price for the pollution that goes into our airways and our water" [S7]. Says the Bull Kelp Act he sponsored is now law [S2]. Serves on the Puget Sound Partnership Ecosystem Coordination Board [S4].
- **Health care:** calls for local health-care investment (urgent care, walk-in clinics, nursing and technician training, oversight of St. Michael) [S5][S7].
- **Reproductive policy:** "We must keep fighting for women's reproductive care, including enshrining the right to choose with an amendment to our state constitution" [S5]. No statements on sports eligibility or gender-affirming care found.
- **Parents and schools:** co-sponsored HB 1296 (2025), which changed the parental rights adopted in Initiative 2081, and voted for it; he had voted for I-2081 in 2024 (see Record) [S30][S29][S28].
- **Campaign money:** says his campaign is "100% people-powered (no corporate $)" [S6].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08. Related: voted for ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S11].
- **I-1 (parental rights in public schools):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08. Related: voted for I-2081 in 2024 and co-sponsored its 2025 rewrite [S28][S30].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports / sex-based eligibility):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- **ESSB 6346 (2026, tax on income over $1 million):** voted yes on House final passage, 51-46, on March 9, 2026; he was excused for the motion to postpone the bill indefinitely [S11][S12].
- **ESSB 6346 floor amendments:** voted yes on eight amendments that failed: 2555 (base taxable income on a five-year average), 2564 (cut the state sales tax from 6.5% to 5% from July 2028), 2566 (exclude public pensions), 2571 (exempt military pay and veterans benefits), 2573 (require a referendum on any cut to the standard deduction), 2581 (back-to-school sales tax holiday), 2590 (exclude business and pass-through income) and 2614 (send 49% of revenue to a new Affordability and Tax Relief Account). He voted no on most other failed amendments [S11][S13][S14][S15][S16][S17][S18][S19][S20][S21].
- **Other revenue votes (2025):** voted yes on ESHB 2081 (B&O tax surcharges and rates), 50-48, and ESSB 5813 (more progressive capital gains and estate tax rates), 53-45 [S22][S23][S24][S25].
- **Initiative 2111 (2024, prohibiting state and local personal income taxes):** voted yes when the House adopted it, 76-21 [S26][S27].
- **Initiative 2113 (2024, police vehicular pursuits):** voted yes; the House adopted it 77-20 [S35][S36].
- **Parents' rights:** voted yes on I-2081 (2024, 82-15); co-sponsored HB 1296 (2025) and voted yes on final passage [S28][S30][S29].
- **EHB 1217 (2025, limits on rent and fee increases):** voted yes on final passage, 54-44 [S31][S32].
- **ESHB 1589 (2024, clean energy transition):** voted yes on final passage, 50-45 [S33][S34].
- **PDC (updated 2026-10-01):** $91,084.66 raised, $29,395.41 spent [S9].

## Scoring notes
- Voted for the 2026 tax on income over $1 million and 2025 revenue increases, but also for eight failed amendments that would have narrowed that tax or cut other taxes; his opponent opposes any state income tax [S11][S14][S15][S22][S24].
- Defended the Climate Commitment Act and voted for the 2024 clean-energy transition bill; his opponent wants to restore natural gas protections [S7][S33].
- Voted for I-2081 in 2024, then co-sponsored and voted for its 2025 rewrite; his opponent's campaign centers on reversing that rewrite [S28][S30].
- Voted for I-2113 (police pursuits) and promotes non-police mental-health response (Poulsbo Fire CARES) [S35][S5].
- Incumbent since 2023 with a ferry, environment and workforce record; his opponent has not held public office [S1][S2].
