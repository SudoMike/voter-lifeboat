## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House, for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets: K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding, ferries and criminal-justice statutes. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the top two finishers from the August primary.

## Race dynamics
The 23rd District covers north Kitsap County and Bainbridge Island [S4]. Democratic Rep. Greg Nance, in the House since 2023, faces Republican Lance Byrd, an IT program manager who lost the district's 2024 State Senate race to Sen. Drew Hansen [S1][S2]. In the three-way primary Nance received 27,915 votes, Byrd 14,202 and Democrat Kristin Lillegard 5,882 [S3].

Nance runs on ferries ($895 million in ferry funding), Puget Sound protection and cost of living, with labor, WEA-PAC and tribal endorsements [S1][S2] (greg-nance.md S8). Byrd pitches "balance" against one-party control to moderate Democrats and independents, and targets three Nance votes: for the 2026 tax on income over $1 million, for keeping the Climate Commitment Act, and for HB 1296, the 2025 rewrite of the I-2081 parents' rights law [S2] (lance-byrd.md S2). The roll calls confirm Nance voted for ESSB 6346 and HB 1296 and voted for I-2081 in 2024; on ESSB 6346 he also voted for eight failed amendments, including a sales-tax cut and exemptions for pensions and military pay (greg-nance.md S11, S14–S21, S28, S29). Nance reports about $91,000 raised to Byrd's about $3,500 (greg-nance.md S9; lance-byrd.md S7).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Nance voted for the tax on income over $1 million and 2025 revenue bills (with some tax-relief amendment votes); Byrd opposes any state income tax and called for a lower gas tax.
2. **Spending:** school, health and ferry investment vs. limiting budget growth to population and inflation.
3. **Climate and energy:** Nance defends the Climate Commitment Act and voted for the 2024 clean-energy transition bill; Byrd would restore natural gas protections and criticizes the CCA and CETA.
4. **Parents and schools:** Nance co-sponsored HB 1296; Byrd makes restoring the I-2081 notification and records provisions a central plank.
5. **Housing / local control:** Nance favors permit and zoning reform and voted for 2025 rent-increase limits; Byrd wants Growth Management Act reform and fewer state mandates on local housing decisions.
6. **Public safety:** Nance backs non-police mental-health response but voted for I-2113 (police pursuits); Byrd emphasizes more officers and support for law enforcement.
7. **Record vs. renewal:** incumbent since 2023 vs. a challenger who has not held office.
