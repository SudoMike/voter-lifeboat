## What the office does

The prosecuting attorney prosecutes criminal and civil actions for the state and county, defends suits brought against the county, and is legal adviser to the county commissioners, county officers and school districts [S2]. Kitsap's office has more than 75 employees and a budget above $12 million [S3]. The winner serves a four-year term [S3].

## Race dynamics

Both candidates prefer the Democratic Party [S1]. Incumbent Chad M. Enright has been prosecutor since 2019 and has worked in the office since 2008 [S1]. Challenger Joe Lombardi is a former Kitsap deputy prosecutor and assistant attorney general who now works as a Kitsap public defender [S1][S3]. As a two-candidate race, it went to the general without the primary narrowing the field [S4]. Lombardi says voters "overwhelmingly favored me as a challenger in the primary" [S3]. This dossier could not verify the primary vote shares.

Lombardi's case centers on management. He says a "toxic management culture" has driven experienced prosecutors out, produced erratic charging and mishandled cases, and eliminated victim services [S1][S3]. Enright points to his record and experience: diversion and treatment-court programs, full staffing and no case backlog, balanced budgets, and a Kitsap Bar Association survey that he says rated him "highly qualified" while more than 40% rated Lombardi "not qualified" [S1][S3]. Enright has the party and establishment endorsements: the Kitsap County Democrats, the Deputy Prosecuting Attorney Guild, Attorney General Nick Brown and two former sheriffs. Lombardi has the Kitsap County Central Labor Council and a group of current and former prosecutors, public defenders and court staff [S1][S3]. The race comes as the county weighs a sales-tax increase or layoffs to close a 2027 gap. Enright has warned that cutting staff would raise attorney caseloads [S5].

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Record vs. renewal:** an eight-year incumbent who has spent 18 years in the office, against a challenger who has never held office or a supervisory role and runs on changing the office's management.
2. **Money and politics:** Lombardi refuses corporate PAC and special-interest contributions. Enright campaigns on endorsements from every local Democratic elected official and the county party.
3. **Public safety:** the two largely agree. Both promise to prioritize violent crime and use diversion for low-level offenses. Enright's diversion programs are already running. Lombardi adds enforcing probation terms and limiting ICE involvement in county cases.
