## Background

- Chad M. Enright (Prefers Democratic Party) has been Kitsap County Prosecutor since 2019. He was first elected in November 2018 [S1][S2].
- He began practicing law in 2004 at Case & Hemstreet, a private firm that held a county felony public-defense contract. Prosecutor Russ Hauge hired him in 2008 as a felony trial attorney. He later spent four years as chief of the Felony and Juvenile Division under Prosecutor Tina Robinson [S2].
- Education: North Kitsap High School; B.A. in political science, University of Washington; J.D., Seattle University [S1][S2].
- Roles: Washington State Minority and Justice Commissioner; executive board member, secretary, treasurer and judicial-evaluation committee chair of the Washington Association of Prosecuting Attorneys; past president of the Kitsap Bar Association; board member of Kitsap Community Resources and Peninsula Services; member of the state Substance Use Recovery Services Advisory Committee created after the *Blake* decision [S1][S2].
- His challenger says Enright "has never run opposed" before this race [S5].

## Positions

### Public safety and prosecution
- Will keep prioritizing violent crime, sex offenses and crimes against vulnerable people. "For eligible lower-level offenses, treatment or diversion can improve public safety" [S5].
- Wants police to have discretion to send people to treatment instead of arresting them: "I want an officer to have the discretion to take somebody and get them into treatment" [S6].
- Says his office will be the first in Washington to use race-blind charging, which redacts race-identifying information from police reports [S1]. Requires implicit-bias training for all attorneys in the office [S3].
- Is building a real-time public data website showing cases filed and resolved [S1][S3].
- Victim services: says the office added four trained victim advocates and launched an online portal for victims [S5].

### Immigration and local enforcement
- Says the office does not collect or store immigration status and wants undocumented crime victims and witnesses to feel safe reporting crimes [S7]. In 2025 he and Sheriff Gese held in-person meetings with immigrants about the role of local police and prosecutors [S3].

### Budget and spending
- Says the office manages more than 75 employees and a budget above $12 million, and that he will "continue producing balanced budgets" [S5].
- Cites savings from a civil-litigation team that takes lawsuits against the county to trial instead of settling, and from a paperless office upgraded "without using any county general fund dollars" [S1][S3].
- At the June 2026 county budget town halls on a projected $9.8 million gap, he warned that attorneys could face larger caseloads if staffing declines [S8]. No stated position on the proposed county sales-tax increase as of 2026-10-08.

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No public position found as of 2026-10-08. His office partnered with Kitsap Superior Court on the state's first juvenile Girls Court, a therapeutic court for young women [S1][S3]. That is a court program, not a position on this axis.

### Parents and schools

## Record

- Worked with the City of Poulsbo to create the North Kitsap Recovery Resource Center, diverting misdemeanor defendants into treatment and dismissing their cases when they finish [S3].
- Launched Kitsap County's first pre-arrest diversion program with the Poulsbo police chief in November 2025. It covers misdemeanors only. People who do not engage with the recovery center within 48 hours are referred for expedited charging [S6][S3].
- Expanded Kitsap THRIVE Court for trafficking victims and the Port Gamble S'Klallam Tribe's Welcome Home reentry program, and created Girls Court with Kitsap Superior Court [S1][S3].
- His office won aggravated-murder convictions and life sentences for three Bandidos motorcycle gang members in 2024 after a five-month trial [S3].
- Reports all prosecutor positions filled and no case backlog [S1][S3].
- Says state data shows double-digit crime declines across Kitsap and that Kitsap prosecutors exceed statewide trial-success rates [S5].
- Says a Kitsap Bar Association survey found nearly 85% of attorneys rated him "highly qualified" and more than 40% rated Lombardi "not qualified" [S5]. This dossier did not find the survey itself; the figures are the candidate's.
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $44,354.40 raised, $24,439.94 spent, $7,500 in loans [S9].
