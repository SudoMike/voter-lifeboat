## Background
Travis Couture, a Republican, has held the 35th District's Position 2 House seat since 2023 and is the House Republican budget leader [S1][S2]. He has served on the House appropriations and education committees [S2]. He is a Navy veteran who served on the ballistic-missile submarines USS Alabama and USS Nebraska and was honorably discharged in 2011 [S1][S3]. He later managed defense assets supporting Naval Base Kitsap operations for a Fortune 500 company and directed operations at a Kitsap County nonprofit serving people with disabilities [S1][S3]. He and his wife own a construction and manufacturing business in Shelton, and he is a licensed general contractor [S3][S1]. He holds an MBA and a B.A. in organizational leadership from Brandman University [S1][S2].

He faces Democrat Maria Littlesun in the November 3 general; the winner serves a two-year term starting in 2027 [S2]. This package did not find a published August primary tally for this race.

## Positions
- **Taxes:** pamphlet: "Olympia Democrats passed the largest tax increases in state history - including an income tax"; promises to keep fighting for "less taxes" [S1]. As budget leader he has proposed to "reject unnecessary tax increases" and "provide meaningful tax relief" [S2]. His campaign site says he "voted to ban a state income tax" and "increased property tax relief for seniors and veterans" [S3].
- **Spending:** says he would "control unsustainable spending ... eliminate waste ... and prioritize essential services" and that "Government should live within its means" [S2]. Pamphlet criticizes "reckless spending" and the use of a retired first responders' pension fund [S1]. Says success "shouldn't be measured by how much government spends" [S2].
- **Public safety:** says Washington has the fewest law-enforcement officers per capita in the nation; pledges to support law enforcement, "hold repeat offenders accountable" and "put victims first" [S2]. Cites restored police pursuits and penalties for hard-drug possession, and work to clean up homeless encampments [S3]. Pamphlet criticizes "soft-on-crime policies" [S1]. Says he fought the placement of sexually violent predators in district communities [S2][S3].
- **Child welfare:** a critic of the 2021 Keeping Families Together Act, which he says leaves children in homes with parents using fentanyl [S4]. Says he passed legislation protecting medically fragile and vulnerable children [S2].
- **Housing and cost of living:** supports "policies to generate more housing, childcare, and lower costs at the gas pump" [S3].
- **Constitutional rights:** opposes "attacks on our 2nd Amendment and property rights" [S3].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no direct campaign statement found. Record: voted against ESSB 5599 (2023, youth seeking protected health care services) and ESHB 1469 (2023, access to reproductive health care and gender-affirming treatment) [S14][S15]. In defending I-1 he said of its opponents, "What they think is abuse is not affirming your pronouns" [S4].
- **Parents and schools:** pamphlet: "I fought to ... defend parental rights" [S1]. His site says he "voted to enact the Parents Bill of Rights," supports school choice, and opposes "unfunded mandates that crush school budgets" [S3]. Wants more education dollars reaching classrooms, stronger special-education support and "greater parental involvement" [S2]. Says he drafted the parental-notification amendment in current law, and defends I-1's provisions on immediate notification when a child is a crime victim at school [S4].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Related: voted against ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal, and offered amendments to it [S5][S6][S7]; campaigns against "an income tax" [S1].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** supports. Calls the governor's claim that it weakens parents' rights "a complete and utter lie" and urges "Vote yes for parents' rights and vote no for stranger danger" [S4]. Voted for I-2081 (2024) and against ESHB 1296 (2025) [S11][S12]; I-1 would restore I-2081 by repealing ESHB 1296's changes [S13].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- ESSB 6346 (2026, tax on income over $1 million): voted nay on House final passage, 2026-03-09 (passed 51-46), and yea on the motion to postpone it indefinitely (failed 38-58) [S5]. His floor amendment 2546 would have delayed the tax until the state had two consecutive fiscal years of declining revenue [S6]. His amendment 2592 would have eliminated the sales and use tax on advertising services [S7]. Neither was adopted [S5].
- ESSB 5813 (2025, higher capital gains and estate tax rates): voted nay on House final passage, 2025-04-23 (passed 53-45) [S8].
- ESHB 2081 (2025, business and occupation tax increases): voted nay on House final passage, 2025-04-22 (passed 50-48) [S9].
- Initiative 2111 (2024, barring state and local income taxes): voted yea, 2024-03-04 (passed 76-21) [S10].
- Initiative 2081 (2024): voted yea, 2024-03-04 (passed 82-15) [S11]. ESHB 1296 (2025): voted nay on final passage, 2025-04-24 (passed 59-39) [S12].
- ESSB 5599 (2023): voted nay, 2023-04-12 (passed 57-39) [S14]. ESHB 1469 (2023): voted nay, 2023-02-28 (passed 59-38) [S15].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-06): $110,079.66 raised, $38,016.25 spent [S17].
