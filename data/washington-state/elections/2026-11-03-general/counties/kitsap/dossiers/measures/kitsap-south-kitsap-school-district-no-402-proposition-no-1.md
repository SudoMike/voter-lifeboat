## Ballot title (verbatim)

> CAPITAL LEVY FOR SAFETY, TECHNOLOGY AND FACILITY IMPROVEMENTS
> The Board of Directors of South Kitsap School District No. 402 adopted Resolution No. 1397, concerning a proposition to fund safety, technology and facility improvements. This proposition would authorize the District to levy the following excess taxes on all taxable property within the District to fund Districtwide school improvements, including upgrading security cameras, access controls, emergency communication systems, educational technology, internet access/cybersecurity, parking, school accessibility, theatre, athletic facilities for student/community use, and other educational facilities:
>
> | Collection Year | Estimated Levy Rate/$1000 Assessed Value | Levy Amount |
> |---|---|---|
> | 2027 | $1.50 | $23,488,622 |
> | 2028 | $1.50 | $24,192,839 |
> | 2029 | $1.50 | $24,918,539 |
>
> all as provided in Resolution No. 1397. Should this proposition be approved?

[S1]

## Explanatory statement (verbatim, prepared by the attorney for the district)

> Proposition 1 would authorize a three-year capital levy to make District-wide safety, technology and facility improvements, including, but not limited to: (1) upgrading security cameras, access controls and emergency communication systems; (2) improving parking, campus access and school accessibility; (3) making theatre upgrades and athletic facility improvements for student and community use; and (4) making other safety, security, athletic and educational facility improvements to facilities and infrastructure District-wide. Additional information at www.skschools.org/levy.
> The proposed three-year levy would authorize the collection of $23,488,622 in 2027, $24,192,839 in 2028, and $24,918,539 in 2029. The levy rate required to produce these amounts is estimated to be $1.50 per $1,000 of assessed value. Exemptions from taxes may be available to certain homeowners, contact the Kitsap County Assessor at 360-337-7160.

[S1]

## What it does

Proposition 1 authorizes a three-year capital projects levy for South Kitsap School District, collected 2027-2029, totaling $72.6 million [S1][S2][S5]. It needs a simple majority [S2][S5]. The board approved Resolution No. 1397 on July 29, 2026 [S2][S5]. The district built the project list with community Facilities Steering and Finance Advisory committees that met from January to June 2026 [S2].

Projects the district and press list [S2][S3][S5]:

- **Safety and security:** security cameras in elementary schools, upgraded intercom and emergency communication systems, updated entrance access controls, an upgraded bus radio system, and parking, drop-off and bus access at Burley Glenwood and East Port Orchard elementaries.
- **Technology:** replacement Chromebooks for instruction and required testing, classroom displays and document cameras, network infrastructure and cybersecurity.
- **Facilities for school and community use:** South Kitsap High School theater renovation (lighting, seating, carpet, paint, acoustics, sound), gym bleacher replacement, tennis-court resurfacing with pickleball, synthetic turf for the high school's baseball and softball fields, and synthetic turf for the soccer/football, baseball and softball fields at Marcus Whitman Middle School with improved parking and access.

## Cost

- Estimated $1.50 per $1,000 of assessed value each year, collecting $23.5 million (2027), $24.2 million (2028) and $24.9 million (2029) [S1].
- On a $500,000 home that is $750 a year by the stated rate. The district rounds it to $63 a month, or $756 a year [S2][S3], and the Kitsap Daily News reports $756 [S5].
- Collections are capped at the voter-approved amounts. The actual rate moves with total assessed value [S2][S3].
- The district offers a senior tax exchange program (tax relief for volunteering in schools). The county assessor administers exemptions for qualifying seniors, people with disabilities and disabled veterans [S1][S2][S3].

## Fiscal context / New or replacement?

- No source describes this levy as replacing an expiring one. The ballot title and explanatory statement call it a capital levy and do not use the word "replacement." The district's page and flyer present it as a new proposal developed in 2025-2026 [S1][S2][S3].
- South Kitsap "does not have any bond debt" [S2]. Its last successful bond was in 1988 [S9]. Its 2023 $271 million bond failed [S8]. School supporters say the last nine bonds each won a majority but not the required 60% [S9].
- In November 2018, voters approved a $21 million capital projects levy 55-45 for pool renovation, roof replacements and ADA work, while a $184 million bond for a second high school failed with 53.4% [S7][S4]. The term of the 2018 levy and whether any of it is still being collected were not found. In 2024 the board discussed a capital levy as "something the district found success with in 2018" [S8].
- Voters renewed the district's four-year, $133 million educational programs and operations (EP&O) levy in 2025 [S9]. This capital levy would be collected alongside it.
- Superintendent Tim Winter said in February 2026 that "a few years down the road, is a bond" [S6].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, by Katherine Ander (chair) and Carl Olson, committee for the measure (www.yesforsk.org) [S1]:

- "The state doesn't provide funding for most school repairs or for technology. South Kitsap relies on voter-approved levies to take care of the schools our community has built."
- Technology: the levy "would replace aging devices, improve school technology systems, and help protect student information."
- "Even South Kitsap's newest schools are more than 30 years old." It cites the high school theater and gym, synthetic turf, and accessibility work at the high school and Marcus Whitman.
- "Capital levy dollars stay in South Kitsap and may be used only for voter-approved safety, technology, and facility projects... Acting now prevents more disruptive and expensive failures later."

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed. The Auditor's note in the voters' guide: "After recruitment attempts including texts, emails, press releases and social media, no person in the district contacted the Kitsap County Auditor's Office to write a statement against the measure" [S1]. No committee formed against it, so there are no rebuttals [S1].

## Endorsements and campaign

- South Kitsap School Supporters, a nonprofit formerly called "Yes4SK," is leading the campaign. It planned to raise $30,000 from residents and local businesses [S9]. It is the committee listed in the voters' guide (www.yesforsk.org) [S1][S4].
- No organized opposition was found [S1]. PDC filings were not checked.

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote adds an estimated $1.50 per $1,000 capital levy for three years, $72.6 million in total [S1][S2]. No source describes it as replacing an expiring levy [S1][S2][S3]. Following the Spokane convention, that makes it a new tax (+2). The open question is whether any part of the 2018 capital levy is still being collected [S7]. If it were, the case for +1 (replacement) would strengthen. Nothing found suggests it is.
- `spending`: not mapped. The proposal funds specific capital projects. Neither side's statement argues oversight versus expansion.
- `parental-rights`: not supported. This is a capital levy for buildings, devices and security systems. Nothing in the ballot title, statement or district materials concerns curricula, parental notification, record access or opt-outs. The technology plank mentions protecting "student information" and data privacy [S1][S2], which is cybersecurity, not parents' access to records.
- `social`, `local-control`: not addressed.
