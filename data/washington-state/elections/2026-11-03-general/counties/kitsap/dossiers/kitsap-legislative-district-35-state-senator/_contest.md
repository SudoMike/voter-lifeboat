## What the office does
A Washington State Senator represents one legislative district and serves a four-year term [S2]. Senators write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets. Those budgets cover K-12 and higher-education funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health and behavioral-health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The 35th District covers all of Mason County and parts of Thurston and Kitsap counties; the winner takes office in 2027 [S2].

## Race dynamics
Republican Sen. Drew MacEwen of Shelton has represented the district since 2013, in the House until 2023 and in the Senate since then, and is the Senate Deputy Republican Leader [S1][S2]. His challenger, Democrat Carolina Mejia, has been a Thurston County commissioner since 2021 and chairs the county Board of Health (carolina-mejia.md S1, S2). This package did not find a published August primary tally for the Senate race.

MacEwen runs on spending restraint and tax relief: end tax hikes, cut the sales tax, cap property taxes (drew-c-macewen.md S2). He voted against ESSB 6346, the 2026 tax on income over $1 million, on both Senate passage votes [S3], and against the 2025 capital-gains/estate and B&O tax increases (drew-c-macewen.md S7, S8). Mejia's pitch is cost of living, rural hospitals and infrastructure, and "It's time the messenger changed" (carolina-mejia.md S2). She has not stated a position on the income tax or I-645. She is listed as opposing I-1 and I-638 and backs reproductive-care access (carolina-mejia.md S4, S7). MacEwen has made no campaign statement on the initiatives; his record has a yes vote on the 2024 parents' bill of rights (I-2081), a no vote on its 2025 rewrite (ESHB 1296), and no votes on the 2023 reproductive and gender-affirming care bills (drew-c-macewen.md S10, S11, S13, S14). PDC reports show MacEwen with $280k raised and Mejia with $126k (drew-c-macewen.md S16; carolina-mejia.md S10).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** MacEwen has a voting record and a platform against new taxes; Mejia has no stated position on state taxes.
2. **Spending:** MacEwen makes "tighten its belt" budgeting his central theme. Mejia calls for dedicated road and bridge funding, broadband, water systems and full state funding of schools.
3. **Record vs. renewal:** a 13-year legislator in Senate leadership against a county commissioner who argues the district "keeps falling behind."
4. **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** Mejia supports reproductive-care access and opposes I-638. MacEwen's evidence is his 2023 votes against ESSB 5599 and ESHB 1469.
5. **Parents and schools:** Mejia is listed as opposing I-1. MacEwen voted for I-2081 and against ESHB 1296, the changes I-1 would repeal.
6. **Housing and local control:** Mejia pairs permitting streamlining with county housing funds and "local control over what development looks like."
7. **Tech:** Mejia wants strict guardrails on data centers' energy costs and pollution. No MacEwen statement was found.
