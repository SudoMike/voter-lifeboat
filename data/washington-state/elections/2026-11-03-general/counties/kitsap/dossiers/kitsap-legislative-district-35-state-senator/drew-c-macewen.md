## Background
Drew MacEwen, a Republican from Shelton, has represented the 35th District since 2013: as a state representative from 2013 to 2023 and as state senator since 2023 [S1][S2]. He is the Senate Deputy Republican Leader and sits on the Senate committees on Environment, Energy and Technology; Transportation; Labor and Commerce; and Rules [S2]. He served six years in the Navy submarine force, including five patrols on the USS Nevada, is a graduate of the Navy Nuclear Propulsion Program, holds a B.S. from Excelsior College, and is managing partner of Mountain Lakes Capital [S1][S2]. He is a member of the Shelton Rotary Club [S1].

He announced his reelection run in April 2026 [S3] and faces Democrat Carolina Mejia, a Thurston County commissioner, in the November 3 general; the winner serves a four-year term [S2]. The district covers all of Mason County and parts of Thurston and Kitsap counties [S2].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** his top priority is to "end the tax hikes," reduce the sales tax and enact caps on property taxes [S2]. In his reelection announcement he cited "the largest tax increase in state history, the passage of an income tax" as consequences of one-party control [S3]. His campaign site says he wants to keep taxpayer dollars "in residents' wallets rather than government coffers" [S4].
- **Spending:** "Spending needs to be reigned in" and "It is time for Olympia to tighten its belt" [S2]; he says state spending "continues to grow faster than revenue - even with record tax collections" [S3]. His pamphlet statement says he will "continue to speak out about the state's ongoing spending challenges and advocate for responsible budgeting" [S1]. His site says "government should live within its means" and calls for reducing "wasteful spending and unfunded mandates" [S4].
- **Economy and rural areas:** his priorities include economic development, infrastructure and transportation investment [S1]. He says the Legislature "has continued to pass legislation that has threatened small businesses" and opposes policies that favor urban centers over communities beyond the I-5 corridor [S4].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no campaign statement found. Record: voted against ESSB 5599 (2023, youth seeking protected health care services) and ESHB 1469 (2023, access to reproductive health care and gender-affirming treatment) [S13][S14].
- **Parents and schools:** no campaign statement found. Record: voted for Initiative 2081, the parents' bill of rights, in 2024 [S10], and against ESHB 1296 in 2025 [S11], the act that modified those rights and that I-1 would repeal [S12].
- **Balance in the Legislature:** says "Washington needs a more balanced approach in the legislature" [S1] and "We must restore balance to the legislature" [S3]; cites "a bipartisan track record and a record of working for fiscal restraint while meeting our obligations" [S2].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Related: voted against ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal, on every Senate roll call [S6], and has campaigned against "the passage of an income tax" [S3].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08. Related: voted for I-2081 (2024) and against ESHB 1296 (2025) [S10][S11]; I-1 would restore I-2081 by repealing ESHB 1296's changes [S12].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- ESSB 6346 (2026, tax on income over $1 million): voted nay on Senate third reading on 2026-02-16 (passed 27-22) and on final passage as amended by the House on 2026-03-11 (passed 27-21) [S6]. Voted yea on Republican floor amendments 693, 695 (his own) and 696, all of which failed [S6].
