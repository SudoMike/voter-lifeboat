## Background
Carolina Mejia, a Democrat, has been a Thurston County commissioner since 2021 and chairs the Thurston County Board of Health and the Thurston-Mason Behavioral Health Administrative Services Organization [S1][S2]. She co-chairs the Washington State Association of Counties Legislative Steering Committee and serves on the County Road Administration Board and the state Affordable Housing Advisory Board [S1]. She holds a B.A. from Christian Brothers University and attended the University of Washington School of Law [S1][S2]. She came to the United States from Honduras at 11 [S4], has lived in Olympia for 10 years and has two daughters in public schools [S5]. She is a Smith Lake precinct committee officer [S1].

She filed on May 4, 2026 to challenge Sen. Drew MacEwen; her county term runs to 2028 [S9]. She faces him in the November 3 general [S2].

## Positions
- **Cost of living:** names cost as her top issue; says she will focus on "clearing out the red tape and permitting delays that make housing more expensive to build" and on making sure public help reaches "regular families and seniors, not just whoever has a lobbyist" [S2]. Her campaign says, "lower the burden on working families — not adding to it" [S4].
- **Taxes and revenue:** says she has gone "after state and federal dollars so our local tax money stretches further" [S2]. No statement on the 2026 tax on income over $1 million or other state taxes found.
- **Housing:** "I'll cut the red tape and permitting delays that stall construction, while protecting local control over what development looks like in each community," aiming for workforce, starter, senior and affordable rental housing [S4]. As commissioner she helped found the Regional Housing Authority and established the countywide Home Fund [S5][S1].
- **Growth and local decisions:** says growth "must be shaped by the people who actually live here, not dictated from the outside" [S4].
- **Spending and investment:** calls for dedicated road and bridge preservation funding, rural broadband, water and utility investment, and ferry service [S4]; wants the state to "fully meet its responsibility to fund public education fairly," with career and technical education [S4].
- **Healthcare:** keep rural hospitals open, address doctor and nurse shortages, expand behavioral health and addiction treatment, cap prescription drug prices and protect "the coverage people already have" [S4][S2].
- **Data centers:** says data centers and their owners need "strict guardrails" so they do not pass energy costs to ratepayers or pollute air and water [S4].
- **Public safety:** prioritizes funding for domestic-violence and sexual-assault victim services and resources for missing and murdered Indigenous women; says first responders need funding and training, the addiction response should be "centered on treatment," and backs safe-storage and extreme-risk gun laws while protecting "responsible gun owners" [S4].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** "I'll always work to keep reproductive health care safe, legal, and accessible here in Washington" [S4]. Listed by NO HATE in WA State among elected officials opposing I-638 [S7]. Endorsed by Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates and Washington Stonewall Democrats [S6].
- **Parents and schools:** listed by NO HATE in WA State as opposing I-1 [S7]. No other statement on parental notification, record access or opt-outs found.
- **Experience pitch:** argues the 35th "keeps falling behind" and "It's time the messenger changed," saying her opponent "lines up with the people who already have plenty of influence in Olympia" [S2].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** opposes; listed among elected officials endorsing a no vote on I-001 [S7].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** opposes; listed among elected officials endorsing a no vote on I-638 [S7].

## Record
- Thurston County commissioner since 2021 [S1]. Her campaign credits her with developing the first USDA-certified meat processing facility in Western Washington, a Community Workforce Agreement for the new county courthouse, the county's Integrated Water Initiative, and the Home Fund; and with directing more than $20 million to struggling families and businesses during the pandemic [S1][S5][S4].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-07): $125,571.64 raised, $47,367.51 spent [S10].

## Endorsements
