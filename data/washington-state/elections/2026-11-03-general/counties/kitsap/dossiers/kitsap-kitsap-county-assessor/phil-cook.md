## Background

- Phil Cook (Prefers Republican Party) has been Kitsap County Assessor since 2015 [S1].
- He reports nearly 25 years in real estate and property management in Kitsap County, more than a decade as a sales manager and mortgage originator with a Fortune 500 company, and earlier work in operations and logistics management, education and youth coaching; he also manages residential rental properties in the county [S1][S4].
- Education: Master of Public Administration (University of Southern California), M.Ed. (Azusa Pacific University), B.A. (University of Arkansas); Lean Six Sigma trained [S1][S4].
- Community: Scouting America volunteer and Eagle Scout, Navy League member, former United Way of Kitsap County board member [S1][S4].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $26,513.95 in contributions and $5,630.84 in expenditures; status "Qualified for general" [S5].

## Positions

- **Office priorities:** "accuracy and transparency in the valuation process, excellent customer service and fiscal responsibility with taxpayer dollars," adopting new technologies as they become available [S1][S2].
- **Nonpartisan framing:** says the office "is not about partisan politics. It is about data, accuracy, uniformity, and service" and that he has worked with leaders across the political spectrum [S2][S1].
- **Property taxes:** says property tax rates and increases "are driven by taxing districts, municipalities, levies, and voter-approved measures — not by the Assessor's Office itself" [S3].
- **Fiscal restraint:** cites handling a more-than-76% increase in senior/disabled exemption applications after a 2023 legislative change "without additional staff or taxpayer funding," using Lean/Six Sigma workflow changes [S3].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Parents and schools:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record

- Says the office has been ranked by the Department of Revenue as one of the most efficiently operated assessor's offices in the state [S3][S1].
- Says the office's senior/disabled exemption workflow improvements were recognized by the National Association of Counties and became a state-recommended best practice [S1][S3].
- Cites partnerships with title companies, surveyors and appraisers to clean up online parcel data and legal descriptions [S3].

## Endorsements

- His campaign site quotes former Kitsap County Treasurer Meredith Green (D, 2010-2022) in support [S2]. The site's endorsements page is image-only and was not readable.

## Scoring notes

- Twelve-year incumbent running on office efficiency and an exemption-processing record; his opponent has not held office [S1][S3].
- Says he absorbed a large workload increase without added staff or funding [S3].
- Says the office does not set tax rates; does not describe valuation inequities between neighborhoods, which is his opponent's central claim [S3].
