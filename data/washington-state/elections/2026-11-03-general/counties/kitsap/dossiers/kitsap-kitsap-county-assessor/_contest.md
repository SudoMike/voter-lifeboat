## What the office does

The assessor values all taxable property in the county. State law requires property to be valued at 100% of its true and fair value, based mainly on sales of the same or similar properties in the past five years [S2]. The assessor also handles valuation appeals and the senior, disabled and other exemption programs, but does not set tax rates: rates and levies come from taxing districts and voter-approved measures (phil-cook.md S3; michael-simonds.md S1).

## Race dynamics

Incumbent Phil Cook (Prefers Republican Party), assessor since 2015, faces Michael Simonds (Prefers Democratic Party), a Navy veteran and Redfin real-estate agent who has not held office [S1]. Both qualified for the general from the August primary (phil-cook.md S5; michael-simonds.md S2). Cook points to office efficiency, a nationally recognized senior/disabled exemption workflow, and absorbing a 76% rise in exemption applications without added staff (phil-cook.md S3). Simonds says outdated models over-assess working-class neighborhoods relative to high-value properties, and proposes data analytics, online valuation and appeal tools, and expanded exemption outreach [S1]. Cook reports $26.5k raised; Simonds had filed no finance reports (phil-cook.md S5; michael-simonds.md S2). No news coverage of this race was found.

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Experience:** a 12-year incumbent against a first-time candidate offering modernization.
2. **Taxes (distribution, not level):** Simonds says high-value properties are under-assessed relative to working-class neighborhoods. Cook frames the office around accuracy and uniformity and stresses that it does not set rates. Both candidates say the office should not raise revenue.
3. **Spending:** Cook stresses doing more work without added staff or funding. Simonds proposes new tools and outreach but gives no cost.
