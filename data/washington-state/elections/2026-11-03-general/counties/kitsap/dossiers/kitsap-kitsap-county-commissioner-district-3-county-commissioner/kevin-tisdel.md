## Background

- Kevin Tisdel (Prefers Republican Party) is a fourth-generation Kitsap County native and 1988 North Kitsap High School graduate; he has not held elected office [S1][S4].
- He reports 20 years in the collision-repair industry as business owner, general manager and production manager, and 14 years of civil service, mainly at Seattle Public Utilities (drainage and wastewater operations and maintenance; solid-waste planning; budgets, staffing, purchasing, contracts, permits and regulatory compliance) [S1]. His campaign site describes this as 15 years "in big-city government" [S4].
- Education: Sacramento State University [S1]. Community service: chair/advisor on BKAT (district 3) 2018-2022 and salmon-creek restoration in the 1980s [S1].
- PDC (updated 2026-09-22): $3,919.52 in contributions and $3,068.03 in expenditures; status "Qualified for general" after the August primary [S5].

## Positions

- **Growth and the Growth Management Act:** says "Repeal the Growth Management Act first — then Ordinance 631-2024, HB 1491, and SB 5148"; as commissioner would pass a resolution and send a letter formally requesting GMA amendments [S2][S3][S4]. Says every land-use, infrastructure and economic-development decision "must deliver measurable net-positive revenue to Kitsap County taxpayers and good family-sustaining jobs" while protecting rural character, farms, fisheries and the Navy presence [S3]. Campaign slogan: "Keep Kitsap" [S1][S2].
- **Housing:** says Kitsap has about 97,000 jobs and 147,000 homes, so it faces "an affordability problem, not a shortage," driven by competition from commuters who work in King County [S3]. Says "We will not achieve affordable housing by building apartments everywhere for King County's workforce" and proposes repealing the "regional formula" and related builder's-remedy law in favor of "workforce zoning" for people who already live and work in Kitsap [S3][S4].
- **Taxes:** calls for ending the Multifamily Tax Exemption ("the 20-year tax holiday"), saying it shifts school, fire, utility and transit costs onto other residents [S2][S3]; supports "no farm taxation" and an end to "taxing working farms off the land" [S2][S3]. Says "growth does not pay for growth — you do" [S4].
- **Spending and infrastructure:** describes himself as "focused on outcomes rather than photo-op projects" [S1]. Criticizes the proposed Silverdale Regional Center sewer plan, saying connection fees cover 8% of a $684 million plan and ratepayers about 82% [S2][S3]. Proposes a Clear Creek-to-Chico Creek heritage trail, a Pike Place-style local market, freeway on-ramps diverting traffic from the Silverdale core, and support for a racetrack and lodging [S3].
- **Economy and energy:** wants to attract Navy contractors for drone boats, autonomous maritime systems and defense manufacturing at PSIC-Bremerton, plus a waste-to-energy plant and a small modular reactor for "cheap, clean energy" [S3].
- **Environment and fisheries:** proposes legislation requiring treatment plants serving dense populations to remove pharmaceuticals and to dose effluent with aragonite against ocean acidification; says the Climate Commitment Act "currently provides little dedicated funding for this" [S3].
- **Farmland:** says Kitsap lost 61% of farmland acres from 1997 to 2017 and that transfer of development rights "has not delivered"; wants stronger preservation tools and farm-to-market infrastructure [S3].
- **Health care:** says he will publish positions on hospital staffing and the OB-GYN shortage after meeting with stakeholders [S3].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Parents and schools:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
