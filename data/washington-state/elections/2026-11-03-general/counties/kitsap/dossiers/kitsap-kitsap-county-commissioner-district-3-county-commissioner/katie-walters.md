## Background

- Katie Walters (Prefers Democratic Party) is the incumbent Kitsap County Commissioner for District 3 and has held the seat since 2023 [S1][S4].
- Before office she was executive director of Leadership Kitsap, marketing and community relations director at the Bainbridge Island Museum of Art, an adjunct professor at Olympic College and a public relations consultant [S1].
- Education: M.A. in communications, Washington State University; B.A. in public relations with a political science minor, Gonzaga University [S1].
- Regional roles listed in her statement: chair of Kitsap Transit, past chair of Housing Kitsap, chair of the Olympic Consortium, vice chair of the Kitsap Regional Coordinating Council, 2nd vice chair of the Washington State Association of Counties, and former Kitsap Regional Library trustee [S1].
- She has lived in Kitsap County for more than 35 years; her husband is a retired fourth-generation shipyard worker [S1][S4].
- PDC (updated 2026-09-17): $23,627.69 in contributions, $14,040.14 in expenditures, $2,000 in loans; status "Qualified for general" after the August primary [S10].

## Positions

- **Growth and land use:** says Kitsap must manage growth "thoughtfully, protecting our rural character and natural environment while investing in infrastructure, public safety, and economic opportunity" [S1]; campaign site commits to "strong planning, infrastructure investment, and environmental stewardship" [S3].
- **Housing and homelessness:** wants "more housing options at every income level," focused on expanding affordable and workforce housing, and "compassionate, practical solutions to homelessness that combine services, accountability, and long-term stability" [S3]. Says the county expanded affordable housing options and advanced regional solutions to homelessness during her term [S2].
- **Public safety:** says public safety and behavioral health "go hand in hand"; supports "well-trained, accountable law enforcement while investing in youth mental health, treatment services, and coordinated solutions that address challenges before they become crises" [S3].
- **Fiscal policy:** describes a "collaborative, practical, fiscally responsible approach grounded in transparent decision-making" [S1]; says taxpayer dollars "should be spent wisely, strategically" with transparent, accountable budgeting [S3]. No statement found on the sales-tax increase the county discussed for 2027 [S11].
- **Economy, childcare and health care:** supports workforce training, childcare access and employer-education partnerships [S3]; cites advancing the Olympic College Allied Health campus and healthcare workforce efforts [S1][S4]. On childcare she said "the biggest barriers in Kitsap County are cost and availability" and supported making childcare a permitted use in rural zones [S8].
- **Transportation:** cites expanded transit and ferry service and broadband access [S1][S3].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Parents and schools:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record

- Says she helped lead completion of the county's Comprehensive Plan Update, Parks, Recreation and Open Space Plan and Critical Areas Ordinance, and championed the Silverdale Regional Center Plan [S4].
- In June 2025 she and Commissioner Christine Rolfes voted 2-1 (Oran Root dissenting) to remove all 14 rural residential rezone requests, totaling 135-140 potential units, from the county docket after staff found they conflicted with rural growth targets and policies directing growth to urban areas [S6].
- In December 2025 the three-member board adopted a $650 million 2026 budget; the $137.9 million general fund was balanced with a 3% across-the-board cut and a one-time $300,000 reserve drawdown, after a hiring freeze that eliminated about 25 vacant positions [S7]. (The article does not report individual votes.)
- In 2026 the board, including Walters, held town halls on a projected $9.8 million 2027 shortfall and discussed a 0.1% sales tax under HB 2015, 45-60 position cuts, or a hybrid; final budget adoption is expected in December [S11].
- Accepted a 2026 National Association of Counties Achievement Award for the county's Pacific Building Housing Navigation Center, a 75-bed, low-barrier, continuous-stay shelter with on-site behavioral health and housing navigation that has moved 72 residents to permanent housing since January 2025 [S9].
- Says she helped appoint two state senators, a state representative, a county commissioner and a district court judge to fill vacancies [S4].

## Endorsements

- Elected officials include state Sens. Drew Hansen and Deb Krishnadasan, Reps. Greg Nance and Adison Richards, Commissioner Christine Rolfes, Sheriff John Gese, Prosecutor Chad Enright, and the mayors of Bremerton, Port Orchard, Poulsbo and Bainbridge Island [S5][S1].

## Scoring notes

- One-term incumbent with a regional-board record (Kitsap Transit chair, Housing Kitsap past chair) against a challenger who has not held elected office [S1].
- Housing approach combines affordable/workforce housing and a low-barrier shelter with the county's urban-growth-focused land-use plans; her opponent wants to repeal the GMA and the state density laws [S3][S6][S9].
- Pairs support for law enforcement with investment in treatment and behavioral health [S3].
- Has not stated a position on the 0.1% sales tax the board is weighing for 2027 [S11].
