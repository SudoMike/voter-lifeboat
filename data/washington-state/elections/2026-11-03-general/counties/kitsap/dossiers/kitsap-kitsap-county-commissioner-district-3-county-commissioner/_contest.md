## What the office does

The three-member Board of Commissioners is Kitsap County's legislative authority: it fixes county taxes, manages county funds and property, audits county accounts, builds and maintains courthouses, jails and county roads, and adopts county ordinances, including building, health and land-use codes, for unincorporated areas [S2]. Commissioner candidates are nominated by the voters of their own district in the primary and then go before the whole county in the general election [S3][S1].

## Race dynamics

Incumbent Katie Walters (Prefers Democratic Party), in office since 2023 (katie-walters.md S4), faces Kevin Tisdel (Prefers Republican Party), a North Kitsap native and former Seattle Public Utilities manager who has not held office [S1]. Both advanced from the August 4 District 3 primary (katie-walters.md S10; kevin-tisdel.md S5). Walters lists endorsements from Kitsap mayors, the sheriff, the prosecutor, two tribes and the county Democrats; Tisdel lists the Washington Farm Bureau PAC (katie-walters.md S5; kevin-tisdel.md S2). Walters reports about $23.6k raised to Tisdel's $3.9k (katie-walters.md S10; kevin-tisdel.md S5).

The county faces a projected $9.8 million general-fund shortfall in 2027, driven mainly by labor, jail medical and sheriff overtime costs. The board has discussed a 0.1% sales tax under HB 2015, 45-60 position cuts, or a mix, and is expected to adopt a budget in December [S4].

Growth is the main policy divide. Walters helped complete the Comprehensive Plan update and the Silverdale Regional Center Plan, and in 2025 voted with the board majority to drop 14 rural residential rezone requests that conflicted with policies steering growth to urban areas [S5] (katie-walters.md S4). Tisdel wants the Growth Management Act and recent state density and builder's-remedy laws repealed, argues that Kitsap has a housing surplus rather than a shortage, and opposes the multifamily tax exemption and the cost-sharing in the Silverdale sewer plan (kevin-tisdel.md S3).

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Housing / growth:** affordable and workforce housing within the county's urban-growth plans, and a low-barrier navigation shelter (Walters); GMA repeal, opposition to state density mandates and "workforce zoning" (Tisdel).
2. **Taxes:** Tisdel opposes the multifamily tax exemption and farm taxation. Walters has stated no tax position; the board is weighing a sales-tax increase.
3. **Spending:** Walters stresses investment in infrastructure, transit and services alongside "fiscal responsibility." Tisdel wants every land-use and infrastructure decision to be net-positive for revenue, while also proposing trail, market and road projects.
4. **Safety:** Walters pairs law enforcement with behavioral-health and treatment investment; Tisdel has no stated public-safety platform.
5. **Experience:** a one-term incumbent with regional board chairs, against a first-time candidate.
