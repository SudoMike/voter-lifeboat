## Background
Dan Griffey, a Republican from Allyn, has held the 35th District's Position 1 House seat since he was first elected in 2014 [S2][S4]. He began volunteering with his local fire department at 16 and worked more than 30 years for Central Mason Fire and EMS before retiring [S2]. His pamphlet also lists work as a truck driver, firefighter lieutenant and EMT instructor, union president, small business owner and emergency training instructor in local schools [S1]. He attended North Mason High School (1989), the North Bend Fire Academy and Olympic College (industrial electronics certificate) [S1]. His campaign site says he sits on the Local Government, Transportation, and Community Safety, Justice and Reentry committees and was elected House Republican Whip in 2019 [S4].

In the August 4 primary he led a three-candidate field with just over 52% (21,293 votes), ahead of Democrat Jim Pierson at just over 29% (11,809) and Democrat Shaena Garberich at about 18% (7,511), as reported by the Kitsap Daily News [S3].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** says the state income tax on "millionaires" is "driving away jobs" and that its revenue will leave with high earners, shifting the burden to those "who cannot afford another financial hit" [S2]. His pamphlet cites his "No" vote on the income tax as support for small businesses [S1]. His campaign site has a "No State Income Tax" plank, says taxing some and not others is unconstitutional, and proposes lowering overall rates by limiting special-interest exemptions [S5]. Wants to lower the gas tax, which he calls the second-highest in the nation [S2].
- **Spending:** says budgets should be based on "actual revenues- not hoped for revenues"; supports a "Budget Sunshine Act" five-day review period before final budget votes and a requirement that the Legislature adopt a balanced budget [S5].
- **Economy:** names affordability, crime and businesses leaving the state as his top three issues [S2]. Says burdensome taxes and regulations hinder job creation and that legislation should be judged by whether it creates or hurts private-sector jobs [S5].
- **Housing and land use:** names protecting private property rights as a priority [S2]. Supports legislation that keeps housing affordable, protects "citizen's rights to use their land," does not tax people unfairly for property they cannot fully use, and "keeps local decisions local" [S5].
- **Public safety:** "We must put families before felons" [S5]. Supports tougher penalties for child abuse and gang activity, GPS monitoring of sex offenders, and limits on prisoners' public-records requests [S5]. Says crime is "running rampant" under majority-party policies and calls for "applying the law as written" and supporting victims "instead of making excuses for their attackers" [S2]. His pamphlet cites work on community notification of sexually violent predators and on ending "loopholes allowing criminals to walk free" [S1].
- **Energy:** supports recognizing hydropower as an eligible renewable resource, citing lower consumer energy costs [S5].
- **Education funding:** says the budget should "fund education first" and that property-poor districts need stable funding [S5].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no campaign statement found. Record: voted against ESSB 5599 (2023, youth seeking protected health care services) and ESHB 1469 (2023, access to reproductive health care and gender-affirming treatment) [S14][S15].
- **Parents and schools:** no campaign statement found. Record: voted for Initiative 2081, the parents' bill of rights, in 2024 [S11], and against ESHB 1296 in 2025 [S12], the act that changed those rights and that I-1 would repeal [S13].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Related: voted against ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S6], and campaigns against it [S1][S2].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08. Related: voted for I-2081 (2024) and against ESHB 1296 (2025) [S11][S12]. I-1 would restore I-2081 by repealing ESHB 1296's changes [S13].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- ESSB 6346 (2026, tax on income over $1 million): voted nay on House final passage, 2026-03-09 (passed 51-46), and yea on the motion to postpone it indefinitely (failed 38-58) [S6]. His floor amendment 2566 would have excluded public pensions from income subject to the tax; it was not adopted [S7][S6].
- ESSB 5813 (2025, higher capital gains and estate tax rates): voted nay on House final passage, 2025-04-23 (passed 53-45) [S8].
- ESHB 2081 (2025, business and occupation tax increases): voted nay on House final passage, 2025-04-22 (passed 50-48) [S9].
- Initiative 2111 (2024, barring state and local income taxes): voted yea, 2024-03-04 (passed 76-21) [S10].
- Initiative 2081 (2024): voted yea, 2024-03-04 (passed 82-15) [S11]. ESHB 1296 (2025): voted nay on final passage, 2025-04-24 (passed 59-39) [S12].
- ESSB 5599 (2023): voted nay, 2023-04-12 (passed 57-39) [S14]. ESHB 1469 (2023): voted nay, 2023-02-28 (passed 59-38) [S15].
- His campaign site claims 141 bills passed and more than $424 million for the district [S4].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $94,579.69 raised, $32,563.05 spent [S17].
