## What the office does
The prosecuting attorney prosecutes criminal and civil actions for the state and county, defends suits brought against the county, and is legal adviser to the county commissioners, county officers and school directors (S2). In Benton County the office has about 30 prosecutors and handles adult felony, juvenile and district court cases (S4).

## Race dynamics
Two Republicans meet in November. Incumbent Eric Eisinger, prosecutor since 2023 after winning a close 2022 race, faces Julie Long, a 28-year trial prosecutor who worked in the Benton and Franklin County offices (S1; eric-eisinger.md S7; julie-long.md S3). The Tri-City Herald's May headline described Long as a deputy prosecutor saying "her old Benton Co. boss is failing" (S5). In the certified August 4 primary Eisinger received 22,046 votes (54.1%) and Long 18,218 (44.7%) (S3).

The central dispute is case backlog and office management. Long's statement says more than 500 cases await charging decisions and makes clearing them her first-year goal (S1; julie-long.md S5). Eisinger says the office inherited about 360 adult felony cases in 2023, has reduced that number, and has cut deputy prosecutor vacancies from five to one. He also says the office needs about twice its current number of criminal prosecutors (S4). Both candidates run on enforcement: Eisinger on charging the highest provable offense and moving juvenile violent cases to adult court, Long on high bail and no plea bargains with violent defendants. Both back therapeutic and veterans courts for suitable defendants (eric-eisinger.md S2; julie-long.md S1, S2).

Endorsements split the local Republican establishment. The Benton County Republican Party, Sheriff Mike Clark, the Tri-Cities mayors and two county commissioners back Eisinger (eric-eisinger.md S1, S2). Long lists former Sheriff Tom Croskrey, Commissioner Jerome Delvin, Clerk Josie Delvin, Sen. Nikki Torres, former Prosecutor Andy Miller and many retired police officers, plus the Pasco Police Officers Association (julie-long.md S4). Eisinger reports $67.4k raised and Long $37.8k (eric-eisinger.md S6; julie-long.md S6).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience/record:** the incumbent's first-term record (staffing, backlog, charging policies) against a career line and chief deputy prosecutor who has not held elected office and says she would keep trying cases while leading the office.
2. **Safety:** both are enforcement-first. Long stresses ending lenient plea bargains and clearing the charging backlog. Eisinger stresses aggressive charging, juvenile cases in adult court and clearing encampments. The difference is emphasis, not direction.
3. **Spending:** Eisinger argues the state underfunds local criminal justice and that the office needs roughly double its criminal prosecutors. Long's sources contain no comparable budget position.
4. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives: none found for either candidate.
