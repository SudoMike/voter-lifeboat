## Background

Julie Long has spent 28 years as a deputy prosecuting attorney in Benton and Franklin counties and says she served as chief deputy prosecutor for more than 15 years (S1, S3). She reports more than 100 jury trials as lead counsel and cases argued before the Court of Appeals and the Washington Supreme Court (S1, S3). Her prosecutions include aggravated first-degree murder, rape, arson and drug trafficking (S1). She has worked with the Tri-City Metro Drug Task Force, the Kennewick Criminal Apprehension Team and the Richland and Pasco street crimes units (S1). She has not held elected office (S1). She holds a B.A. in Law and Justice from Central Washington University and a J.D. from Loyola University New Orleans (S1, S3). She prefers the Republican Party (S1).

The Tri-City Herald's May 2026 headline described her as a deputy prosecutor who says "her old Benton Co. boss is failing" (S7). Her site says she is running "to bring that experience and proven leadership back to the office" (S3).

In the August 4 primary she received 18,218 votes (44.7%) to Eisinger's 22,046 (54.1%) (S8).

## Positions

### Public safety and charging
- "I have a proven record of asking for high bail and not plea bargaining with violent criminals. I aggressively prosecute drug trafficking and gang-related crimes" (S1).
- Says she "will go to trial against those who falsely claim insanity to avoid responsibility" (S1).
- Campaign priorities include ending "lenient plea bargaining," restoring working relationships with local law enforcement and giving victims a greater voice (S2).
- "As the daughter of a Marine, I value our therapeutic and Veteran courts and support appropriate candidates for these programs" (S1). Her site lists Drug Court and Veterans Court among the therapeutic courts she supports (S2).
- "I am a prosecutor, not a politician," and she says she will continue "to aggressively prosecute crimes and hold offenders accountable while listening to and putting victims at the highest priority" (S5).

### Backlog and office management
- "I will prioritize the office's backlog of over 500 cases awaiting charging decisions so the victims finally have their day in court" (S1). Her site says "More than 500 law enforcement referrals are waiting for charging decisions" (S2).
- Would judge her first year by how many of the backlogged cases receive charging decisions. She plans to keep trying cases and to mentor younger deputy prosecutors while leading the office (S5).
