## Background

- Michael Alvarez (Prefers Republican Party) took office as Benton County Commissioner for District 2 on Jan. 1, 2023, and is chair of the board [S1][S2][S3].
- He served on the Richland City Council from 2018 to Jan. 1, 2023, including as mayor [S2].
- He is a U.S. Marine Corps veteran and a U.S. Coast Guard Auxiliary member [S1][S2]. He has owned a mortgage company for 30 years [S1]. He is a Washington-licensed instructor in finance, business management and ethics [S1][S2].
- He holds a bachelor's in business administration from New Mexico State University [S1][S2].
- He won the seat in 2022 with 65.1% over William "Bill" Jenkin [S4]. He was unopposed in the August 2026 District 2 primary, with 12,051 votes [S5].

## Positions

- **Taxes and spending:** Pledges to ensure "taxes are NOT raised unnecessarily" and claims "a proven record of saving taxpayers millions of dollars each year" [S1]. His campaign site promises to keep the county "on a sustainable financial track without raising taxes" [S3].
- **Public safety:** "Strongly support[s] our police officers and firefighters" and their tools and resources [S1]. Says he is endorsed by IAFF Local 1052 firefighters [S3].
- **Behavioral health:** His site promises to work on "building a mental health and substance abuse treatment facility and program in Benton County without raising taxes or fees" [S3].
- **Economic development and agriculture:** Backs "Smart, Sustainable Economic Development" [S1]. His site pledges to "protect our Agriculture Industry and its water rights" [S3].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

- **I-645 (income-tax repeal), I-1 (parental rights), I-638 (girls' sports):** No public position found in the sources reviewed.

## Record

- County commissioner since 2023 and board chair [S1][S2]. He serves on the Benton County Emergency Services and Benton-Franklin Health District boards, among others [S1].
- Richland City Council 2018-2022, including as mayor [S2].
