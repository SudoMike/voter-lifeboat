## Background
Kyle Palmer, of Richland, is a Democrat running for office for the first time; he lists no elected experience [S1][S2]. He is a small business owner and real-estate professional with experience in housing, land use and regional development [S1]. He attended Hanford High School, did undergraduate work in social sciences at Washington State University and graduate study in real-estate development at Portland State University [S1][S2]. He is a former two-term member and chair of the Richland Planning Commission, a former AmeriCorps VISTA member, a former chair of the Tri-Cities Association of REALTORS Education Committee and a board member of Friends of the Richland Library [S1][S2]. He describes himself as a third-generation Mid-Columbia resident and a father [S1][S2].

In the August 4 primary he placed second with 13,998 votes (39.55%) to incumbent Mark Klicker's 21,367 (60.36%) [S4]. He was scheduled to appear at the October 8 AAUW forum in Walla Walla, which Klicker did not attend [S5]. The PDC record (updated 2026-10-01) reports no contributions or expenditures [S6]; the Union-Bulletin reported the same as of July 7 [S2]. No campaign website was listed in the voters' guide or the PDC record [S1][S6].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** at a July forum he said he wants the state to find programs without measurable results and get the budget "lean" before imposing new taxes. On the tax on income over $1 million, he said a person earning that much paying 9.9% on that portion is not a concern for him, but that he is "really concerned about people making less money than that and some of the affordability issues," adding "I don't want to tax first, I don't want to just cut to cut, I don't want to spend just to spend" [S3].
- **Spending:** says Washington "cannot continue making long-term spending commitments without a sustainable plan to pay for them," that every program should be evaluated on measurable outcomes, and that the state should cut administrative costs and prioritize education, public safety, infrastructure and services for vulnerable residents before new commitments [S2].
- **Education funding:** would protect K-12 and early learning while measuring which programs deliver results, and strengthen career and technical education and apprenticeships [S2].
- **Housing and growth:** names housing, water security, reliable energy and economic opportunity as priorities and calls for "streamlining barriers that prevent progress and innovation" [S1]. Cites his planning-commission work on "responsible growth" [S2].
- **Technology and data centers:** says skepticism of data centers should be taken seriously; if they come, the Legislature should require transparency about power and water use, make companies pay for the infrastructure they need and secure investments in local jobs, training, schools and services, and use them to accelerate advanced energy development [S2].
- **Climate and energy:** lists "reliable energy" as a priority and wants to accelerate advanced energy development [S1][S2]. No position on the Climate Commitment Act found.
- **Bipartisanship:** says he will "work with anyone, regardless of party" [S1].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no public position found in the sources reviewed.
- **Parents and schools:** no position on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs found in the sources reviewed.

## Scoring notes
- First-time candidate with appointed planning-commission experience against a three-term incumbent [S1][S2].
- Takes a mixed position on the income tax: unconcerned about the rate on top earners but wants a lean budget before new taxes [S3].
- Stresses outcome-based budgeting and no new long-term commitments without a funding plan [S2].
