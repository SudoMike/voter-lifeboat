## Background
Mark Klicker, a Republican from Walla Walla County, has represented the 16th Legislative District, Position 1, in the State House since 2021 [S1][S11]. He is ranking minority member of the House Local Government Committee and assistant ranking member of Environment and Energy, and serves on the Transportation and Rules committees and two joint committees (Administrative Rules Review; Energy Supply, Conservation and Resilience) [S1][S2]. Outside the Legislature he is a business owner, licensed real-estate professional, timber producer and farm-and-ranch realtor; he attended Walla Walla Community College and the Rockwell Institute School of Real Estate [S1]. His community service includes president of the Walla Walla County Farm Bureau, chairman of the Walla Walla County Voluntary Stewardship Program and board seats with Jubilee Christian Academy and the Fort Walla Walla Museum [S1].

In the August 4 primary he placed first with 21,367 votes (60.36%) to Democrat Kyle Palmer's 13,998 (39.55%) [S13]. He did not attend the AAUW candidate forum on October 8, saying he was out of state on that date [S14]. The PDC reported $68,175 raised and $23,136 spent as of 2026-09-28 [S15].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** his statement pledges to keep "championing tax relief" [S1]. At a July forum he called the 2026 tax on income over $1 million "the way to get it in the door" and said that unless the repeal initiative succeeds "eventually, every one of us will be paying income tax. It's not good"; he added that "we have to be able to find a way to stop the taxes" [S12].
- **Spending:** says the state "needs to cut wasteful spending and decrease the size of government," that the majority "has overspent on unneeded programs and administrative waste," and that Republicans proposed a balanced budget without new taxes [S11]. Cites the near-$82 billion operating budget against $33 billion not long ago [S12].
- **Education funding:** says the 2026 cuts to Local Effort Assistance, Running Start, Transition to Kindergarten and school bus depreciation should be restored under the state's paramount-duty clause; House Republicans ran amendments to block them [S11].
- **Public safety:** pledges to work on "rectifying harmful police reform bills" [S1].
- **Climate and energy:** advocates "more reliable energy sources, including nuclear, hydroelectric and natural gas" [S1]. Voted against the 2025 clean fuels program bill (see Record) [S7].
- **Regulation and business:** pledges to dismantle regulations that hinder small businesses and farmers [S1].
- **Technology and data centers:** says the data center in the district is a cloud-computing (not AI-training) facility in a rural area with no residents, and that Washington already holds data centers to strict rules on power, water, noise and environmental review [S11].
- **Housing:** voted against the 2025 tenant housing-stability bill [S8]; prime sponsor of bills creating a housing cost driver task force and allowing small businesses in residential zones (see Record) [S9].
- **Bipartisanship:** lists "bridging partisan divides" as a goal and says he remains committed to bipartisan ideas [S1].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no public position found in the sources reviewed.
- **Parents and schools:** voted against ESHB 1296 (2025), the bill amending the parents' rights law enacted by initiative I-2081, on House passage and on final passage [S6]. No statement on parental notification or opt-outs found.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** said at a July forum that if "this initiative (to repeal the tax) can defeat that law, we won't have it," and that otherwise everyone will eventually pay an income tax [S12]. Record: voted against ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S3].
- **I-1 (parental rights):** no public position found in the sources reviewed. Record: voted against ESHB 1296, the 2025 amendments to the parental-rights law that I-1 would undo [S6].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found in the sources reviewed.

## Record
- Voted nay on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million (2026-03-09, passed 51-46); voted yea on the motion to postpone the bill indefinitely (failed 38-58) and offered floor amendment 2555 (failed 43-51) [S3].
- Voted nay on House final passage of ESHB 2081, business and occupation tax increases (2025-04-22, passed 50-48) [S4], and of ESSB 5814, excise taxes on select services (2025-04-23, passed 50-47) [S5].
- Voted nay on ESHB 1296 on House passage (2025-03-12, 56-37) and on final passage as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24, 59-39) [S6].
- Voted nay on the clean fuels program bill, HB 1409, on House passage (2025-03-10, 54-41) and final passage as amended by the Senate (2025-04-21, 54-43) [S7].
- Voted nay on EHB 1217, "improving housing stability for tenants," on House passage (2025-03-10, 53-42) and on final passage as recommended by the conference committee (2025-04-27, 54-44) [S8].
- Prime sponsor of ESHB 1293 on litter, signed into law (effective 2025-07-27) [S9][S10]; E2SHB 1108, a housing cost driver task force, passed the Legislature and was vetoed by the governor (2025-05-20) [S9]; E2SHB 1175, allowing small business establishments in residential zones, was returned to House Rules for third reading in 2026 [S9]. Other 2025-26 prime-sponsored bills include HB 1994 (tax policy for communities hosting renewable energy), HB 2175 (sales-tax exemption for free durable medical equipment providers) and HB 2223 (irrigation district directors) [S9].
