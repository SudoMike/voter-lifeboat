## What the office does

Benton PUD is a publicly owned, not-for-profit electric utility. It was organized by a local vote in 1934 and began operating in 1946 [S4]. It reports 59,008 connections and an average residential bill of $121 a month [S5], and says it serves "over 59,000 customers in Kennewick, Finley, Benton City, Prosser, and outlying areas" [S4]. Nearly all of its wholesale power comes from the Bonneville Power Administration (209.2 of 210.3 average megawatts) [S5]. Three elected commissioners govern it. Each serves a six-year term, and the terms are staggered so that one seat is on the ballot every two years [S3][S2]. Regular meetings are held on the second and fourth Tuesdays in Kennewick [S3].

## How commissioners are elected

- **Nominated by district, elected PUD-wide.** Under RCW 54.12.010(3), only a registered voter living in a commissioner district may run for or hold that district's seat. Only voters of the district vote in its primary, but "voters of the entire public utility district may vote at a general election to elect a person as a commissioner of the commissioner district" [S2]. Holliday's campaign FAQ says the same: "Everyone across the Benton PUD service area can vote in this race, not just District 2" [S15].
- **No primary this year.** Only two candidates filed for the six-year term, so the race went straight to the general [S14][S12][S13].
- **Open seat.** District 2 is held by Jeff Hall. He was appointed in January 2002, won election that fall, and began his fourth full term in January 2021 [S3][S5]. He is not on the 2026 ballot [S14]. In 2020 he won the seat over Pat Tucker, 58.38% to 41.08% [S11].

## Does Benton PUD cover all of Benton County, including Richland?

No. The PUD's own records and past results show that Richland is outside the PUD's electorate:

- **Service area.** The PUD lists its service area as Kennewick, Finley, Benton City, Prosser and outlying areas, and Richland is not on that list [S4]. The Commission page says commissioners are "elected by citizens of Benton County" and that "each commissioner represents a different sector of the county" [S3]. That describes the PUD's home county; it does not mean the whole county votes.
- **District map.** The PUD's Commissioner Districts map, adopted March 12, 2024, shades Districts 1 and 3 (parts of the Kennewick urban area) and District 2 (the rest of the county, including Prosser, Benton City, Finley, the Horse Heaven Hills and the Hanford area). It leaves the Richland and West Richland urban area unshaded, so that area is in none of the three districts [S6][S7].
- **Past ballots.** In the November 2024 general, the Benton PUD Commissioner Pos. 3 race drew 47,850 votes. The countywide Benton County Commissioner District #1 race on the same ballot drew 88,498 [S8][S9]. Of the 246 precincts that voted on the county commissioner race, 90 had no PUD contest [S9]. Those include every precinct numbered 4001-4102 and all but two of those numbered 6105-6322 [S9]. In the county's 2026 precinct committee officer filings, those precinct numbers carry Richland and West Richland addresses [S10].
- **Size of the electorate.** The PDC lists the Benton PUD jurisdiction at 80,872 voters [S12][S13].

- **Not found.** No Benton PUD page that lists, in words, the cities inside its electoral boundary was found. No 2026 precinct-level ballot style list was retrieved. The conclusion above rests on the 2023-2024 maps and the 2024 precinct results [S6][S7][S9].

## Race dynamics

- **Two first-time PUD candidates.** Roy C. "Dewey" Holliday is an agricultural executive from Prosser. He is president of the Columbia Snake River Irrigators Association and an appointed West Benton Fire and Rescue commissioner [S1][S14]. Micah Valentine is a Kennewick business owner in his second term on the Kennewick School Board [S1][S14].
- **Shared themes.** Both say Benton PUD is well run today and promise reliable, affordable power [S1].
- **Different framing.** Valentine runs on opposition to "Olympia" energy mandates, protecting dams, expanding nuclear, and opposing reliance on wind and solar. He campaigns openly as a conservative with Benton County Republican Party backing (see his dossier) [S1]. Holliday stresses financial oversight, infrastructure planning and agricultural loads. His site says the PUD should not "pick a favorite technology" (see his dossier) [S1][S15].
- **Money.** As of the Sept. 17, 2026 PDC update, Holliday files full reports and had raised $2,485. Valentine registered under the "mini" reporting option [S12][S13].

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

- **Energy-mix framing.** Valentine opposes "forcing" wind and solar onto the grid and wants more nuclear and possibly a gas plant. Holliday wants hydropower to stay the backbone and would weigh every option on reliability, cost and risk [S1][S15].
- **Background.** Holliday comes from agricultural operations, irrigation and power policy, and a fire-district board. Valentine comes from small business and four-plus years on an elected school board [S1].
- **Partisan profile in a nonpartisan race.** Valentine lists Republican Party endorsements and frames the race as getting "conservatives back in local government". Holliday says he is not running as a Republican or a Democrat (see dossiers).
