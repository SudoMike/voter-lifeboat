## Background

Roy C. "Dewey" Holliday is a senior executive in agricultural operations. He says he has managed more than $70 million a year in operating and capital budgets across farming operations in Washington, Oregon, Idaho and California [S1][S2]. He is president of the Columbia Snake River Irrigators Association, principal of Holliday Management Consulting LLC, and an accredited farm manager [S1]. His one public office is an appointed seat as a West Benton Fire and Rescue commissioner [S1][S2]. Earlier in his career he was a firefighter, paramedic, lieutenant and paramedic instructor, and president of IAFF Local 4366 [S1]. He holds a B.S. in agriculture from Oregon State University [S1]. His filing lists a Prosser address [S5].

## Positions

**Utility governance and rates**
- Says the seat requires "disciplined financial oversight" and promises "reliable service, responsible infrastructure investment, transparency, and protecting the long-term interests of ratepayers" [S1].
- Says Benton PUD is "not broken" and "is in a strong position today." He is running because of harder decisions ahead on power supply, transmission constraints, large new loads and capital investment [S3].
- On rates: much of the cost pressure comes from BPA and regional transmission. "Responsible rates doesn't mean just voting no on every increase," but making decisions now "so costs don't spike later" [S3].

**Energy sources**
- Says "Hydropower should stay the backbone of our regional power supply." Wind and solar "can help, but they're not always there when demand peaks" [S2].
- Wants the PUD to consider conservation, shifting demand off-peak, storage, line capacity, regional contracts and "newer technology like small modular reactors" [S2].
- Says the PUD should not "pick a favorite technology" and that he will judge each option on "reliability, affordability, and risk" [S3].

**Large loads and agriculture**
- On data centers and other large loads: "Case by case." A new large customer should bring a credible power and infrastructure plan, and existing customers "shouldn't have to subsidize the cost of serving it" [S3].
- Stresses that summer irrigation, cooling, cold storage and processing loads drive peak demand. Says growers should help design PUD programs that affect them [S2][S3].

**Partisanship**
- Says PUD races are nonpartisan by law and that he is not running as a Republican or a Democrat [S3]. His site describes the PUD as serving "this valley, not outside interests, not state politics, not ideology" [S2].
