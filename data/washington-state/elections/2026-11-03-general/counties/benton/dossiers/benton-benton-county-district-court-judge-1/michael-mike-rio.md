## Background

Michael Rio is a prosecutor who says he has spent 21 years prosecuting the kinds of cases heard in Tri-Cities district courts [S1][S3]. He began in 2002 as a Spokane County deputy prosecutor handling property crimes and moved to the Tri-Cities in 2005 [S3]. Since then he has been a contract city prosecutor for Prosser, Richland, West Richland, Pasco, College Place and Othello, and a contract district-court prosecutor for Franklin County (2025) and Adams County (2026) [S1]. His site describes him as the primary prosecutor for Pasco, Richland and West Richland and says he now practices at Sant Law Firm in Kennewick [S3]. He co-founded the Rio Foltz firm and was a partner at Bell, Brown & Rio [S1][S2].

- **Judicial and quasi-judicial roles.** Judge pro tem in Franklin County District Court; Benton City hearing examiner since 2007 [S1][S3].
- **Teaching and training.** Instructor at the state Criminal Justice Training Commission's regional law-enforcement academy in Pasco. Adjunct professor of business law at WSU Tri-Cities and in Charter College's paralegal program [S1][S2]. Completed the IACP Drug Evaluation and Classification Program in 2009 [S3].
- **Education.** B.A. in molecular biology, University of Montana (1995); J.D. cum laude, Gonzaga (1999); LL.M. in intellectual property, University of Houston (2001). Member of the U.S. Patent Bar. Admitted to the Washington bar in 2001 [S1][S3].

## Positions

- **Judicial approach.** His campaign rests on three words, "Consistent. Experienced. Principled." He says "People deserve to know that the same standards apply every time a case comes up" [S1][S2]. He states that "an exemplary judge must be impartial, maintain order, protect the victims and community, and the rights of the accused," and pledges to be "fair, firm, and follow the rule of law" [S1].
- **Accountability and recidivism.** "Our communities expect safe neighborhoods and accountability when laws are broken" [S1]. His site says "Without legal accountability, recidivism rates rise" [S3].
- **Bail, diversion, therapeutic courts.** No position found in the sources reviewed.

## Endorsements

- **Judges.** All seven Benton-Franklin Superior Court judges and Commissioner Quinn Bell. Benton County District Court Judges Jennifer Azure, James Bell, John Ziobro, Dan Kathren and Terry Tanner, plus Commissioner Jennifer Johnson. Franklin County District Court Judge Trinity Orosco, Pasco Municipal Court Judge Carlos Villarreal, and several retired and out-of-county judges [S4].
- **Prosecutors.** Benton County Prosecutor Eric Eisinger, Franklin County Prosecutor Shawn Sant, Adams County Prosecutor Randy Flyckt, and former Benton County Prosecutor Andy Miller [S1][S4].
- **Law enforcement.** West Richland Police Officers Association; Fraternal Order of Police Tri-City Lodge 7; Benton County Sheriff Mike Clark; current and retired police chiefs and officers from Pasco, Richland, West Richland, College Place and Othello [S4].
- **Others.** State Sen. Nikki Torres, Benton County Coroner Bill Leach, and Othello's former mayor and city administrator [S4]. His endorsements page also lists a recommendation from the Benton County Republican Party [S4].

## Scoring notes

- Formation: his career is almost all prosecution (2002 to the present). He also trains law enforcement and is endorsed by prosecutors and police groups [S1][S3][S4].
- He campaigns on consistency, accountability and lower recidivism. He has stated no bail or diversion position [S1][S3].
- Open seat: he is not an incumbent, but he runs on two decades of district-court prosecution and pro tem service, endorsed by the incumbent and most of the local bench [S1][S4].
