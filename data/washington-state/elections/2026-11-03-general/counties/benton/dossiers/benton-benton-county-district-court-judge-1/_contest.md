## What the office does

Benton County District Court is a court of limited jurisdiction. It hears misdemeanor and gross-misdemeanor cases, traffic and other infractions, anti-harassment matters, small claims, and civil cases up to $100,000 [S2]. The court has five elected judges and a commissioner [S3], and each judge is elected countywide to a four-year term [S1]. The court runs Mental Health, Recovery and Veterans therapeutic courts [S2]. A judge's discretion here is mostly in pretrial release, sentencing and probation on misdemeanors, and in supervising therapeutic-court participants.

## Race dynamics

- **Open seat.** Judge Terry Tanner holds Position 1. He was appointed in 2009 and was re-elected in 2018 and 2022 [S3][S4][S5]. He is not on the 2026 ballot [S1].
- **No primary.** Two candidates filed, so the race goes straight to the November ballot [S1].
- **Candidates.** Michael "Mike" Rio is a contract city and district-court prosecutor with 21 years of local prosecution, a Franklin County District Court judge pro tem, and Benton City's hearing examiner [S1]. Talesha Sams is an attorney with 25 years in private practice. She has also worked as a prosecutor and a public defender and as an Adult Protective Services attorney, and she has been a King County District Court judge pro tem since 2015 [S1].
- **Rematch for the seat.** Sams ran for Position 1 in 2018 and lost to Tanner, 34.5% to 65.5% [S4].
- **Endorsements.** Rio lists all seven Benton-Franklin Superior Court judges, all five sitting Benton County District Court judges (including Position 1 incumbent Tanner), District Court Commissioner Jennifer Johnson, the Benton, Franklin and Adams county prosecutors, Benton County Sheriff Mike Clark and two police associations [S6]. No endorsement list for Sams was found.
- **Money.** As of Sept. 21, 2026, Rio reported $5,594 in contributions, a $20,000 loan and $19,395 in spending [S7]. Sams registered under the PDC's mini-reporting option, so her campaign files no itemized reports [S8].
- **Bar ratings.** No Benton-Franklin County Bar Association poll or rating for this race was found in the sources reviewed.

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

- **Professional formation (`safety`).** Rio's career is almost entirely prosecution: Spokane County deputy prosecutor from 2002, then contract prosecutor for Tri-Cities-area cities and district courts. He campaigns on accountability, "safe neighborhoods" and reducing recidivism [S1][S6]. Sams has a mixed background: prosecutor, public defender, private practice, Adult Protective Services attorney, Title 11 court visitor and Veterans Court mentor [S1]. Neither candidate has a documented position on bail or diversion.
- **Experience (`experience`).** Neither candidate is an incumbent. Rio offers two decades of district-court prosecution, pro tem service and the backing of most of the local bench [S1][S6]. Sams offers 25 years of practice across civil, criminal and administrative law and more than ten years as a pro tem judge in King County [S1].

## Not differentiating

- Both describe a restrained judicial role: Rio pledges to "follow the rule of law" and Sams says she "would uphold the law and would not legislate any personal agenda" [S1]. Neither statement goes far enough to separate them on `judicial`.
- Both have Gonzaga law degrees [S1].
