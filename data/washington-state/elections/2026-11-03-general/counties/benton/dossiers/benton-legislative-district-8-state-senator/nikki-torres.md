## Background
Nikki Torres (Prefers Republican Party), of Pasco, has been a state senator for the 15th Legislative District since 2023; she was elected in 2022 and before that was elected to the Pasco City Council in 2021 [S1][S16]. The Legislature lists her as Republican Deputy Floor Leader, ranking member on Local Government, assistant ranking member (operating) on Ways & Means, and a member of Law & Justice [S2]. She worked in commercial lending and business development at Gesa Credit Union, holds an MBA, and is a past president of the Tri-Cities Hispanic Chamber of Commerce [S1][S13].

The court-ordered 2024 redrawing of Central Washington's legislative districts, after a federal judge found the earlier boundaries hurt Latino voters' opportunity to elect preferred candidates, left her outside the new 15th District; she announced on 2026-01-22 that she would run in the 8th, where Sen. Matt Boehnke is leaving to run for Congress [S16]. She has criticized the court's decision and the redrawn maps [S16].

In the August 4 primary she placed first with 16,065 votes (about 52%) to Gabe Galbraith's 14,297 (about 46%), with 511 write-ins [S19]. PDC reports (updated 2026-10-06) show her 8th District campaign raised $206,006.60 and spent $53,660.59 [S18].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** says she will "continue to oppose wasteful spending, unfair taxes, burdensome regulations" and that the state should manage its finances "conservatively, responsibly" [S1]. Her campaign issues page opposes increases in the state gas tax and the carbon-pricing costs on fuel, pledging to "fight hard to stop this gas tax increase" [S14]. (The issues page cites 2021-2022 figures and appears to date from her 2022 campaign [S14].)
- **Spending:** pledges "responsible state budgets that put working families first" and says she secured more than $500 million in capital funding for regional infrastructure, public safety and community projects [S1]. The funding figure is candidate-reported [S1].
- **Public safety:** names support for law enforcement as the work she is "especially proud of" [S1]. Her issues page pledges full police funding, regional law-enforcement training centers, changes to the 2021 police reform laws including lifting restrictions on vehicle pursuits, and a critical review of officer decertification [S14].
- **Climate and energy:** calls herself a "fierce defender" of the lower Snake River dams, saying dams and salmon runs can coexist and the dams are a clean energy source [S14]; opposes the fuel-price effect of the state's carbon-emissions program [S14].
- **Housing:** lists affordable housing among her priorities and proposes a dialogue with home builders and local governments on costs [S1][S14]. Voted against rent-increase limits (see Record) [S10].
- **Wildlife:** wants the Department of Fish and Wildlife to shorten the list of protected predator species [S14].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no public position found in the sources reviewed.
- **Parents and schools:** lists "strong public schools" among her commitments [S1]. Record: voted for Initiative 2081 (2024 parents' bill of rights) and against ESHB 1296 (2025), which amended it (see Record) [S8][S9]. Sponsored the 2025 school emergency-response law (see Record) [S11][S17].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found in the sources reviewed. Record: voted against ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal, on Senate passage and on final passage [S3].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no statement on the initiative found in the sources reviewed. Record: voted for Initiative 2081 in 2024 and against ESHB 1296 in 2025, the amendments I-1 would undo [S8][S9].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found in the sources reviewed.

## Record
- **ESSB 6346 (2026, 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million):** voted nay on Senate third reading (2026-02-16, passed 27-22) and on final passage as amended by the House (2026-03-11, passed 27-21) [S3].
- **2025 revenue bills:** voted nay on final passage of ESHB 2081, business and occupation tax increases (2025-04-26, passed 26-22) [S4]; ESSB 5813, capital gains and estate tax changes (2025-04-24, passed 27-21) [S5]; and ESSB 5814, excise taxes on select services (2025-04-24, passed 26-22) [S6]. Voted for several Republican floor amendments to ESHB 2081, all of which failed [S4].
- **2025-27 operating budget (ESSB 5167):** voted nay on Senate passage (2025-03-29, 28-21) and on the conference report (2025-04-27, 28-19); voted for the failed Republican striking amendment offered by Sen. Gildon (19-30) [S7].
- **Parents' rights:** voted yea on Initiative 2081, the parents' bill of rights, which passed the Senate 49-0 (2024-03-04) [S9]. Voted nay on ESHB 1296, which amended it, on Senate passage (2025-04-11, passed 30-19), after voting for a failed amendment by Sen. Gildon (19-30) [S8].
- **Rent limits:** voted nay on EHB 1217, residential rent-increase limits, on Senate passage (2025-04-10, 29-20) and on the conference report (2025-04-27, 27-20) [S10].
- **Sponsored bills:** prime sponsor of SB 5004 (school emergency response systems, modeled on "Alyssa's Law"), which passed 49-0 and 98-0 and took effect 2025-07-27 [S11][S17]; prime sponsor of SB 6066 on crash prevention zones, enacted as chapter 123, Laws of 2026 [S12].

## Endorsements
Her voters'-guide statement names Reps. April Connors and Stephanie Barnard (both 8th District) [S1]. Her website also lists U.S. Rep. Michael Baumgartner, former U.S. Rep. Doc Hastings, state Sens. Shelly Short, Mark Schoesler, Judy Warnick, Perry Dozier and Curtis King, several Republican state representatives, Pasco Mayor Charles Grimm, Franklin County Prosecutor Shawn Sant, and the Adams and Yakima county sheriffs [S15].
