## What the office does

A state senator writes and votes on Washington law and on the operating, capital and transportation budgets; the term is four years [S1]. The Senate roll calls on ESSB 6346, the 2026 tax on income over $1 million, and on ESHB 1296, the 2025 amendments to the parents' bill of rights, are examples of the votes members cast [S4][S5]. The 8th District ballot covers parts of Benton and Franklin counties [S1].

## Race dynamics

Two Republicans advanced from the August 4 primary: Sen. Nikki Torres with 16,065 votes (about 52%) and Kennewick School Board President Gabe Galbraith with 14,297 (about 46%) [S3]. The seat is open because Sen. Matt Boehnke is running for Congress [S2]. Torres has represented the 15th District since 2023; the court-ordered 2024 redistricting left her outside it, and she announced for the 8th in January 2026 [S2]. Galbraith, already in the race, criticized her entry, and his voters'-guide statement contrasts his 35 years in the area with "a politician who moved here in February" [S1][S2].

Torres runs on her Senate record and caucus leadership and is endorsed by both 8th District House members; Galbraith runs on his school-board record and local roots and is endorsed by the Benton County Republican Party [S1]. As of early October Torres had raised about $206,000 and Galbraith about $24,500 [S7][S8].

## What genuinely differentiates these candidates

- **experience:** a four-year incumbent senator in caucus leadership versus a first-time legislative candidate running on school-board service and a local-roots, anti-establishment message [S1][S2].
- **parental-rights:** both lean toward parental notification and opt-out. Torres voted against ESHB 1296 [S5]; Galbraith makes it a campaign centerpiece and says he was lead plaintiff in a lawsuit challenging it [S6].
- **social:** Galbraith states a sex-based position on girls' sports from his school-board work [S6]; no position from Torres was found.
- **reform / local-control:** Galbraith backs legislative hearings for citizen initiatives and opposes shifting authority away from elected sheriffs [S9]; Torres has no comparable statements in the sources reviewed.
- On taxes, public safety and energy their stated positions are close; Torres has the recorded votes (no on ESSB 6346 and the 2025 revenue bills) [S4].
