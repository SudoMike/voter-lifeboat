## Background
Gabe Galbraith (Prefers Republican Party), of Kennewick, was elected to the Kennewick School Board in 2021, re-elected in 2025, and has been board president since 2023 [S1]. He is a Kamiakin High School graduate, served in the U.S. Marine Corps 2003-2007 as an administrative clerk, worked in retail and operations management and at Amazon, and is now a security specialist at Hanford; his family runs a bounce-house rental business [S1][S6]. He holds a bachelor's in accounting (Strayer University), an MBA (Ashford University) and a non-licensure master's in elementary education (Grand Canyon University), and was a substitute teacher [S1][S6]. His statement says he was named a national School Board Director of the Year [S1]. This is his first run for the Legislature; he was in the race before Sen. Nikki Torres entered [S7].

In the August 4 primary he placed second with 14,297 votes (about 46%) to Torres's 16,065 (about 52%) [S9]. PDC reports (updated 2026-10-06) show $24,483.00 raised and $19,624.93 spent [S8].

## Positions
- **Race framing:** contrasts his 35 years in the area with "a politician who moved here in February to advance a political career" and calls Torres "a political opportunist focused on serving the state establishment" [S1]. When she entered he said the seat "is not an Indeed job application for career politicians" [S7]. His website says "Olympia politicians talk conservative at election time, then go quiet when it matters most" [S3].
- **Taxes:** opposes "higher taxes on working families" [S1] and says he is "fighting to reduce taxes, fees, and regulations" that raise living costs [S3]. Says that as a board member he helped reduce Kennewick's school tax rate from $4 to $3 per $1,000 of assessed value (self-reported) [S4].
- **Spending:** calls for "smarter spending and real oversight" and responsible budgets [S3]; cites the Kennewick district's 3.7-of-4 financial health rating during his tenure [S4].
- **Public safety:** pledges full funding for police, deputies and first responders, alongside legislation that "addresses the root causes of violence" [S3]. On homelessness, favors mental-health and substance-abuse treatment combined with enforcing laws "so communities remain safe and clean" [S3]. Says he led creation of a school safety officer program in Kennewick schools [S4].
- **Housing:** "more housing, built faster and at lower cost," with less bureaucracy and "local control" [S3].
- **Climate and energy:** supports an "all-of-the-above" energy strategy including nuclear, opposes breaching the lower Snake River dams, and wants environmental policies that do not raise costs on families and farmers [S3].
- **Local government:** opposes "efforts to strip authority from elected sheriffs and shift that power to unelected state bureaucrats" [S3].
- **Initiatives and process:** supports the initiative process and says initiatives with hundreds of thousands of signatures deserve public legislative hearings; opposes "procedural games or legislative delay" [S3].
- **Wildlife:** wants accountability at the Department of Fish and Wildlife driven by "sound science—not political ideology" [S3].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** says that as board president he backed a Kennewick position that girls' athletic competition should be "based on biological differences," and that the district filed a federal complaint challenging state rules on girls' sports (self-reported) [S4].
- **Parents and schools:** lists defending parental rights among his commitments [S1]. Says he was lead plaintiff in a lawsuit against the state challenging HB 1296 (the 2025 amendments to the parents' bill of rights) (self-reported; no independent source reviewed) [S4]. Says he led a district policy keeping instruction focused on academic standards and "not divisive theories like Critical Race Theory," supported review of sexual-health curriculum with parent review and opt-out, and introduced a board resolution opposing a state COVID-19 vaccine requirement for K-12 students [S4]. Favors "keeping education decisions local, not dictated by one-size-fits-all mandates from Olympia" [S3]. Lists Parents' Rights in Education among supporters [S5].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no public position on the initiative found in the sources reviewed; opposes higher taxes generally [S1][S3].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no statement on the initiative itself found in the sources reviewed. His website describes his lawsuit against HB 1296, the 2025 law I-1 would undo [S4].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no statement on the initiative itself found in the sources reviewed. His website describes his school-board stand for sex-based eligibility in girls' sports [S4].

## Record
No legislative record. School-board actions he describes (self-reported on his campaign website): school safety officer program; girls'-sports position and federal complaint; curriculum policy on Critical Race Theory; resolution opposing a COVID-19 vaccine mandate for students; school levy rate reduction from $4 to $3 per $1,000 [S4].

## Endorsements

Benton County Sheriff Mike Clark, Prosecutor Eric Eisinger and Coroner Bill Leach; Benton County Commissioner Will McKay, Treasurer Ken Spencer and Assessor Bill Spencer; Franklin County Commissioner Clint Didier; Kennewick Mayor Jason McShane and other Kennewick, Pasco, West Richland and Benton City council members; Kennewick, Pasco and Prosser school board members; Benton PUD General Manager Rick Dunn; and organizations including Washington Patriot PAC, Latinos United for Conservative Action, Republican Liberty Caucus of Benton and Franklin counties and Stand for Health Freedom [S5].
