## What it does

Proposition No. 2 is a levy lid lift. It would raise Benton City's regular property tax levy to $1.60 per $1,000 of assessed value for collection in 2027 [S1]. The council adopted Resolution 2026-50 to place it on the ballot [S1].

- From 2028 through 2036 the levy could grow each year by the Consumer Price Index (CPI-U, Western Region), never exceeding $1.60 per $1,000 [S1].
- The 2036 levy amount becomes the base for later levy limits under chapter 84.55 RCW [S1]. State law lets a lid lift run up to 10 consecutive years. After it, the higher amount is used to compute future limits only if the ballot measure says so [S7]. This one does, so the higher base continues after 2036 [S1][S7].
- The ballot title says the money is for "law and code enforcement community safety efforts" [S1]. The explanatory statement says it would let the city "continue to maintain and dedicate proceeds to community safety funding for law enforcement and code enforcement" [S1].

## Cost

- **Rate:** $1.60 per $1,000 for 2027 [S1]. On a $300,000 home that is $480 a year in city property tax (computed for this dossier from the ballot rate).
- **Current rate:** for taxes due in 2025, Benton City's regular levy was $0.71435 per $1,000 and raised $281,160 [S2]. At that rate a $300,000 home paid about $214. The increase to $1.60 would be about $266 a year on that home (computed for this dossier; the 2026 rate was not found, so this uses the 2025 rate).
- **Revenue:** the statement for says the city will receive about $301,474 in property tax in 2027 without the measure, and that the lid lift "would add about $400,000" [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

**Why "restore."** The ballot title calls this restoring the levy rate [S1]. Department of Revenue levy detail shows the city's rate falling over two decades as assessed values grew faster than the levy [S2][S3][S4][S5][S6]. The statement for says state law limits the city to a 1% annual increase in property tax revenue [S1].

| Taxes due | Assessed value | Rate per $1,000 | City regular levy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2005 | $69,504,954 | $1.67535 | $116,445 [S6] |
| 2015 | $126,356,803 | $1.37061 | $173,186 [S5] |
| 2020 | $193,495,851 | $1.04234 | $201,689 [S4] |
| 2024 | $328,411,857 | $0.71812 | $235,840 [S3] |
| 2025 | $393,586,322 | $0.71435 | $281,160 [S2] |

The rate was above $1.60 in 2005 [S6]. The table's years are samples; the research did not check every year.

**The cost gap, as the city describes it.** From the statement for [S1]:

- 2027 costs: $456,806 for Sheriff's Office services and $230,554 for code enforcement. The statement gives the total as "$699,280." The two listed items add to $687,360; the statement does not itemize the other $11,920.
- 2027 property tax revenue of about $301,474, leaving "a $397,806 shortfall."
- The sheriff's contract rises 3% a year, while state law limits growth in property tax revenue to 1% [S1].
- Water and sewer funds cannot be used for police or code enforcement [S1].

The research did not find Resolution 2026-50, council minutes, or the city budget. The city's website (ci.benton-city.wa.us) blocked automated access during this research.

## Arguments for

The statement for has no author listed in the voters' guide [S1]. It says:

- Without the measure, "the city will have to make hard decisions that could reduce public safety, slow response times, and weaken code enforcement efforts" [S1].
- The lid lift "does not create new programs or expand services." It keeps deputies on patrol, 911 response, and code enforcement [S1].
- It is "modest, predictable, and accountable to voters" [S1].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed in the voters' guide [S1]. No committee opposing the measure was registered with the PDC as of the query date [S8].

## Endorsements and campaign

- PDC lists a committee in favor, "Committee for a better Benton City," registered September 21, 2026 for the "Levy Cap Lift," with Len Burton as treasurer. It uses mini reporting and had no reports on file [S8]. Burton is Benton City's mayor [S9].
- No news coverage of this measure was found.

- `spending`: the stated purpose is keeping existing sheriff and code enforcement contracts, with no new programs [S1]. No mapping.
