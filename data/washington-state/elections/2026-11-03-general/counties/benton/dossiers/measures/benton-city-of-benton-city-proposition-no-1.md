## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would end Benton City's mayor-council form of government (chapter 35A.12 RCW) and adopt the council-manager form (chapters 35A.06 and 35A.13 RCW), effective March 1, 2027 [S1]. The city stays a non-charter code city under Title 35A RCW [S1]. The council adopted Resolution 2026-49 to place the change on the ballot [S1].

- **Now:** the elected mayor is the city's chief administrative officer [S1].
- **If approved:** the elected council hires a professional manager as chief administrative officer. The council picks a mayor from among its members to chair meetings and serve as ceremonial head of the city [S1].
- Under chapter 35A.13 RCW, councilmembers become the city's only elected officers. The council appoints the city manager, who is the chief executive officer [S3]. The manager is chosen "solely on the basis of his or her executive and administrative qualifications" and need not live in the city unless the council requires it [S3]. One manager may serve two or more cities [S3].

Mayor Len Burton described the current system as one where the mayor is the chief executive and the council "functions only as a legislative body." He said a strong mayor "can undertake items without full council approval, which has occurred in Benton City's past" [S2]. Under the change, he said, the council would have "more control over the city manager" and "the authority to change city managers" [S2]. Apple Valley News Now described the current structure as 80 years old [S2].

## Cost

- The measure sets no tax and states no cost [S1].
- The statement for says the change "is not an increase in taxes." It also says it "will replace the Mayors position with a full-time employee" and "does add additional payroll expenses," which the authors say are outweighed by the benefits [S1]. No salary or budget estimate for a city manager was found.
- Burton said the mayor's job averages over 50 hours a week, done "without compensation" [S2].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

The mayor-council structure is replaced on March 1, 2027 [S1]. The research did not find the text of Resolution 2026-49, council minutes, or a city estimate of the manager position's cost. The city's website (ci.benton-city.wa.us) blocked automated access during this research.

## Arguments for

From the statement for, credited in the voters' guide to the City of Benton City [S1]:

- State regulation has grown, and the strong-mayor form "relies on a local resident to have the knowledge and skill set to manage and implement these rules" [S1].
- "Previous administrations have failed to navigate these regulations, resulting in the mismanagement of funds, lost development opportunities, and chaos among residents and business owners" [S1].
- A professional manager under the council's direction would guide the city through "the era of over-regulation and taxation" [S1].
- The authors hope small cities may one day return to a strong-mayor form [S1].

Mayor Burton added that the council would gain oversight of the executive and that the unpaid workload deters people from serving as mayor [S2].
