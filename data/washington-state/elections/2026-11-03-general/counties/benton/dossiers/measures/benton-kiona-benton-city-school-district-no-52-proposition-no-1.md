## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would let the Kiona-Benton City School District (Ki-Be) collect a two-year educational programs and operations (EP&O) levy in 2027 and 2028, "in place of an expired levy" [S1]. The board adopted Resolution No. 6-2026 [S1].

- The levy pays for costs the state does not fund, "including athletics, the arts, technology support, class sizes, nurses, counselors, safety and security, advanced courses, and extracurricular activities" [S1].
- The levy, plus state levy equalization money that depends on its passage, would provide about 11% of the district's annual General Fund budget [S1][S2].
- Passage would make the district eligible for an estimated $803,841 in state Local Effort Assistance (LEA) over the two years. The district would not receive LEA if the levy fails [S2][S10].

## Cost

Ballot title figures [S1]:

| Collection year | Estimated rate per $1,000 | Levy amount |
|---|---|---|
| 2027 | $1.30 | $2,208,341 |
| 2028 | $1.30 | $2,318,758 |

- On a $300,000 home, $1.30 per $1,000 is about $390 a year (computed for this dossier from the ballot rate).
- The board's statement for uses an average Benton County property value of $443,267, for which the levy would cost $576 a year, "an average of $11.00 per week" [S1].
- Exact rates and amounts depend on actual assessed values and state limits. Some homeowners may qualify for exemptions through the Benton County Assessor [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

**The expired levy.** Voters approved a Ki-Be EP&O replacement levy in February 2023 with 52.5% (904 yes, 818 no) [S5]. Department of Revenue levy detail shows the district's enrichment levy at $1.42025 per $1,000 ($1,747,483) for taxes due in 2024 [S7] and $1.28699 per $1,000 ($1,876,467) for 2025 [S6]. The district calls this measure a replacement of "our past two-year levy" [S2].

**Two failed attempts in 2025.** Both earlier replacement attempts were proposed at $1.50 per $1,000 [S8]:

| Election | Yes | No | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| February 11, 2025 special | 790 (48.47%) | 840 (51.53%) | Failed [S3] |
| November 4, 2025 general | 1,030 (49.19%) | 1,064 (50.81%) | Failed [S4] |

This measure asks for $1.30, $0.20 less than the two failed requests. Superintendent Pete Peterson said the lower rate is "trying to really show our community that we're listening to voters" [S8].

**Cuts and state oversight.**

- The district cut about $1.5 million in the 2025-26 school year, including eight staff positions, transportation for athletics and field trips [S9]. In May 2026 it was preparing for about $2 million more in cuts. Peterson said it was expected to enter "binding conditions," a state financial oversight process [S9]. Enrollment fell by about 150 students over four years [S9].
- The 2026-27 budget, adopted in late July 2026, cut about $1.6 million, for about $3 million over two years. The district did not replace eight certificated teachers and three district administrators who left [S8]. Athletics busing is still cut [S8].
- NewsTalk 870 reported that Ki-Be is under state-monitored binding conditions "due to negative financial standing, largely due to the levy failures in 2025." It also reported a freeze on administrative salaries and cuts to some nursing and counseling positions [S10].
- The board's statement says more than $3.00 million has been cut from the operating budget in the past two years [S1].

## Arguments for

From the statement for, written by the Board of Directors of Kiona-Benton City School District No. 52 [S1]:

- "The Ki-Be School District Levy failed twice, and no one is better for it." The failures affected the district, parents, staff and students [S1].
- Passage would "return school counselors, support positions, athletic event transportation and a memorable school experience to our students" [S1].
- The 2026-27 cuts cost the district "outstanding teachers," and students have left because of them [S1].
- "Many make this about topics other than the students." The board says it is "working towards transparency, consistency and open communication" [S1].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed in the voters' guide [S1]. No committee opposing the measure was registered with the PDC as of the query date [S11]. The two failed 2025 votes, by 50 and 34 votes, are the record of opposition [S3][S4].

## Endorsements and campaign

- PDC lists a committee in favor, "KiBe Kids Yes!," registered October 6, 2026, with Michael Peterson as treasurer. It uses mini reporting and had no reports on file [S11].
- No other endorsements were found.

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote reinstates the district's EP&O levy at $1.30 per $1,000. That is close to the $1.29 collected in 2025 and below the $1.50 rejected twice in 2025 [S1][S6][S8]. It is a replacement, not a new kind of tax. The expired levy is not being collected now, so a yes vote raises Ki-Be property owners' bills compared with 2026 [S1].
- `spending`: the stated aim is restoring cut staff and services (counselors, support positions, athletics transportation), not new programs [S1][S8]. No mapping.
- `parental-rights` / `social`: not addressed. The board's remark that "many make this about topics other than the students" [S1] does not identify those topics, so it is not evidence on either axis.
