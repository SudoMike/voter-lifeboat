## What the office does
The 16th District elects two State Representatives, each to a two-year term; this is Position 2 [S1]. Representatives sponsor and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including education, health care and economic development [S2]. The district covers Walla Walla County and parts of Benton, Franklin and Grant counties [S2].

## Race dynamics
Republican Skyler Rude, in the seat since 2019 and Assistant Minority Floor Leader, faces Democrat Derek Sarley, a Walla Walla school-board member since 2015 and past WSSDA president [S1][S2]. Rude led the primary 57.97% to 41.95% (skyler-rude.md S18).

On September 29 Rude announced he will resign from the House effective October 31 to become legislative director for the Secretary of State's office. He remains on the ballot and asked voters who want a Republican representative to still vote for him; if he wins, the county legislative authorities appoint a successor from three nominees chosen by the Republican county central committee [S3]. If Rude wins, the new term would be served by that appointee rather than by Rude [S3].

On the merits the two differ on taxes and budget framing. Rude voted against the 2026 income tax and the 2025 B&O and services tax bills, calls himself a "strong opponent" of the income tax, and backs zero-based budgeting and expenditure limits (skyler-rude.md S3, S5, S8, S9, S10). Sarley calls the tax code regressive and the recent progressive revenue steps "important," while calling for prioritizing within available revenue and rebuilding reserves (derek-sarley.md S2, S6) [S2]. Both opposed the 2026 K-12 cuts [S2]. Both favor faster housing construction and fewer building barriers (skyler-rude.md S4; derek-sarley.md S3). On data centers, Rude stresses local control and more energy and transmission; Sarley opposes special tax breaks and wants ratepayer protections [S2]. Rude voted against ESHB 1296 and the clean fuels bill (skyler-rude.md S11, S12); Sarley's endorsements include the WEA, the state labor council and Planned Parenthood Advocates (derek-sarley.md S5). Neither has a stated position on the three statewide initiatives.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Rude's recorded no votes and stated opposition to the income tax vs. Sarley's description of recent progressive revenue as important.
2. **Spending:** both preach budget discipline; Rude favors structural limits and rolling back new programs, Sarley favors reserves and prioritization while opposing education and childcare cuts.
3. **Record vs. renewal:** a long-serving incumbent who is leaving the seat vs. a school-board veteran new to the Legislature.
4. **Technology:** Rude emphasizes local control of data-center siting; Sarley opposes data-center tax breaks.
5. **Parents and schools:** Rude voted against ESHB 1296; Sarley has no stated position on notification or opt-outs.
