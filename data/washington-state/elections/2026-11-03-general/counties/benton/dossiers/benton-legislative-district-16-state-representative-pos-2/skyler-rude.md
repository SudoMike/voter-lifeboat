## Background
Skyler Rude, a Republican born and raised in Walla Walla, has represented the 16th Legislative District, Position 2, since January 2019, succeeding Terry Nealey [S1][S17]. He is Assistant Minority Floor Leader and, per his campaign site, ranking member of the House Education Committee [S1][S2][S7]. He was a legislative assistant in the State Senate (2017-18) and House (2015-16), an insurance agent (2009-14), and is a real-estate broker and small business owner; he attended Walla Walla Community College and Western Governors University [S1]. He is chairman, state committeeman and precinct committee officer for the Walla Walla County Republican Party [S1].

**Resignation.** On September 29, 2026, Rude announced he will resign from the House effective October 31 to become legislative director for the Washington Office of the Secretary of State, starting November 1 [S17]. His name remains on the November 3 ballot, and he encouraged voters who want a Republican representative to still vote for him. If he wins, the seat becomes vacant and is filled by appointment by the county legislative authorities from three nominees submitted by the Republican county central committee (state Constitution Article II, Section 15) [S17]. He said announcing after the election would have been "politically safer" but would "compromise my values and erode trust" [S17].

In the August 4 primary he placed first with 20,493 votes (57.97%) to Democrat Derek Sarley's 14,831 (41.95%) [S18]. He did not attend the October 8 AAUW forum, saying he would be out of town [S20]. The PDC reported $71,165 raised and $17,034 spent as of 2026-10-07 [S19].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** "I was a strong opponent of the income tax passed by the Legislature this year," arguing that case law treats income as property subject to constitutional limits and that the tax is already driving "capital flight"; opposes the 2025 B&O and sales-and-use tax increases [S3]. His site calls for stopping tax increases that raise the cost of living [S4].
- **Spending:** says the state budget "has doubled in just the last 10 years," supports zero-based budgeting, performance-based budgeting, expenditure limits, a fully funded rainy-day fund and revenue assumptions matched to economist forecasts, and calls the $600 million in new programs adopted during a deficit "inexcusable" [S5][S15]. Would restore 2026 K-12 cuts by using the ending fund balance, rolling back new programs, eliminating the state's reparations program and adding work requirements to the Working Families Tax Credit [S15]. Says social program resources should be directed to vulnerable populations and that policy should reduce the need for social welfare programs [S5].
- **Housing:** supports faster permitting, streamlined environmental review and cost-based limits on building-code mandates; says recently enacted statewide rent control and landlord-tenant changes "discourage investment in rental housing and reduce the supply" [S4]. Voted against the 2025 tenant housing-stability bill (see Record) [S13].
- **Climate and energy:** says recent fuel-related taxes raise prices and supports "affordable energy and transportation while maintaining environmental stewardship" [S4]. Voted against the 2025 clean fuels program bill [S12].
- **Technology and data centers:** says the question is the quality of regulation rather than its amount, that "local control is paramount," and that the Legislature should increase clean, reliable energy and transmission to accommodate data-center investment while safeguarding local decisions [S15].
- **Regulation:** wants the Legislature to have the final say over agency rulemaking and regular reviews to eliminate low-benefit rules; wants fewer regulations limiting childcare capacity [S3][S4].
- **Education:** priorities include literacy (a bipartisan literacy bill signed into law), classroom cell-phone limits, mandatory financial education for graduation, state funding of materials, supplies and operating costs, and reducing unfunded mandates on school districts [S6][S16].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no public position found in the sources reviewed.
- **Parents and schools:** voted against ESHB 1296 (2025), the bill amending the parents' rights law enacted by initiative I-2081, on House passage and final passage [S11]. His site lists "inclusion of parents in their student's success" among his Education Committee goals [S7]. No statement on parental notification or opt-outs found.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found in the sources reviewed. Record: voted against ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal, and calls himself a "strong opponent" of it [S3][S8].
- **I-1 (parental rights):** no public position found in the sources reviewed. Record: voted against ESHB 1296, the 2025 amendments to the parental-rights law that I-1 would undo [S11].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found in the sources reviewed.

## Record
- Voted nay on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the 9.9% tax on household income over $1 million (2026-03-09, passed 51-46); voted yea on the motion to postpone it indefinitely (failed 38-58) and offered floor amendment 2562 (failed 45-51) [S8].
- Voted nay on House final passage of ESHB 2081, business and occupation tax increases (2025-04-22, passed 50-48) [S9], and of ESSB 5814, excise taxes on select services (2025-04-23, passed 50-47) [S10].
- Voted nay on ESHB 1296 on House passage (2025-03-12, 56-37) and final passage as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24, 59-39) [S11].
- Voted nay on the clean fuels program bill, HB 1409 (2025-03-10, 54-41; 2025-04-21, 54-43) [S12], and on EHB 1217, "improving housing stability for tenants" (2025-03-10, 53-42; conference report 2025-04-27, 54-44) [S13].
- Prime sponsor of E2SHB 2636, requiring reviews of the performance, operations and funding of the public education system, which he says passed the House 97-0 and narrowly failed in the Senate; it was returned to House Rules in March 2026 [S14][S15]. Prime sponsor of 2SHB 1285 (financial education as a graduation requirement), HB 2661 (public records task force), HB 2460 and HJR 4212 (vacancies in partisan elective offices), HB 1676 (charter school contracts) and HB 2593 (school district accounting) [S6][S14].
- Early in his tenure co-introduced, with Democratic Rep. Jared Mead, the resolution that began remote testimony in House committees [S7].
