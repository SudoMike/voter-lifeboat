## Background
Derek Sarley, of Walla Walla, is a Democrat who has served on the Walla Walla Public Schools board since 2015, including four terms as board president and service as the board's legislative representative; he is a past president of the Washington State School Directors' Association (WSSDA) [S1][S4][S8]. He has 25 years in small business leadership, helping grow a PR agency into a 100-person firm, and the Union-Bulletin lists his occupation as communications consultant [S1][S8]. He holds a B.A. in economics and political science from Rice University, coached for a decade with the Blue Mountain Girls Softball Association and is a Blue Mountain Evening Rotary member [S1][S8]. He says he entered the race after the 2026 cuts to K-12 and early education [S8].

In the August 4 primary he placed second with 14,831 votes (41.95%) to Skyler Rude's 20,493 (57.97%) [S10]. Rude has since announced his resignation from the House effective October 31 while remaining on the ballot [S11]. Sarley's site says "a lot has changed in this race" and that he is still campaigning through Election Day because voters "deserve the opportunity to choose who represents them" [S2]. He was scheduled to appear at the October 8 AAUW forum, which Rude did not attend [S13]. The PDC reported $88,751 raised and $29,181 spent as of 2026-10-05 [S12].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** says Washington has "the second-most regressive tax code in the nation" and a "structurally broken state budget" [S2]. In a June post he wrote that the Legislature "has taken important steps" in recent years to fix the "upside-down tax code," and that there is "good reason to be skeptical" that new taxes on high earners will drive many wealthy residents away, while warning that capital-gains and high-income taxes are volatile revenue [S6]. His statement says "each year brings new taxes combined with cuts to priority areas like childcare and education" [S1]; his site says new taxes and regulations "make life hard for small business owners" [S3].
- **Spending:** says Democratic majorities "have expanded services beyond the capacity of existing revenue" and Republicans have claimed the budget could be balanced without real cuts; favors prioritizing "to do many things well" and starting from available revenue [S8]. Wants expenditures held enough below revenues to rebuild the Budget Stabilization Account, warning against both new spending and tax breaks in flush years [S6]. Opposes balancing the budget "on the backs of our region's children and families" and the 2026 K-12 and childcare cuts [S8].
- **Education:** priorities include early-grade literacy, mental-health supports, safe classrooms, educator tools and accountability for results [S3]; says the state "is going to have to pony up" for literacy instruction and should invest in principal training [S9]. Walla Walla schools already restrict phones in class [S9].
- **Housing:** "Washington needs more homes, more quickly, and fewer outdated barriers that make it harder to build" [S3]; says the state cannot "eternally subsidize our way out" of housing and energy scarcity and must "build our way to a better Washington" [S8].
- **Healthcare:** wants to move "beyond the issue of coverage" to "go after the middlemen" that inflate costs [S3]; argues Washington should take "a very hard look" at certificate-of-need laws that block new providers, especially in rural areas [S7].
- **Climate and energy:** calls for "a statewide commitment to reliable, affordable, and abundant energy" [S3]. No position on the Climate Commitment Act found.
- **Technology and data centers:** data centers should not get special tax breaks, must follow the same environmental and water rules, and residential ratepayers need protection from rising bills; but cautions against new rules that make building harder [S8].
- **Economy:** criticizes an economy "organized around giant corporations" with "monopolistic practices" and pledges support for family farms and small businesses [S3].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** endorsed by Planned Parenthood Advocates of Greater Washington and North Idaho [S5]. No statement of his own found in the sources reviewed.
- **Parents and schools:** his site cites increased family engagement on the Walla Walla board [S4]. No position on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs found in the sources reviewed.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no public position on the initiative found in the sources reviewed. He has written that recent legislative steps toward a less regressive tax code were "important" [S6].
- **I-1 (parental rights):** no public position found in the sources reviewed.
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found in the sources reviewed.

## Record
No state legislative record. Walla Walla Public Schools board member since 2015 (four terms as president), during which he says the district raised graduation rates, kept budgets balanced, built reserves and was named School Board of the Year [S1][S4][S6].

## Endorsements
Washington State Labor Council, Washington State Nurses Association, Association of Western Pulp and Paper Workers (Washington Area Council), IAM District 751, Washington Education Association, AFT Washington, AFSCME Council 28 / Washington Federation of State Employees, Planned Parenthood Advocates of Greater Washington and North Idaho, and the Benton, Franklin and Walla Walla County Democratic parties [S5].
