## Background

Amanda McKinney is a Yakima County commissioner, first elected in 2020 and now in her second term [S14][S9]. She lists more than 20 years as a small-business owner and mortgage banker in Central Washington and degrees from Central Washington University [S1]. In county roles she has served on the Washington State Association of Counties board, as vice chair of the National Association of Counties' rural and agriculture committee, on NACo's Western Interstate Region board, and on the federal Wildland Fire Leadership Council [S1][S3]. She led the 11-candidate August primary with 34.9% [S6][S7]. She describes herself as a "wife, mother, Christian" and "the only Trump-Endorsed Candidate" [S1]; the White House confirmed to the Tri-City Herald that Trump endorsed only McKinney in the race [S10].

## Positions

- **Taxes:** says Washington's high fuel prices come from state gas taxes and the Climate Commitment Act, and hopes federal "energy dominance" will push the Legislature "to rethink how they're approaching these fuel taxes" [S11]. She has called gun-purchase fees and training mandates "taxes on constitutional rights" [S3]. No federal tax plan found in the sources reviewed.
- **Spending:** "Washington spends too much and delivers too little"; pledges "fiscal discipline, transparency" and to "rein in spending" [S2]. Said she wants a seat on the Appropriations Committee [S5].
- **Public safety:** pledges "law and order" and support for first responders [S1]; would "dismantle fentanyl trafficking networks" and rejects "anti-police rhetoric" [S2]. Opposes "anything that requires people to do anything other than walk up to a licensed dealer and purchase a gun" [S13]; defends Second Amendment rights [S1][S2].
- **Energy and climate:** backs the Columbia and Snake River dams, advanced nuclear, fusion, an expanded Hanford mission and LNG [S1]. Opposes "radical energy mandates" [S2] and opposed the Horse Heaven Hills wind project [S3]. Worked with Let's Go Washington on Initiative 2066 to repeal natural gas restrictions [S3]. At the Sept. 30 debate she blamed high prices on the state's natural gas restrictions and clean-energy mandates and said "it's false to say the tariffs and the war in Iran are responsible for high prices" [S5]. Says the Endangered Species Act has produced "diminishing returns" and wants less environmental regulation of farms [S11].
- **Immigration:** supports the administration's deportation push and criticizes Washington's sanctuary laws [S5]. "I do not support amnesty or creating a special pathway to legal status" for people in the country unlawfully [S12]. Would streamline the H-2A guest-worker program; of guest workers she said "they must go home, because we want people in our country legally" [S5][S12].
- **Healthcare:** no federal health-care position found in the sources reviewed. Opposed COVID vaccine mandates for children [S3].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** supports keeping "women's sports ... for biological females" under Title IX [S2][S3]. "I believe that all life has meaning and value and must be protected from conception" [S3]. Has aligned with Trump on eliminating DEI programs [S14].
- **Parents and schools (recorded, not scored for a federal race):** says her opposition to masking and COVID vaccine mandates for children defended "parental rights and personal medical choices" [S3]. Lists service as board president of CTCS/St. Paul Cathedral School [S1].
- **Technology:** "To secure energy dominance and win the AI race" she would prioritize nuclear power [S1]. Opposes "heavy-handed regulation that stifles innovation" in AI, supports cryptocurrency and opposes "using regulation to monitor or control lawful financial activity" [S2]; at a July forum she said she supports some crypto regulation [S13].
- **Defense and foreign policy:** pledges to "rebuild and modernize our military" and supports "strong national defense" [S2][S3]. Asked at the debate about checking the executive, she said congressional inaction on issues like the Iran war reflects information that "continues to convince them that they should continue to support the president's actions" [S5].
- **Government and reform:** pledges to "rein in unelected bureaucrats" and "end the weaponization of government" [S2]. Supports the SAVE America Act and says she would build Senate support by addressing senators' concerns [S13].

- **I-1 (parental rights in public schools):** No public position found in the sources reviewed.

## Record

- As commissioner: implemented fraud screening of COVID aid that flagged more than $6 million in claims [S3]; used ARPA funds to buy senior water rights for a county water bank; supported the Yakima Basin Integrated Plan and floodplain restoration [S3][S14]; funded sheriff resources and a new emergency communications system [S2][S3].
- Won a June 2026 Thurston County Superior Court order requiring primary rival Jerrod Sessler to remove a claimed Trump endorsement from his voters' pamphlet statement [S10].

## Endorsements

President Trump, House Speaker Mike Johnson, Majority Leader Steve Scalise, Whip Tom Emmer, Judiciary Chair Jim Jordan, Agriculture Chair Glenn Thompson, Rep. Michael Baumgartner, state Sen. Curtis King, Benton County commissioners Jerome Delvin and Will McKay, Kennewick Mayor Jason McShane and many county officials [S4]; Turning Point Action [S1]. Primary rival Elpidia Saavedra endorsed her; Jerrod Sessler said he will vote for her; Rep. Dan Newhouse will not endorse [S6].

## Campaign finance

Nearly $982,000 in receipts through the pre-primary FEC report, including a $50,000 candidate loan; top contributors include GOP Winning Women 2026 and several PACs [S8]. Outside groups spent about $2.1 million to $2.2 million supporting her in the primary, mostly super PACs backed by the cryptocurrency and AI industries [S6][S9].
