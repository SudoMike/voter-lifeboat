## Background

Mike Clark joined the Benton County Sheriff's Office in 2003 and worked as a corrections officer, patrol deputy, corporal, sergeant and lieutenant (S1, S2). As a lieutenant he led the Community Services Division, overseeing outreach, marine patrol, school resource officers, training and SWAT operations. He was also the office's spokesperson and helped launch a deputy wellness program (S7, S8). He spent more than a decade on the Tri-City Regional SWAT Team, rising to team leader and incident commander (S2, S8). His site lists acceptance to the FBI National Academy and more than 3,500 hours of advanced training (S2). He is a lifelong Tri-Cities resident (S2). He prefers the Republican Party (S1).

Commissioners named him interim sheriff after Sheriff Tom Croskrey resigned in September 2025 (S7). On November 20, 2025, they appointed him sheriff from three Republican Party nominees, with the appointment running until the November 2026 general election (S8, S13). His site says the commissioners' vote was unanimous (S2). In the August 4 primary he received 28,696 votes (69.5%) to Brad Klippert's 12,051 (29.2%) (S12).

## Positions

### Public safety and policing
- "Since taking office, local crime has continued to decline," and he says he will build on that "through strong partnerships with local, state, and federal law enforcement agencies" (S1). The crime-decline figure is the candidate's own claim.
- Campaign issues include "holding offenders accountable" through "thorough investigations, strong case development, and close collaboration with prosecutors." They also include "modern, proactive policing strategies" using "data-driven methods, updated technology, and community partnerships to prevent crime before it occurs" (S3).
- Backs the Internet Crimes Against Children Task Force's work to find hidden and deleted images on devices "so that we can hold people accountable" (S5).
- After his appointment he said he wants to use drones and other technology "to really work towards apprehending criminals, closing out cases, bring justice to victims," and to adapt the budget to pay for it (S9).
- Lists membership on the Benton Franklin Behavioral Health Advisory Committee and the Benton County Law & Justice Committee (S1).

### Budget and operations
- "It's really important to go back and look at our budget and be able to figure out how it is that we are going to provide better service to everybody with the resources that we have, or really combing through that budget" (S5).
- Promises to streamline workflows and modernize systems so that "taxpayer resources are used responsibly" (S3).
- After the Hatcher-era "mass exodus" of staff, he says the office rebuilt and "filled a ton of our vacancies," and he wants to give the department "some stability" (S9).

### Transparency and community
- Promises "clear communication, ethical decision-making, and high professional standards," plus community feedback and public involvement in decisions (S3).
- Says he is committed to "honoring the traditional, family oriented values that define our community" (S1).

## Record

- Sheriff since November 2025, after serving as interim sheriff from October 2025 (S7, S8).
- In January 2026 he disputed a provider's claim that the Sheriff's Office had blocked a Level 3 sex offender transition home in Finley, saying the meeting predated him and that the office has no legal authority over where such homes go. He said he would oppose such a home in Benton County if the law allowed it (S10).
- In July 2026 commissioners approved buying 19 Axon automatic license plate reader cameras for the Sheriff's Office for up to $227,107 from patrol funds, replacing its Flock cameras after the county sent Flock a termination letter in March 2026 (S11).
