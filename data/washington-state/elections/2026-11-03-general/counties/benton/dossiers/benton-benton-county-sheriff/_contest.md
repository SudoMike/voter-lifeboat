## What the office does
The sheriff is the county's chief executive law-enforcement officer and conservator of the peace. The sheriff and deputies enforce state law, make arrests, serve court process and warrants, and keep the peace in cooperation with other agencies (S2). Clark's statement describes the Benton County Sheriff's Office as 88 deputies and support staff with a multi-million-dollar budget (S1).

## Why the ballot says "short and full term"
Sheriff Tom Croskrey resigned at the end of September 2025 with more than a year left in his term. Croskrey had come out of retirement in 2021 to replace a recalled sheriff (S5). Commissioners named Lt. Mike Clark interim sheriff (S10). On November 20, 2025, they appointed Clark sheriff from three nominees put forward by the Benton County Republican Party: Clark, Brad Klippert and former deputy Jeremy Carrigan (S6, S7). NonStop Local called it the third mid-term sheriff appointment by the commission in a decade (S8). State law says a county appointee holds office "until the next general election, and until their successors are elected and qualified" (S3). When a vacancy occurs after the last election at which the office could have been voted on, the ballot carries a "short term" alongside the full term. The short term runs from certification of the general election until the next full term starts (S4). The November winner therefore takes office when the election is certified, serves out the rest of Croskrey's term, and then begins the four-year term (S1, S4).

## Race dynamics
Both candidates are Republicans with long careers in the Sheriff's Office. Appointed Sheriff Mike Clark is a 23-year veteran who rose through the ranks. Brad Klippert has been a sheriff's deputy since 1999, served 14 years in the state House, and joined the Kennewick City Council in 2025 (S1; brad-klippert.md S2, S4). Klippert was one of the three nominees Clark beat for the 2025 appointment (S6). In the certified August 4 primary Clark received 28,696 votes (69.5%) and Klippert 12,051 (29.2%) (S9).

Clark has the Benton County Republican Party, all three county commissioners, the county's other elected officials, the Tri-Cities mayors, the Benton County Sheriff's Command Guild and 31 sitting Washington county sheriffs (michael-mike-clark.md S4). Klippert's site lists no endorsements (brad-klippert.md S2). Clark reports $57.4k raised to Klippert's $9.6k (michael-mike-clark.md S6; brad-klippert.md S5). Neither candidate has said much about policy differences. Clark runs on continuity, budget management and modern policing. Klippert runs on public safety, support for deputies, constitutional rights and transparency (S1).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience/record:** the sitting appointee and career sheriff's office manager against a long-serving deputy who brings legislative and military command experience but has not led the agency.
2. **Spending:** Clark emphasizes combing through the budget to maximize existing resources. Klippert says he will "invest in" and equip deputies but gives no budget detail.
3. **Safety:** both are enforcement-oriented, so this axis does not separate them much. Clark stresses accountability, proactive and data-driven policing and regional partnerships. Klippert stresses public safety, fighting drug addiction and, as a legislator, "swift and sure" capital punishment.
4. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives: none found for either candidate.
