## What the office does

Position 4 is one seat on the Richland City Council, the city's elected, nonpartisan governing body. The council chose an interim member to fill this seat when it became vacant [S2]. In their statements, both candidates describe the job in terms of the city budget, taxes, municipal codes, growth and infrastructure, public safety and parks [S1]. This is a three-year unexpired term [S5][S1].

## Why the term is unexpired, and who holds the seat now

- In November 2025, voters elected Donald Landsman to Position 4 with 56.23% of the vote over John Maier [S4]. The Tri-City Herald reported that the seat is on the 2026 ballot "after voters elected a candidate who died during the 2025 election cycle," and that the winner "will complete the term voters gave to the late Donald Landsman" [S2].
- The council selected Todd Samuel, a group manager at Pacific Northwest National Laboratory, "to serve as an interim member until the election can take place" [S2]. Samuel ran in the August primary and finished third, so he is not on the November ballot [S3].
- Not found: the date Landsman died, the date of Samuel's appointment, and the council's appointment vote. The City of Richland website (richlandwa.gov) returned HTTP 403 to automated requests and to WebFetch, so council minutes were not retrieved.

## Primary result (Aug. 4, 2026, certified)

Kyle Saltz 5,958 (40.4%), Mary S. Lipton 4,107 (27.9%), Todd Samuel 2,322 (15.7%), David Tveraas 1,643 (11.1%), Ragan Faylor 676 (4.6%), write-in 39, of 14,745 votes [S3].

## Race dynamics

- **Saltz** led the primary. He is an Army counterintelligence veteran and Army reservist on the Hanford Patrol Tactical Response Team [S1][S3]. He ran for council in 2025 and lost Position 6 to incumbent Kurt H. Maier, 52.18% to 47.67% [S4]. He told the Herald he re-entered "out of respect for his friend" Landsman: "This is for Donald's legacy" [S2]. His statement focuses on responsible spending, safe neighborhoods, cutting outdated codes and bureaucracy, and "reducing taxes on an overburdened population." He pledges to donate his council pay to local charities [S1].
- **Lipton** is a Pacific Northwest National Laboratory staff scientist, a WSU adjunct professor, and a Richland resident of more than 30 years [S1][S2]. She told the Herald that "Richland is walking a fine line between growth and maintaining its identity" and that she favors "a balanced approach" [S2]. Her statement lists managing growth and infrastructure, economic stability, workforce development, public safety, parks, riverfront access and environmental resources [S1].
- **Money.** As of the Sept. 17, 2026 PDC update, Saltz had raised $8,561.94 and spent $6,101.94. Lipton had raised $2,300 and spent $1,566.54 [S6][S7].

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

- **Taxes and spending:** Saltz explicitly calls for a budget surplus and lower taxes. Lipton speaks of "fiscal stewardship" without a stated direction on tax levels [S1].
- **Growth:** Lipton frames growth as something to balance against Richland's character and environmental resources. Saltz's platform stresses a business-friendly code review and supporting economic development (see dossiers) [S1][S2].
- **Public safety:** Saltz centers his campaign on law-enforcement expertise, crime and homelessness. Lipton lists public safety among several priorities (see dossiers) [S1].
- **Experience:** Neither has held city office. Saltz has run before and led the local VFW; Lipton's experience is in science, teaching and volunteer service [S1][S4].
