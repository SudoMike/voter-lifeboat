## Background

Kyle Saltz served 11 years as an active-duty Army counterintelligence agent, including multiple deployments to Afghanistan [S1]. He has been in the Army Reserve since 2023 [S1]. He has worked for the Hanford Patrol since 2017 on its Tactical Response Team [S1]. He has held elected leadership roles in the Richland Veterans of Foreign Wars for 12 years, five of them as commander [S1]. He holds master's degrees in criminal justice and in liberal arts (politics and history) and is a Defense Language Institute graduate [S1][S5]. He volunteers with the VFW honor guard, as a Benton County Veterans Therapeutic Court mentor, and with his therapy dog at Kadlec Hospital [S1]. In 2025 he ran for council Position 6 and lost to incumbent Kurt H. Maier, 47.67% to 52.18% [S6]. He led the August 2026 primary for Position 4 with 40.4% [S7].

## Positions

**Taxes and spending**
- Says "We need to not only balance our budget but create a surplus while reducing taxes on an overburdened population" [S1].
- Promises "responsible spending" and "removing unnecessary bureaucracy." Says he has "already identified outdated municipal codes and inefficiencies" [S1].
- Pledges to donate his council pay to local charities [S1].

**Public safety and homelessness**
- Lists "safe neighborhoods" as a priority [S1]. His 2025 campaign site says population growth "has brought more crime and gang activity than Richland is used to." It says the council needs members with "a professional level understanding of law enforcement" to support Richland police [S3].
- Calls homelessness "a growing problem in Richland" and promises "a commonsense approach" that prioritizes community safety [S3].

**Business and codes**
- Wants to review municipal codes affecting business so that it is "reasonable and affordable to open and maintain a business." Supports the city's economic-development approach [S3].

**Veterans**
- Supports the proposed veterans cemetery in Richland and more veterans' services [S3].

**Governance style**
- Says leadership "should focus less on politics and more on people" [S1].
- Told the Herald he re-entered the race for his friend Landsman: "He stood for integrity, accountability, leadership" [S2].
