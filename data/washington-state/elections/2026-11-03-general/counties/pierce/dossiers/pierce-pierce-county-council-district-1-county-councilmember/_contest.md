## What the office does
The seven-member Pierce County Council is the county's legislative branch: it adopts the biennial budget, ordinances and land-use and zoning policy, sets county taxes within state limits, and oversees county services; each member represents one district for a four-year term. Per the primary overview, District 1 covers Bonney Lake, Buckley, Carbonado, Orting, Wilkeson, South Prairie, Graham, South Hill, Lake Tapps, southeast Auburn and nearby unincorporated areas. The seat is open: District 1 Councilmember Dave Morell endorses O'Leary (jerome-o-leary.md S5). The printed Pierce County local voters' pamphlet for the general could not be read (Cloudflare block); both candidates' general-election statements were read from their VoteWA candidate records (jerome-o-leary.md S8; kelsey-barrans.md S10).

## District and race dynamics
November 3, 2026 general election, two candidates. Republican Jerome O'Leary, a commercial real-estate broker, Planning Commission chair and Charter Review commissioner, led the four-way August primary with 53.5%; Democrat Kelsey Barrans, a Green River College student-services director who chaired the Charter Review Commission, was second with 21.0%, and two eliminated Democrats took a combined 25.4% (jerome-o-leary.md S7; kelsey-barrans.md S9). O'Leary has raised about $62k against Barrans's about $13k (PDC; jerome-o-leary.md S6, kelsey-barrans.md S8).

In their statements, O'Leary names rising housing costs, infrastructure, public safety, homelessness and stewardship of taxpayer dollars as challenges without specific proposals, while Barrans prioritizes affordable housing, access to care and rural infrastructure (jerome-o-leary.md S8; kelsey-barrans.md S10). O'Leary runs on business-minded, nonpartisan stewardship of taxpayer money and is endorsed by the county Republican Party, the deputy sheriffs' guild, the Master Builders, realtors and the Farm Bureau (jerome-o-leary.md S4, S5). Barrans runs on infrastructure money for unincorporated areas, transit for rural towns, and housing strategies including subsidies, and is endorsed by Democratic organizations, the Central Labor Council, WEA and environmental groups (kelsey-barrans.md S4, S6, S7).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Barrans supports progressive taxes and higher developer impact fees; O'Leary states no tax position beyond stewardship of taxpayer dollars.
2. **Housing:** O'Leary names rising housing costs as a challenge but states no mechanism, and his coalition is the building and real-estate industry; Barrans backs public or subsidized housing investment alongside density and accessory-unit incentives.
3. **Transit and infrastructure spending:** Barrans would extend bus service to Orting, Buckley and Graham and push more road and bridge money to unincorporated areas.
4. **Public safety:** O'Leary is endorsed by the deputy sheriffs' guild; Barrans frames safety around structural oversight of the sheriff and rural outreach.
5. **Parents and schools (recorded, not scored for County):** Barrans supports LGBTQIA+ perspectives and books in education at all levels; O'Leary states no position.
