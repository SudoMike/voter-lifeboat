## Background
His general-election statement comes from his VoteWA candidate record, where the "Statement" field holds the text he submitted (not yet marked approved for display on 2026-10-08) [S8]; the Pierce County printed local voters' pamphlet for the general could not be read (Cloudflare block).

Jerome O'Leary (prefers Republican Party) says he has worked in commercial real estate for more than 30 years and is a Senior Vice President at Kidder Mathews, handling acquisition, leasing and sale of office, retail and multifamily properties [S4]. He says he began as an estimator and project manager in commercial and industrial construction, and at 19 founded a Pierce County camper and canopy retailer, Canopy Land, which he later sold [S4]. His primary statement also describes him as a partner in a family farm and a lifelong Pierce County resident [S1]. His general statement lists his occupation as "Senior Vice President of commercial real estate firm, broker serving diverse clientele, and partner in family farm," says he graduated from Washington High School and attended TCC and WSU, and says he and his wife raise their daughter on a multi-generational family Christmas tree farm [S8]. It cites more than 30 years in real estate, construction and business [S8].

He lists service on the Pierce County Agricultural Advisory Commission, Charter Review Commission, Conservation Futures Board and Transportation Commission, as Pierce County Planning Commission chair, on the Master Builders Association of Pierce County board, and on the boards of the Puyallup Food Bank, the Puyallup/Sumner Chamber of Commerce and St. Francis House, plus more than 35 years in South Hill Puyallup Rotary [S4]. The County Council confirmed his appointment as a District 1 member of the Planning Commission on consent in 2025 [S2], and the county listed him as an elected District 1 Charter Review commissioner [S3].

In the August 4 primary he finished first of four with 15,295 votes (53.5%), ahead of Democrat Kelsey Barrans with 6,012 (21.0%); Terrance Mayers (15.9%) and Kenneth King (9.5%) were eliminated [S7].

## Positions
- **Government and spending:** says local government "should be built to serve the people, not the bureaucracy," and promises "transparent decision-making, responsible stewardship of taxpayer dollars, and policies that strengthen families, support local businesses" [S4]. His primary statement called for a business-minded approach to housing costs, infrastructure, public safety and homelessness, and long-term stewardship of taxpayer funds [S1].
- **Statement (says he will):** names "rising housing costs, strained infrastructure, public safety concerns, homelessness, and the need for responsible, long-term stewardship of taxpayer dollars" as the county's challenges and promises "a practical, business-minded, community-first approach"; says "Government should be efficient, accountable, and grounded in real-world perspective" and that he "will always put people ahead of partisan politics" [S8]. The statement gives no specific proposal on any of those issues [S8].
- **Approach:** describes a "practical, business-minded, community-first approach" that puts the community "ahead of partisan agendas," and says he is "not running to be a career politician" [S4].
- No specific tax, housing, public safety or climate proposals were found on his campaign site or in his statement as of 2026-10-08 [S4][S8].
