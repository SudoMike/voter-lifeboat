## Background
David Olson (prefers Republican Party) has served on the Peninsula School Board, District 5, since 2013, ran for Superintendent of Public Instruction in 2024, and served on the Pierce County Charter Review Commission [S1][S2]. He is a retired U.S. Navy officer (Chief Warrant Officer, 28 years, including service as a Navy diver), a former public finance banker and a former defense contractor [S1][S2]. He has a B.S. in Organizational Leadership from SNHU [S1]. He is board president of the Greater Gig Harbor Foundation and a past president of the City Club of Tacoma [S1]. He has never served in the Legislature [S1]. In the August 4, 2026 primary he finished first with 22,256 votes (about 43.8%) to Representative Adison Richards' 17,012 (about 33.5%) and Natalie Bornfleth's 11,467 (about 22.6%) in official results [S4].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** criticizes Olympia for "passing an income tax" [S1]; his site calls to "Vote against future tax increases" and for "no more broad-based taxes," and says he will oppose business tax increases "such as HB 2081" [S2].
- **Spending:** calls to "reduce spending" and "cut wasteful spending," and wants "budgets tied to results, not bureaucracy" [S2]; criticizes cuts to K-12 programs including Running Start and Transitional Kindergarten and "diverting money from the police and firefighter pension fund" [S1].
- **Housing:** says Washington has "a housing supply and cost-to-build problem," supports predictable permitting, fee transparency, and subdivision and zoning reforms, and will oppose "rent-cap approaches like HB 1217" and "broad rent control frameworks" [S2].
- **Local control:** calls himself a "Strong advocate for local control," and wants permit steps standardized across jurisdictions "with local control preserved" [S2].
- **Public safety:** calls to "restore public safety" and says he will stand with law enforcement [S1][S2].
- **Schools:** wants K-12 "back to basics" in reading, writing and math, full state funding of special education and student transportation, and less reliance on local levies [S2]; cites his board's literacy overhaul and classroom cellphone restrictions [S2].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Says he "will defend sex-based sports so girls have a level playing field" and "protect privacy in locker rooms and overnight travel" [S2].

### Parents and schools
- Lists "Support Parental Rights" and says he will "Defend parents' right to be informed and involved in their child's education," support "meaningful access to curriculum information," and "Push back on policies that cut parents out of decisions that affect their kids" [S2].
