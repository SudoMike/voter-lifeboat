## What the office does
Each legislative district elects two State Representatives to two-year terms in the 98-member House. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets. LD26 spans parts of Pierce and Kitsap counties (adison-richards.md S15); this contest is also on the Kitsap package's ballot and Pierce researched it once.

## District and race dynamics
November 3, 2026 general election: one-term Democratic Representative Adison Richards, an attorney, faces Republican David Olson, a 13-year Peninsula School Board member, retired Navy officer and former public finance banker (adison-richards.md S1; david-olson.md S1). In a three-way primary Olson led with about 43.8%, Richards had about 33.5% and Democrat Natalie Bornfleth about 22.6% (adison-richards.md S15). Richards has raised about $293k to Olson's about $145k (PDC; adison-richards.md S14, david-olson.md S3).

Richards voted against ESSB 6346 and against the major 2025 revenue bills, while describing "tax fairness" as more than taxing the wealthy; he campaigns on reproductive rights, police funding and supply-side housing (adison-richards.md S3, S5, S10-S13). Olson opposes new taxes and rent caps, calls for spending cuts, and campaigns on sex-based school sports and parental rights (david-olson.md S1, S2).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** Richards campaigns on reproductive freedom and "who we love, or who we are"; Olson supports sex-based school sports.
2. **Parents and schools:** not a clean split. Richards voted for ESHB 1296, which amended the Initiative 2081 parental-rights list, but earlier the same day voted yea on all twelve recorded House floor amendments that would have added, restored or strengthened parent and guardian rights in it; ten failed and two were adopted (adison-richards.md S6, S7, S16-S27). Olson campaigns on parents' right to be informed and involved and on curriculum access, but has no legislative record (david-olson.md S2).
3. **Spending:** Olson calls for spending cuts; Richards for pre-K and technical-school investment with "accountability and efficiency."
4. **Record vs. renewal:** one-term incumbent vs. a long-serving school board member new to the Legislature.
5. **Taxes and housing are closer than party labels suggest:** both oppose the 2025 B&O package and favor more housing supply; Olson goes further with opposition to rent caps and "broad-based taxes."
