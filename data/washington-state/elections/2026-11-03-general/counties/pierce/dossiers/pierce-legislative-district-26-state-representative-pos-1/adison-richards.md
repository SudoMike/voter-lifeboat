## Background
Adison Richards (prefers Democratic Party) has been the 26th District State Representative, Position 1, since 2025 [S1][S8]; the 2023-24 roster does not list him [S9]. He is an attorney who worked at the Northwest Justice Project serving domestic violence and human trafficking victims, at Kitsap Legal Services serving people in housing crises, and as an associate attorney at PCVA [S1]. He attended Peninsula High School and the University of Washington and holds a JD from Villanova Law [S1]. He coaches Peninsula High cross country and volunteers with Rotary and the Foundation for Washington Courts [S1]. In the August 4, 2026 primary he finished second with 17,012 votes (about 33.5%), behind Republican David Olson's 22,256 (about 43.8%) and ahead of Democrat Natalie Bornfleth's 11,467 (about 22.6%), in official results [S15].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** says he "voted against raising taxes on working families and seniors" and secured "a tax cut for Washington farmers" [S1][S3]; says he is committed to "tax reform that reduces the burden on working families and seniors" and that "tax fairness requires more than simply raising taxes on the wealthy – it requires more accountability and efficiency and must also deliver real tax relief for regular folks" [S3]; would lower the B&O tax for small businesses [S3].
- **Spending:** wants dedicated funding toward universal pre-K and continued investment in career and technical schools, and cites helping complete the West Sound Tech trade school [S1][S3].
- **Housing and homelessness:** attributes rising prices "in large part" to low housing supply, and supports building "more homes in our urban cores" and making it "easier to build the housing we need" [S3]; says homelessness needs more treatment capacity, incentives for treatment and "more accountability at every level" [S3].
- **Public safety:** says he sponsored legislation to fund more local police officers and to increase penalties for sexual assault [S1][S3].
- **Healthcare:** supports "increasing competition among hospitals and healthcare providers to rein in costs" while fighting federal cuts [S3].
- **Climate and energy:** wants to expand clean energy careers and says more diverse energy sources will lower bills [S3].
- **Immigration:** says he "voted to ban law enforcement – including ICE – from wearing face masks in WA" [S3].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Says government "shouldn't be involved in our most personal decisions, whether that is reproductive freedom, who we love, or who we are," and promises to "stand up for the right to choose" [S2][S3]; supports Washington's lawsuit over federal mifepristone restrictions [S3].
- Endorsed by Planned Parenthood and Pro-Choice Washington per his campaign site [S4].

### Parents and schools
- Voted yea on ESHB 1296 at House third reading (2025-03-12) and on final passage as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24) [S6]; the bill amended the parental-rights provisions enacted by Initiative 2081 [S7].
- Before the third-reading vote on 2025-03-12, he voted yea on all twelve recorded floor amendments to the House striking amendment (amendments 382, 383, 384, 396, 420, 428, 435, 437, 442, 444, 448 and 462) [S6]. Ten failed, each with 39 to 42 yeas against 52 to 54 nays; amendment 428 was adopted 93-0 and amendment 462 was adopted 92-1 [S6].
- The ten failed amendments would have:
  - added at least monthly evening and weekend opportunities for parents to examine curriculum, textbooks and instructional materials (382) [S16];
  - added medical or health records (435) and mental health counseling records (442), to the extent federal law permits, to the education records parents may inspect [S17][S18];
  - restored parental notification when medical services or medications given to a child could affect the parent's health insurance payments or copays (396) [S19];
  - restored prior notification when medical services, or medications the parent had not authorized, are offered to a child, except in emergencies (420) [S20];
  - restored notification when the school arranges medical treatment or unauthorized medications that result in follow-up care beyond school hours (448) [S21];
  - restored immediate notification if a child is taken from campus without parental permission, including to a youth shelter or host home (444) [S23];
  - added a parental right not to have a child under 18 authorize excused absences (437) [S24];
  - required certificated school staff to work with families to support implementation of the Initiative 2081 parents' bill of rights (383) [S26];
  - removed the provision that the listed parental rights do not create a private right of action (384) [S27].
- The two adopted amendments added a right to immediate notification when a criminal act is alleged against a child, including alleged sexual misconduct by a school employee (428) [S22], and a right to request enrollment in a charter school (462) [S25].
