## Background
Bettina Gese (prefers Republican Party) is a Puyallup High School graduate and a member of the Puyallup Tribe; she says she is the granddaughter of a former Tribal Chief [S2][S4]. She grew up working at the Thunderbird Trading Post, a family business her mother started in 1975, became a commercial geoduck diver in 2006, and later became a licensed real-estate agent and small-business owner [S1][S4]. She says she is responsible for more than 75 employees [S3]. She has served as a PTA president, school volunteer and football booster president [S2][S4]. She has not held public office and says she is "not a career politician" [S2][S4].

In the August 4 primary she received 7,575 votes (38.3%) to incumbent Bryan Yambe's 12,179 (61.5%) [S6].

## Positions
- **Taxes and spending:** would "push back on unnecessary tax increases" and ensure county government "lives within its means"; says "We don't have a money problem — we have a spending problem" and that she would protect essential programs while "identifying wasteful and unnecessary spending" [S2][S3]. Her primary statement also called for scrutiny of taxes and county spending [S1].
- **Public safety:** cites a home break-in that was never prosecuted and says repeat crime, addiction and disorder continue "without meaningful intervention"; says people with addiction need "a pathway to recovery" but "enabling destructive behavior under the guise of compassion helps no one" [S3]. Pledges to support law enforcement and first responders and "ensure accountability" [S3].
- **Housing:** would "lower unnecessary costs, streamline permitting, increase housing supply where it makes sense," and protect single-family homes rather than "force one-size-fits-all development onto established neighborhoods"; says her opponent "has supported policies that erode traditional single-family neighborhoods" [S3].
- **Small business:** would "cut red tape" and "push back on burdensome regulations" [S2][S3].
- **Infrastructure:** wants the Canyon Road project and War Memorial Bridge completed to ease traffic between Frederickson, the Port of Tacoma and I-5, and the deteriorating Milroy Bridge addressed; says pieces of the Milroy Bridge falling into the Puyallup River harm the river's ecosystem [S2][S3].
