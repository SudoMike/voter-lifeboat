## What the office does
The seven-member Pierce County Council is the county's legislative branch: it adopts the biennial budget, ordinances and land-use and zoning policy, sets county taxes within state limits, and oversees county services; each member represents one district for a four-year term. Per the primary overview, District 5 includes Fife, the Port of Tacoma, Parkland, parts of Spanaway, and parts of east, south and northeast Tacoma. The printed Pierce County local voters' pamphlet for the general could not be read (Cloudflare block); both candidates' general-election statements were read from their VoteWA candidate records (bryan-yambe.md S12; bettina-gese.md S7).

## District and race dynamics
November 3, 2026 general election, two candidates, the same two as in the primary. Incumbent Democrat Bryan Yambe, appointed in 2025 after eleven years on the Fife City Council, took 61.5% in August to Republican Bettina Gese's 38.3% (bryan-yambe.md S2, S11). Gese is a Puyallup Tribal member, former geoduck diver, real-estate agent and family-business operator, endorsed by the Puyallup Tribe of Indians (bettina-gese.md S2, S4). Yambe is endorsed by the governor, the Central Labor Council, firefighters and environmental groups (bryan-yambe.md S5). Yambe has raised about $49k against Gese's about $21k (PDC; bryan-yambe.md S10, bettina-gese.md S5).

The council record sets up the contrast: Yambe voted for the 0.1% criminal-justice sales tax the council imposed in March 2026 and for the 2026-2027 budget, (bryan-yambe.md S7, S8). The sales tax passed 5-2 with Councilmembers Herrera and Cruver voting nay and Morell voting aye; the budget passed 4-3 with Morell, Herrera and Cruver voting nay (bryan-yambe.md S7, S8). Gese runs on lower taxes and says the county has "a spending problem" (bettina-gese.md S3). In their statements, Yambe runs on his first-year record (646 affordable homes, the Justice Fund, childcare, behavioral health, food assistance) and Gese on roads and bridges, recovery pathways to "end the revolving door," less red tape for small businesses, and transparent spending, while asking voters to look past party labels (bryan-yambe.md S12; bettina-gese.md S7).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes and spending:** Yambe's votes for the public-safety sales tax and the budget, against Gese's pledge to resist tax increases and cut wasteful spending.
2. **Housing:** both cite permit streamlining; Yambe adds public affordable-housing investment and voted for cottage housing, while Gese stresses protecting single-family neighborhoods from denser development.
3. **Public safety:** Yambe pairs first-responder funding with crisis response and youth intervention; Gese stresses accountability for repeat crime and criticizes "enabling" addiction.
4. **Record vs. renewal:** a one-year incumbent with eleven years of city service, against a first-time candidate.
