## Background

Mike Sommerfeld has been a Pierce County deputy prosecuting attorney since 1995 [S2][S3]. He describes his career as "evenly divided between criminal and civil law practice" [S3].
- **Criminal side:** he prosecuted cases from DUI and domestic violence to murder, kidnapping, human trafficking, child abuse and sexual assault [S3][S4]. He later worked in the office's appellate division, arguing before the Washington Supreme Court [S3]. He says he won a Supreme Court case on the admissibility of controlled-substance DUI evidence and received a Washington Traffic Safety Commission judicial excellence award for it [S2][S3]. The case is not named in his materials and was not independently identified.
- **Civil side (since 2010):** he has advised Pierce County District Court judges and court staff, Pierce County Juvenile Court and, from 2012 to 2016, the Sheriff's Office [S2][S3]. He leads the Government Operations & Litigation Team in the office's Civil Division [S2].

He has served as a judge pro tem in Thurston County District Court, presiding over criminal and civil matters [S2][S3][S4]. His campaign quotes Thurston County District Court Presiding Judge Brett Buckley describing that pro tem work [S2]. He holds a J.D. from Willamette University College of Law and lists Whitman College [S3][S4]. His statement says he served on the Brady Committee [S4]. Community roles [S2][S3]:
- Board member, Jumpstart Community Services (youth aging out of foster care).
- Volunteer with the TPCBA Veterans Legal Clinic, Habitat for Humanity and Read to Me Tacoma.

Primary result: he placed second in the August 4, 2026 primary with 57,950 votes (35.9%), 738 votes behind Pam Nogueira (58,688, 36.4%); Eric J. Lawless was eliminated with 27.4% [S10].

## Positions

Judicial approach:
- Lists integrity, fairness, impartiality, preparation and "respect for every person who comes before the court" as core qualifications, and says judges must understand "the real-world impact their decisions have" [S3].
- Says he would improve language access, including multilingual court websites, and address disability access [S3].
- Says judges should be "aware of how historical inequities and systemic barriers can affect people's experiences within the legal system." He also says courts must ensure that BIPOC individuals are well represented in jury selection [S3].

Criminal-justice formation, bail and diversion:
- His formation is prosecution: about 15 years of criminal prosecution before moving to civil advice work in 2010 [S2][S3].
- His statement says he "will hold violent offenders accountable while expanding therapeutic and alternative programs that reduce repeat offenses" [S4].
- He says the District Court "has considerable room for improvement" in treatment courts and behavioral health and substance abuse services, and that he intends "to advance these programs in a responsible manner" [S3].
- He says "accountability remains an important component" alongside rehabilitation and repairing harm [S3].
- He says efficiency "cannot come at the expense of constitutional rights" and that defense attorneys must be prepared for trial "as due process requires." He would press local officials for appointed-counsel funding [S3].
- No sourced statements or rulings on bail or pretrial release were found.

Court funding and fees:
- Says "excessive reliance on court fees can create barriers to justice" and that courts should be funded through stable public sources [S3].
