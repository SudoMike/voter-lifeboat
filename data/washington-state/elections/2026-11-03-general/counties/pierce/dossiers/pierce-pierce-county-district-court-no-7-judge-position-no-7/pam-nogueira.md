## Background

Pam Nogueira is an attorney and judge pro tem who served as a full-time municipal court judge and superior court commissioner in Thurston County [S2][S4]. Her campaign lists this judicial service [S2]:
- Judge, Olympia Municipal Court, October 2023 to February 2026.
- Court Commissioner, Thurston County Superior Court, March 2021 to September 2023.
- Judge pro tem, June 2019 to December 2021, in Aberdeen and Hoquiam municipal courts, Grays Harbor District and Superior Courts, and Olympia Municipal Court.
- Judge pro tem since March 2026 in Grays Harbor District Court and the Aberdeen, Cosmopolis and Elma municipal courts.

Thurston County's November 2025 results show "Pamella Nogueira" elected unopposed to Olympia Municipal Court Judge, Position No. 1, with 97.8% of the vote [S12]. Her campaign dates the end of that service to February 2026 [S2]. No source found during research explained why she left the Olympia bench after the 2025 election.

Her questionnaire lists her current work as an attorney at McGavick Graves (since March 2026) [S5]. Her campaign site lists a Grays Harbor County Office of Public Defense contract over the same period [S2]. Earlier legal work [S2][S5]:
- Attorney at Ingram, Zelasko & Goodwin, 2016 to 2021.
- Contracted public defense and drug court defense work in Grays Harbor County Superior Court, 2017 to 2021.
- Conflict defense work for Thurston County's Office of Public Defense in 2020.
- Pacific County Senior Legal Aid Clinic.

She holds a J.D. from the University of Washington School of Law (2015) and a B.A. from UW Tacoma, and taught trial advocacy as a UW Law adjunct [S2][S4][S5]. She served on the Raymond City Council from 2017 to 2020 [S2][S5]. She describes Tacoma as "the first place I called home in this country" and lists Brazilian Portuguese as her native language [S4][S5].

Primary result: she placed first in the August 4, 2026 primary with 58,688 votes (36.4%), ahead of Mike Sommerfeld (57,950, 35.9%) and Eric J. Lawless (44,217, 27.4%) [S11].

## Positions

Judicial approach:
- Says "our judiciary must reflect the values of the community it serves" and that she knows "how to run a courtroom that is fair, efficient, and accessible" [S4].
- Lists integrity, impartiality, judicial temperament and setting aside "personal opinions, political pressures, and outside influences" as core qualifications [S5].
- Supports interpreter and translated resources, plain-language explanations from the bench, remote appearances where appropriate, and "data-driven evaluation of court practices to identify disparities in outcomes" [S5].

Criminal-justice formation, bail and diversion:
- Her formation includes contracted public defense and drug court defense in Grays Harbor County, and volunteer drug court panel work in Pacific County Superior Court [S2].
- She says she would support "diversion programs, treatment courts, community-based alternatives, restitution-focused resolutions" where legally authorized. She adds that restorative justice "is not appropriate in every case" and that victim safety and community safety must be weighed [S5].
- She served on the District and Municipal Court Judges Association's Therapeutic Courts committee and the Washington Jail Modernization task force [S2][S5].
- No sourced statements or rulings on bail or pretrial release were found.

Court funding and fees:
- Says courts "should primarily be funded through stable and transparent public funding mechanisms rather than through financial burdens placed disproportionately on people involved in the court system" [S5].
