## What the office does

A district court has jurisdiction, concurrent with the superior court, over misdemeanors and gross misdemeanors committed in the county. Its sentences are capped at a $5,000 fine and one year in jail unless a statute provides otherwise [S1]. It also hears traffic infractions and conducts preliminary hearings as a committing magistrate [S1]. On the civil side, it hears claims up to $100,000 per claimant [S2]. District judges serve four-year terms [S3]. In practice, the judge's discretion lies in pretrial release, sentencing, protection orders and therapeutic-court supervision. The Pierce County court's own pages could not be read (piercecountywa.gov returns a Cloudflare challenge).

## District and race dynamics

- **Open seat.** Neither candidate is a sitting Pierce County District Court judge. The court directory lists eight judges, and neither name is among them [S5]. No source found identified the departing Position 7 judge.
- **Primary.** In the August 4 primary, Pam Nogueira (36.4%) edged Mike Sommerfeld (35.9%) by 738 votes. Eric J. Lawless was eliminated with 27.4% [S4].
- **Bar rating.** The Tacoma-Pierce County Bar Association rated Sommerfeld "Exceptionally Well Qualified." Its 2026 release lists no rating for Nogueira and says candidates who did not request an interview were not rated [S6].
- **Money.** Sommerfeld reported about $116,900 in contributions (as of October 4); Nogueira about $8,400 (as of September 22) [S7].
- **Endorsements.** The 31st LD Democrats endorsed both candidates [S8]. Each campaign lists judges and officials, with some overlap (see the candidate dossiers).

## Differentiating issue axes

- **Experience (record vs. renewal).** Nogueira has about five years of full-time judicial-officer service in Thurston County, as municipal judge and superior court commissioner. Sommerfeld has 31 years in the Pierce County Prosecutor's Office, including service as legal advisor to this court, but only pro tem bench time. Both run on a record; the type differs.
- **Safety (formation).** Nogueira's formation includes public defense and drug court defense. Sommerfeld's is prosecution: serious felonies, then civil advice to the courts. Both endorse therapeutic courts. Sommerfeld pairs this with "hold violent offenders accountable." Nogueira lists diversion and community-based alternatives with case-by-case limits. Neither has a documented bail record.
- **Judicial approach.** Nogueira's judicial-association work centered on access, diversity and inclusive-court projects. Sommerfeld's stated reforms are language access, jury representativeness and fee relief. Both express these within neutral, rule-of-law framing; the evidence for a philosophy split is thin.

## Not differentiating

Both promise impartiality, dignity for court users, remote-hearing access and less reliance on court fees. Party and club endorsements, including Sommerfeld's listed Republican and Democratic organizational support, are recorded as facts only. They are not evidence of how either would rule.
