## What the office does
The seven-member Pierce County Council is the county's legislative branch: it adopts the biennial budget, ordinances and land-use and zoning policy, sets county taxes within state limits, and oversees county services; each member represents one district for a four-year term. District 7 covers Gig Harbor, Fox Island, the Key Peninsula and parts of north and west Tacoma including Ruston (mike-solan.md S5; brenda-lykins.md S8). The Pierce County local voters' pamphlet for the general could not be read (Cloudflare block) and VoteWA had not posted statements as of 2026-10-08.

## District and race dynamics
November 3, 2026 general election for an open seat: Councilmember Robyn Denson did not seek a second term and endorses Lykins (brenda-lykins.md S8). Democrat Brenda Lykins, a neonatal nurse practitioner, former Gig Harbor councilmember and Charter Review commissioner, took 56.9% in the four-way August primary; Republican Mike Solan, a 27-year Seattle police officer and former Seattle Police Officers Guild president, took 29.2%; independent Chuck West (11.8%), who later endorsed Solan, and Ann E. Jolie (2.1%) were eliminated (brenda-lykins.md S13; mike-solan.md S4, S9). Solan reached the ballot only after a court ordered him restored over the charter's one-year voter-registration rule (mike-solan.md S3). Solan has raised about $77k against Lykins's about $57k (PDC; mike-solan.md S8, brenda-lykins.md S12).

The central local issue is Sheriff's Office staffing on the peninsula and the 0.1% public-safety sales tax the council imposed in March 2026: Lykins supports the tax as stable funding for the justice system, while Solan opposes it as written and passed without a public vote (brenda-lykins.md S9; mike-solan.md S5).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes and spending:** Lykins backs the public-safety sales tax; Solan opposes it and says the county spends beyond its means.
2. **Public safety:** both call for more deputies; Solan centers deputy pay, retention and union contracts, while Lykins pairs staffing with behavioral-health responders and violence prevention. On the November charter amendments, Lykins backed the sheriff-oversight ombuds and an appointed sheriff on the Charter Review Commission; Solan opposes both (brenda-lykins.md S14; mike-solan.md S10).
3. **Growth and housing:** Lykins backs the Comprehensive Plan's shrinking of the rural growth area and urban-focused housing; Solan wants less land-use regulation and a more predictable permit process.
4. **Environment:** Lykins emphasizes shoreline, forest and habitat protection and climate resilience, endorsed by conservation groups; Solan states a pro-conservation view of parks and open space.
5. **Record vs. renewal:** a former city councilmember against a first-time candidate from law enforcement.
