## Background
Her general-election statement comes from her VoteWA candidate record, where the "Statement" field holds the text she submitted (not yet marked approved for display on 2026-10-08) [S15]; the Pierce County printed local voters' pamphlet for the general could not be read (Cloudflare block).

Brenda Lykins (prefers Democratic Party) is a neonatal nurse practitioner who has practiced for more than 35 years and founded the SW Washington Perinatal/Neonatal Training Consortium; she holds nursing degrees from the University of Washington [S5][S11]. Her statement lists her occupation as "Nurse Practitioner/ARNP; Gig Harbor City Councilmember (2022-2025); Pierce County Charter Review Commissioner (2026)," her education as an MSN (neonatal nurse practitioner) and BSN from the University of Washington, and says she has lived in Pierce County for more than 45 years, in Gig Harbor and North Tacoma [S15]. She grew up in the Lakewood/Steilacoom area and now lives in the Proctor area of North Tacoma [S5][S8].

She was elected to the Gig Harbor City Council in 2021 and served as mayor pro tempore; her campaign gives her council service as 2022 to 2025, while Gig Harbor Now reported that she resigned in January 2025 after moving to North Tacoma [S5][S11]. She represented Gig Harbor on the Pierce County Regional Council [S5]. In 2025 she was elected to the Pierce County Charter Review Commission for District 7 [S3][S5][S11].

The seat is open because Councilmember Robyn Denson did not seek a second term [S8][S9]. In the August 4 primary Lykins finished first of four with 25,075 votes (56.9%), ahead of Republican Mike Solan with 12,856 (29.2%); independent Chuck West (11.8%) and Ann E. Jolie (2.1%) were eliminated [S13]. Gig Harbor Now's election-night report showed similar shares [S10].

## Positions
- **Overall:** her campaign and primary statement center on public safety, responsible growth, infrastructure, affordability and responsive government, and link her healthcare career to behavioral health and emergency response [S1][S2].
- **Statement (says she will):** says she will "prioritize investing in public safety, expanding broadband access, and improving road safety with intentional maintenance," focus on "creating affordable housing while protecting our natural environment," keep county regulations "clear and fair for small businesses," expand job training and workforce development, and ensure "the voice of rural areas is heard" [S15].
- **Taxes and public safety funding:** supports the 0.1% public safety sales tax the council approved in March 2026 "because it provides a stable, dedicated funding source," saying District 7 residents "want a stronger law enforcement presence, particularly on the Peninsula," which "requires sustainable funding for recruitment, retention, and training"; she also cites state-mandated public-defense costs, tort liabilities, labor agreements, jail medical services and aging jail facilities; says about 76% of the county's general fund goes to criminal justice [S9]. Told KNKX that "having a direct source to help fund public safety I think is critical" and that visitors also pay the tax [S14].
- **Sheriff oversight and the charter amendments:** on the Charter Review Commission she pushed for the amendment creating a Public Safety Ombuds office to investigate complaints about the Sheriff's Office, saying oversight can prevent tort claims and "fraud and abuse," and she supported the amendment making the sheriff appointed by the County Executive; both are on the November ballot [S14].
- **Ballot access:** said she was "okay with" the court ruling that restored her opponent to the ballot: "I do believe in the right of people to run for office" [S14].
- **Public safety:** supports "adequate staffing and resources" for the Sheriff's Department, behavioral-health and crisis-response programs "so law enforcement can focus on crime while mental health professionals respond to appropriate calls," expanded mental-health and addiction treatment, and community-based violence prevention [S6]. Says she would advocate for staffing and funding to let the Sheriff increase Peninsula Detachment staffing as vacancies are filled [S9].
- **Growth and housing:** supports the 2025 Comprehensive Plan's removal of about 400 acres from the District 7 urban growth area and elimination of the rural density bonus, and directing housing toward urban and transit-oriented areas with existing infrastructure; cites cottage housing as one tool [S9]. Her platform calls for "housing development where infrastructure already exists to reduce sprawl" and partnering with cities and nonprofits on homelessness [S6].
- **Environment:** would protect shorelines, forests, wetlands and habitat, conserve open space, and require development to follow "sound environmental science" with mitigation; calls environmental protection an investment in "climate resilience" [S6][S9].
- **Fox Island Bridge:** wants comprehensive fiscal analysis and public outreach before choosing a funding approach; would evaluate county funds, grants, special taxing districts and tolling, though she "personally do[es] not favor tolls," and would seek state and federal money [S9].
- **Government:** supports transparency, spending "taxpayer dollars ... responsibly and effectively," and "policies that strengthen diversity equity and inclusion" [S6].
