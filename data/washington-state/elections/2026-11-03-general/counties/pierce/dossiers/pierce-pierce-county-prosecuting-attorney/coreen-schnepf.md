## Background
Coreen Schnepf is Chief Criminal Deputy in the Pierce County Prosecutor's Office (since 2024), leading the criminal division [S2]. The campaign gives the division's size as 180 attorneys and staff on one page and 200 employees in its announcement [S2][S5]. She supervised the office's Domestic Violence and Human Trafficking Unit from 2019 to 2024, and before that was a deputy and then senior deputy prosecuting attorney in Kitsap County from 2006 to 2019 [S2]. The campaign says she has 20 years as a prosecutor and has taken more than 65 cases to trial, listing homicide, sexual assault, human trafficking, domestic violence, child abuse and organized-crime convictions [S2]. Her statement describes nearly thirty years of work, starting as a CNA in a nursing-home dementia unit while in college, an internship at a prosecutor's office, and a law degree from Seattle University; it says she leads the criminal division of 180 attorneys and staff and is "trusted statewide to train police and prosecutors" [S8]. She was raised in Port Orchard, worked as a nursing assistant from age 17, and lives in Tacoma with her husband, a law enforcement officer, and their four children [S2]. She announced her run after Prosecuting Attorney Mary Robnett told staff she would not seek re-election, and Robnett endorsed her [S5].

## Positions
- **Violent crime:** campaigns on her record convicting "murderers, rapists, human traffickers, and domestic abusers" and on holding people who commit serious violence accountable with cases "built on strong evidence" [S1][S3].
- **Quality-of-life crime:** says she will put "more focus" on stolen cars and organized retail theft [S1][S3]. Her statement says she "has no patience for crime of any kind," that stolen cars "mean parents can't get to work or kids to school," and that retail theft is "driving up the prices of everyday essentials"; it says that as Prosecutor she "will stand up for you by holding criminals accountable who make life for you challenging, expensive, and unsafe" [S8].
- **Technology:** says the office must keep pace with digital evidence (body-camera footage, GPS, phone records, "soon AI"); Robnett credits her with leading a "digital transformation" in the office [S3][S5].
- **Office governance:** pledges to run the office "free of politics," with decisions "based on the evidence and the law," and says she will "show you what the office delivers for every dollar" and attend town halls [S3].
- **Victims and approach:** states her focus as "protect our community, support victims, and make sure cases are prosecuted firmly, fairly, and based on the evidence" [S5]. State Rep. Mari Leavitt, in an endorsement quote, describes a "human approach" that includes "compassion and helping people get on a better path," and cites work with Schnepf on the state anti-trafficking task force and on raising penalties for adults who expose children to fentanyl [S5].
- **Independence:** her statement quotes Robnett saying Schnepf "is committed to justice, not a political party" and says she is endorsed by "serious leaders from both parties" [S8].
- No stated position on diversion, bail or charging thresholds was found [S1][S3][S8].
