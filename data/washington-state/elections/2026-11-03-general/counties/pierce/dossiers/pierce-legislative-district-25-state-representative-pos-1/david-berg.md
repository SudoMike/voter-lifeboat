## Background
David Berg (prefers Democratic Party) has been a Puyallup School District director since 2021 and was re-elected in 2025 [S1][S4]. He served on OSPI's Advisory Committee for Gifted Education (2011-2021), was president of the Washington Coalition for Gifted Education (2013-2021), and has been a PTA legislative chair at the Puyallup council and state Region 5 levels [S1]. He says he began his career in information technology and cybersecurity [S2]. He holds a BA in Politics and Government from the University of Puget Sound [S1]. He has held no legislative office; the "Berg" in 2025-26 House roll calls is Rep. April Berg of the 44th District [S13]. In the August 4, 2026 primary he received 36.28% to incumbent Michael Keaton's 50.19% [S11][S12].

## Positions
- **Priorities:** "strengthening public education, protecting workers' rights, and expanding economic opportunities"; says state policy "must prioritize the economic stability of working people" [S1].
- **Education funding:** says he has testified to the Legislature about education funding and special-education services [S4].
- **AI and data centers:** calls himself "an AI skeptic"; says data centers' power and water demands are "increasing costs for everyone" and the Legislature should revisit a bill requiring large facilities to report water and energy use and to run on new renewable or non-emitting electricity, "to ensure that those who are creating the increased costs are also responsible for paying them" [S5]. Wants a systematic bias review of any AI program before classroom use and says AI should be "a tool and not a final authority" in hiring and firing [S5].
- **Approach:** describes himself as collaborative and focused on "community conversations" [S2][S4].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- In a 2021 school-board campaign post he defended diversity, equity and inclusion work, citing the state's definition that includes gender identity and sexual orientation and noting LGBTQ+ students among those who must "feel safe and welcomed" [S6].
- Endorsed by Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates and the Washington Stonewall Democrats [S3][S9].

### Parents and schools
- In 2021 he objected when board members delayed a teacher-recommended world history curriculum over content concerns, saying teacher input "was sought according to policy... and then disregarded... because of politics" [S7]. In the same post he acknowledged parents' view that the district does not adequately listen to their input and said "there is absolutely room to improve our processes" [S7].
- Says that as a director he has worked "to amplify parent and community voice" [S4].
