## Background
Andrew Barkis (prefers Republican Party) has represented the 2nd Legislative District, Position 1, in the Washington House since February 2016 [S1][S3][S14]. He owns and is designated broker of Hometown Property Management in Lacey and says he has worked in property management for over 30 years [S1][S3]. He holds an AA from Centralia College and a BA in Communications from Seattle University and completed Army ROTC [S1][S3]. He is Ranking Minority Member on the House Transportation Committee and a member of the Housing Committee and the Joint Transportation Committee [S1][S14]. In the August 4, 2026 primary he received 58.08% to William Dehnel's 41.74% (18,389 to 13,214 votes) [S18].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** describes himself as "a vocal opponent of state income taxes and gas tax increases" and says he is "fighting against new taxes" [S3]; says he "blocked a billion-dollar grocery tax and $275 million in annual utility hikes" (a campaign claim not independently verified here) [S2].
- **Spending and budgets:** says he helped deliver "fiscally responsible budgets" as Transportation ranking member [S1] and is "fighting the deficit spending that outpaces our tax revenue" [S2].
- **Transportation:** says he helped secure $340 million for road preservation and maintenance and introduced HB 2181 to repair winter-storm damage "without forcing drivers to pay higher gas taxes" [S2]; the Legislature's record shows HB 2181 (prime sponsor Barkis) concerns paying for response to the December 2025 atmospheric river event and was referred to Appropriations [S15].
- **Housing:** calls himself "a leading voice on housing" who supports increasing housing availability [S1]; says he advocates "increased housing supply and removing bureaucratic barriers to affordable development" [S3] and opposed "frequent, costly building code updates that drive up home prices" [S2]. His site says he "championed" HB 1345 (rural housing options) and HB 2418 (permitting delays) [S2]; the Legislature lists Rep. Low and Rep. Duerr as the prime sponsors of those bills [S16].
- **Public safety:** says he worked to stop efforts "to shorten sentences for serious crimes" and to maintain accountability for registered offenders [S2]; names the drug epidemic as a priority [S3].

- ESHB 2081 (2025 B&O tax rate increases and surcharge on large companies): voted nay on House final passage, 2025-04-22 [S8].
- ESSB 5814 (2025 excise taxes on select services): voted nay on House final passage, 2025-04-23 [S9].
