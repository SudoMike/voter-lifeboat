## What the office does
Each legislative district elects two State Representatives to two-year terms in the 98-member House. Representatives write and vote on state law and the state's operating, capital and transportation budgets. The 28th District is in Pierce County; Leavitt's local endorsers are officials of University Place, Lakewood, Fircrest, Steilacoom, DuPont and Tacoma (mari-leavitt.md S4).

## District and race dynamics
November 3, 2026 general election, two candidates, the same two who were on the primary ballot. Democrat Mari Leavitt, in the House since 2019 and now Deputy Majority Whip, received about 64.5 percent in the primary to Republican Kathy Richardson's about 35.3 percent (mari-leavitt.md S5, S23, S27). Leavitt has raised about $243,000 for 2026 against Richardson's about $23,000 (mari-leavitt.md S22; kathy-richardson.md S10).

Both candidates lead with military ties: Leavitt co-chairs the Joint Committee on Military and Veterans Affairs and calls herself "a military mom and daughter"; Richardson is a 20-year Army officer and nurse practitioner (mari-leavitt.md S1; kathy-richardson.md S1, S4). Leavitt runs on a "bipartisan record" and has a broad endorsement list that includes labor, law enforcement, the Association of Washington Business, realtor and multifamily-housing groups, and Planned Parenthood (mari-leavitt.md S1, S4, S24, S25). Her floor record departs from her caucus on several 2025 votes: she voted against the capital gains and estate tax bill, the services sales tax bill, the gas tax bill and the rent-increase limit, while voting for ESSB 6346 and the business and occupation tax bill (mari-leavitt.md S6, S12, S14, S16, S18, S20). Richardson's campaign centers on budget growth, State Auditor findings and "a government that lives within its means," and frames public safety as a balance of enforcement and treatment (kathy-richardson.md S3, S5, S6, S7).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Spending:** Richardson's repeated calls for budget discipline and audit accountability against Leavitt's investment record in childcare, housing and education.
2. **Taxes:** Leavitt's mixed record (yes on ESSB 6346 and ESHB 2081; no on ESSB 5813, 5814 and 5801) against Richardson's concern about tax increases and high earners leaving the state.
3. **Record vs. renewal:** a seven-year House member in caucus leadership against a candidate with no public office.
4. **Parental rights and social policy:** Leavitt voted for ESHB 1296 and ESSB 5599 and is endorsed by Planned Parenthood and ProChoice Washington; Richardson states no position.
5. **Housing:** Leavitt voted against the 2025 rent-increase limit; Richardson cites permitting costs and regulation and calls for "reducing unnecessary barriers and delays."
