## Background
Kathy Richardson (prefers Republican Party) lists no public office; her pamphlet's elected experience is a board seat on ARNPs United of Washington State [S1][S4]. She served 20 years in the U.S. Army, including assignments at Joint Base Lewis-McChord and a deployment to Iraq with a Forward Surgical Team [S1][S4]. She is a board-certified family and emergency nurse practitioner and a former graduate program director and associate dean for academic affairs at Pacific Lutheran University [S1][S4]. She owns Cossmedic Wellness & Aesthetics and Pacific Northwest Aesthetics Academy, and left the ARNPs United board to serve as a pro-tem member of the Washington State Board of Nursing [S4]. She holds a doctorate from Marymount University, a master's from Pacific Lutheran University, a bachelor's from Eastern Washington University and an associate degree from Spokane Falls Community College [S1][S4]. In the August 4, 2026 primary she received 8,927 of 25,307 votes (about 35.3%) to Mari Leavitt's 16,321 in official results [S11]. The PDC reports $23,401.14 raised and $8,374.84 spent for 2026 [S10].

## Positions
- **Spending and budget:** supports "responsible stewardship of taxpayer dollars" and "a government that lives within its means" [S3]; says the state budget "has grown dramatically," that "government should operate with the same level of responsibility, discipline, and accountability expected from the people funding it," and cites State Auditor findings on accountability and internal controls [S5]; asks whether "current spending levels" are sustainable and whether "taxpayers [are] receiving measurable value for the money being spent" [S6].
- **Taxes:** says residents face "continued discussions around additional taxes and fees" [S5]; warns that if "high-income taxpayers relocate, the state eventually faces difficult financial realities," and lists tax increases among the options government turns to when revenue falls short [S6]; says small businesses face rising "taxes, regulations, and economic uncertainty" and that government should "reduce unnecessary barriers" [S9].
- **Public safety:** calls public safety "a basic responsibility of government" [S7]; lists support for "law enforcement accountability," first responders, mental health and addiction treatment, and prevention and intervention, while saying "communities cannot thrive without safety and order" and "We cannot arrest our way out of every problem" [S7]; notes residents' concerns about theft, open drug use and repeat offenders [S7].
- **Housing:** attributes rising costs partly to "permitting expenses," "regulatory complexity" and property taxes, and lists "increasing housing supply responsibly," "protecting neighborhoods," and "reducing unnecessary barriers and delays" [S8].
- **Health care:** wants "practical, patient-centered solutions" and says health policy should focus on "helping patients—not expanding bureaucracy" [S3].
- **Approach:** says she is running for "accountable, service-driven leadership ... not partisan politics or political theater" [S1].
