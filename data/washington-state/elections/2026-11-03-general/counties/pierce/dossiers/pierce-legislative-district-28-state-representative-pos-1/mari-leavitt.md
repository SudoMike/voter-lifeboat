## Background
Mari Leavitt (prefers Democratic Party) is the 28th District State Representative, Position 1, and is running for re-election to that seat [S1][S2][S5]. She appears on the House roster from the 2019-20 biennium onward and not in 2017-18 [S26][S27]. She is Deputy Majority Whip [S5], co-chairs the Joint Committee on Military and Veterans Affairs, and serves on the Postsecondary Education and Workforce, Appropriations, Capital Budget and Rules committees [S1]; her undated campaign biography also lists her as vice chair of the House Housing Committee [S3]. She was a longtime administrator at Pierce and Tacoma community colleges and a former deputy director of Pierce County human services, and co-owns a family small business, which her site describes as an orthodontics practice [S1][S3]. She holds a PhD from Oregon State University, an M.Ed. and BA from Western Washington University, and an AAS from Tacoma Community College [S1]. She is a former PTSA leader, a former YMCA board member and a past chair of the Pierce County Ethics Commission, and is a mother of seven, including two adopted from foster care [S1]. In the August 4, 2026 primary she received 16,321 of 25,307 votes (about 64.5%) to Republican Kathy Richardson's 8,927 (about 35.3%) in official results [S23].

## Positions
- **Bipartisanship:** describes "my bipartisan record delivering for local families and businesses" [S1].
- **Public safety:** says she "worked with law enforcement to secure resources and tools to keep us safe" [S1].
- **Housing:** says she "supported emergency housing and affordability for seniors, working people, and military families" [S1]; her site says she sponsored policy that "expands affordable housing, addresses homelessness" [S3].
- **Education and childcare:** cites investment in "affordable childcare, training, and improved K-12 outcomes" and focuses on workforce education [S1][S3].
- **Military and veterans:** describes herself as "a military mom and daughter" and cites legislation on pay equity for the Washington National Guard and occupational options for military families [S1][S3].
- **Health care:** her site says she sponsored policy that broadens access to behavioral health care, responds to the opioid crisis and protects health care workers from workplace violence [S3].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Voted yea on ESSB 5599 (2023), which lets licensed youth shelters and host homes treat a minor's seeking gender-affirming or reproductive care as a compelling reason not to contact parents [S10][S11].
- Endorsed by Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates and ProChoice Washington per her campaign site [S4].

### Parents and schools
- Voted yea on ESHB 1296 at House third reading (2025-03-12) and final passage as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24) [S8]; the bill modified the list of parental rights enacted by Initiative 2081 [S9].
- Endorsed by the Washington Education Association per her campaign site [S4].

## Record
- ESSB 6346 (2026): yea on House final passage as amended (2026-03-09; passed 51-46) [S6][S7]. Of the 65 floor amendments the House rejected, she voted yea on 33 [S6].
- 2025 revenue bills: yea on ESHB 2081 (business and occupation tax rate increases and surcharges) [S12][S13]; nay on ESSB 5813 (capital gains tax of 9.9 percent above $1 million and estate tax changes), ESSB 5814 (sales tax extended to select services) and ESSB 5801 (motor vehicle fuel tax up 6 cents per gallon) [S14][S15][S16][S17][S20][S21]. She was one of six House Democrats voting nay on ESSB 5813, nine on ESSB 5814 and eight on ESSB 5801 [S14][S16][S20][S28].
- EHB 1217 (2025, limits annual rent increases to the lesser of 7 percent plus CPI or 10 percent): nay on House passage (2025-03-10) and on final passage of the conference report (2025-04-27); one of five House Democrats voting nay on the final version [S18][S19][S28].
- ESHB 1296 (2025) and ESSB 5599 (2023): yea [S8][S10].
- Campaign finance (PDC, data updated 2026-10-05): $242,810.03 raised and $169,613.99 spent for 2026 [S22].
