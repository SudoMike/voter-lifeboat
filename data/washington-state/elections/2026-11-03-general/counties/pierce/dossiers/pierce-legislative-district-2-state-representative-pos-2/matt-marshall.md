## Background
Matt Marshall (prefers Republican Party) is the 2nd District's Position 2 state representative; the Legislature lists his voting records for 2025 and 2026 only, consistent with a first term beginning in 2025 [S1][S2]. He is a former Eatonville School Director, a former vice-chair of the 2nd LD Pierce County Republicans, and an elected precinct committee officer [S1]. He reports being a small-business owner, an Army captain and combat veteran with 16 years of service, a physician assistant for 14 years, and an adjunct professor at the University of Maryland for 12 years [S1]. He holds a J.D. from Seattle University School of Law and physician assistant degrees from the University of Nebraska Medical Center [S1]. PDC records show he also ran for this position in 2020 (lost in the primary) and in 2024, when he won the general against fellow Republican John Snaza [S14]. He is Assistant Ranking Minority Member on Health Care & Wellness and sits on Appropriations and Education [S2]. In the August 4, 2026 primary he received 56.90% to Angela Taylor's 33.56% and Martin L Miller's 9.41% [S15]. His campaign website is a landing page with contact details and no issue content [S17].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** "I will oppose new taxes, fight the unconstitutional income tax, and demand a responsible budget that funds public safety, education, and infrastructure" [S1].
- **Spending and regulation:** running to "stop out of control government spending" and to "cut fees, regulations, and hidden costs that punish working families and small businesses" [S1].
- **Constitutional rights and sheriffs:** says he will "protect your constitutional rights" and "defend your right to elect and keep a sheriff of the people" [S1].
- **Airport siting:** "I will oppose any mega airport here" [S1].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No statement or recorded vote found (he was not in the Legislature for the 2023 ESSB 5599 vote [S2]).

### Parents and schools
- "I will defend parents' authority to raise and protect their children" [S1].
- Record: offered floor amendments to SHB 1296 adding students' medical or health records, and records of mental health counseling, to the education records parents may inspect (both not adopted) [S11][S12]; voted nay on ESHB 1296 [S9], which amended the Initiative 2081 parental-rights list [S10].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645):** No statement on the initiative by name found as of 2026-10-08. His pamphlet statement pledges to "fight the unconstitutional income tax" [S1]; he voted nay on ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S3][S4].
- **I-1 (IL26-001):** No statement on the initiative by name found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted nay on ESHB 1296 and offered parental record-access amendments to it [S9][S11][S12].
- **I-638 (IL26-638):** No public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- ESSB 6346 (tax on income over $1 million): voted nay on House final passage, 2026-03-09 [S3][S4]. Offered amendments (not adopted) to apply the $1 million standard deduction per individual and to direct 25% of the tax's revenue to a Medicaid reimbursement-rate account [S5][S6].
- ESHB 2081 (2025 B&O tax increases): voted nay on House final passage, 2025-04-22 [S7].
- ESSB 5814 (2025 excise taxes on select services): voted nay on House final passage, 2025-04-23 [S8].
- ESHB 1296 (2025): voted nay on House passage and on final passage as amended by the Senate [S9].
- Campaign finance (PDC, data updated 2026-10-02): $86,305.18 raised and $54,137.23 spent for 2026 [S13].
