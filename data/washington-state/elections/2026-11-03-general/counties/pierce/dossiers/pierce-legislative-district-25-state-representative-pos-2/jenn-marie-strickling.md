## Background
Jenn Marie Strickling (prefers Democratic Party) was elected in 2025 as a Pierce County Charter Review Commissioner for County Council District 5 [S1]. She is a small-business owner, home renovator and artist, and a former member of LiUNA Locals 242 and 252 [S1][S3]. She graduated from Orting High School, rowed on scholarship at Washington State University, and also attended The Evergreen State College [S1]. She has served on the Family Promise board and as a church deaconess [S1]. In the August 4, 2026 primary she received 46.72% to incumbent Cyndy Jacobsen's 48.59% [S10]; The Urbanist noted she "managed nearly 47% of the vote" [S11].

## Positions
- **Taxes and spending:** says "Washington's tax system is fundamentally broken"; would "cut wasteful spending, and fix our upside-down tax code so the wealthiest pay their share while delivering real, tangible relief to working-class households" [S4]. Says she will be "an independent, practical voice... refusing to be a rubber stamp for the Governor or party leadership in Seattle" [S4].
- **Cost of living:** would lower costs "by holding utility companies accountable, incentivizing affordable and workforce housing, and cutting unnecessary red tape for local small businesses," and protect paychecks from the cost of gas, groceries and housing [S1][S4].
- **Housing:** says that as charter commissioner she is delivering "housing protections that keep seniors & veterans in their homes" [S1]; her site lists a charter amendment she submitted, "C-27: Housing Stability & Manufactured Home Protections" (March 2026) [S6]. The text of that amendment is on the Pierce County site, which could not be read (Cloudflare challenge).
- **Labor:** would defend collective bargaining and invest in apprenticeships [S4]; her site lists charter amendment C-12 on project workforce and labor agreements [S6].
- **Public safety:** would ensure police, firefighters and EMS "have the staffing, training, and mental health resources needed" while "investing in community-based solutions that prevent crime" [S4].
- **Schools and healthcare:** supports full K-12 funding and more special-education resources; would keep local clinics and hospitals open, cap prescription drug prices, and expand mental-health and addiction treatment [S4].
- **Tech and privacy:** would establish "strict state protections against corporate data harvesting" and ban "invasive mass surveillance systems like Flock cameras"; would make data centers "pay their fair share" for power and grid costs [S4].
- **Local decision-making:** favors "bottom-up solutions designed by local workers, farmers, and small businesses," saying "we do not need more top-down mandates from out-of-touch politicians who ignore our rural communities" [S3].
- **Infrastructure and flooding:** would fund road and bridge repair, broadband, clean water systems, and flood prevention and stormwater management [S4].
