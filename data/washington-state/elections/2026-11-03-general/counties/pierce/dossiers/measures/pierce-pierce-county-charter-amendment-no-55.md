## What it does

The ballot title reads: "The Pierce County Charter Review Commission proposed an amendment to Section 5.40 of the Pierce County Charter concerning initiative procedures. If approved, this amendment will provide initiative sponsors 180 days to collect signatures in support of a proposed initiative." [S1]

- Initiative sponsors now have 120 days to collect signatures from Pierce County registered voters to qualify an initiative for the next general election. The amendment extends that to 180 days [S1].
- The signature threshold does not change. It stays at 10% of the votes cast in the most recent County Executive election [S2]. The statement for puts that at "currently around 41,000" signatures [S1].
- The ballot title and explanatory statement describe initiatives only. They do not mention referendum deadlines [S1].
- Passage: simple majority of voters [S1]. The Pierce County local voters' pamphlet was not read for this dossier (piercecountywa.gov returned HTTP 403 to automated requests).

## Cost

- The VoteWA record has no fiscal statement or ten-year cost projection [S1].
- The statement for says "This won't cost you a penny!" because signature gathering is paid for by the initiative campaign, with "no added costs to the County government" [S1].
- The con rebuttal argues the longer window lets campaigns "drive up election costs and clog our ballots" [S1]. It cites no figure [S1].

## Arguments for

From the statement for, by "Yes on Charter Amendment 55: Extend time to gather initiative signatures" (Sharon Hanek, Mike Lonnergan, Grant Pelesky) [S1]:

- "Pierce County has never completed an initiative because it allows only 120 days to get signatures equal to 10% of the votes in the last County Executive election." [S1]
- "Allowing two more months to get over forty-thousand signatures is only fair." The extra time "allows more time for mailing and outreach to the people and reduces the need for paid signature gatherers." [S1]
- Rebuttal: the amendment "was passed by a large majority of commissioners elected by local citizens" and is "not backed by 'well-funded special interests.'" "Pierce County has never had a County Initiative make it to your ballot!" [S1]

## Arguments against

From the statement against, by the Committee to Protect Our Initiative Process (Martha Lantz, Justin Leighton, Troy Serad) [S1]:

- "More time doesn't empower everyday citizens. It gives well-funded special interests more time to finance paid signature gathering campaigns." [S1]
- "Pierce County voters have repeatedly rejected changes to the initiative process because the current system works." [S1]
- Rebuttal: "the signature deadline isn't the barrier – bad ideas are. Several referendums have qualified in a 120-day window." [S1]

## Background / context

- **Who is on each side.** Sharon Hanek, the lead pro signer, is a Charter Review commissioner who voted against the appointed-sheriff proposal [S6]. The con committee includes Martha Lantz and Justin Leighton, two of District 7's three commissioners [S1][S2][S3]. The commission's vote count on this amendment was not found. The pro rebuttal calls it "a large majority" [S1].
- **Track record.** The pro side says no county initiative has ever qualified for the ballot [S1]. The con side says several referendums have qualified within 120 days, and that voters "have repeatedly rejected" changes to the process [S1]. This research could not check either claim against county election records, because piercecountywa.gov was not reachable.
- **Campaign money (PDC).** "Citizens for a Better Pierce County" lists Sharon Hanek as treasurer and gives the same email and phone as the Amendment 55 pro committee. It registered on September 11, 2026 in support of "Charter Amendments" and had filed no reports [S5]. The Pierce County Socialist PAC lists Amendment 55 among the amendments it supports [S5]. No committee opposing it was found [S5].
- **Coverage.** News coverage is limited to one-line summaries in roundups [S2][S4].
