## What it does

The ballot title reads: "The Pierce County Charter Review Commission proposed an amendment to Section 2.35 of the Pierce County Charter concerning meetings of the County Council. If approved, this amendment will require the County Council to meet at least 45 times each year and require all Council meetings that require public comment to have an option for remote attendance and public comment." [S1]

- The charter now requires the County Council "to meet at least once in each of 50 weeks during the calendar year" [S1]. The amendment changes that to "at least 45 times during the calendar year" [S1].
- It also requires "all meetings where public comment is required by law or rule to have an option for remote attendance and public comment" [S1].
- Gig Harbor Now quotes the amended text: it removes "once in each of fifty weeks", substitutes "fourty-five times", and adds that "All meetings where public comment is required by law or rule shall have an option for remote attendance and public comment" [S2].
- Tacoma Weekly describes it as requiring "a minimum of 45 annual council meetings instead of 50," eliminating "the weekly meeting requirement," and requiring a remote option where public comment is required [S4].
- Passage: a charter amendment needs a simple majority of voters. The VoteWA record lists the outcomes as "Approved" or "Rejected" [S1]. The Pierce County local voters' pamphlet was not read for this dossier because piercecountywa.gov returned a Cloudflare challenge (HTTP 403) to automated requests.

## Cost

- The VoteWA record has no fiscal statement and no ten-year cost projection for this measure [S1].
- Neither the statement for nor any news source found gives a cost estimate for providing remote attendance [S1][S2][S4].

## Arguments for

From the statement for, submitted by the Committee for Flexible Council Meetings (Troy Serad, Dick Muri, Dave Morell) [S1]:

- The amendment "replaces a rigid scheduling rule with a more practical standard while maintaining regular Council meetings" [S1].
- Putting remote attendance and public comment in the charter "helps ensure residents who cannot attend meetings in person can still participate" [S1].
- The commission sent it to the ballot "with overwhelming support" and "zero votes in opposition" [S1].

## Arguments against

No statement against was submitted: "No statement was submitted against this issue." [S1]

## Background / context

- **The commission.** Pierce County's charter, adopted by voters in 1980, calls for electing a 21-member Charter Review Commission every 10 years, three members from each of the seven council districts [S2]. Its proposals go to voters or to the County Council; it cannot change the charter itself [S3]. Eleven of the 21 commissioners must support an amendment to place it on the ballot [S3]. In 2026 it sent seven amendments to voters and three more to the County Council [S2].
- **Commission vote.** The statement for says no commissioner voted against sending this amendment to the ballot [S1]. A news report of the vote count was not found; The News Tribune's coverage of the final commission votes could not be retrieved (the site timed out).
- **Committee.** The pro committee includes Troy Serad, a commissioner who co-sponsored the sheriff ombuds proposal (Amendment 54) [S5], and Dave Morell, one of three Republicans on the seven-member County Council [S5].
- **Campaign money (PDC).** No committee registered specifically for or against Amendment 52 was found. The Pierce County Socialist PAC (registered September 14, 2026, mini reporting) lists Amendment 52 among six charter amendments it supports [S6]. "Citizens for a Better Pierce County" (treasurer Sharon Hanek) registered in support of "Charter Amendments" without listing numbers [S6].
- **Coverage.** News coverage of this amendment is limited to summaries in roundups of the seven proposals [S2][S4].
