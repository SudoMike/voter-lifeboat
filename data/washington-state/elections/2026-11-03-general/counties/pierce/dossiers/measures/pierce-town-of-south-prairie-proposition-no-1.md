## What it does

Proposition 1 is a levy lid lift. Under Ordinance No. 670, it would raise the Town of South Prairie's regular property tax levy "by approximately $1.33/$1,000, for a maximum rate of $2.90/$1,000 for collection in 2027." The money would fund "comprehensive governmental services, including police, fire, emergency management, parks, open spaces, roads, and other Town operations and services" [S1]. For 2028-2032 the ballot title sets the limit factor for the town's total regular levy at 6%. It also authorizes "the 2032 levy amount as the basis to calculate subsequent levies (RCW 84.55)" [S1].

The explanatory statement says the town provides police, fire, EMS, parks, animal control and open spaces, directly or by contract, and that their costs "have outpaced Town revenues." It gives two examples: "the fire service contract increased approximately 40% between 2024-2025," and "the annual EMS contract cost is approximately 30% greater than EMS levy revenue" [S1]. Without more revenue, it says, "the Town would need to reduce police patrols, parks maintenance and improvements and other services" [S1].

The ballot materials do not state the passage threshold [S1].

## Cost

- The explanatory statement estimates the first-year increase at "approximately $1.33 per $1,000 of assessed property value or, for a $450,000 home, approximately $50.00 per month" [S1].
- The ballot title caps the 2027 rate at $2.90 per $1,000 [S1]. Subtracting the $1.33 increase implies a current town rate of about $1.57. That figure is arithmetic from the ballot title, not a published number [S1]. On that reading the town's regular levy rate would rise by roughly 85%.
- From 2028 through 2032 the town's levy could grow by up to 6% a year instead of the usual 1% limit [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- This is a new lid lift, not a renewal. The ballot materials do not mention an earlier town lid lift [S1].
- **Duration.** The explanatory statement and the pro statement both describe a six-year measure starting with 2027 collections [S1]. The ballot title also authorizes the 2032 levy amount as the base for later levies [S1]. The higher levy amount would therefore stay in the town's base after 2032, even though the 6% growth factor ends then. This reading of RCW 84.55 is the dossier's own and was not confirmed with the town.
- **EMS background.** South Prairie gets emergency services from East Pierce Fire & Rescue. In April 2018 voters made a town EMS levy permanent, 44 to 17 [S2]. The 2026 explanatory statement says the EMS contract now costs about 30% more than that levy raises [S1].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, by committee members David Brubaker and Dean Moerer ("Pro Statement Committee", TownofSP.com) [S1]:

- "A yes vote helps South Prairie continue delivering the core public safety and town services residents rely on every day" [S1].
- "This limited six-year measure provides the resources needed to keep streets, public spaces, and essential operations functioning properly" [S1].
- "This measure is not about expanding government. Town leadership seeks to maintain the basic functions of a small town" [S1].
