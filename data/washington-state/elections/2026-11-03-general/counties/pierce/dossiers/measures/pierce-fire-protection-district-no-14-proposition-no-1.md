## What it does

The ballot title asks whether Pierce County Fire Protection District No. 14 (Riverside Fire & Rescue) should "be authorized to continue funding emergency medical services for its citizens with a regular property tax levy of $0.50 or less per $1,000.00 of assessed valuation for a period of six consecutive years to be collected beginning in 2027" [S1].

The explanatory statement says voters approved a six-year EMS levy of up to $.50 per $1,000 in 2020, and that it "will expire at the end of 2026 unless the voters approve this measure." The money "will be used by the District exclusively for emergency medical services" [S1]. The district's board adopted Resolution No. 2026-416 to put the measure on the November 3, 2026 ballot [S2].

Riverside Fire & Rescue serves the lower Puyallup River Valley, "spanning from the City of Puyallup toward Tacoma along the river corridor and south along Pioneer Way." Its area includes the Puyallup Tribal community and Chief Leschi Schools [S3]. It is a combination department, mostly volunteer firefighter/EMTs with a career fire chief, two lieutenants and an executive assistant. It runs one station (74) with at least three firefighters on duty [S3]. Three elected commissioners govern it [S3].

The ballot materials do not state the passage threshold [S1].

## Cost

- At most $0.50 per $1,000 of assessed value. The explanatory statement says the tax "would not exceed $50.00 per year or approximately $4.17 per month" per $100,000 of assessed value [S1]. On a $500,000 home that is at most $250 a year, a figure computed for this dossier [S1].
- This is the same maximum rate as the expiring 2020 levy [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- This is a renewal. It continues the 2020 EMS levy, which expires at the end of 2026, at the same $.50 maximum for six more years (2027-2032) [S1][S2].
- The explanatory statement says approval "will enable the District to maintain the level of emergency medical services currently provided" [S1].
- **Terminology.** The pro statement calls the measure "The Maintenance and Operations levy" and says "the M&O levy is responsible for approximately 25% of Riverside's overall funding" [S1]. The ballot title and explanatory statement describe an EMS levy [S1]. This research did not find a district budget to check the 25% figure.

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, by committee members Holly Leiter, Dustin Garrett and Ethan Longknife ("Riverside Firefighters For EMS") [S1]:

- "The Maintenance and Operations levy is not a new tax, only a continuation of the Maintenance and Operations (M&O) funding provided from our citizens for many years now" [S1].
- "Emergency call volume in our district continues to increase." The levy "is essential to maintaining the level of service our residents rely on" [S1].
- Support "ensures we can retain current staffing levels, keep our equipment and apparatuses properly maintained, and continue providing the high quality, dependable emergency care our community deserves" [S1].

## Arguments against

VoteWA shows "No statement was submitted against this issue" [S1]. Before the August 4, 2026 deadline, the district invited residents to join both pro and con committees [S2].
