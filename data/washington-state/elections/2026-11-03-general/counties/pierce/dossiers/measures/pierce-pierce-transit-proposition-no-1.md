## What it does

Proposition 1, under Pierce Transit Resolution No. 2026-006, would authorize "an additional sales and use tax of three-tenths of one percent, or three pennies for every $10 spent," effective April 1, 2027 [S1]. The ballot title lists the uses: "adding bus routes and special event service; expanding daytime, evening, and weekend service; reducing wait times between buses; improving access to light rail, healthcare, schools, and jobs; expanding service for people with disabilities; and providing fare-free bus service to seniors and youth" [S1].

The agency's dedicated sales tax would go from 0.6% to 0.9% [S2][S4]. Pierce Transit says this would fund "a 47% increase in transit across the Pierce Transit service area," with improvements phased in from 2027 and complete by 2032 [S2]. The tax applies only within Pierce Transit's current service area, and the measure does not add new cities [S2]. The presentation lists four new routes (63 Northeast Tacoma, 40 Port of Tacoma and East Tacoma, 51 Central Tacoma to Lakewood, 491 Puyallup), seasonal waterfront trolleys, special event service, later evening service and expanded SHUTTLE paratransit [S3]. Seniors 65 and older would ride free, and youth 18 and under would keep riding free [S2][S3].

The Pierce Transit board voted unanimously in July 2026 to put the measure on the ballot [S4]. It is the first Pierce Transit funding measure on the ballot since measures failed in 2011 and 2012 [S4][S5].

## Cost

- 0.3% of taxable purchases, "3 cents on a $10 taxable purchase" [S1]. Pierce Transit's examples: 3 cents on a $10 book, 6 cents on a $20 sweater, 24 cents on $80 of electronics, and 68 cents on a $225 piece of furniture [S2].
- Pierce Transit says sales tax "does not apply to rent or mortgage, groceries or most healthcare, and it also does not apply to other essentials like gas and utilities" [S2].
- **Combined rates.** Tacoma's combined rate is 10.5% now and would be 10.8% [S6]. The statement against cites the same 10.8%, and 11.3% for vehicles [S1]. Lakewood and Puyallup are at 10.3% and would be 10.6%; that arithmetic was done for this dossier [S6]. The unincorporated PTBA rate is 10.2% now, and unincorporated areas outside the PTBA and Sound Transit district are at 8.2% and would not change [S6].
- No annual revenue estimate was found in the materials reviewed [S1][S2][S3].
- The tax has no expiration date in the ballot title [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- This is an increase in an existing tax, the first since 2002 [S1][S4]. Sales tax provides about 80% of Pierce Transit's operating budget [S1][S3]. The agency's 0.6% rate is the lowest of the peer agencies it lists: Sound Transit 1.4%, King County Metro 1.2%, Community Transit 1.2%, Intercity Transit 1.1%, Kitsap Transit 0.9%, Spokane Transit 0.8% [S3].
- If the measure fails, the explanatory statement says Pierce Transit "would begin planning service reductions due to rising costs" [S1]. The agency says it could use reserves for a limited time, start planning cuts in 2027, and that "service reductions could begin as early as 2031" [S2]. In May 2026, CFO Christopher Schuler said expenses could exceed revenues "as early as 2027" and that reserves are projected to fall below minimum requirements by 2032 [S5].
- The agency projects adding about 56 buses, 3 paratransit vehicles, 139 operators and 12 diesel technicians [S3].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, by committee members Alice Phillips, Faaluaina (Lua) Pritchard and Krystal Monteros [S1]:

- "For many people, transit is the difference between reaching a job, a medical appointment, or the grocery store, and not reaching it at all" [S1].
- "For just 3 cents on a $10 purchase, Proposition 1 funds the first local transit investment since 2002." It will "increase service county-wide by 47%" [S1].
- "Proposition 1 makes transit free for seniors and protects free youth fares" [S1].
- Rebuttal: "Under state law, the sales tax is Pierce Transit's only funding option — there is no other source. It isn't charged on groceries, rent, or medicine" [S1].

## Arguments against

From the statement against, by committee member John Miles [S1]:

- "The sales tax is too high. If this measure passes, Tacoma purchases will incur a tax of 10.8%; for vehicles: 11.3%" [S1].
- "Pierce Transit asked people if they want more service. They want more service. They don't want higher sales taxes" [S1].
- "Higher sales taxes hit the poor hardest," and higher rates encourage tax avoidance that "reduces funding for other services" [S1].
- Legislators should be asked "to identify another funding source" [S1].
- Rebuttal: "The 'for' statement is 100% correct... But higher sales tax is no way to fund it. Demand better!" [S1]

The pro rebuttal's claim that sales tax is Pierce Transit's only funding option was not checked against state law. Pierce Transit's own slides show it also gets grants (6%), fares (4%) and other revenue (10%) [S3].

## Campaign committees and money (PDC)

- **Keep Pierce Moving** (committee 42191) is registered for a Pierce County measure titled "expansion of transit in Pierce County" [S7]. As of October 5, 2026 it reported $146,087.26 in contributions, $7,956.12 in expenditures and $43,056.90 in debts [S7].
- Its largest contributors are CAM Action Fund Inc ($25,000), Amalgamated Transit Union ($15,000), and Huitt-Zollars, Gillig and WSP USA ($10,000 each). Other contributors include Cardinal Infrastructure ($7,500), SEIU 775 and SEIU 925 ballot funds, NW Bus Sales and Absher Construction ($5,000 each) [S8].
- No opposition committee was found [S9].

## Endorsements

- The pro rebuttal claims support from "labor unions, and businesses" but names none [S1].
- At the July board meeting, Pierce County Executive Ryan Mello spoke in support, and so did Laura Svancarek, executive director of the advocacy group Tacoma On The Go [S4].

## Context / Coverage

- The Urbanist reports that the expansion would add four new routes, longer hours on 23 routes, and 20-to-30-minute weekend service on 21 routes, up from four today [S4]. Its figure is "around 40%" more service, while Pierce Transit's materials now say 47% [S2][S4].

## Axis notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote raises Pierce Transit's sales tax from 0.6% to 0.9%, a 50% increase in the agency's rate, with no end date [S1][S2]. The con case is about the sales tax rate and its regressivity [S1].
- `spending`: the measure funds a 47% service expansion, new routes and new fare-free service for seniors, not only preservation of current service [S1][S2][S3].
- `local-control`: no change in governance or service area [S2].
- `climate`: the materials talk about traffic and access, not emissions; `climate` also does not apply to measures in the rubric [S1][S2].
