## What it does

The ballot title reads: "The Pierce County Charter Review Commission proposed an amendment to Section 9.70 of the Pierce County Charter creating a Juvenile Detention Advocate Office. If approved, this amendment will create a Juvenile Detention Advocate Office to respond to complaints, monitor conditions and comply with regulations. Pierce County Juvenile Detention will be subject to an independent audit or accreditation review at least once every five years" [S1]

- The office would sit within Pierce County Juvenile Detention. It would be available to detainees and their families "to receive and respond to complaints, monitor conditions, and comply with regulations governing mandatory reporters" [S1].
- It would contribute to the Superior Court's annual publications on juvenile detention operations. Those would include data on "use of force, room confinement, staff training, policy and procedures compliance, the function and activities of the Office, and facility population characteristics" [S1].
- Juvenile Detention would face "an independent audit or accreditation review by a nationally or state-recognized juvenile detention audit or accreditation authority at least once every five years" [S1].
- Gig Harbor Now reports that the office would sit within the Juvenile Division. The Superior Court would appoint its director, and the County Executive and the Superior Court presiding judge would supervise it [S2].
- Tacoma Weekly describes it as providing "independent reviews of juvenile detention facilities in the county with hopes of promoting accountability, transparency, and youth safety" [S3].
- Passage: simple majority of voters [S1]. The Pierce County local voters' pamphlet was not read for this dossier (piercecountywa.gov returned HTTP 403 to automated requests).

## Cost

- The VoteWA record has no fiscal statement or ten-year cost projection [S1].
- The con statement says a new office "will increase costs that taxpayers must ultimately bear," but gives no figure [S1]. The pro statement says oversight failures "create significant costs for taxpayers," also without a figure [S1]. No independent cost estimate was found.

## Arguments for

From the statement for, by the Committee for Safe Youth, Strong Oversight (Justin Leighton, Rick Talbert, Hunter George) [S1]:

- "Every child in Pierce County's custody deserves to be safe, treated with dignity, and protected from abuse." [S1]
- "Recent events, outlined by The News Tribune's investigations, have shown that failures in oversight can cause lasting harm to vulnerable youth, undermine public confidence, and create significant costs for taxpayers." [S1]
- Independent oversight, transparent reporting and regular outside reviews "are recognized best practices" [S1]. Putting them in the charter keeps them in place "regardless of future leadership" [S1].
- Rebuttal: "Judges should make detention decisions, and no one is proposing otherwise. A new facility won't prevent abuse, investigate complaints, or provide accountability." [S1]

## Arguments against

From the statement against, by "No on New Charter Section 9.70 Juvenile Detention Oversight and Audit" (Brenda Milewski) [S1]:

- "This proposal creates an additional layer of government that is unnecessary." [S1]
- Juvenile Detention "has made significant improvements over the past decade" and "today serves a very small detention population. The greatest need: investment in a modern facility." [S1]
- The proposal "blurs the constitutional separation between the judicial and executive branches" and "opens the door to greater executive influence" over a court function [S1].
- Rebuttal: the amendment "duplicates accountability measures and reforms in Pierce County Juvenile Court," including a Family Council, parents' direct access to detention supervisors, and mental-health assessments for youth [S1].
