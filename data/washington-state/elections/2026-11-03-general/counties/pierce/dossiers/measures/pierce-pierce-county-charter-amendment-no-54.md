## What it does

The ballot title reads: "The Pierce County Charter Review Commission proposed an amendment to Section 3.75 of the Pierce County Charter creating a Public Safety Ombuds. If approved, this amendment will create a new executive department of Public Safety Ombuds to provide independent oversight of the Sheriff and Sheriff's Office employees. Subject to limitations imposed by law, the Ombuds shall have authority and resources to monitor all matters involving the Sheriff's Office." [S1]

- The ombuds would have "authority, funding, staffing, and resources to monitor all matters involving the Sheriff's Office" [S1]. It would publish findings of fact and recommendations when an investigation ends and refer complaints to legal and investigatory authorities [S1].
- **Selection.** The County Council appoints the ombuds by majority vote from a list submitted by a panel [S1]. In the commission draft (Proposal C-30), a five-member panel runs a public recruitment and sends three finalists to the council [S4]. One panel member each comes from the County Executive, the sheriff, the Superior Court judges, the Pierce County Bar Association, and the largest Sheriff's Office bargaining unit [S4][S5]. The ombuds would serve four-year terms, at most two [S3][S4][S8]. The draft allows removal only for cause by a two-thirds council vote [S4].
- **Powers.** The draft gives the ombuds subpoena power "subject to the same limits that apply to court-issued subpoenas". It directs the Sheriff's Office to cooperate "fully and in good faith" [S4][S5].
- **Community Advisory Committee.** The amendment requires a committee to advise the ombuds on law enforcement and corrections [S1].
- **Details left to ordinance.** If voters approve, the County Council must pass an ordinance to implement the office [S2]. Sponsors deliberately left the ombuds' duties broad [S2][S3].
- Passage: simple majority of voters [S1]. The Pierce County local voters' pamphlet was not read for this dossier (piercecountywa.gov returned HTTP 403 to automated requests).

## Cost

- The VoteWA record has no fiscal statement or ten-year cost projection [S1].
- Former commissioner Hollie Rogge, who opposes the amendment, said "there was no fiscal note attached to this and it's a whole new department." She said a similar office in Spokane has "like a million-dollar budget" [S6]. That figure is her characterization; this research did not verify it.
- The statement against says the amendment "requires funding and staff but leaves the budget, staffing level, and many important details to be decided later by ordinance" [S1].
- The pro rebuttal cites "national data" of "$22 in litigation cost savings for every $1 invested," without naming the source [S1]. Sponsor Kate Ginn told NWPB that claims and litigation expenses for the sheriff's department totaled over $26 million from 2021 to 2025, the highest of any county department [S3].
- Any wages, working conditions or other terms affecting deputies would be bargained with the Pierce County Deputy Sheriff's Independent Guild [S2][S3].

## Arguments for

From the statement for, by Pierce County for Community Safety Oversight (Kate Ginn, Kristy Gledhill, Lyle Quasim) [S1]:

- The ombuds would "provide impartial oversight of the Pierce County Sheriff Department" [S1].
- Complaints now go through "multiple systems that are difficult to navigate," and "requested information can take months, years, or not be provided at all" [S1].
- "An Ombuds identifies recurring problems before they become costly crises for taxpayers." [S1]
- Rebuttal: "Existing oversight is slow or nonexistent." Internal investigations are "biased," records requests take over 10 months, the state Office of Independent Investigations "only investigates fatalities," and the certifying board has "a 3-year backlog." "An Ombuds is a role, not a whole department." [S1]

## Arguments against

From the statement against, by Pierce County Citizens for Accountable Public Safety (Brian Sonntag, Bruce Dammeier, Paul Pastor) [S1]:

- The county already has accountability tools: the Sheriff's Internal Affairs Unit, independent deadly-force review teams, the state Office of Independent Investigations, and the Criminal Justice Training Commission [S1].
- "If more oversight is needed, the County can create it by ordinance—not by permanently changing the County Charter." [S1]
- "Public safety resources should be focused on reducing crime, responding to emergencies, and supporting the deputies and corrections officers." [S1]
- Rebuttal: the state's independent agency for deadly-force cases "began operating in Pierce County just this May." The amendment creates "another unelected bureaucracy with subpoena powers" and "no direct accountability to voters." [S1]

## Background / context

- **Sponsors.** The draft was sponsored by Commissioners Lantz, Justin Leighton, Lykins, Troy Serad and Kate Ginn [S4]. Brenda Lykins, now a County Council candidate, pushed for it on the commission [S7]. Ginn said it is "not about that one role, whether it's elected or appointed. It's about public safety in Pierce County" [S3]. Commissioner Elizabeth Herrera said it "duplicates oversight functions already performed by current internal and external review processes" [S2]. A commission vote count for this amendment was not found. The News Tribune's report on the vote (headline "Oversight of Sheriff's Office, jailed youth to be sent to Pierce County voters," July 8, 2026) could not be retrieved [S11].
- **Current process.** Complaints can be filed on the county website and go through a Sheriff's Office internal-affairs investigation [S2][S3].
- **Sheriff.** Sheriff Keith Swank opposes it. He called it "little more than virtue signaling" and "an ineffective layer of bureaucracy" [S4], and said added oversight "could further discourage qualified candidates" from joining the office [S2][S3].
- **Deputies' guild.** Guild president Shaun Darby said the guild was not consulted although the office's functions are "a mandatory subject to bargain" [S2]. After the election the guild "will be utilizing all of our legal authority to address it if it passes" [S3].
- **Executive.** Executive Ryan Mello has taken no position. His office says the county will bargain in good faith if voters approve it [S3]. As council chair in 2023 he supported a civilian oversight board that did not advance [S4].
- **Outside view.** George Perezvelez, Spokane's deputy police ombuds, reviewed the text. He raised concerns about the term limit, the lack of specificity about the ombuds' powers, and the absence of a community member on the selection panel [S3].
- **Council District 7 race.** Lykins supports the ombuds. Mike Solan opposes it as "activist led" [S7].
- **State oversight context.** The Criminal Justice Training Commission certifies officers. Bolts reports it is reviewing open misconduct complaints against more than half the state's sheriffs [S10].
- **Campaign money (PDC).** No committee registered specifically on Amendment 54 was found. The Pierce County Socialist PAC lists it among the amendments it supports [S9].
