## What it does

The ballot title reads: "The Pierce County Charter Review Commission proposed an amendment to Section 3.70 of the Pierce County Charter concerning the office of Sheriff. If approved, Sheriff shall cease to be an elective office, and the current elected term of office shall terminate early, effective January 1, 2027. The Sheriff will be appointed by the Executive and confirmed by a majority of the County Council." [S1]

- Today the sheriff is "a nonpartisan elective office" [S1]. If the amendment passes, on January 1, 2027 the office stops being elective "and the current term of office shall terminate" [S1].
- The sheriff would then be appointed by the County Executive and confirmed by a majority of the County Council [S1].
- The incumbent, Sheriff Keith Swank, was elected in November 2024 [S5]. His term would otherwise run to January 1, 2029 [S4]. If the amendment passes, Executive Ryan Mello would appoint the replacement [S7].
- Tacoma Weekly's October roundup says the amendment would "also establish minimum professional qualifications including law enforcement certification and supervisor experience" [S11]. The ballot title and explanatory statement do not mention qualifications [S1]. The full amendment text was not available for this dossier, so that point could not be checked.
- Passage: simple majority of voters [S1]. The Pierce County local voters' pamphlet was not read for this dossier because piercecountywa.gov returned a Cloudflare challenge (HTTP 403) to automated requests.

- The VoteWA record has no fiscal statement and no ten-year cost projection [S1].

## Arguments for

From the statement for, by Pierce County for a Professional Sheriff (Nathe Lawver, Jake Hunter, Lyle Quasim) [S1]:

- "Hiring a sheriff through a transparent appointment process ensures that this power is used justly, legally, and fairly." [S1]
- "A sheriff needs more than just good campaigning." A hiring process "would generate a broad pool of qualified candidates who would be screened and vetted." [S1]
- "An elected sheriff can remain in office even after committing serious misconduct... An appointed sheriff could be placed on administrative leave or removed for serious misconduct." [S1]
- Rebuttal: appointment "was foundational to our first charter in 1981" until the 2006 change, and "the then-appointed Sheriff went on to be re-elected twice." An appointment process "draws dozens of qualified, professional applicants." Appointed sheriffs are "accountable to voters through election of the officials who hire them." [S1]

## Arguments against

From the statement against, by Pierce County Citizens for Accountable Public Safety (Brian Sonntag, Bruce Dammeier, Paul Pastor) [S1]:

- "Charter Amendment 53 takes away your right to elect the Pierce County Sheriff." It "cuts short the term voters elected the current Sheriff to serve." [S1]
- "An appointed Sheriff answers first to the elected official who appoints them." The sheriff should stay independent so decisions rest on "the law, public safety, and the needs of our community—not political pressure." [S1]
- "Across Washington, county Sheriffs are elected and directly accountable to the people." [S1]
- Rebuttal: voters "can replace a Sheriff at the ballot box, recall a Sheriff, and any Sheriff who breaks the law is subject to prosecution." Appointment "only transfers the power to choose the Sheriff from the voters to the County Executive." [S1]

## Background / context

- **History of the office.** The sheriff was elected from 1852 until the 1980 home rule charter made the office appointed [S2]. The charter was adopted after a federal jury convicted Sheriff George Janovich of racketeering [S3]. In 2006 voters approved a Charter Review Commission proposal to return to an elected sheriff [S2], with nearly two-thirds voting yes [S3]. If the amendment passes, Pierce would join King County as the only Washington counties that appoint a sheriff [S4]. King County voters approved a similar change in 2020 [S9].
- **Commission action.** Commissioner Jake Hunter of Tacoma sponsored the proposal, designated C-1 [S2][S3]. He said appointment allows a hiring process that "focuses on qualifications rather than political popularity" [S2]. Commissioner Justin Leighton of District 7 added the January 1, 2027 effective date that ends the current term [S2][S5]. Commissioner Caleb Heimlich of Puyallup proposed starting appointment on January 1, 2029 instead, letting the current term run out; that failed 11-10 [S2]. The commission voted 12-9 on June 15, 2026 to put the amended proposal on the ballot [S2][S4]. Commissioner Hollie Rogge called it "an executive office power grab" [S2]. Commissioner Sharon Hanek questioned the legal exposure of ending a term midway [S4].
- **Current sheriff.** Swank took office in January 2025 [S6]. He won the 2024 election with 50.81% of the vote [S4]. Before that he retired from the Seattle Police Department in 2023 [S4] and twice ran for Congress as a Republican [S9]. Coverage reports repeated controversies over his public statements and social media posts, including statements about transgender people [S4][S6][S9]. It also reports conflicts with Executive Mello over cooperation with ICE and executive orders [S9][S13], and complaints before the state Criminal Justice Training Commission. According to Bolts, he had 18 open complaints as of August, more than any other sheriff [S9]. Swank calls the amendment an attempt to remove him: "This move is purely political... my opponents could not beat me at the ballot box. So they want to cancel me" [S3][S6]. Leighton and Hunter say the question predates Swank and concerns accountability for the office [S2][S4][S9].
- **Lawsuit.** Hollie Rogge, former county auditor and clerk Brian Sonntag, and Jeff Joseph, a former Republican committee officer, sued in Thurston County Superior Court to keep the amendment off the ballot [S7]. On September 4, 2026 a judge denied a preliminary injunction, so the amendment stays on the ballot [S7][S8]. The plaintiffs' attorney said the court found they had not met the "very high hurdle" for pre-election relief [S8]. Swank said he is not a party to the suit but supports it [S7]. He also filed his own "People's Charter Amendment" initiative to keep the elected sheriff and add term limits [S7].
- **State law.** A 2026 state law sets eligibility requirements for sheriffs, elected or appointed, including five years of law enforcement experience. It lets the Criminal Justice Training Commission remove decertified sheriffs [S3][S9]. Bolts reports that a Thurston County judge blocked the sheriff provisions in April and the state is appealing [S9].
- **Partisan setting.** Mello is a Democrat. A narrow Democratic majority on the council would confirm the appointee [S9]. The council has a 4-3 Democratic majority [S13]. Swank is a Republican, though the 2024 sheriff race was nonpartisan [S9].
- **Candidates' positions.** In the County Council District 7 race, Brenda Lykins, a Democrat, supported the amendment as a commissioner. Mike Solan, a Republican, opposes it. Independent Chuck West is "a little up in the air" [S10].
- **Campaign money (PDC).** "VOTE NO ON AMENDMENT 53" (treasurer Jason Michaud) reported $14,520 raised and $0 spent [S12]. "Vote No On C-53" lists Keith Swank as treasurer and had filed no reports [S12]. "Protect Pierce PAC" (treasurer Jake Hunter), registered for "C-1 Appointment of Sheriff", reported $55 raised [S12]. The Pierce County Socialist PAC lists Amendment 53 among the amendments it supports [S12]. Figures are as of the PDC record's last update (October 2-5, 2026) [S12].
