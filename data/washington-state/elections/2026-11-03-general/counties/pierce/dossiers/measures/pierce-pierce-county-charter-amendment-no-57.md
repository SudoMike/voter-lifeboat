## What it does

The ballot title reads: "The Pierce County Charter Review Commission proposed an amendment to Section 9.65 of the Pierce County Charter concerning Nondiscrimination. If approved, this amendment will revise the prohibition on discrimination in the performance of County duties. The prohibition on discrimination may not infringe on, diminish, or impair any right secured by the U.S. Constitution or Washington State Constitution." [S1]

- **Current text.** Section 9.65 prohibits discrimination "because of race, creed, color, sex, age, handicap or any other basis not reasonably related to the accomplishment of a legitimate government purpose" [S2]. The explanatory statement summarizes it as "race, creed, color, sex, age and handicap" [S1].
- **New list.** The amendment would prohibit discrimination based on "sex; race; color; national origin or ancestry; creed; disability; sexual orientation; gender identity or expression; age except by minimum age and retirement provision; genetic testing results; status as a family caregiver; pregnancy, childbirth, or lactation; and military or veteran status" [S1]. Gig Harbor Now's quotation of the text keeps the closing catch-all, "or any other basis not reasonably related to the accomplishment of a legitimate governmental purpose" [S2].
- **Savings clause.** "The prohibition on discrimination may not infringe on, diminish, or impair any right secured by the U.S. Constitution or Washington State Constitution." [S1]
- Tacoma Weekly describes it as modernizing and expanding the anti-discrimination language [S3].
- The pro statement describes the section as "setting standards for county hiring and contracting" [S1]. The ballot title says it applies to "the performance of County duties" [S1].
- Passage: simple majority of voters [S1]. The Pierce County local voters' pamphlet was not read for this dossier (piercecountywa.gov returned HTTP 403 to automated requests).

## Cost

- The VoteWA record has no fiscal statement or ten-year cost projection [S1].
- Each side says the other's position invites litigation. The pro statement says alignment "prevents expensive lawsuits from current and future employees" [S1]. The con statement says the new categories create "uncertainty and inviting litigation" [S1]. Neither gives a figure [S1].

## Arguments for

From the statement for, by Pierce County for Aligned Employment Practices (Devin Rydel-Kelly, Latasha Palmer, Ayanna Colman) [S1]:

- The section "hasn't been updated since 1980" and is "missing language protecting essential and growing workforces, such as veteran spouses or families, parents requiring family leave for infant and elder care, and many other protected classes" [S1].
- Updating it "ensures that our most important governing document complies with the Washington State and US Constitutions" and gives "clear direction to county offices employing 3,000+ workers" [S1].
- Rebuttal: "Every group added is defined through constitutions or court decisions. Including them tells Pierce County's varied communities that we respect their dignity at work and their constitutional rights." [S1]

## Arguments against

From the statement against, by Common Sense for Pierce County (Mason Fletcher, Hollie Rogge, Jeff Joseph) [S1]:

- "Pierce County's existing Charter already prohibits discrimination and has served our community well for decades." [S1]
- New terms "such as genetic testing results, status as a family caregiver, and lactation" leave voters "to guess how these categories will be interpreted" [S1].
- "A county charter should establish enduring principles, not continually expand into evolving policy debates." [S1]
- Rebuttal: "The amendment removes veterans' preference, which hurts not helps their families." The charter "should remain clear, neutral, and not impose values that represent the few, not all." [S1]
- The veterans'-preference claim could not be checked. The full amendment text was not available, and neither the ballot title nor the explanatory statement mentions veterans' preference [S1].

## Background / context

- **Commission debate.** Sheriff Keith Swank testified against the proposal at the commission's June 8 and June 15 meetings. On June 8 he called the language change "offensive to me" and said it discriminates against "normal people" [S2]. He later said complaints against him cited his statement that he would not use people's pronouns even if voters approved the anti-discrimination amendment [S4]. Bolts reports that some of the state misconduct complaints against Swank concern his public statements about LGBTQ+ people [S7]. The commission vote count for this amendment was not found.
- **Con committee.** Hollie Rogge, a former commissioner, and Jeff Joseph, a former Republican committee officer, were also plaintiffs in the lawsuit against Amendment 53 [S6]. On the commission, Rogge opposed the appointed-sheriff proposal, calling it "an executive office power grab" [S2].
- **Campaign money (PDC).** No committee registered specifically on Amendment 57 was found. The Pierce County Socialist PAC lists it among the amendments it supports [S5].
- **Coverage.** Beyond the June testimony, news coverage is limited to summaries in roundups [S2][S3].
