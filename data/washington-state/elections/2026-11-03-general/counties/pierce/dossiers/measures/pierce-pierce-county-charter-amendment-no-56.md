## What it does

The ballot title reads: "The Pierce County Charter Review Commission proposed an amendment to Section 6.35 of the Pierce County Charter concerning a four-year budget outlook. If approved, this amendment will require the Chief Financial Officer to prepare a four-year budget outlook for the next and succeeding biennial budgets. The outlook shall establish that projected expenditures will not exceed projected available funds." [S1]

- The new charter section requires the Chief Financial Officer to prepare a four-year outlook covering "revenues, expenditures, reserves, fund balances, and other information necessary to demonstrate the financial condition of the Current Expense Fund" [S1].
- The outlook "shall establish that projected expenditures under the budget will not exceed projected available funds over the forecast period" [S1].
- Gig Harbor Now reports the amendment also directs the County Council to "consider the outlook" when it adopts the biennial budget [S2]. Tacoma Weekly says the outlook would be presented with the proposed biennial budget [S3].
- The ballot materials found describe the outlook as a planning and certification requirement. They do not describe any penalty or automatic spending cut if projections show a shortfall [S1][S2]. The full text was not available to confirm this.
- Passage: simple majority of voters [S1]. The Pierce County local voters' pamphlet was not read for this dossier (piercecountywa.gov returned HTTP 403 to automated requests).

## Cost

- The VoteWA record has no fiscal statement or ten-year cost projection [S1].
- The two sides disagree on cost. The con statement calls it "an incomplete, unfunded mandate" that requires "new modeling across every department and ongoing upkeep every cycle, work with no staff assigned and no funding attached" [S1]. The pro rebuttal says "No new staff or funding is required. The Finance Director already prepares many elements of a four-year outlook." [S1]

## Arguments for

From the statement for, by "Yes on New Charter Section 6.35 Four Year Budget Outlook" (Wolf Opitz, Doug Richardson) [S1]:

- A four-year outlook makes "Pierce County leadership show if today's commitments are affordable in the future" [S1].
- Planning ahead "will reduce the need for sudden budget cuts, emergency fixes, or rushed tax and fee increases" [S1].
- Putting it in the charter "will bolster Pierce County's exceptional credit rating" [S1]. It "will create greater fiscal stability for taxpayers" [S1].
- Rebuttal: it "builds on Pierce County's strong record of fiscal responsibility by making long-term financial planning a permanent requirement in the Charter" [S1].

## Arguments against

From the statement against, by Pierce County for Local Budget Control (Cameron Severns) [S1]:

- The amendment "was designed to pair with companion amendment O-1, which was not sent to voters. O-1 would have created the position to manage Amendment 56." It assigns "a duty to a position that does not exist" [S1].
- "Pierce County already produces a biennial budget, a six-year forecast, and an annual independent audit." A mandatory four-year certification "pulls staff time from budget work" [S1].
- Rebuttal: "56 lets government hide bloated spending in plain sight." It cites a June 2023 statement by then-Executive Bruce Dammeier that a AAA Moody's rating would lower interest costs on bonds for the building at 1501 Market. The rebuttal calls that "a $50 million legacy project, featuring new offices for the Executive and Council" [S1].

## Background / context

- **Title discrepancy.** The ballot title and explanatory statement assign the outlook to the "Chief Financial Officer" [S1]. The pro rebuttal refers to the "Finance Director" [S1]. The con statement says the companion proposal O-1, which was not sent to voters, would have created the position meant to manage the outlook [S1]. This research could not confirm from county sources whether the charter currently has a Chief Financial Officer position.
- **Commission vote.** The vote count for this amendment was not found [S2].
- **Campaign money (PDC).** No committee registered for or against Amendment 56 was found. It is the only one of the seven amendments that the Pierce County Socialist PAC does not list in its registration [S4]. "Citizens for a Better Pierce County" registered in support of "Charter Amendments" without listing numbers [S4].
- **Coverage.** News coverage is limited to summaries in roundups [S2][S3].

## Axis notes

- `spending`: a YES vote writes into the charter a requirement that projected spending fit within projected available funds over four years [S1]. The pro case is framed as fiscal discipline: "show if today's commitments are affordable," avoid "rushed tax and fee increases," protect the credit rating [S1]. That fits the rubric's pole A ("Audit & restrain: oversight ... performance standards, and restraint before any new spending"). Two things limit the strength. First, the outlook is a planning and certification duty with no enforcement mechanism described [S1][S2]. Second, the con side also argues from restraint ("Local Budget Control", "hide bloated spending") and objects to duplication and an unfunded duty, not to restraint itself [S1]. Suggested mapping: `spending` -1.
- `taxes`: the pro statement mentions avoiding "rushed tax and fee increases," but the measure sets no tax rate and does not limit taxes [S1]. Too indirect to map.
- `local-control` / `social` / `parental-rights`: not addressed [S1].
