## What it does

Under Resolution No. 09-25-26, Proposition No. 1 would authorize Yelm Community Schools to levy excess property taxes for two years: up to $11,194,449 for collection in 2027 and $12,278,072 in 2028, at an estimated $1.50 per $1,000 of assessed value [S1]. The money would pay for "essential educational programs, extracurricular activities and operations that are not fully funded by the State." The explanatory statement lists teachers, specialists, paraeducators, nurses, counselors, social workers, school resource officers and safety staff, graduation and career readiness, technology, athletics, extracurricular activities, facilities and curriculum materials [S1].

The explanatory statement says the levy would maintain the programs "established for the 2025-26 school year into the 2026-27 and 2027-28 school years" [S1]. The district says the levy would also provide "a limited amount of additional local funding" to reinstate some programs, such as counselors, student behavior support, safety and security, and some previously reduced student activities [S3].

The district lies in both Pierce and Thurston counties; VoteWA lists "Pierce, Thurston" [S1]. The board resolution designates the Thurston County Auditor to handle the ballot title [S3]. Ballot drop boxes include Roy City Hall in Pierce County [S2].

The ballot materials do not state the passage threshold [S1].

## Cost

- An estimated $1.50 per $1,000 of assessed value in 2027 and 2028 [S1][S3]. The ballot materials give no per-home example [S1]. On a $400,000 home, $1.50 per $1,000 is $600 a year, a figure computed for this dossier [S1].
- The levy authorizes dollar amounts, not a rate. If assessed values rise, the rate falls [S3]. The state allows EP&O levies of up to $2.50 per $1,000 [S3]. The district says it chose $1.50 because that is the minimum rate that qualifies it for full state levy equalization (Local Effort Assistance) [S3].
- The district's EP&O page lists the 2028 amount as $12,278,073, one dollar more than the ballot title's $12,278,072 [S1][S2].
- Seniors (61 or older) and people with disabilities who meet income limits may be exempt. The district gives contact numbers for both the Thurston and the Pierce County assessor [S3].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- **This is not a replacement levy.** Yelm Community Schools "has been operating without a local EP&O levy since 2024," and its schools "have functioned without a local levy for the last two years" [S3][S5]. OSPI's 2026 levy status data shows Yelm School District with $0 in estimated local levy funding for 2026 and a status of "Failed." It is one of six districts statewide in that category, against 283 with active levies [S6]. A yes vote would therefore add a $1.50 levy to tax bills that currently carry no Yelm EP&O levy [S3][S6].
- **Earlier failures.** The statement against says "Four times Yelm voters have said No" [S1]. The pro statement refers to "multiple levy failures" [S1]. This research did not find the dates and results of the earlier elections.
- **District finances.** Yelm has been under state "Binding Conditions" since September 3, 2024 [S4]. The 2026-27 General Fund budget has $88.12 million in revenue and $95.18 million in expenditures and transfers out. The $7.06 million gap is covered from fund balance, including a one-time transfer of money previously reserved for capital projects [S5]. The district says the transfer provided $9 million in 2025-26 and that "There is no additional reserve" [S5]. The con rebuttal cites different figures ("$99.7 million in expenditures against $90.3 million in revenue, with 84.7% of expenditures going to compensation") [S1]. This research did not reconcile the two sets of numbers.
- **Staffing.** Since 2024 the district has cut 163 positions [S3]. Enrollment has fallen by about 475 students since 2023-24 [S5].
- **If it fails.** The district says OSPI has told it the state would move the district from Binding Conditions into Financial Oversight. A state-appointed committee would then have authority over district decisions, "up to and including the possibility of dissolving" the district [S3][S4]. The district projects about $9.5 million in further cuts and 80-90+ layoffs for 2027-28, larger class sizes, and the end of athletics and other before- and after-school activities [S3][S4]. The district notes the last dissolution was Vader School District in 2007 [S4].

## Arguments for

From the statement in favor, by Yelm Kids First (Judson Willis, Debbie Edwards and Kathryn Cullum) [S1]:

- "Yelm Community Schools has faced significant financial challenges after multiple levy failures and ongoing funding shortfalls. As a result, the district has already made difficult cuts to staffing, programs, athletics, and student opportunities" [S1].
- "The state does not fully fund many of the services our community expects from its schools" [S1].
- Passing the measure "will provide critical local funding to help restore stability, maintain important programs and services, and preserve opportunities for students" [S1].
- Rebuttal: "Washington's school funding system is flawed, and Olympia should be held accountable. But rejecting this levy will not fix that failure... When levies fail, the burden falls on Yelm's students, educators, and families" [S1].

## Arguments against

From the statement against, by Martin Miller and Frank Vance (No2taxpoverty@gmail.com) [S1]:

- "Four times Yelm voters have said No. Instead of addressing that message, YCS is asking again for more money while restoring none of the programs already cut" [S1].
- "The district used one-time capital resources to support recurring operating expenses while approving raises and maintaining compensation costs above state funding allocations" [S1].
- "Stop making neighbors tax neighbors to compensate for a funding problem created in Olympia. Take the fight to the state" [S1].
- "Bring expenses under control, demand that Olympia fulfill its responsibility, and fight for meaningful local authority over education" [S1].
- Rebuttal: "The board controls those obligations; voters control the levy. Four No votes require change, not another request" [S1].

The con statement says the levy restores "none of the programs already cut," while the district says it would allow a limited reinstatement of some programs [S1][S3].

## Axis notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote adds a $1.50 per $1,000 levy, about $11-12 million a year, to bills that have had no Yelm EP&O levy since 2024 [S1][S3][S6]. It lasts two years [S1]. The con case is explicitly anti-tax ("Stop making neighbors tax neighbors") [S1].
- `spending`: the levy mainly keeps current programs, with limited restoration [S1][S3]. The con argues for spending restraint and accountability before more revenue [S1].
- `local-control`: both sides invoke local control. The district says failure would hand decisions to a state oversight committee [S3][S4], and the con asks for "meaningful local authority over education" [S1]. The measure itself does not change governance [S1].
- `parental-rights`: neither the measure nor the statements address parental notification, records access or opt-outs [S1].
