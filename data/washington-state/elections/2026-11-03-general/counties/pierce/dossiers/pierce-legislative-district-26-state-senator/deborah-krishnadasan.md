## Background
Deborah (Deb) Krishnadasan (prefers Democratic Party) is the current 26th District State Senator and a former Peninsula School District board member and president [S1]. The primary-stage review of her Legislature profile recorded that she entered the Senate by appointment and then won the district's special election [S18]; in the November 4, 2025 special election she received 52.55% to Republican Michelle Caldier's 47.36% [S16]. The Legislature's 2025-26 roster lists her as the 26th District senator, and its 2023-24 roster lists Emily Randall in that seat [S8][S9]. She held professional roles at the Cystic Fibrosis Foundation and in mergers and acquisitions at Microsoft, and has a BA from Western Washington University [S1]. She founded the school advocacy group Stand Up for Peninsula Schools and led the district's 2019 bond campaign, the first capital bond the district had passed in 20 years [S1][S2]. In the August 4, 2026 primary she received 28,575 votes to Republican Gary Parker's 22,328 (about 56.1% to 43.8%) in official results [S17].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** says she voted against a gas tax increase and "taxes on small businesses" and proposed "the first sales tax cut in Washington in 40 years" [S1][S3]; her campaign site says she voted "NO on tax increases" and describes standing "up to my own party" on taxes [S2][S3].
- **Property tax relief:** says she led the expansion of the Property Tax Relief Program (SB 6162) for seniors, people with disabilities and disabled veterans [S1][S3]; the Legislature lists her as prime sponsor of SB 6162, "Concerning property tax reform," with an effective date of June 11, 2026 [S14].
- **Spending and programs:** cites an energy assistance program, increased support for local healthcare and childcare programs, $59 million secured for the district, and a vote to establish the PreK Promise account [S1][S2][S3].
- **Housing:** supports "expanding affordable housing, protecting renters and first-time homebuyers" and says she will work to "hold corporations accountable for price hikes" [S3].
- **Public safety:** supports full funding of first responders, behavioral health services and community-based violence prevention, and cites a vote for $100 million for new law enforcement officers and public safety programs [S3].
- **Healthcare:** supports capping prescription drug prices, fair provider reimbursement and rural and Tribal healthcare infrastructure, and says the state should lessen the impact of federal Medicaid cuts [S3].
- **Climate:** supports "bold but balanced climate action," clean energy jobs, and protecting forests, waters and farmland [S3].
- **Immigration:** her campaign site lists "Banned ICE from wearing face masks" among her actions [S2].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Supports "maintaining strong legal protections for abortion" and expanded reproductive and maternal care, and says she sponsored and passed legislation on reproductive care in the emergency room [S2][S3].
- Endorsed by Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates [S1].

### Parents and schools
- Says "families should always have a voice in their children's education," working with teachers and school leaders [S3].
- Voted yea on ESHB 1296 in the Senate on 2025-04-11 [S6]; the bill amended the parental-rights provisions enacted by Initiative 2081 in 2024 [S7].
- Proposed legislation to lower the vote threshold for school districts to pass construction bonds [S3].
