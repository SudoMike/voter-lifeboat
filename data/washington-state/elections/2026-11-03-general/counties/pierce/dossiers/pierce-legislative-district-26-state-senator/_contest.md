## What the office does
The 26th Legislative District elects one State Senator to a four-year term in the 49-member Senate. Senators write and vote on state law and budgets and confirm gubernatorial appointments. LD26 spans parts of Pierce County and Kitsap County; official primary results report votes from both counties (deborah-krishnadasan.md S17). This contest is also on the Kitsap package's ballot; Pierce researched it once.

## District and race dynamics
November 3, 2026 general election, two candidates, the same two who were on the August primary ballot: Democratic Senator Deborah Krishnadasan, who won a November 2025 special election 52.55% to 47.36% over Republican Michelle Caldier (deborah-krishnadasan.md S16), and Republican Gary Parker, a Gig Harbor restaurant owner and former Intel engineer and executive with no elected experience (gary-parker.md S1). In the primary Krishnadasan had about 56.1% to Parker's 43.8% (deborah-krishnadasan.md S17).

Krishnadasan campaigns as a tax skeptic within her party: she voted against ESSB 6346 and against the major 2025 revenue bills, and cites a proposed sales tax cut and property tax relief, alongside abortion protections and school funding (deborah-krishnadasan.md S1, S3, S5, S10-S14). Parker runs on tax relief, spending accountability, deregulation and law-enforcement funding, as "Not a politician" (gary-parker.md S2, S3). Krishnadasan has raised about $325k to Parker's about $76k (PDC; deborah-krishnadasan.md S15, gary-parker.md S5).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** sitting senator and former school board president vs. a first-time candidate from business.
2. **Spending:** Krishnadasan cites program funding (energy assistance, PreK, healthcare); Parker emphasizes "out-of-control spending" and accountability.
3. **Social policy and schools:** Krishnadasan campaigns on abortion protections and voted for ESHB 1296; Parker states no position on either.
4. **Public safety and climate:** Krishnadasan pairs police funding with prevention and supports "bold but balanced" climate action; Parker emphasizes law enforcement and expanding energy supply.
5. **Taxes do not separate them sharply:** both campaign against tax increases; Krishnadasan has the voting record against ESSB 6346 and the 2025 revenue bills.
