## What the office does
Each legislative district elects two State Representatives to two-year terms in the 98-member House. Representatives write and vote on state law and the state's operating, capital and transportation budgets. The 28th District is in Pierce County; both candidates live in Lakewood (dan-bronoske.md S2; kerry-hills.md S2).

## District and race dynamics
November 3, 2026 general election, two candidates. Democrat Dan Bronoske, a West Pierce Fire & Rescue captain in the House since 2021 and chair of the Health Care & Wellness Committee, was the only name on the August primary ballot and received about 90.7 percent; write-ins took about 9.3 percent (dan-bronoske.md S1, S22). Kerry Hills, a probation officer who prefers the "Nonpartisan Party," was not on the primary ballot. VoteWA lists a filing date of August 18, two weeks after the primary, and the PDC records a declaration on July 27, during the primary voting period; with write-ins well above the 1 percent threshold in state law, the record is consistent with Hills having advanced as a declared write-in, though no source reviewed gives a by-name write-in tally (kerry-hills.md S2, S3, S4, S5). Bronoske has raised about $142,000 for 2026; Hills reports under the PDC mini-reporting option (dan-bronoske.md S21; kerry-hills.md S3).

Bronoske runs on lowering costs, health care (a $35 insulin cap), housing supply through permit streamlining, and public safety, and is endorsed by the Washington State Labor Council, nurses and firefighters (dan-bronoske.md S1, S2, S23). His floor record departs from his caucus on taxes and housing: he was one of eight House Democrats to vote against ESSB 6346 and one of five to vote against the final 2025 rent-increase limit, while voting for the 2025 capital gains, B&O and gas tax bills (dan-bronoske.md S5, S11, S13, S17, S19, S25). Hills's pamphlet statement focuses on "increased taxes enacted," an Olympia "spending problem," and "balanced government" (kerry-hills.md S1).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Spending:** Hills says "Olympia has a spending problem"; Bronoske cites investments in schools, behavioral health, transit and Medicaid reimbursement.
2. **Taxes:** Hills objects to recent tax increases; Bronoske's record is split (no on ESSB 6346 and ESSB 5814, yes on ESSB 5813, ESHB 2081 and ESSB 5801).
3. **Record vs. renewal:** a sitting committee chair against a candidate with no public office.
4. **Parental rights and social policy:** Bronoske voted for ESHB 1296 and ESSB 5599; Hills states no position.
