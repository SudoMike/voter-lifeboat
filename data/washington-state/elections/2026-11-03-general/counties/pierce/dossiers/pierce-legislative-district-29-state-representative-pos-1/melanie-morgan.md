## Background
Melanie Morgan (prefers Democratic Party) is the incumbent 29th District State Representative, Position 1, in her fourth term; the Legislature's voting records for her run from 2019 [S1][S2]. Her pamphlet statement lists her as Deputy Majority Floor Leader, chair of the Black Members Caucus and the Members of Color Caucus, and co-chair of the Social Equity in Cannabis Task Force [S1]. Her current committees are Agriculture & Natural Resources (vice chair), Capital Budget, Consumer Protection & Business, and Rules [S2]. She is a former Franklin Pierce Schools board director and a U.S. Army veteran [S1][S3]. She has worked as a landlord/tenant coordinator, mortgage banker, diversity trainer, and domestic-violence and sexual-assault advocate, and holds a B.A. in psychology from Saint Martin's University [S1]. Her caucus biography says she has personally experienced homelessness and has served as a Pierce County Housing Authority commissioner [S3]. She lives in Spanaway [S3]. She has raised $96,644.35 and spent $57,819.23 (PDC, data updated 2026-10-05) [S15]. She has no campaign website; the pamphlet lists a Gmail address [S1].

## Positions
- Says she "supported legislation and appropriations that benefit the 29th; capped rental increases, reformed zoning laws for affordable housing, supported small business, and made public safety investments" [S1].
- Describes herself as "unbought and unbossed," saying leadership requires "independent thinking — not political pressure or ideology" [S1].
- Says she will keep working on "equity, public safety, healthcare, housing, and opportunities for everyone in our district" [S1].
- Her caucus biography says she applies an "equity, diversity, and inclusion lens" to policy [S3].

### Taxes
- Voted nay on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the millionaires' tax, on 2026-03-09; the bill passed 51-46 [S4].
- Voted yea on the 2021 capital gains tax (ESSB 5096) and on three 2025 revenue bills: ESSB 5813 (higher capital gains and estate tax rates), ESSB 5814 (excise taxes on select services) and ESHB 2081 (B&O tax changes and a surcharge on large companies) [S7][S8][S9][S10].
- On 2026-03-09 the House took 71 recorded votes on floor amendments to ESSB 6346 [S4]. On the 51 votes on Republican-sponsored amendments she voted nay 46 times, each on an amendment that failed, and yea five times [S4][S14]. Two of those five were adopted almost unanimously (amendment 2563, 93-0; amendment 2572, 93-1) [S4]. The other three failed: amendment 2569, which would have exempted income of small forestland owners from the tax (44-52) [S4][S18]; amendment 2582, which would have waived penalties and interest on certain excise taxes due before July 1, 2026 (47-49) [S4][S19]; and amendment 2538, which would have removed the bill's necessity clause and sent the act to voters at the next general election (43-50) [S4][S20]. On the 20 votes on Democratic-sponsored amendments she voted yea 14 times and nay six times [S4][S14].
- Voted nay on House final passage of ESSB 5801 (transportation resources) on 2025-04-24; it passed 51-47 [S16]. The enacted bill raised the state fuel tax by $0.06 per gallon starting July 1, 2025, added a 2 percent annual inflation adjustment from July 1, 2026, and raised passenger vehicle weight fees [S17].
- No public statement explaining the ESSB 6346 or ESSB 5801 votes was found as of 2026-10-08.
