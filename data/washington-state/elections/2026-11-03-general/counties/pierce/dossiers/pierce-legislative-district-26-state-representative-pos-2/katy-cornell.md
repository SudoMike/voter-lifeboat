## Background
Katy Cornell (prefers Republican Party) is a nonprofit leader, small business owner and pastor with 18+ years leading teams and programs serving families, refugees, and people leaving poverty and addiction [S1]. Her elected-experience entry lists the Gig Harbor Planning Commission [S1]. She grew up in Port Orchard, graduated from South Kitsap High School, and holds an associate degree from Olympic College, a bachelor's in psychology from NU and a master's in nonprofit leadership from Seattle University [S1][S2]. She is co-president of Walk In The Light International and vice president of Heart for the City [S1]. She is running for the open Position 2 seat; Representative Michelle Caldier (Caldier-Valdez), the incumbent, endorses her as a successor [S2][S4]. In the August 4, 2026 primary she finished first with 22,635 votes (about 44.7%), ahead of Democrat Renee Hernandez Greenfield's 20,023 (about 39.6%), Democrat Tedd Wetherbee (about 13.3%) and Randy Phillips (about 2.4%) in official results [S10].

## Positions
- **Approach:** describes herself as "a common-sense Republican" who is "not running as a political activist or to focus on polarizing social battles" [S3].
- **Taxes:** calls for "smarter spending, not higher taxes" [S1]; would "Work to reverse ESSB 5814 and the expansion of sales taxes on services" and "Oppose the income tax especially in pass through income on LLCs," and would reduce "unnecessary taxes, mandates, and regulations" [S3].
- **Spending:** wants to "get our budget back on track, reduce waste and unnecessary bureaucracy" and "Streamline government," while reducing costs "without cutting core services" [S3]; wants state K-12 funded fully so the burden does not shift to local property taxpayers, and an evaluation of how education dollars are spent [S8].
- **Housing:** would "Reduce costly regulations, impact fees, and red tape," streamline permitting and environmental review, and "Oppose policies and mandates that continue making housing less affordable" [S3][S7].
- **Public safety:** supports court-mandated treatment for repeat offenders in crisis, stronger prosecution of chronic property crime, and reform of "the revolving-door system" [S3]; wants "meaningful consequences" for hard-drug possession after the Blake decision alongside treatment, and would reverse restrictions on automated license plate readers "with appropriate privacy safeguards" [S5].
- **Homelessness and recovery:** would pair housing assistance with "treatment, recovery expectations, and accountability" [S3].
- **Energy:** would extend Clean Energy Transformation Act deadlines to 2040 to limit utility costs, while supporting "balanced, practical clean energy solutions" [S3][S6].
- **Constitutional freedoms:** would protect "freedom of speech, religious liberty, and individual rights" and "represent all people in our district" [S3].
