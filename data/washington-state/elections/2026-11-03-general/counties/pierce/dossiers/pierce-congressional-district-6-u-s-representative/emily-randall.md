## Background

Emily Randall is the incumbent U.S. Representative for Washington's 6th Congressional District, which she describes as the Olympic Peninsula and South Puget Sound [S1]. She grew up in Port Orchard as the daughter of a shipfitter and a paraeducator, earned a BA from Wellesley College as the first in her family to finish a degree, and worked for 10 years at Planned Parenthood, Children's Hospital and Wellesley College [S1]. She served two terms in the Washington State Senate, from 2019 to 2024, before entering Congress in 2025 [S8][S11]. She sits on the House Natural Resources and Oversight committees, founded the Congressional Ferry Caucus, co-chairs the Congressional Equality Caucus, and lists membership in both the Congressional Progressive Caucus and the New Democrat Coalition [S9].

**Primary result.** In the certified August 4, 2026 primary she placed first with 132,805 votes (58.8%), ahead of Teresa Fox with 57,700 (25.5%); three other candidates shared the rest [S12].

## Positions

### Taxes, spending and costs
- Says she wants "a tax structure that requires big business to pay its fair share" and that "there is too much wealth in too few hands"; describes her approach as an economy built "from the middle class out–not trickle down" [S3].
- Says Republican leaders let the enhanced ACA premium tax credits expire "to pay for tax breaks for billionaires" [S16].
- Voted against final passage of H.R. 1, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 3, 2025) [S17].
- Cites nearly $18 million in federal community-project funding she secured for all six counties of the district [S1][S11].
- Her July 2026 primary questionnaire lists bills to "build millions more homes," stop oil-company price gouging, end "corporate surveillance pricing," and raise wages [S11].

### Health care
- Pledges to "protect and strengthen the ACA" and to give states more flexibility with Medicaid dollars to expand coverage; wants lower out-of-pocket medication costs [S4].
- Cites sponsoring the state law that created Washington's Universal Healthcare Commission; her issue page does not mention Medicare for All or single-payer [S4].
- After the House passed a three-year ACA tax-credit extension in January 2026 she said "we don't have a genuine health care system in this country. We have a sick care system" [S16].

### Climate and energy
- Calls for "a just and fair transition away from fossil fuels" and investment in clean-energy jobs and infrastructure [S5].
- Voted against H.J.Res. 88 (May 2025), which disapproved the EPA waiver for California's vehicle-emission standards [S24].
- Sponsored the Wild Olympics Wilderness and Wild and Scenic Rivers Act (H.R. 3369) [S10].

### Public safety
- Voted against the HALT Fentanyl Act (H.R. 27, February 2025) [S21].
- Her campaign lists endorsements from Moms Demand Action (2024 Gun Sense Candidate) and the Alliance for Gun Responsibility; her House biography lists "common-sense gun reform" as a priority [S2][S8].

### Immigration
- Voted against the Laken Riley Act (S. 5, January 2025) [S20].
- In June 2025 said ICE raids were "blatantly circumventing due process to pick up people with brown skin" and called the deployment of troops to Los Angeles "authoritarian" [S15].
- Her pamphlet statement says "When ICE acted illegally, I didn't stay quiet" [S1].
- Introduced the Emergency Responder Protection Act (H.R. 5603), announced as legislation "to Protect Emergency Responders from Immigration Enforcement During Disaster Response," and co-introduced a bill "to Prevent FAFSA Data From Being Used for Immigration Enforcement" [S10][S27].
- Her office has held a vigil at and sent letters about conditions in the Tacoma Northwest ICE Processing Center [S27].

### Defense and foreign policy
- Secured provisions in the House FY2026 NDAA, including the ANCHOR for Military Families Act, but voted against the House bill because of floor amendments she said "deny LGBTQ+ service members their rights" [S14].
- Voted against final passage of the FY2026 NDAA conference bill (S. 1071, December 2025), which passed 312-112 [S22].
- In July 2026 voted for the Massie amendment to H.R. 8595 to cut $3.3 billion in annual military aid to Israel (it failed); she said she wants "no more additional funding for offensive weapons" while protecting humanitarian aid [S13][S23].
- Voted for War Powers resolutions to remove U.S. forces from hostilities with Iran (March 2026) and from Lebanon (June 2026), and sponsored an Iran War Powers resolution (H.Con.Res. 102) [S25][S26][S10].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- States a "100% lifetime pro-choice voting record" and lists state laws she worked on, including Reproductive Health Access for All (2019), extended postpartum coverage and crisis-pregnancy-center regulation (2024) [S7].
- Her health-care page cites expanding access to "comprehensive reproductive healthcare and gender affirming care" [S4].
- Voted against the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act (H.R. 28) and the Born-Alive Abortion Survivors Protection Act (H.R. 21) in January 2025 [S18][S19].
- Endorsed by Planned Parenthood Action Fund and Equality PAC [S1][S2].

### Parents and schools
- Sponsored the Protecting Student Privacy Act (H.R. 9134, June 2026); the bill text was not reviewed [S10]. Her education plank centers on affordable college, apprenticeships and "well-compensated teachers" [S1][S8]. No position found on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs.

### Campaign finance and governance
- Says she has refused corporate PAC money since she first ran for office [S2][S6].
- Used her Oversight Committee seat to challenge the Trump administration [S1][S11]. Sponsored an Office of Management and Budget Inspector General Act (H.R. 2221) [S10].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, repeal of the tax on income over $1 million):** No public position found as of 2026-10-08. Her stated federal tax view is that big business should pay its fair share [S3].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** No public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **I-638 (IL26-638, K-12 girls' athletics):** No public position found as of 2026-10-08. She voted against the federal Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act [S18].

## Record
- 119th Congress votes: against H.R. 1 (July 2025) [S17]; against H.R. 28 and H.R. 21 (January 2025) [S18][S19]; against the Laken Riley Act [S20]; against the HALT Fentanyl Act [S21]; against the FY2026 NDAA conference bill [S22]; for the Massie amendment on Israel military aid [S23]; against H.J.Res. 88 [S24]; for the Lebanon and Iran War Powers resolutions [S25][S26].
- Sponsored bills include the FERRIES Act (H.R. 7774), the ANCHOR for Military Families Act (H.R. 3566), the Defense Community Infrastructure Program Tribal Eligibility Act (H.R. 7710) and a resolution for a National LGBTQ+ Servicemembers and Veterans Day (H.Res. 1572) [S10].

## Endorsements
- Pamphlet: Sen. Patty Murray, Planned Parenthood Action Fund, National Education Association, Washington State Labor Council, League of Conservation Voters Action Fund, Women of Color in Politics, Kitsap County Democrats [S1].
- Campaign site adds Equality PAC, Alliance for Gun Responsibility, Jamestown S'Klallam Tribe, Sheet Metal Workers Local 66, South Kitsap Professional Firefighters (IAFF Local 2876), WFSE/AFSCME Council 28, Majority Leader Andy Billig and former Lands Commissioner Peter Goldmark [S2].

## Scoring notes
- First-term incumbent with six years in the state Senate before Congress [S8][S11].
- Clear progressive-revenue, climate-transition and abortion-rights positions on her campaign site, backed by House votes [S3][S5][S7][S17][S24].
- Departs from many House Democrats on defense: voted against the final FY2026 NDAA and for cutting military aid to Israel [S22][S23].
- Refuses corporate PAC money [S6].
- Opponent Teresa Fox campaigns on public safety, lower taxes, term limits and sex-based school sports [see teresa-fox.md].
