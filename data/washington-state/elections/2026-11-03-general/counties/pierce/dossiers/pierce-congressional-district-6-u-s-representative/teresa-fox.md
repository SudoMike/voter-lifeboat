## Background

Teresa Fox is a lifelong Bremerton resident who has been a Kitsap County precinct committee officer (PCO) for five years and has not held other elected office [S1]. She reports 30 years in regulatory compliance and safety work, including as Environmental, Health & Safety Manager for the Naval Inactive Ships office, regional safety and risk management for Washington Labor & Industries, Olympic College and Icicle Seafoods, and work with the Kitsap County Department of Community Development and Fire Marshal's Office [S1]. Her campaign site describes seven years overseeing safety operations for the Naval Inactive Ships Maintenance Office in Bremerton and a Secret security clearance [S2]. She attended Olympic College and the National Fire Academy [S1][S3].

**Primary result.** She placed second in the certified August 4, 2026 primary with 57,700 votes (25.5%), behind Emily Randall's 132,805 (58.8%) [S4].

## Positions

### Taxes, spending and regulation
- Her site's "Pragmatism, Restraint, Accountability" section says "Income Tax? Climate Commitment Act? Washingtonians are being burdened by big government. It's time to say enough!" [S2].
- Pledges to "Stop runaway government spending," "Keep money in family pockets, not bureaucrat budgets," and cut regulation ("Let businesses operate without constant government interference") [S2].
- Pamphlet closes on "public safety, balanced budgets & economic vitality" [S1]. In a July 2026 questionnaire she said voters are concerned about "high taxes and the terrible mismanagement of our public funds, along with fraud, waste, and abuse" [S3].

### Public safety
- Calls public safety "a paramount duty of the government" and pledges resources for local law enforcement and first responders to address "the growing homelessness & drug crises" [S1].
- Proposes a "Federal Public Safety Floor": direct federal incentives for local agencies to hire and retain officers; cites Washington ranking last in police staffing [S2].
- Wants federal partners to stop the flow of illegal drugs and human trafficking [S1].

### Climate, energy and environment
- Calls for "practical, science-based stewardship" of waters and forests "rather than neglectful closure," and supports timber and maritime industries [S1].
- Opposes federal mandates that prioritize "unproven" maritime technologies over fleet maintenance; says "Rapid electrification of support vessels threatens job stability" [S2].

### Defense
- Pamphlet pledges "building our Naval dominance" [S1]. Her site says "We need ships that can fight today, not social experiments that might float tomorrow" and wants to "Fund actual shipyard workers, not DC consultants" [S2].

### Health care
- Proposes expanded telehealth, federal incentives for doctors who serve rural areas, and keeping community health facilities open, citing OB-GYN and mental-health shortages in Clallam and Mason counties [S2].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Under "Protecting the 50-Year Legacy of Title IX," says "Allowing biological males to compete effectively erases the progress of the last 50 years" and that "girls should not be forced to undress in front of biological males" [S2]. No position found on abortion.

### Parents and schools
- No position found on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs.

### Governance and reform
- Signed the U.S. Term Limits pledge; her site says "Congress should be a service, not a career" [S2].
- Calls herself "a citizen volunteer" and says the district needs "a pragmatic steward, not a political extremist" [S1].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, repeal of the tax on income over $1 million):** No public position on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. Her site lists the income tax as a burden of "big government" [S2].

- **I-638 (IL26-638, K-12 girls' athletics):** No public position on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. Her site backs sex-based eligibility in girls' sports under Title IX [S2].
