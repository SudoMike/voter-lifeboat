## Background
Sharlett Mena (prefers Democratic Party) has been the 29th District State Representative since 2023 and chairs the House State Government & Tribal Relations Committee [S1][S22]. The Legislature lists her in House Position 2, with seats on the Environment & Energy and Finance committees and the Elections Administration & Certification Board [S22]. Her professional experience is as a special assistant at the Washington Department of Ecology, with earlier roles in Congress, the state Senate and the Governor's Office; she holds a B.A. from Washington State University [S1][S3]. Her campaign says that at Ecology she "leads special initiatives to protect clean air and water, implement climate action, and advance environmental justice," including work on PFAS in drinking water and Hanford cleanup funding [S3]. Her campaign describes her as the daughter of immigrant farm workers, raised in the Tri-Cities, and a former board member of Fuse Washington, Progreso and the Planned Parenthood Votes Washington PAC [S3]. Fuse Washington describes the seat as open because Senator Steve Conway is retiring [S25]. She has raised $163,747.78 and spent $59,352.94 for the Senate race (PDC, data updated 2026-10-05) [S24]. Her opponent is fellow Democrat David Anderson [S1].

## Positions

### Taxes and spending
- Lists "the Millionaire's Tax" among wins and says she voted for it; her site says it "relieves the burden on low-income Washingtonians while asking the wealthiest in our state to pay a little bit more" [S1][S4].
- Says she "helped remove a $500 million corporate giveaway from the final version" of the tax bill [S4].
- Says she is "committed to making our tax structure fairer" [S8].
- Supports state budgets that fund food assistance, K-12 education and childcare [S4][S5].

### Housing
- Says she "voted to cap rent increases so landlords can't price you out of your own housing" [S4][S12].
- Lists rent stabilization among legislative wins [S1].

### Public safety
- No platform statement on policing or crime found; see the Record section for her Initiative 2113 vote [S21].

### Climate, energy and technology
- Her agenda includes stopping a federal restart of coal power at Centralia and ensuring data centers "minimize climate, utility ratepayer, and water impacts" [S6].
- Her site says she voted for guardrails on new data centers (HB 2515), for ending data-center refurbishment tax breaks (SB 6231), and for a state Electric Transmission Authority (SB 6355) [S7].
- Says she sponsored the 2026 statewide utility-bill assistance program (HB 1903) and the ban on heat-wave utility shutoffs (HB 1329) [S7].

### Immigration
- Calls federal immigration enforcement practices "illegal, unconstitutional, immoral, and deeply un-American" and supports state challenges to them [S8].
- Says she sponsored and passed the Immigrant Workers' Protection Act [S9], and lists the ban on deploying other states' National Guard in Washington without consent (HB 1321) as an accomplishment [S3].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Her education agenda includes "an inclusive education policy that protects LGBTQ+ students"; her site says she "opposed measures that would restrict protections for LGBTQ+ students, including proposals affecting school athletics" [S5].
- Former board member of Planned Parenthood Votes Washington PAC; endorsed by Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates [S3][S10].

### Parents and schools
- Her site says she "advocated for inclusive, nondiscriminatory school environments when education issues arose in broader civil rights or parental rights debates" [S5].
- Supports "full, fair, and equitable funding across school districts" and more resources for high-need districts [S5].

### Elections and labor
- Says she passed the Washington Voting Rights Act 2.0 (HB 1048) and a preclearance law (HB 1710) [S11].
- Supports collective bargaining rights for agricultural workers and reports a 100% Washington State Labor Council rating as of February 2026 [S9].
