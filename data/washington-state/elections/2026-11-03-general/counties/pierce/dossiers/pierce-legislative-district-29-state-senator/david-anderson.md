## Background
David Anderson (prefers Democratic Party) reports no elected experience [S1]. After graduating from Kentwood High School in the Kent School District in 2006 he worked about 11 years at Costco, moving from carts and cashiering to the membership counter [S1][S4]. Since 2018 he has owned Sumner Ninja, an American Ninja Warrior-style training gym for youth and adults [S1][S4]. His site says he grew up in Kent in a lower-middle-class family and describes himself as "not a politician" [S2][S4]. The PDC lists his Senate committee with $0 raised and $0 spent (data updated 2026-09-17) [S7]. His opponent is State Representative Sharlett Mena [S1].

The primary-election dossier described him as a Tacoma Community College STEM professor. Neither the Edition 09 statement nor his campaign site supports that description, so it is not carried forward [S1][S4].

## Positions

### Taxes and spending
- Calls budget management his "primary focus" and says "Olympia has a spending issue," citing revenue growth of 7.5% against spending growth of 10% [S1].
- His site headline: "I am the correct choice to stop out-of-control spending"; he says he will "fight to balance the budget, control state spending" [S2][S6].
- On the millionaires' tax: "So much is wrong with this bill. First, it violates the state constitution." He writes, "I am for taxing the rich," but says that once an income tax stands "the threshold will only get lower" [S5].
- The pamphlet says the millionaires' tax "will increase revenue" but that the state must return to a fiscally responsible budget and rebuild the rainy-day fund [S1].
- Would cap property tax on homes bought before 2000 and worth less than $1 million, and raise property tax on homes and condos worth more than $1 million [S3].
- Would extend a car-tab fee statewide to fund transit, rather than only in King and Pierce counties [S3][S5].
- Questions where more than $100 million in homelessness-prevention spending went [S3].

### Public safety
- Says social workers, not police, should handle mental-illness calls, with police accompanying them when appropriate [S1][S3].
- Says "laws that prevent police from doing their jobs are getting in the way of public safety" and wants police able to act on car theft, squatting, retail theft and break-ins [S1][S3].
- Supports treatment and rehabilitation over prison, and involuntary facilities for people who have committed a violent crime or are a danger to themselves or others [S3].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Writes that "the more we teach kids that being LGBTQ is ok, the more our youth will grow to respect those they see as different" and that "the more protection we can place on vulnerable communities the safer our society will become" [S3].
- Says he will fight for the rights of LGBTQ+ people and "woman's right to bodily autonomy" [S4][S5].
- Names Christian nationalism and the Heritage Foundation as concerns and supports separation of church and state [S5].

### Parents and schools
- Wants more K-6 help so students leave elementary school able to read, write and do math, and would make PE, music and art mandatory [S5][S6].

### Technology
- "No data center will be built in Washington if I am elected," citing water, energy and heat impacts [S5].

### Environment and infrastructure
- Public land should stay public, with no sales to loggers; wants vulnerable levees inspected and repaired [S3].
- Proposes a WSDOT pothole team and more bridge inspectors [S3].

### Other
- Would provide protection from price gouging for small businesses [S6].
