## Background

Marilyn Strickland has represented Washington's 10th Congressional District since 2021 and was Mayor of Tacoma from 2010 to 2017 [S1]. She was also President and CEO of the Seattle Metropolitan Chamber of Commerce and served on the Sound Transit board [S1]. Born in Seoul and raised in Tacoma, she describes herself as an "Army brat" and as the first African-American and Korean-American woman elected to Congress [S2]. She holds a BA from the University of Washington and an MBA from Clark Atlanta University [S1]. She sits on the House Armed Services and Transportation and Infrastructure committees, is Chief Deputy Whip in the House Democratic Caucus, Secretary of the Congressional Black Caucus, Whip of the New Democrat Coalition and chair of its National Security Task Force [S2][S4]. She chairs ASPIRE PAC, the political arm of the Congressional Asian Pacific American Caucus [S2].

**Primary result.** She placed first in the certified August 4, 2026 primary with 73,689 votes (47.3%). Republican Chris D. Chung was second with 48,953 (31.4%); Democrats Alex Scheel (10.3%) and Adam Arafat (9.4%) and two others followed [S11].

## Positions

### Taxes, spending and costs
- Says she secured $50 million for local jobs, housing, main streets, pedestrian safety, bridges, transit and clean water, including funding for police and firefighters [S1].
- Says she opposes "Trump's tariffs, his cuts to health care and food assistance" [S1].
- At an August 2026 Lakewood roundtable, discussed reinstating ACA subsidies and preserving and expanding SNAP, WIC, LIHEAP and housing-assistance programs [S8].
- Voted against final passage of H.R. 1, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 3, 2025) [S12]. In 2023 she voted for the Fiscal Responsibility Act, in a statement headed "Vote to Avoid Economic Crisis" [S10].

### Health care
- Pledges to "protect Medicare, and make health care more affordable" and to resist "extreme attempts to take away health care" [S3].

### Climate and energy
- Says "In Congress, I'm working to boost renewable energy" [S3]. Endorsed by the League of Conservation Voters and Sierra Club [S1].
- Voted against H.J.Res. 88 (May 2025), which disapproved the EPA waiver for California's vehicle-emission standards [S19].

### Public safety
- Voted for the HALT Fentanyl Act (H.R. 27, February 2025) [S16].
- Pamphlet cites securing funding for police and firefighters [S1].

### Immigration
- Voted against the Laken Riley Act (S. 5, January 2025) [S15].

### Defense and foreign policy
- Serves on the Armed Services subcommittees on Military Personnel and Readiness [S4].
- Voted for final passage of the FY2026 NDAA conference bill (S. 1071, December 2025) [S17].
- Voted against the July 2026 Massie amendment to H.R. 8595 to cut military aid to Israel [S18].
- In June 2025 said "Israel is the eternal homeland of the Jewish people and has the right to defend herself" and that "Iran must be stopped from developing nuclear weapons" [S5]. Later that month she said President Trump's strikes on Iran "dangerously escalated tensions" and called for "multinational diplomacy" [S6].
- In a March 2026 Armed Services hearing she asked whether operations against Iran were consistent with the National Defense Strategy's focus on China, and said "nothing says distracted like killing the leader of a foreign nation without even a hint of strategy or planning" [S7].
- Voted for the March 2026 War Powers resolution on Iran and against the June 2026 War Powers resolution on Lebanon [S21][S20].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Member liaison of the Reproductive Freedom Caucus and member of the Congressional Equality Caucus [S4]. Her September 2026 newsletter reports reintroducing the Abortion is Health Care Everywhere Act [S9].
- Voted against the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act (H.R. 28) and the Born-Alive Abortion Survivors Protection Act (H.R. 21) in January 2025 [S13][S14].
- Endorsed by Planned Parenthood [S1].

### Parents and schools
- No position found on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs. Her priorities mention training students for careers in nursing, health care, teaching and the building trades [S3].

### Military families and veterans
- Says she has led bipartisan legislation on food insecurity and affordable housing for military families [S3].

## Record
- 119th Congress votes: against H.R. 1 [S12]; against H.R. 28 and H.R. 21 [S13][S14]; against the Laken Riley Act [S15]; for the HALT Fentanyl Act [S16]; for the FY2026 NDAA conference bill [S17]; against the Massie amendment on Israel military aid [S18]; against H.J.Res. 88 [S19]; against the Lebanon War Powers resolution and for the Iran War Powers resolution [S20][S21].
