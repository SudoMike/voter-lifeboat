## Background
Damon Townsend was Pierce County Elections Supervisor from 2014 to 2019 and Interim Elections Manager in Clallam and Cowlitz counties during the 2020 presidential election [S1]. He describes two decades in telecommunications and critical-systems operations, including CenturyLink field-operations management and current work as Network Operations Supervisor at Lightcurve, and holds a LEAN Green Belt [S1][S2]. He says he was a Secretary of State trainer on voter registration and led the modernization of Pierce County's tabulation system [S1]. He holds an MBA from Western Governors University and a B.S. in accounting and business administration [S4]. He was born in Puyallup and lives in the Gig Harbor area [S1]. He also ran for Pierce County Auditor in 2022, when Tacoma Weekly reported he was running as an independent and said he would not associate with any party [S4]. In 2026 he says he is a Cascade Party member and serves on that party's board [S1].

## Positions
- **Office operations and cost:** says modernized workflows will bring "stronger security, more transparency, and lower cost," with savings coming "from process, not headcount" and AI used "as a tool for staff rather than a replacement for accountability" [S1].
- **Transparency and accountability:** proposes public dashboards and open data on processing times, signature acceptance rates, licensing wait times and recording turnaround, plus annual plain-language reporting [S1].
- **Service across the county:** wants better remote access to recording and licensing and auditor services that travel to rural and peninsula communities [S1].
- **County structure:** started the Peninsula County Exploratory Committee in February 2026 and says he is working on both forming a new county and reforming the existing one [S1].
- **Partisanship:** says the office is nonpartisan by charter and that he discloses his Cascade Party membership rather than "quietly belonging to a major party in private" [S1].
- **2022 questionnaire (four years old; given for a different cycle):** answered yes to "tax reform and progressive taxes as wealth increases," adding that the auditor's office has little influence on tax reform [S5]; answered no to robust investment in public/subsidized housing, saying he supports subsidized housing as a bridge but favors moving people into home ownership [S5]; answered yes to higher developer impact fees [S5]; answered yes to "local investments to address climate change," explaining it as cutting waste, energy use and cost, with less paper at the elections office as his example [S5]; and said election laws need "administrative cleanup, but no major overhaul" [S5].
