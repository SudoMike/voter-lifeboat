## Background

James Armstrong describes himself as a U.S. Marine Corps veteran with a 26-year legal career as a "veteran trial attorney, Judge Pro Tempore, and Presiding Judge" [S1]. He lists a J.D. from Seattle University School of Law and a B.A. from Western Washington University [S1].

Identity. The VoteWA filing gives only a Lakewood address and a Gmail campaign address, and no campaign website was found. The identification rests on these points:
- A District Court judge in Pierce County must be a Washington-admitted lawyer [S12].
- The WSBA directory's search for "James Armstrong" returns one lawyer, James Wesley Armstrong (WSBA No. 30408, admitted November 6, 2000). His profile lists judicial service as a "WA Municipal Court" judge from January 27, 2025 to December 31, 2025 [S4]. That matches the Bremerton Municipal Court presiding judgeship the candidate describes for 2025 [S1][S2].
- His firm site describes James W. Armstrong, Jr. as a Marine veteran who earned his J.D. from Seattle University in 2000, matching the candidate's statement [S1][S5].
- His 31st LD Democrats questionnaire lists his home legislative district as the 28th [S2].
- His PDC committee address is in Lakewood [S9].

Legal practice. His WSBA profile and firm site describe a solo practice, Armstrong Law Offices, P.S., in Kent/Auburn, focused on workers' compensation, Social Security disability and personal injury [S4][S5]. He served on the WSBA Board of Governors (at-large) from 2011 to 2014, including as Board of Governors representative to the Diversity, Equity & Inclusion Council [S4]. He says he served as president of the Loren Miller Bar Association and of the Washington State Bar Foundation, and took part in youth outreach such as the Youth & Law Forum [S2].

Judicial service. He says he has been a judge pro tem for four years in multiple jurisdictions. He also says he was appointed Presiding Judge of the single-judge Bremerton Municipal Court from January to December 2025 [S2]. In that role he reports [S1][S2]:
- Full responsibility for the court's budget, personnel and operations.
- Restructuring staff "to restore public trust."
- Presiding over its Therapeutic Court.
- Having the court's rights advisements translated into Mam for Mam-speaking defendants.

He was not a candidate for that seat in November 2025. Thomas (Tom) E. Weaver and Steve King were on the Bremerton Municipal Court judge ballot [S10].

Only two candidates filed for Position 8, so the race did not appear on the August primary ballot.

## Positions

Judicial approach:
- Says a judge "must be fair, impartial, and have sound judicial temperament," and that he would "command a fair, orderly, and efficient courtroom" [S1][S2].
- On backlogs, says judges should be "more discerning about the number of continuances granted." He proposes a designated pool of pro tem judges to hear trial-ready cases by agreement of the parties [S2].
- On equity, says "judges have the authority to ensure equal justice for BIPOC without Judicial reforms." He emphasizes sentencing consistency across "similar fact scenarios" and judges noticing and calling out inconsistencies [S2].
- Favors keeping remote hearings, citing travel, childcare and work burdens on court users [S2].

Criminal-justice formation, bail and diversion:
- His formation is civil practice (workers' compensation, disability and personal injury), not prosecution or public defense [S4][S5].
- Says his priority is "to emphasize accountability and rehabilitation in the form of substance use treatment, mental health counseling, anger management, DV-MRT" [S2].
- Says "imposing a sentence or accepting a guilty plea without an acknowledgement that other issues contributed ignores what may have been a central component of the behavior" [S2].
- Supports restorative justice and addressing "the underlying issue... and not just the imposition of an in-custody sentence" [S2].
- He presided over the Bremerton Municipal Court's Therapeutic Court in 2025 (self-reported) [S2].
- No sourced statements or rulings on bail or pretrial release were found.

Court funding and fees:
- Says courts "rely too heavily on fees" and that legal financial obligations keep people "in the system, in the system without end in sight" [S2].
