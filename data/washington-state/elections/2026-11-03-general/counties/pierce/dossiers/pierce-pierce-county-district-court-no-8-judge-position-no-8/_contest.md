## What the office does

A district court has jurisdiction, concurrent with the superior court, over misdemeanors and gross misdemeanors committed in the county. Its sentences are capped at a $5,000 fine and one year in jail unless a statute provides otherwise [S1]. It also hears traffic infractions and conducts preliminary hearings as a committing magistrate [S1]. On the civil side, it hears claims up to $100,000 per claimant [S2]. District judges serve four-year terms [S3]. In practice, the judge's discretion lies in pretrial release, sentencing, protection orders and therapeutic-court supervision. Position 8 is a countywide seat, not a geographic district [S6]. The Pierce County court's own pages could not be read (piercecountywa.gov returns a Cloudflare challenge).

## District and race dynamics

- **Open seat.** Judge Jeanette A. Lineberry is listed as a sitting Pierce County District Court judge [S4]. Sven Nelson's statement and campaign describe her as the retiring Position 8 judge, and she endorses him [S5][S6]. Neither candidate is a sitting judge of this court [S4].
- **No primary.** Only two candidates filed, so the race skipped the August primary.
- **Candidates.** The race pairs two different paths to the bench:
  - Sven Nelson: a 30-year Pierce County deputy prosecutor and judge pro tem since 2019 [S5][S6].
  - James Armstrong: a civil practitioner (workers' compensation and disability law), Marine Corps veteran and judge pro tem, who was presiding judge of Bremerton Municipal Court in 2025 [S11].
- **Ratings.** TPCBA rated Nelson "Exceptionally Well Qualified" [S7]. JAJEC rated Armstrong "Exceptionally Well Qualified" for Pierce County District Court [S8]. Neither candidate appears on the other rater's 2026 list [S7][S8].
- **Money.** Nelson reported about $59,000 in contributions; Armstrong about $7,500, with $7,500 in loans [S9].
- **Endorsements.** The 31st LD Democrats endorsed both [S10]. Nelson's campaign lists endorsements from 7 of the 8 sitting District Court judges, 22 Superior Court judges, county executives past and present, police and firefighter unions, and both major county parties [S6]. Armstrong lists none [S11].

## Differentiating issue axes

- **Experience (record vs. renewal).** Both run on courtroom experience. Nelson's is three decades of prosecution plus pro tem service. Armstrong's is a year as a full-time presiding judge of a small municipal court plus pro tem service and a civil practice.
- **Safety (formation).** This is the clearest split. Nelson's formation is prosecution, centered on sexual assault and elder abuse. He frames treatment and community court as compatible with accountability and warns restorative justice must not "minimize accountability." Armstrong's formation is civil. He emphasizes treatment-based rehabilitation (substance use, mental health, DV-MRT), restorative justice and the burden of legal financial obligations. Neither has a documented bail record.
- **Judicial approach.** Evidence is thin for both. Nelson stresses decisions from "the facts, the law, and the record." Armstrong stresses sentencing consistency and says equal justice is achievable "without Judicial reforms."

## Not differentiating

Both promise impartiality, dignity for court users and continued remote-hearing options, and both are critical of fee-funded courts. Party and club endorsements are recorded as facts only; they are not evidence of how either would rule.
