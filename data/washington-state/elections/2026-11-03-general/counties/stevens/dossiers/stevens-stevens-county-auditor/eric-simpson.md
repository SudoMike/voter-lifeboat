## Background

Eric Simpson grew up on a family dairy farm near Springdale and lives on a small farm south of Chewelah; he and his wife are raising ten children (S1, S2). He worked eight years at ANBO Manufacturing in Arden as a laborer, welder and production lead, and eight years as a heavy equipment operator at Simpson Logging Inc.; he is a Washington Contract Loggers Association accredited logger (S1). He was home-schooled and holds a GED (S1). His elected experience is two terms as precinct committee officer for Valley 2, and he chairs the Election Integrity Committee of the Stevens County Republican Central Committee, which he formed (S1, S2). He prefers the Republican Party (S1).

He says he does "not have a professional background in the various duties the auditor carries out," and at the July forum said "I don't have the experience Larsen has" (S2, S4). In the August 4 primary he received 4,744 votes (40.24%) (S6).

## Positions

### Elections administration
- Says his primary reason for running is "the catastrophe of Vote-by-Mail that is not currently being addressed," and that legislation from Olympia results from voters no longer being able to hold representatives accountable (S2).
- Proposes "proactive cleaning of the voter list," "restoring community involvement by implementing hand counting of ballots," and prompt responses to public records requests (S2).
- At the July forum: "Maybe we need to hand count ballots to increase accuracy. We need to clean the voter registration database"; he said 200 Stevens County residents are registered in more than one county. Asked about stopping mail-in ballots and in-person voting, he said, "We should return to hand counting. We would need a large team, and it would result in more transparency" (S4).
- His statement promises "restoring transparency, community involvement, and verifiable results in our elections" (S1).
- Says Larsen "hasn't shown any interest in addressing any of these issues, but has staunchly defended and praised our current election system" (S2).

### Office management and spending
- Commits to "partnering with Stevens County officials to ensure responsible stewardship of your money" (S1).
- Says people working in and around the courthouse have described to him "the difficult environment, lack of job execution, and poor financial stewardship originating from the auditor's office," and promises to restore a unified effort among county offices (S2). These claims are his and were not checked.
- Cites owning his home outright with no debt on a single income as evidence that he understands cash flow, budgeting and prioritizing spending (S2).
- Says he would complete all required training (S4).
