## Background

Lori Larsen, of Chewelah, has been Stevens County Auditor since 2018 (S1, S5). She won the office in 2018 with 61.60% against Democrat Tim Gray and was re-elected unopposed in 2022 (S11, S12). She prefers the Republican Party (S1).

Her statement lists a Chewelah School Board seat (2017-2019), elected leadership positions in the Washington State Association of County Auditors (2021 to present) and in the Stevens County Republicans (2013-2019), and current membership on the federal Election Assistance Commission Standards Board and the Washington State Property Deed Fraud Task Force (S1). Before taking office she was administrator and legislative advocate for an online learning and school choice program (2003-2013); at the July forum she said she co-founded a school choice program in Valley (S1, S5). She holds a Ph.D. in public policy and administration (2023), a master's in counseling and a bachelor's in psychology (S1).

In the August 4 primary she received 6,860 votes (58.19%) (S10).

## Positions

### Elections administration
- Describes the auditor as "an executive-branch officer charged with administering elections as required by law, not making or resisting policy." If the Legislature or Congress changed the law, for example to require in-person re-registration with proof of citizenship, her office would implement it, with staffing and budget consequences for the commissioners to address (S5).
- Says her office audits every election, ballots are paper and auditable, and voters can already vote in person at her office (S5).
- Says the office performs voter-list maintenance daily, using Department of Licensing data, vital statistics for deceased voters and multi-state checks for dual registrations; voters are responsible for their own registration, and state law provides a formal challenge process (S5).
- Co-wrote a 2025 guest column with Pend Oreille County Auditor Marianne Nichols, co-signed by 23 other election officials, arguing that Washington's vote-at-home system is secure and that returning to polling-place voting "is not necessary, not efficient and not more secure" and would add cost and reduce access (S6).
- In 2024 proposed a $220,000 automated mail sorter to be paid mostly from state and federal grants; the commissioners declined it, citing residents' concerns (including a petition for in-precinct hand counting) and economic concerns. Larsen said it was time for the county to "get the tools and the equipment" its size needs (S7).
- Has expanded ballot drop boxes, including a new box in Kettle Falls, which she called one of her highest priorities for 2026 (S14).
- Her statement emphasizes "election integrity and security, clean audits, precise financial reconciliation, and voter verification procedures for every lawful ballot received" (S1).

### Office management and costs
- Says she has improved services and efficiency "while keeping costs low" (S1), and describes a "careful, compliant and conservative approach to taxpayer resources" (S4).
- Lists expanded online access to historical recorded documents, a free property-fraud notification program, staff cross-training, licensing by appointment and expanded licensing subagents in Kettle Falls, Colville, Nine Mile Falls and (reopening) Chewelah (S1, S3, S4).
- Says budget cuts reduced licensing staff from five to two, which led to the appointment system for vehicle tabs; walk-in service continues at subagent locations (S5).
- Says the office has filed every annual financial report on time with no financial findings (S5).

## Record

- Auditor since 2018 (S1).
- In November 2025 the Secretary of State reported that automatic voter registrations sent by state agencies had not appeared in Stevens and Ferry counties' work queues from July 2024 to October 2025; 924 eligible people across both counties might have remained unregistered. Larsen's office said a VoteWA permissions problem hid the records, and she urged voters to keep their own registrations current (S8).
- In June 2026 she petitioned for a temporary anti-stalking protection order against Commissioner Mark Burrows after Burrows and Simpson campaign signs were found inside her property's fence line; an Adams County judge denied the temporary order on July 7 without a hearing, finding Burrows "has not engaged in acts of stalking," and gave her 14 days to amend (S9).
