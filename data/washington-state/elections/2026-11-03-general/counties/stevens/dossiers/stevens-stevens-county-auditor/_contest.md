## What the office does
The county auditor records deeds and other documents, keeps the county's accounts against the treasurer's, and prepares the county's annual financial statements (S2). The auditor is also the county's election officer, responsible for voter registration and for conducting elections (S3). Larsen's statement adds vehicle and vessel licensing to that list (S1).

## Race dynamics
Two-term Auditor Lori Larsen, in office since 2018, faces Eric Simpson, a logger and welder who chairs the Stevens County Republican Central Committee's Election Integrity Committee (S1). Both prefer the Republican Party (S1). In the August 4 primary Larsen received 6,860 votes (58.19%) and Simpson 4,744 (40.24%) (S4).

The race is about how elections are run. Simpson calls vote-by-mail a "catastrophe," and wants hand counting of ballots and proactive cleaning of the voter list. Larsen says the auditor administers the law as written, describes Washington's vote-at-home system as secure, and runs on her record and qualifications (S1, S5; candidate dossiers). Simpson says he does not have Larsen's experience (S5). By its October reports Simpson's campaign had raised $38,740 to Larsen's $6,900.54 (S7).

The race also carries a dispute between Larsen and Commissioner Mark Burrows. In June 2026 Larsen sought an anti-stalking protection order against Burrows over campaign signs for Burrows and Simpson found inside her property's fence line. A visiting judge denied the temporary order on July 7, finding Burrows "has not engaged in acts of stalking" (S6).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience/record:** an eight-year incumbent with a doctorate in public policy and statewide auditor-association roles, against a first-time candidate who says he lacks professional background in the office's duties.
2. **Reform (election administration):** Simpson's campaign centers on changing how elections are run (hand counts, voter-list purges, ending reliance on vote-by-mail); Larsen defends the current vote-at-home system and says she will implement whatever law the Legislature or Congress sets.
3. **Spending:** Simpson promises "responsible stewardship" and alleges poor financial stewardship in the office; Larsen cites clean audits, on-time financial reports and keeping costs low.
4. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives: none found for either candidate.
