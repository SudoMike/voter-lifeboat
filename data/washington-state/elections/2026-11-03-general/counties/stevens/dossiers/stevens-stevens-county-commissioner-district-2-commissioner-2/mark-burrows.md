## Background

Mark Burrows, of Chewelah, says he has been a Stevens County commissioner for 5.5 years (S1). He won the District 2 seat in the November 2022 general with 13,113 votes (65.14%) against Carl R. Taylor (S2). He is a U.S. Air Force veteran and noncommissioned officer and a 30-year law-enforcement veteran who retired as Chewelah's police chief (S1). He lists practical courses in budgeting, management and executive leadership (S1). He prefers the Republican Party (S1).

In the August 4 primary, voted in District 2 only, he received 3,493 votes (76.77%) (S3). He did not answer questions at the July candidate forum in Colville (S6). His campaign website listed in the voters' guide (retaincommissionermarkburrows.com) showed a parked-domain page on 2026-10-08, so no campaign site was reviewed.

## Positions

### County budget and staffing
- "Being a good steward of your tax dollars means focusing on efficiency and accountability in all levels of county operations" (S1).
- Says employee turnover is costly and supports "competitive pay and benefits to attract and retain quality county employees" (S1).

### State government and regulation
- Goals include working "alongside other counties resisting overregulation," working with neighboring counties on efficiency and economic prosperity in Northeast Washington, and working with legislators and regional sheriffs "to fight unconstitutional legislation from the State" (S1).
- Places "a very high value on protecting private property rights" (S1).

## Record

- November 2025: cast the lone vote against the county's new Veterans Relief Fund policy, which passed 2-1. He said the policy felt rushed, that he expected a line-by-line review with the Veterans Advisory Board, and that "the current system still works" (S4).
- In 2024 the board of commissioners declined the auditor's proposed $220,000 automated mail sorter, citing residents' concerns and "economic concern within the county"; the article reports the board's decision, not individual votes (S8).
- June-July 2026: Auditor Lori Larsen sought an anti-stalking protection order against him over campaign signs for Burrows and auditor candidate Eric Simpson placed inside her property's fence line. He denied placing the sign, said he went to retrieve it after learning law enforcement had been called, and told a deputy the Larsens blocked his vehicle and threatened him. A visiting judge denied the temporary order on July 7, finding he "has not engaged in acts of stalking" (S5).
