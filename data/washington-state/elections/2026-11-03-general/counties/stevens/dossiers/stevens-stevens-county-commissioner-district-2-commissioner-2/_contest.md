## What the office does
The three county commissioners are Stevens County's legislative authority: they adopt the county budget, set policy, and provide for courthouses, jails, roads and other county facilities (S2). Stevens is a non-charter county, so commissioners are nominated by district in the primary and elected by the voters of the whole county in the general (S3); in 2020 both commissioner races appeared on all 58 Stevens precincts (S5).

## Race dynamics
Commissioner Mark Burrows, a retired Chewelah police chief who says he has served 5.5 years, faces first-time candidate Jack Griffin Jr. of Valley, a retiree from corporate, tech and healthcare work (S1). Burrows prefers the Republican Party; Griffin states no party preference (S1). In the district-only primary Burrows received 3,493 votes (76.77%), Griffin 779 (17.12%) and write-ins 278 (6.11%) (S4); write-in candidate Gary Roach, a Navy veteran and former American Legion state commander, had campaigned for the seat (S7). Neither Burrows nor Griffin answered questions at the July candidate forum in Colville (S6).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience/record:** an incumbent and 30-year law-enforcement veteran against a first-time candidate who stresses private-sector problem solving.
2. **Spending/accountability:** Burrows talks of efficiency, accountability and competitive pay to retain county staff; Griffin of doing "a great deal with limited resources."
3. **State/county relations:** Burrows promises to resist "overregulation" and "unconstitutional legislation from the State" with other counties and sheriffs; Griffin's statement does not address state policy.
4. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives: none found for either candidate.
