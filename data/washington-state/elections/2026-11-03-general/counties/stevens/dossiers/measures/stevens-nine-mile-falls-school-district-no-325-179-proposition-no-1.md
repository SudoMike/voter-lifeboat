## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would replace Nine Mile Falls School District's educational programs and operations (EP&O) levy, which expires in 2027, with a three-year levy collected in 2028-2030, under Resolution No. 11-26 (S1). It pays for costs the state does not fund: staffing and supports for safety, music and fine arts, nurses and counselors, class size reduction, athletics and extracurricular activities, enrichment and summer activities, transportation, maintenance and operations, technology, nutrition services and special education (S1).

The district spans Spokane and Stevens counties; in Stevens it covers the southern tip around Suncrest and Nine Mile Falls (WA DOR SCH2025 `179J`; builder notes). Superintendent Jeff Baerwald says the levy is 17% of the district's budget, and breaks each levy dollar into 45 cents for teachers, support staff and other supplemental positions, 42 cents for extracurricular and enrichment activities, 10 cents for maintenance and operations, and 3 cents for technology (S2).

## Cost

Ballot title figures (S1):

| Collection year | Estimated rate per $1,000 | Levy amount |
|---|---|---|
| 2028 | $2.10 | $4,354,837 |
| 2029 | $2.10 | $4,428,827 |
| 2030 | $2.10 | $4,504,075 |

The current levy collects $2.27 per $1,000 in 2026 (S2). If both of the district's November propositions pass, the district estimates a combined school rate of $2.48 per $1,000 from 2028, against $2.65 in 2026 (S2).

## Fiscal mechanics / what it replaces

- A replacement of the existing EP&O levy at a lower estimated rate ($2.10 against $2.27) (S1, S2).
- **The April 2026 vote** was a separate $6.2 million capital levy, which failed with 45.2% on election night (S3); Stevens County voters in the district rejected it 990 yes (45.96%) to 1,164 no (S4). The statement against's "90 days" refers to that vote (S1, S3).

## Arguments for

No statement for was received by the deadline; the guide lists the committee "Supporting Nine Mile Students" (Ann Fackenthall) (S1). Baerwald: "Without that, what we are able to offer kids looks a lot different" (S2).

## Arguments against

From the statement against, by "NO Means NO! Voters Said NO Just 90 Days Ago!" (David Joplin) (S1):

- "The Voters said No only 90 days before this Proposal was submitted!" and the proposal was "written last year as a contingency."
- The levy wording "permits NMFSD to spend on anything 'not funded by the State;' an end-around on voters' mandate."
- "Out of 294 Washington School Districts, NMFSD is 50th in income, but 235th in dollars/student!" (the committee's figures, not checked).
- The district "committed $40,000 taxpayer dollars" to the failed April measure.
