## What it does

Proposition No. 2 would authorize a new six-year capital levy, collected 2027-2032, under Resolution No. 12-26 (S1). The money would replace a failing roof and condemned portable classrooms at Lakeside High School and modernize "aged security systems, fire systems, facilities, and infrastructure District-wide" (S1). The statement for adds an updated fire alarm system and video surveillance, with any remaining funds for HVAC controls (S1).

Superintendent Jeff Baerwald said the high school's asphalt-shingle roof, installed after a 2008 failure, was "engineered incorrectly" and has leaked into the wood shop and gym; the two-classroom portable, installed in 1996 without a slab, has dry rot and is condemned (S2).

## Cost

- Estimated $0.38 per $1,000 of assessed value each year: $774,853 in 2027, $788,018 in 2028, $801,407 in 2029, $815,023 in 2030, $828,871 in 2031 and $842,954 in 2032 (S1), about $4.85 million in all.
- The district says it replaces a 20-year construction bond, paid off at the end of 2026, that costs the same 38 cents, so the rate would not rise (S1, S2). With Proposition No. 1, the district projects a combined school rate of $2.48 per $1,000 from 2028, against $2.65 in 2026 (S2).

## Fiscal mechanics / history

- **The April 2026 vote.** A larger $6.2 million capital levy (about 58 cents per $1,000, for a roof, track resurfacing, boilers and cameras) failed with 45.2% on election night (S3); Stevens County voters in the district rejected it 990 yes (45.96%) to 1,164 no (S4). The district cut projects for this version and is pursuing a grant for a failed boiler (S2).
- The ballot title does not call it a replacement levy (S1); the Spokesman-Review calls it "a new tax" that "would be offset" by the expiring bond (S2).

## Arguments for

From the statement for, by Future Focused For Nine Mile (Melanie Reynolds, Devin Bauer, Candace Haggard) (S1):

- It "will maintain the current local tax rate by replacing a Bond, which expires January 1, 2027, with a new six-year Capital Levy at the same rate."
- After community concerns about taxes, the board "prioritized only the district's most critical facility needs and removed several previously proposed projects."
- Student safety: fire alarm and video surveillance updates for compliance with state school-safety requirements.
- Rebuttal: the revised proposition removes projects, replaces an expiring bond at the same rate, and NMFSD revenue per student is 8.2% below the state average (per OSPI, as cited).

## Arguments against

From the statement against, by "NO Means NO! Voters Said NO Just 90 Days Ago!" (David Joplin) (S1):

- "The Voters said No only 90 days before this Proposal was submitted!"
- The six-year version is "far more vague about 'projects'" and gives the district "carte blanche."
- The district spent "$40,000 taxpayer dollars" on the April measure, and "the Proposal budgets for recovering these sunk costs."
- Rebuttal: the district has operated without the condemned portables for over two years, so they are not "essential."

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: legally a new levy, but it continues the 38-cent rate of a construction bond that expires at the end of 2026, so a yes vote holds the district's rate and a no vote lowers it (S1, S2). Treated as a same-rate replacement: strength 1.
- `spending`: repairs to existing facilities, not expansion; no spending mapping.
- `parental-rights` / `social`: not addressed.
