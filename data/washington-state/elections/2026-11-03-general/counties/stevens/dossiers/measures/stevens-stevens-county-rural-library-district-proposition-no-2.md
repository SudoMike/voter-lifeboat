## What it does

Proposition No. 2 would restore the Stevens County Rural Library District's regular property tax levy from $0.27 to $0.44 per $1,000 of assessed value, set in 2026 for collection in 2027, under Resolution No. 02-2026 (S1). The 2027 levy amount then becomes the base for later levy limits under chapter 84.55 RCW, so growth after that is held to the usual 1% a year (S1). The district, which operates as Libraries of Stevens County, runs seven libraries (S1, S2).

The district says the reset would sustain its libraries for about ten years, funding longer hours, staffing, programs and materials; rejecting it "would diminish funding, requiring additional reductions to library operations and services" (S1). The district's original voter-approved rate was $0.50 (S1).

**Who votes.** The district is not county-wide. In the district's 2014 levy vote the measure was on every Stevens precinct except Colville 1-7 and Kettle Falls 1-2 (S5), and WA DOR's 2025 library-district layer likewise leaves out the two cities (builder notes). Voters in the Cities of Colville and Kettle Falls do not vote on it.

## Cost

- $0.27 to $0.44 per $1,000 of assessed value for 2027 collection, an increase of 17 cents (S1, S2).
- The statement for estimates "about $4.25/month (on a $300,000 home)," about $51 a year (S1). Spring coverage put the levy at about $1.1 million (S2, S3).

## Fiscal mechanics / history

- **The April 2026 vote.** The district put the same rate ($0.27 to $0.44) to voters on April 28, 2026 as Proposition No. 1; it failed, 5,114 yes (46.15%) to 5,968 no (53.85%) (S2, S4). This is the second attempt.
- Library Director Amanda Six said before the April vote that the district would have to cut $250,000 without a levy, and that it had already cut 100 open hours a week countywide since 2011. The district's previous levy request, 12 years earlier, failed by 600 votes, and the last approved levy was 28 years ago (S2).
- After the April count, Six said the district would avoid layoffs by leaving positions unfilled and asking for voluntary hour reductions, and would cut the materials budget (S3).
- The statement for says the state's 1% cap raised the district's levy by $17,100 in 2026 and that the library tax is about 4% of a property tax bill (S1).

## Arguments for

From the statement for, by Sue Poe, Michelle Bauer and Joanie Matter (S1):

- "Passing this levy brings back evening and weekend hours"; it would let the library rehire staff "to restore over 100 open hours/week."
- In 2025 residents borrowed $6.2 million of materials, "four times the amount collected in taxes"; 23,367 people used library Wi-Fi and more than 20,000 attended programs.
- Libraries get no state or federal operating funds, and the 1% cap does not keep pace with costs.

The committee Citizens for Libraries reported $1,750 raised as of September 2026 (S6).

## Arguments against

No statement against was submitted to the voters' guide (S1). No organized opposition was found. The April measure failed with 53.85% voting no (S4); Six acknowledged that many residents are on fixed incomes and facing financial strain (S2).

## Endorsements and campaign

None found beyond the committee for (S1, S6).

- `spending`: the stated purpose is restoring hours and staff cut since 2011 (S1, S2), framed as restoring service rather than new programs; not enough for a spending mapping.
