## What the office does
The coroner takes jurisdiction over the bodies of people who die suddenly, violently, under suspicious or unknown circumstances, by suicide, overdose or accident, in jail, or unclaimed, and determines cause and manner of death (S2).

## Race dynamics
The seat is open: Coroner Ramona Colvin, elected in 2022 with 69.33% (S4), is not on the 2026 ballot (S1). Both candidates are death investigators who have worked in the office, and both prefer the Republican Party (S1). Chief Deputy Coroner Johnathan Waldrop runs on continuity with what he calls the office's revitalization under the current administration: national accreditation, federal grants, a balanced budget (S1). Roseanne Ennis Jezerinac, a deputy medicolegal death investigator for the Spokane County Medical Examiner and a former Stevens deputy coroner, runs on prevention and education, more transparency and more autopsy funding (S1). In the August 4 primary Waldrop received 8,298 votes (73.0%) and Jezerinac 2,911 (25.61%) (S3). Jezerinac did not attend the July candidate forum in Colville (S5).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience/record:** the current chief deputy promising to build on the office's direction, against an investigator from the neighboring medical examiner's office promising changes in transparency and budget priorities.
2. **Safety (prevention):** both mention prevention education; Jezerinac makes it the center of her platform (fentanyl, suicide prevention, mental health, the missing and murdered Indigenous people crisis).
3. **Spending:** Waldrop cites a balanced budget and minimal use of taxpayer funds; Jezerinac would shift more of the current budget to autopsy services.
4. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives: none found for either candidate.
