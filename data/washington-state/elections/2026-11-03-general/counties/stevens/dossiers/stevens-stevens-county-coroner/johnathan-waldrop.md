## Background

Johnathan Waldrop, of Addy, is Stevens County's chief deputy coroner (S1). He reports more than 14 years in medical examiner and coroner work, as a forensic autopsy technician, medicolegal death investigator, operations manager, and Stevens County deputy and chief deputy coroner (S1). At the July forum he said he had been with the coroner's office for three and a half years (S2). He holds a B.S. in human development and business from Lee University and histotechnology training from the Indiana University School of Medicine, and is certified by the American Board of Medicolegal Death Investigators and the Washington State Criminal Justice Training Commission (S1). He has never held public office and prefers the Republican Party (S1).

In the August 4 primary he received 8,298 votes (73.0%) (S3).

## Positions

### Office direction
- Says the office has seen "a remarkable revitalization" during his tenure: federal grant funding, repaired partnerships with other agencies, and national accreditation from the International Association of Coroners and Medical Examiners (S1).
- Says the office has maintained "a balanced budget while minimizing the use of taxpayer funds" through in-house strategies (S1).
- Says a community education program and data-sharing platform now support "public awareness, public safety, and prevention to avoidable deaths," and he wants to bolster the education program and bring new people into the field (S1, S2).
- On partnerships with law enforcement and emergency services: "All of the previous fractured relationships have been mended. We are considered last responders" (S2).
- Lists presentations at high schools and colleges, a coroner internship program, service on consumer product safety, child death review and overdose panels, and memorial services for unclaimed remains (S1).
