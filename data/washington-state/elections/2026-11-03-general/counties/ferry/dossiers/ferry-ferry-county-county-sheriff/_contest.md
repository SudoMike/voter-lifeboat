## What the office does

The sheriff runs county law enforcement and the county jail for all of Ferry County, an elected partisan office with a four-year term [S1]. From 2026, state law (SSSB 5974, Laws of 2026, ch. 258) requires sheriff candidates to swear they meet new qualifications, pass a State Patrol background check and obtain Criminal Justice Training Commission certification within nine months of taking office, on pain of the office being declared vacant; a Thurston County Superior Court preliminary injunction against parts of it remains in place [S4].

## Race dynamics

- **Incumbent against a former deputy.** Ray Maycumber, sheriff since January 2015 and a Ferry County peace officer for more than 25 years, faces Sam Olson of Curlew, a former Ferry County deputy and narcotics detective who spent 20 years with the U.S. Border Patrol [S1]. Both prefer the Republican Party [S1].
- **Primary.** Olson led the fully reported August primary, 1,281 votes to 995 [S2]. Maycumber was unopposed in 2022 (2,598 votes) [S3].
- **The state's new sheriff law.** Maycumber calls SSSB 5974 "an outright assault on the rights of the people to elect and retain their Sheriff" and is one of four northeastern Washington sheriffs suing the State over it [S1][S4]. Olson's statement does not mention it [S1].

## Differentiating issue axes

- `experience`: a three-term sheriff running on his record against a challenger who has never held elected office but has 27 years in law enforcement.
- `safety`: Olson proposes "an aggressive approach towards theft and drug abuse"; Maycumber stresses recruitment and retention and the funding shortfall behind it. Both are enforcement-oriented.
- Local control over the sheriff's office (SSSB 5974) is recorded but is not a scored axis for county offices.
