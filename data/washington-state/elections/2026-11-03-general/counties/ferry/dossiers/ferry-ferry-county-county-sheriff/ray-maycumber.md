## Background

- Ferry County Sheriff since January 2015, now finishing his third term [S1].
- Earlier a Republic police officer (2001-2003), Ferry County deputy sheriff (2003-2009) and chief criminal deputy and 911 coordinator (2009-2014) [S1].
- Bachelor's degree in criminal justice from Eastern Washington University; Republic High School, 1991 [S1].
- Says he has been a certified peace officer in Ferry County for more than 25 years and that his family has lived in the county for over 100 years [S1]. Prefers the Republican Party [S1].
- Unopposed in 2022 (2,598 votes) [S4]; trailed Sam Olson in the August 2026 primary, 995 to 1,281 [S3].
- Registered with the PDC under mini reporting (no itemized contributions) [S6]. His general statement repeats his primary statement [S1][S5].

## Positions

### Public safety and the sheriff's office

- Says statewide interest in law-enforcement careers is "the lowest in the nation" and that "our county's inability to allocate funding to attract applicants has created significant challenges in hiring and retention" [S1].
- Lists natural disaster, "predator conflict," the pandemic and strained police-public relations as challenges of his tenure [S1].

### The state's sheriff-qualification law (recorded; local control is not a scored axis for county offices)

- Calls SSSB 5974 "an outright assault on the rights of the people to elect and retain their Sheriff on their terms" and says he is "helping lead the fight against these attacks by taking them to court" [S1].
- He is a respondent, in his individual and official capacities, in the sheriffs' suit against the State over the law's sheriff provisions; Thurston County Superior Court enjoined parts of them on April 29, 2026, and the Supreme Court's commissioner denied the State's motion to stay that injunction [S2].
- Describes himself as "a working Sheriff ... unafraid to push back against the state when needed" who "believes in individual liberty" [S1].

## Record

- Three terms as sheriff, from January 2015 [S1].
- Co-plaintiff with the Spokane, Pend Oreille and Stevens sheriffs in the 2026 challenge to SSSB 5974 [S2].
