## What the office does

Chelan PUD is run by five commissioners: one from each of three commissioner districts, elected to six-year terms, and two elected at large to four-year terms [S3][S4]. The commission sets policy, approves plans, budgets and expenditures, and reviews operations. The district's legal powers, including setting rates and charges for its services, are exercised through the commission [S4]. Candidates on this ballot point to relicensing of the PUD's Rock Island hydropower project, rising grid demand and data-center growth as the issues ahead [S1][S2].

Under RCW 54.12.010(3), only voters in a commissioner district may vote in its primary, but "voters of the entire public utility district may vote at a general election to elect a person as a commissioner of the commissioner district" [S3]. Chelan PUD covers all of Chelan County, so every Chelan County ballot carries this race (VoteWA lists it as "Pud All") [S2]. With only two candidates filed, the race did not appear on the August primary ballot [S6][S8]. The candidate must live in District 1 [S3].

## Race dynamics

This is an open seat. Garry Arseneault was elected to District 1 in 2014 and re-elected unopposed in 2020 (98.95%, the rest write-ins) to a term running through 2026 [S4][S5]. He is still on the commission; in October 2026 he voted, reluctantly, for an agreement under which the PUD will pay Chelan County at least $1.35 million a year from 2027. Commissioner Carnan Bergren opposed it [S7]. Arseneault is not a candidate this year; the two candidates are Colleen Frei and Aaron Young [S1][S2].

Frei is a Central Washington agricultural and water-law attorney and association consultant running for her first elected office. She emphasizes Rock Island relicensing, demand from new technologies, affordable renewable hydropower, and local control and public ownership of the PUD [S1]. Young is a Malaga orchardist, contractor and EMT who served on the county Republican Party's executive board. He emphasizes grid demand, data-center growth, power costs and representation for south-county communities [S1]. As of early October, Frei had raised $14,042.80 [S6] and Young $1,735.00 [S8].
