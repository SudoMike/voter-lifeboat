## Background

Colleen Frei is running for her first elected office; PUD races are nonpartisan and she lists no party [S1][S2]. She is managing partner of JDSA Law, focused on agriculture, water law, business strategy and long-term planning, and a consultant for associations including Washington Winegrowers, the Washington Growers League and the Columbia Basin Development League [S1]. The Wenatchee World reported in December 2023 that the Washington Winegrowers Association named her its executive director effective January 1, 2024 [S7]. She holds a law degree and a B.S. in natural resources ecology and conservation from the University of Idaho [S1].

She says she has spent more than 20 years as a legal advisor in agricultural, irrigation and water law in Central Washington, "including critical work with our local PUD on fisheries, broadband fiber, and community outreach" [S1]. She was appointed to the Community Foundation of North Central Washington's board of trustees in 2025 [S8]. Her listed community service includes Chelan-Douglas Volunteer Attorney Services, the Chelan-Douglas Land Trust, the Washington Native Plant Society, Sustainable NCW and several Wenatchee arts organizations [S1]. She describes herself as a resident raising a family in Chelan County [S1][S3].

## Positions

### Rates, growth and new large loads
- Says the PUD faces "balancing demand from new technologies" and wants "a future of affordable, renewable hydropower" [S1].
- "As large-scale development and tech demands expand, I will advocate for intentional grid modernization that continues to protect utility rates for local families and small businesses" [S4].
- "My focus is to proactively meet growing grid demands while keeping our public power affordable and reliable" [S3].

### Hydropower relicensing and public ownership
- Calls federal relicensing of the Rock Island project "an incredibly important negotiation" and says she will work toward "long-term power rights that favor local owners" [S1][S4].
- Pledges to "protect local control and public ownership of our PUD" and to "stand firm against external overreach" [S1][S4].

### Water, fisheries and environment
- Would use her water-law background to "protect our region's water access against external pressures" [S4].
- Would "champion the PUD's environmental stewardship commitments, safeguarding our rivers and fisheries without compromising our essential power generation capacity" [S4].
- Supports collaborative watershed planning among local stakeholders [S4].

### Finances and governance
- Pledges "rigorous budget oversight, thorough cost-benefit analysis, and fiscally responsible management of ratepayer funds" [S4].
- Wants an "open-door commission" giving each area of the county, "from our town centers to rural valleys," a seat at the table [S4].

### Broadband
- Would continue to advocate for broadband fiber expansion to connect rural neighborhoods [S4].
