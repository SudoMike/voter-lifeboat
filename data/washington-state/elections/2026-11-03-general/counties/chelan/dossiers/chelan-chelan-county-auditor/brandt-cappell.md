## Background

Brandt Cappell is the acting Chelan County Auditor and prefers the Republican Party [S1][S3]. He is a 30-year Chelan County resident, a graduate of Wenatchee High School and WSU, and lives in Wenatchee with his wife and three daughters [S1]. He was 40 when appointed in June 2026 [S6].

Before joining the auditor's office he was a legislative aide in the state House for 12th District legislators, most recently Rep. Keith Goehner [S5][S11]. He put that work at 13 years in 2024 [S10][S11] and at 15 years "serving Chelan County in the Washington State Legislature" in his 2026 statement [S1]. He ran for Chelan County Commissioner District 3 in 2020 and did not advance from the primary. He ran for the seat again in 2024 [S10][S11].

In August 2025, Auditor Skip Moore hired him as the office's first chief deputy auditor, a position Moore had lobbied commissioners to fund. The salary was $90,969 a year [S5]. After Moore retired at the end of April 2026, the Board of Commissioners asked Cappell in June to serve as auditor for the rest of Moore's term. They chose him over former Chelan County Republican chairman Aaron Young [S6]. (Aaron Young, the former party chairman, is a different person from Marty Young, the County Clerk.)

He finished second in the certified August primary with 7,746 votes (39.08%) to Brian Brett's 12,073 (60.92%) [S4].

## Positions

### Office administration and elections
- Lists four priorities: "the highest standards of election integrity and accuracy"; excellent service in recording, vehicle and vessel licensing and marriage licenses; timely and accurate payroll and accounts payable; and to "protect taxpayer dollars through responsible financial oversight and transparency" [S1][S2].
- Says he will "continue the strong tradition of service established by former Auditor Skip Moore while bringing fresh energy," and calls for "approachable, responsive leadership that puts residents and local businesses first" [S1].

### Spending and county government
- His 2026 statement commits him to "responsible financial oversight and transparency" with taxpayer dollars [S1].
- As a 2024 commissioner candidate he said, "I've spent 13 years navigating the bureaucratic mess of state government. I will work to ensure Chelan County government isn't part of the problem" [S10].
- In 2024 he described himself as "kind of a policy wonk" who does not see himself "really as a politician" [S11].

## Record

- Chief Deputy Auditor from August 2025, the first person to hold the position [S5].
- Acting Auditor since June 2026, overseeing 15 employees in elections, recording, licensing, accounts payable, payroll and financial reporting [S1][S6].
- After a system-wide failure over Memorial Day weekend 2026 disrupted document processing, he announced in June that core recording functions were restored on a limited basis, including marriage license applications [S7].
- Ran the August 2026 primary. On election day he said turnout was "tracking... right around normal" against the 2022 midterm (about 46%) [S8]. He sits on the county canvassing board, which reviews challenged ballots [S9].
