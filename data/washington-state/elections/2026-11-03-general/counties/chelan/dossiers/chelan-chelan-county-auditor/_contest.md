## What the office does
The county auditor runs elections and handles document recording, vehicle and vessel licensing and marriage licenses. In Chelan County the office also handles accounts payable, payroll and county financial reporting, with a staff of 15 (S1, S7). The office is partisan. This race is for the "4 Year Short and Full Term": the winner finishes the current term and serves the next four years (S1, S3).

## Race dynamics
Longtime Auditor Skip Moore retired at the end of April 2026. In June the county commissioners chose Chief Deputy Auditor Brandt Cappell over former Chelan County Republican chairman Aaron Young to finish Moore's term (S5). Cappell, the appointed incumbent, faces Brian Brett, who is retiring as Wenatchee Valley Fire Chief (S5). Both prefer the Republican Party (S1, S3). Brett led the certified August primary 12,073 (60.92%) to 7,746 (39.08%) (S4). NCWLIFE lists the race among the area's featured general-election contests (S6). Neither candidate's statement takes a position on a policy dispute. Cappell runs on hands-on experience in the office and continuity with Moore. Brett runs on a 34-year fire-service career, including 8 years as chief, and his CPA credential, saying it would strengthen the office's audit and finance functions (S1).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** an appointed acting auditor who served as the office's first chief deputy, against a career fire chief and CPA who has never held elected office.
2. **Spending:** both statements stress responsible financial oversight of taxpayer dollars. Brett ties this to his accounting credentials and the audit function. The difference is small.
3. **Taxes, housing, safety, climate, reform:** no evidence in this race.
