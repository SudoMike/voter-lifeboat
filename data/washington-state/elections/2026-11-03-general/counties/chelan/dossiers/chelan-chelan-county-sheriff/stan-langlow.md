## Background

Stan Langlow (Stanley Langlow) prefers the Republican Party. He is a patrol deputy with the Chelan County Sheriff's Office running against his boss, Sheriff Mike Morrison [S1][S7][S8]. He reports 10 years as a commissioned law enforcement officer in Washington. His campaign site describes it as "nearly a decade," beginning with the Basic Law Enforcement Academy in 2016 [S1][S3].

Before Chelan County he worked at the Kitsap County Sheriff's Office. He was a patrol deputy (2016-2021), patrol sergeant (2021-2023) and training sergeant (2023-2025), and on the Kitsap SWAT team he served as operator, team leader and assistant commander (2024-2025) [S1][S3]. He served two terms as 2nd vice president of the Kitsap County Deputy Sheriff's Guild [S1]. Source ONE News reported that he joined the Chelan County office in September 2024 as a lateral deputy [S7]. His campaign site lists the Chelan County post from 2025 and says his family moved to Chelan in 2025 [S3].

He holds a B.S. in business administration (finance) from Central Washington University (2013), where he played football. He says he and his wife started a business in the Tacoma area [S1][S3]. His campaign site lists training that includes crisis intervention, the FBI-LEEDA Executive Leadership Institute, first-level supervision certification and a range of use-of-force instructor courses [S3]. He has coached youth sports [S1].

He placed second to Morrison in the certified August 4, 2026 primary, 5,123 votes (24.06%) to 16,168 (75.94%). Both advanced to the general [S6].

## Positions

### Leadership and accountability
- Says his campaign rests on three pillars: "Leadership, Deputy Support, and Community" [S4].
- Wants an office that is "accountable, results-driven, and honest about its performance," with "clear goals and measurable outcomes." He says transparency means "operating in the open rather than only in crisis" [S4].
- Says he would lead "with the same focus on accountability, fiscal responsibility, and operational excellence that guided me in building and managing my own business." He says the office should deliver "meaningful value and accountability to taxpayers" [S3].
- Says effective leadership is "earned and demonstrated - through accountability, clear communication, and a commitment to continuous improvement." He says he is prepared "not because of a single position or title, but because of the full scope of my experience" [S1][S2].

### Deputy support
- Calls deputies and staff "the face of the agency." He backs leadership that listens to, develops and advocates for them [S7].
- Would create "real pathways for input" from frontline deputies and lead "through collaboration rather than command alone." He would invest in training "going beyond minimum requirements" [S4].

### Public safety and community
- Says safety means "consistent protection for residents, workers, and visitors alike, delivered through proactive, visible service across the entire county" [S4].
- Wants the office seen "as a partner rather than only an authority," and commits "to serve all communities with equal respect, honor diverse beliefs and backgrounds, and always see the person behind every call for service" [S4].
