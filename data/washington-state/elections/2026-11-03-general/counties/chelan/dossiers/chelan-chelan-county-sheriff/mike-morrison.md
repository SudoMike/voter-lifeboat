## Background

Mike Morrison is the incumbent Chelan County Sheriff and prefers the Republican Party [S1][S2]. He won the office in the November 2022 general election over Brian Burnett, 17,300 votes (54.31%) to 14,358 (45.08%) [S6]. His campaign site calls him the 19th elected sheriff in the office's 126-year history [S3].

He reports 20+ years in law enforcement. His career includes service as a city police officer and deputy sheriff, and roles in patrol, training, school safety (school resource officer, Cascade School District) and search and rescue [S1][S3]. He is a past president of the Chelan County Deputy Sheriffs Association and of the Omak Police Department Guild [S1][S3]. He sits on the executive board of the Washington Association of Sheriffs and Police Chiefs and on the Rivercom administrative board, which he chaired in 2025 [S1][S3]. His education is a B.A. in law and justice with a minor in sociology from Central Washington University and an A.A.S. in administration of justice from Spokane Community College [S1][S3]. He is a volunteer track and field coach at Wenatchee High School and serves with the Chelan Douglas Community Action Council [S1][S3].

He won the certified August 4, 2026 primary with 16,168 votes (75.94%) to Stan Langlow's 5,123 (24.06%) [S5]. Langlow is a deputy in his department [S11].

## Positions

### Public safety and the Sheriff's Office
- Says his priority is "the safety of our citizens, preserving public trust, and protecting the quality of life" in Chelan County. He commits to "professional, responsive, and compassionate law enforcement" [S1][S2].
- Says the Marine Patrol, Traffic Unit, U.S. Forest Service deputies, Drug Task Force and Behavioral Health Unit have become "more visible, proactive, and effective" under his leadership [S1].
- If re-elected, pledges to "continue to ensure that all residents are treated with fairness, respect and dignity while protecting constitutional rights" [S3].
- Says the office "remains non-political, community-focused" and is guided by law and professional standards [S3].
- On less-lethal tools: "this is certainly an investment that we want to see, that we have peaceful resolutions and have different opportunities to come to a better conclusion to our incidents" [S7]. On body-worn cameras: "We're not hiding anything... We're in compliance with Washington state law when it comes to de-escalation" [S7].
- On Flock license-plate cameras, told the Cashmere City Council on Sept. 14: "I think the technology is outstanding, excellent, but I think they've done damage to our relationship with citizens in the state, and specifically folks in Chelan County." He also said, "I'm not impressed with the company" [S8].

### Budget and staffing
- Says the office has focused on full staffing, recruitment and retention, and has improved pay, benefits and wellness support for employees [S1].
- His campaign site says that "despite significant financial challenges for the county, essential services were preserved through responsible stewardship of public resources" [S3].
- Says the office has secured "millions of dollars in grants to improve public safety" [S1].
- In September 2026 he asked the county commissioners for a 1% raise for himself and his undersheriff. The raise would create a 3% pay difference between them and the office's commissioned supervisors [S9].

## Record

- Chelan County Sheriff since winning the 2022 election [S6].
- Says the office modernized equipment, vehicles and facilities and raised training standards beyond state requirements [S1].
- With U.S. Rep. Kim Schrier, announced $713,000 in federal Community Project Funding in May 2026 for less-lethal and de-escalation tools: restraint wraps, pepper-ball rifles, 40 mm launchers to be issued to every deputy, and Taser 10s. The office also received $660,000 in federal funding for body-worn cameras in 2024 [S7].
- Says the office responded to wildfires, floods and other critical incidents in partnership with cities and state and federal agencies [S1][S3].
- Told the Cashmere City Council that four or five of the 12 Flock cameras placed in Chelan County since early 2025 had been vandalized [S8].
- On Sept. 22, 2026, a Douglas County judge ruled for him in a dispute with the Chelan County Civil Service Commission. The judge found the commission acted in an "arbitrary and capricious" manner when it declined to produce a list of candidates for a sergeant vacancy he had asked to fill [S10].

## Endorsements
