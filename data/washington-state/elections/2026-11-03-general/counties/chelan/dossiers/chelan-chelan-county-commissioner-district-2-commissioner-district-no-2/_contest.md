## What the office does

Chelan County is governed by a three-member Board of County Commissioners, which sets the county budget and adopts county ordinances and land-use rules for unincorporated areas [S7][S10]. Each commissioner serves a four-year term [S1]. District 2 covers the Upper Wenatchee River Valley, including Leavenworth and its urban growth area [S6][S10].

Only District 2 voters vote in the primary: under RCW 36.32.040, the voters "of each county commissioner district, and they only," nominate the candidates for that district, "to be voted for at the following general election" [S3]. In the general election the seat is on every Chelan County ballot. In the 2022 general, 33,392 votes were cast in this race, out of 34,530 ballots counted county-wide [S4]. In the August 2026 primary, by contrast, 9,409 votes were cast in the race [S5].

## Race dynamics

Incumbent Shon D. Smith of Cashmere (prefers Republican Party), elected in 2022 and board chair since 2025, faces Leavenworth City Council member Clint Strand (prefers Democrat Party) [S1][S6][S9]. Smith won the seat in 2022 by 328 votes county-wide, 16,833 to 16,505 over Anne Hessburg [S4]. The certified primary was also close: Smith 4,368 (46.42%), Strand 4,248 (45.15%) and Nathanial Helligso 793 (8.43%) [S5].

Smith runs on his record in office, fiscal responsibility, public safety and roads [S1]. Strand runs on trust in county operations, regional housing partnerships with builder incentives, and a balanced budget that stops drawing on reserves [S1][S9]. The county budget is a live issue. The 2025 budget had a $4.1 million gap [S7]. In October 2026 the Chelan PUD board approved a payment to the county of at least $1.35 million a year starting in 2027, which PUD commissioners described as helping close a county budget gap [S8]. Strand says the county "can't keep dipping into our reserve funds" [S1]; Smith's position on reserves is in his dossier.

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Record vs. renewal:** a first-term incumbent and board chair who centers his campaign on experience, against a challenger with eight-plus years on a city council who emphasizes a fresh approach to county operations.
2. **Spending:** Strand pledges balanced budgets without reserve draws. Smith describes planned deficit budgets and a healthy reserve, alongside a "spend less than you have" goal.
3. **Housing and growth:** Strand proposes builder incentives and regional partnerships. Smith's record includes voting in 2023 against denser zoning in Leavenworth's growth area (later reversed under a state board ruling), framed around rural character.
4. **Taxes, safety, reform:** Smith says he wants to keep taxes low, voted for the 2024 encampment trespass ordinance, and says he is refusing campaign donations. Strand has no stated positions on these.
5. **Climate:** no evidence for either candidate.
