## Background

Clint Strand prefers the Democratic Party ("Prefers Democrat Party" on the ballot) and lives in Leavenworth [S1][S4][S6]. He has served on the Leavenworth City Council since 2017 and is in his third term; he chairs the council's Finance Committee and has served on its Housing, Downtown Steering and Leavenworth Area Promotions committees [S1][S3]. NCWLIFE reported in 2025 that he co-chaired the council's Affordable Housing Committee [S7]. He was on the 2025 ballot for Leavenworth City Council Position 6 as the incumbent [S8].

He has been executive director of the Wenatchee Valley College Foundation since December 1, 2025 [S7]. His campaign says the foundation manages a $19 million asset portfolio [S3]. Before that he directed marketing and communications for Cascade Medical in Leavenworth, where NCWLIFE reported he helped develop the communication strategy for a 2025 EMS levy; he also worked in communications for Confluence Health and as news director at KOHO 101.1 [S1][S7]. He holds bachelor's degrees in journalism and political science from Gonzaga University [S1]. He says he has lived in Chelan County for 25 years [S1].

He announced his campaign in May 2026 [S6]. In the certified August 4, 2026 primary he placed second among District 2 voters with 4,248 votes (45.15%), 120 behind incumbent Shon Smith [S5].

## Positions

### County budget and reserves
- "We simply can't keep dipping into our reserve funds to make the math work like Chelan County has been" [S1].
- Says he will "lead the hard conversations about how our services get paid for, and what needs to be adjusted so our books balance - just like your family has to do" [S1][S3].
- His site asks why the county "is dealing with a crushing budget deficit" [S3]. (The Wenatchee World reported a $4.1 million gap in the county's 2025 budget [S9].)
- Says his Finance Committee work on the council taught him to balance municipal budgets [S6].

### Housing
- "We all know there's no such thing as a 'starter home' anymore"; says housing for young families, teachers and nurses is out of reach, not only low-cost "affordable" housing [S1][S3].
- Proposes "regional, partnership-based approaches, providing incentives for builders to make housing for the next generation worthwhile," and identifying areas in the county where that development makes sense [S1][S3][S6].

### Trust and county operations
- Calls trust the county's most pressing issue: residents should get "a consistent, professional" experience and county employees should "feel good about" their jobs [S3][S6].
- Says he would first ask questions to find out why "these breakdowns are happening," then set expectations and accountability; listening would be his top first-year priority [S3][S6].
- Says city-government experience has shown him how municipalities and the county could collaborate better [S6].
