## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would let Wenatchee School District No. 246 issue $275,000,000 of general obligation bonds maturing within 20 years and levy annual excess property taxes to repay them, as set out in board Resolution No. 03-26 [S1][S3]. The money would:

- Build a new Wenatchee High School to replace existing classrooms and portables and expand career and technical education (CTE) space, with added classrooms to reduce overcrowding and controlled entries for security [S1].
- Upgrade aging heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC) at seven schools "for clean air (reducing wildfire smoke) and safe temperatures" [S1]. The district lists Lewis & Clark, Newbery, Mission View and Sunnyslope elementaries and Foothills, Orchard and Pioneer middle schools [S4].

The explanatory statement says approval would also qualify the district for an estimated $83,000,000 in state matching funds [S1].

**What "new high school" means in the plan.** The district's plan builds new two-story classroom space on the current student parking lot. It keeps and renovates the gyms, pool, locker rooms, auditorium and band classroom block, then demolishes the rest of the old building for fields and parking [S4]. Students would stay in the current building during construction [S4]. A facility assessment found more than 100,000 square feet of the existing building could be kept, which cut the bond request by $20 million [S6]. The district's page gives the savings as "$20+ million" in one place and "an estimated $27 million" in another [S4].

**Passage.** The measure needs a 60% supermajority [S7]. The board approved placing it on the ballot unanimously on June 23, 2026 [S7].

## Cost

- **Tax rate.** The district estimates the bond tax rate at $1.46 per $1,000 of assessed value per year, which it says "would remain flat for 20 years" [S4]. On a $400,000 home that is about $584 a year, or $48.67 a month (computed for this dossier).
- **Total repayment.** The district's FAQ estimates the total paid over the life of the bonds, principal plus interest, at "approximately $444 million over a 20-year period," including "$169 million in estimated interest" at an assumed borrowing rate of about 5% [S4]. The district models three separate bond sales [S4].
- **Project budget.** The district's breakdown is $299 million for Wenatchee High School plus $58 million for HVAC, minus $83 million in state School Construction Assistance Program (SCAP) money, for a $275 million bond [S4]. The district's FAQ gives the total as about $357 million [S4].
- **Other figures in circulation.** In early June, before the scope cut, the Wenatchee World reported an estimate of $372.9 million, with $79 million from SCAP and a district share of about $293.9 million [S5]. One FAQ answer on the district page still uses earlier figures ("approximately $79 million" state match and a "$295 million" local bond) [S4].
- **Current school taxes.** For 2026 taxes, the district levies $2.1249 per $1,000 for its educational programs and operations (EP&O) levy and $0.6405 for its existing bond, $2.7654 in all [S8].
- **District projection.** The district says that if the bond passes, the combined local school tax rate in 2027 "would be approximately $4.23 per $1,000," below the $4.66 rate in place when voters approved the 2014 bond [S4]. The district's page also says the local rate "would stay stable, because the previous bond debt will be retired" [S4]. Compared with the 2026 combined rate the assessor certified, $4.23 is about $1.46 per $1,000 higher (computed for this dossier from [S4] and [S8]).
- **Growing tax base.** The district expects the rate to fall over time as Microsoft data centers in Malaga and other development add assessed value. It cites Microsoft's own estimates of $1.48 million in 2026-27 and $1.54 million in 2027-28 in tax contributions [S4].
- **Exemptions.** Some homeowners, including qualifying seniors and people with disabilities, may be eligible for exemptions through the Chelan County Assessor [S1][S4].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This is a new bond authorization, not a renewal [S1]. The 2014 bond, which rebuilt Washington Elementary and modernized Lincoln Elementary, is still being repaid. The district refinanced it in 2024 and says that saves $6.6 million over the next nine years [S4]. The assessor's 2026 report shows that bond still levying $0.6405 per $1,000 [S8].

**What the district says the bond addresses.**
- Wenatchee High School was built in 1972 for 1,400 students. The district says it now has 1,900 students, operates at 104% of capacity and uses 12 portable classrooms [S4].
- The district says the 1970s HVAC system, the galvanized plumbing and the electrical system are at the end of their life [S4].
- It reports indoor air-quality readings as high as 130 AQI during wildfire smoke [S4].
- News coverage reports early releases during heat because of failing HVAC, temporary AC units, and extra filtration at the high school for wildfire smoke [S6][S7].
- The district says bond money can be used only for construction, and operating funds cannot legally pay for building replacement [S4].
- It says it has spent over $2 million from the operating budget on HVAC repairs [S4].
- It says construction costs rise 6-7.5% a year, so waiting one year would add $22-28 million [S4].

**Clean energy fines.** The district's FAQ cites possible annual fines under the state Clean Buildings standard [S4]. NCWLIFE reported in July 2026 that a student energy-management program kept the high school in compliance, avoiding a possible $433,000 fine. The district's other buildings face a July 2027 deadline [S16].

**State match timing.** Superintendent Kory Kalahar said the SCAP formula changes next year and the district "would receive less – substantially less – than the $83 million" if the bond did not pass in November or February. A district consultant estimated the loss at $20 million [S6].

**History.** In 2018 a $120 million bond for Wenatchee High School received 57% yes and failed under the 60% requirement [S5].

**Enrollment and the district's finances (claims in the statement against).**
- *Enrollment.* OSPI Report Card counts show 8,019 students in 2015-16 and 6,976 in 2025-26, a drop of 1,043 (computed for this dossier from [S14]). The statement against says enrollment "has fallen by over 1,300 students since 2015" [S2]. The 2026-27 budget projects about 6,190 students, 238 fewer than the year before; it is a budget projection rather than an OSPI headcount. The district projects a further decline of more than 880 students over five years [S13]. The district's FAQ says the new building would be "sized for projected enrollment, not current overcrowding" [S4].
- *The $9 million error and Columbia Elementary.* In January 2024 Superintendent Kalahar described "a nearly $9 million budget error stemming from June 2022" [S10]. The Wenatchee World reported that an error found at an August 2023 budget hearing had made the district's position look better than it was when 2022-23 staffing was set [S9]. The district's cut plan saved $8.9 million by eliminating 63 positions, closing Columbia Elementary, changing high school and middle school schedules and cutting materials and operating costs [S11][S12]. The board voted unanimously to close Columbia on May 14, 2024 [S12]. A North Central Educational Service District review "confirmed that the 2022 budget error is not the reason for the current concerns but that cuts need to be made," the World reported [S11]. The district also cited declining enrollment as a reason for the closure [S10][S11]. Sources describe 63 positions reduced; they do not say how many were layoffs [S11][S12].
- The statement against's claims that the school was "once promoted as a 100-year investment" and that per-student cost is "nearly $16,000" were not checked against an independent source.

## Arguments for

The statement in favor was written by the committee of Chuck Largent, Debbie Walter and Katie Pauly (yesforwenatcheeschools@gmail.com) [S2][S3]. It says:
- Students spend their days in aging buildings "with mechanical systems beyond repair." At WHS (built 1972), HVAC "can't safely filter out wildfire smoke or cool the building," and learning days are lost to closures [S2].
- Small, windowless classrooms with inadequate electrical, plumbing and technical infrastructure limit teaching [S2].
- A "fiscally-conservative design will save over $20 million" by reusing the gyms, pool and auditorium [S2].
- "Every bond dollar stays local," and a yes vote unlocks $83 million in state matching funds [S2].

Its rebuttal says the district serves 6,500+ students. It calls the proposal the result of "a 3-year, well-researched, community-led review process" [S2].

At the June 23 vote, board member Julie Norton, who said she was first skeptical of a bond, called it "an important, responsible and sensible use of taxpayer funds" [S7]. Board member Mark Belton said students "walk into a building that no longer meets the needs of modern teaching and learning" [S7].

## Arguments against

The statement against was written by the committee of Bill Sullivan, Mike Dechaney and Cynthia Adams (savewenatcheehighschool@gmail.com) [S2][S3]. It says:
- Voters should weigh the "$275 million ($440 mil with interest)" bond against declining enrollment, school closures and fiscal responsibility. It says enrollment has fallen by over 1,300 since 2015 and is projected to drop up to 140 students a year [S2].
- Rejecting the bond does not affect the operating budget. It says per-student cost of "nearly $16,000" will rise as enrollment falls [S2].
- The district "already runs multi-million-dollar deficits and failed accounting for a $9 million error that forced the closure of Columbia Elementary and the layoff of more than 60 staff" [S2].
- $275 million in debt "creates a tax burden lasting a generation or more." It says the 50-year-old school "may need maintenance and upgrades rather than replacement," and proposes "a thorough review" [S2].

Its rebuttal says district buildings "meet safety standards, or they'd be shutdown by now." It calls "a $444 million debt" "extravagant, irresponsible and unnecessary" and says existing buildings "can be renovated for a fraction of the cost" [S2].

The district's FAQ answers the renovation point. It says full renovation would cost "nearly as much as new construction," extend the building's life only 15-20 years and require multi-year construction around students [S4].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote adds a new 20-year excess levy, estimated at $1.46 per $1,000 (about $584 a year on a $400,000 home), to repay $275 million plus about $169 million in interest [S4]. The 2014 bond is still levying alongside it [S8]. School and hospital construction bonds elsewhere (e.g. Grant's McKay bond) map to +2. Scored +2.
- `spending`: the measure is capital construction for buildings the district says are at end of life [S4]. The opposition's case is mainly about the size and need of the debt [S2], which is `taxes` evidence. No mapping, consistent with other replacement bonds.
- `parental-rights`: the measure concerns buildings and HVAC, not notification, records, curricula or opt-outs [S1]. No mapping.
- `local-control`: the state match comes through an existing state program and does not change who decides [S1][S4]. No mapping.
