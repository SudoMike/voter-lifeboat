## What it does

Proposition No. 1 is a multi-year levy lid lift for the City of Cashmere [S1][S3]. If approved, it would:
- Set the city's regular property tax rate at $1.5737 per $1,000 of assessed value for collection in 2027 [S1][S2].
- Authorize "a maximum levy rate increase of 9% each year from 2028 to 2032" [S1].
- Use the 2032 levy rate to compute the maximum levy in later years under chapter 84.55 RCW, so the increase becomes permanent in the base [S1][S3].

**Purpose.** The ballot title says the measure would "provide for continual operation of essential government services" [S1]. Ordinance No. 1345, which placed it on the ballot, is more specific. It says the purpose is "to provide for the continual provision of services that ensure public safety, including law enforcement, fire protection, disaster mitigation, and other necessary government services" [S3]. The levy is general-fund property tax; neither the ballot title nor the ordinance divides the money among these services [S1][S3].

**Who provides those services.**
- Cashmere is one of four cities that contract with the Chelan County Sheriff's Office for law enforcement [S5].
- The assessor's tax-code area for the City of Cashmere (code 100) lists only the city and Cashmere School District levies on top of the countywide ones. It carries no fire district levy [S4]. No source found for this dossier says how the city provides fire protection.

**Passage.** A levy lid lift needs a simple majority of voters [S8]. The council passed the ordinance, and Mayor Jim Fletcher signed it, on July 13, 2026, after a public hearing that day [S1][S3].

## Cost

- **Current rate.** For 2026 taxes, the City of Cashmere's regular levy is $1.4441 per $1,000 ($1.4440595225), raising $797,967.78 [S4].
- **2027 rate.** The ordinance describes the measure as an increase "by $0.13 per $1,000 assessed value, to a total regular property tax levy rate of $1.5737" [S3]. That is about 9% above the 2026 rate (computed for this dossier from [S3] and [S4]).
- **On a $400,000 home.** The city portion would go from about $578 a year to about $629, roughly $52 more in 2027 (computed for this dossier). The assessor puts total 2026 property taxes on a $400,000 home in the city at $3,217, at a combined rate of $8.0413 per $1,000 [S4].
- **Citywide.** Applied to the city's 2026 taxable assessed value of $552,586,488 [S4], $1.5737 would raise about $870,000, about $72,000 more than 2026 (computed for this dossier). Actual 2027 collections depend on 2027 assessed values. Neither the city's filing nor the pamphlet gives a revenue estimate [S1][S3].
- **2028-2032.** The ballot caps each year's increase at 9% [S1]. If the full 9% were used every year, the 2032 rate would be about $2.42 per $1,000 (computed for this dossier, on the ballot's wording that the 9% applies to the levy rate). Increases below the cap are up to the council each year.

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This is not a renewal. It raises the city's regular levy above the limit in RCW 84.55.010 [S3].

**What the council's ordinance says.**
- The city "is limited to raising the property tax rate by no more than 1% from the highest previous levy rate, under State law" [S3].
- "The revenue available to the City under the current levy rates and anticipated 1% increases for 2027 and future years will be insufficient to provide for the level of public services that are required by the community" [S3].
- A multi-year lid lift lets officials "plan revenue and expenses for the foreseeable future and avoid administrative costs of preparing multiple ballot propositions" [S3].
- State law now allows a lid lift of up to ten consecutive years, though "the City is only proposing 6 year[s]" [S3]. RCW 84.55.050 allows multi-year lid lifts of "up to 10 consecutive years" at a primary or general election [S8].
- Cashmere has a population under 10,000 [S3].

**How it got to the ballot.** The June 8, 2026 council agenda listed "Discussion for scheduling a Public Hearing and Summary of Tax Proposal" and a "Service Agreement for Public Education on Taxes" [S6]. The council held the public hearing on July 13, 2026 and passed the ordinance the same day [S3]. Chelan County Elections stamped it received July 21, 2026 [S3]. No minutes or staff report giving the council's figures or vote count could be retrieved; the city's website blocks automated access and the archived June 8 council packet is incomplete.

**Related public-safety costs (context, not tied to the levy by any source).** The Wenatchee World reported in September 2026 that Cashmere's base payment to the Chelan County Regional Justice Center jail was about $40,000 in 2023. The base rose to $112,000 in 2024, increased 5% in each of 2025 and 2026, and moves to a daily-rate model for 2027, which the council approved unanimously [S7]. No source found for this dossier links the levy proposal to the jail contract.

## Arguments for

No statement in favor was filed; the pamphlet and VoteWA say "No committee could be found" [S1][S2]. The council's case, as stated in Ordinance 1345, is that revenue under the 1% limit will not cover the services the community requires. It frames the measure as continuing public safety, including law enforcement, fire protection and disaster mitigation, and other services [S3].
