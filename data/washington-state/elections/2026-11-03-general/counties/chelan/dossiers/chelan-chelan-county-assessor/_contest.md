## What the office does

The assessor's office determines the value of taxable real and personal property in Chelan County [S7]. Cornelius describes the office as a staff of 16 that administers exemption programs, current-use and open-space programs, levy calculations and taxpayer appeals under RCW 84 and WAC 458 [S7]. Both campaigns note that the assessor does not set tax rates or taxing-district budgets; those are decided by elected boards, the legislature and voters [S8][S9]. It is a partisan county office with a four-year term [S1][S3].

## Race dynamics

Incumbent Wes Cornelius (prefers GOP Party) joined the office as an appraiser in 2007, was appointed assessor in 2023 and won the November 2023 election with 51.66% over Mark C. Miller [S1][S5]. Challenger Britini Port (prefers Independent Party) is a real estate broker and first-time candidate [S1][S2]. Port led the certified August primary 11,890 (53.02%) to 10,537 (46.98%) [S4].

The race is about how accurately the office values property. Port's campaign says high-value properties (luxury waterfront, second homes and corporate apartment complexes) are assessed below market while modest homes are assessed at or above market, shifting the tax burden onto working families [S1][S6]. She proposes published methodology, sale-to-assessment ratio reports by property tier, high-value outlier reviews and per-parcel valuation worksheets [S8]. Cornelius's campaign site answers these claims point by point. It says the examples misread mass-appraisal law and multi-parcel or open-space sales. It says the office runs a computer-assisted mass appraisal system, not Excel. It also says Port's appraiser accreditation was issued in June 2026, after candidate statements were due [S7]. Both candidates promise transparency, modernized technology and outreach on senior, disabled and veteran exemptions [S1].

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Record vs. renewal:** a 19-year insider and incumbent against a first-time candidate running on a critique of the office's methods.
2. **Taxes:** Port wants assessments that move more of the existing tax burden onto high-value and second-home properties, while saying she does not seek higher overall taxes. Cornelius stresses property tax relief for seniors, disabled residents and veterans and says he is "not interested in taxing people out of their homes."
3. **Spending, housing, safety, climate, reform:** no clear differentiating evidence. Port ties assessments to housing affordability, but she makes no housing-policy proposal.
