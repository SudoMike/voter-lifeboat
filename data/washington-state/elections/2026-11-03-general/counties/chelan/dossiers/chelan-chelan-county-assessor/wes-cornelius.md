## Background

Wes Cornelius prefers the GOP Party and is the incumbent Chelan County Assessor [S1][S3]. He graduated from Leavenworth High School and served in the U.S. Army from 1983 to 1986, including in Germany and Korea [S1][S7]. From 1986 to 2007 he worked in real estate as an agent, property manager, sales manager and designated broker [S1][S7]. He joined the Chelan County Assessor's Office in October 2007 as a real property appraiser and was chief appraiser (2011-2012) and chief deputy (2012-2023) [S7]. The county commissioners unanimously appointed him assessor in April 2023 [S1][S7]. He is a Department of Revenue certified appraiser [S7]. He lists membership in American Legion Post #10 (vice commander), the Chelan County Republicans and the Washington associations of county assessors and county officials, and service as a Republican precinct committee officer [S7].

He won the November 2023 election 9,950 (51.66%) to 9,130 (47.41%) over Mark C. Miller, who also preferred the Republican Party [S5]. He placed second in the certified August 2026 primary with 10,537 votes (46.98%) to Britini Port's 11,890 (53.02%) [S4].

## Positions

### Property assessment and taxes
- Says he is "not interested in taxing people out of their homes" and has gone to Olympia "to fight for property tax reform and relief," including helping raise the senior exemption income limit from $48,000 to $83,000 for married couples [S6].
- Says he worked with 12th District legislators on ESSB 6162, which expands income thresholds and benefits for the senior, disabled and veteran exemption programs beginning in 2027, and helped support HB 2375, which lets accessory dwelling units qualify under senior exemption programs [S1][S7][S8].
- Says assessments must follow state mass-appraisal law (RCW 84.40, WAC 458) at 100% of true and fair market value. His campaign says several of Port's examples were multi-parcel sales, open-space farm land or older sales, and that one sale does not prove an assessment wrong [S9].
- Says under-assessment causes an inequitable distribution of the levy among taxpayers, "not an automatic increase in everyone's property taxes," because most property taxes are levy-based [S9].
- Notes that exemptions shrink the tax base, so taxpayers who do not qualify pay a larger share of voter-approved levies [S9].

### Office operations and transparency
- Priorities: fair and accurate assessments, transparency, customer service, protecting seniors, disabled citizens and veterans, and modernizing operations through technology and staff cross-training [S1][S8].
- Says the office uses the Harris Govern PACS 9.0 computer-assisted mass appraisal system and that the Excel spreadsheets Port cites are quality-control reports. A replacement system would cost an estimated $12-20 million and would need commissioner approval "during a period of tight budgets" [S9].
- Points to a new Chelan County Property Owner's Guide to the Assessment Process, published market reports and neighborhood sales analyses. He calls per-parcel valuation worksheets and county-average comparisons misleading for mass appraisal [S6][S9].

### Port authority tax increment financing (Malaga)
- At a November 2025 public meeting on the Chelan-Douglas Regional Port Authority's proposed Malaga TIF district, he said his office would have to hand-calculate the TIF's effects: "it's huge, and I don't have the manpower for it" [S12].

## Record

- In the Chelan County Assessor's Office since 2007; assessor since April 2023 [S7].
- Held four community meetings in spring 2025 (Entiat, Lake Wenatchee, Manson, Cashmere) to explain how the office runs assessments and uses sales data [S13].
- Says he supported HB 2375 and ESSB 6162 on property tax exemptions [S7][S8].

## Endorsements

His campaign site lists individual endorsers, including retired Chelan County Prosecutor Doug Shae, former Chelan County Assessor Deanna Walter and retired Chelan County Deputy Prosecuting Attorney Susan Hinkle [S10]. He endorsed Treasurer candidate Rick Viall and put Viall's property-tax lookup tool on his own campaign site [S14].

## Scoring notes

- Experience: about 19 years in the office, including more than 10 as chief deputy, and the sitting assessor. He runs on that record.
- Taxes: his tax message is property tax relief and exemptions for seniors, disabled residents and veterans, and he says he worked on two state bills. He also points out that exemptions shift burden to other taxpayers.
- He disputes the challenger's data and methods, and both candidates promise transparency and modernization.
