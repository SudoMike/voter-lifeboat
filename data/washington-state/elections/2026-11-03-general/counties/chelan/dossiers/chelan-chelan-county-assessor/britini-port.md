## Background

Britini Port prefers the Independent Party and is a first-time candidate [S1][S3]. She describes herself as a fifth-generation Wenatchee Valley resident who graduated from Eastmont High School in 2005 and earned a bachelor's degree in environmental studies from the University of Washington [S1][S5]. She served in the U.S. Peace Corps in Guatemala from 2010 to 2012 and then spent more than a decade building an export manufacturing company there, which she says employed more than 15 people [S1][S5]. She returned to Wenatchee in 2020 to work with her family's construction company [S5]. She describes herself as a licensed managing broker, property manager and accredited appraiser [S1][S5]. Her community service includes Court Appointed Special Advocate (CASA), coaching Wenatchee youth soccer and the Lewis & Clark PTSA [S1]. She speaks Spanish [S1].

She led the certified August primary with 11,890 votes (53.02%) to incumbent Wes Cornelius's 10,537 (46.98%) [S4].

## Positions

### Property assessment and taxes
- Says high-value properties ("waterfront properties, luxury vacation homes, and multifamily corporate apartment complexes") are assessed far below their worth while working-class and low-income homes are often assessed at or above market value, shifting the tax burden onto "everyday families" [S1][S6].
- Says "I am not advocating for higher taxes; I am demanding accurate taxes," and that the total the county collects "doesn't have to change at all" [S5][S6].
- Her campaign says a model comparing county parcels with recent sales found 23,672 parcels under-assessed and 3,607 over-assessed out of 44,966 scored, and estimates about $1.1 million a year in tax shifted within its recent-sales sample [S5]. The campaign also says about 3,500 vacant residential parcels are coded the same as developed homes [S6].
- Says the office relies largely on Excel spreadsheets and promises "advanced appraisal technology" and uniform valuations across price tiers [S1][S5].
- Would "actively promote property tax exemptions and deferrals," including senior, disabled-veteran and fixed-income outreach, mailing an eligibility checklist with every assessment notice [S1][S7].

### Transparency and audits
- Ten dated commitments, among them: an annual methodology document, downloadable multi-year assessment history, per-parcel valuation worksheets, quarterly sale-to-assessment ratio reports by property tier, a review of every high-value parcel above a set threshold each cycle, an expanded open-data parcel feed, a plain-language appeals guide with quarterly office hours, and a public service-level dashboard [S7].
- Says every commitment can be delivered "under existing Washington law," and that the assessor does not set tax rates or district budgets [S7].

### Housing
- Says "We cannot fix the housing crisis until we fix our tax assessments," arguing that inaccurate assessments raise mortgage payments and rents [S5][S6]. Her campaign says $10 million or more in taxes was "shifted to homeowners to fund the construction of corporate-owned apartments in Wenatchee" [S5].

### Independence
- Her campaign site says "Determining your home's value shouldn't be political" next to the label "Independent" [S5].
