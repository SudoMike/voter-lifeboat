## What the office does
The county clerk is the clerk of the Superior Court. The office keeps all records filed with the Superior Court system and maintains court-record confidentiality, retention standards and legal reporting (S1, S6). Chelan County's office also runs the jury system, the family-law court facilitator program and passport processing (S1). The office is partisan, with a four-year term (S1, S3).

## Race dynamics
This is a rematch of 2022. That year Marty Young (Prefers Republican Party) beat Sandra Arechiga (Prefers Independent Party) 16,456 (50.64%) to 15,978 (49.17%) (S5). At the time Arechiga was a deputy clerk and Young was a DSHS program specialist and secretary of the Chelan County Republican Party (S6). In the four-candidate August 2026 primary, Arechiga finished first with 8,553 votes (37.99%) and Young second with 6,916 (30.72%). Samantha J Thompson (3,841) and Charlene Beck (3,205) were eliminated (S4, S7). NCWLIFE lists the race among the area's featured general-election contests (S8).

Neither candidate's statement takes a position on a policy dispute. Arechiga, a 12-year clerk's-office employee, says the office "is not about party politics" and points to her front-line court experience (S1, S2). Young runs on his first term and his management background as a Republican incumbent and precinct committee officer (S1).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** a one-term elected incumbent against a long-serving staff member of the same office who has never held elected office.
2. **Partisanship of the office:** Arechiga frames the clerk's job as nonpartisan. Young campaigns as a Republican and party officer. This is not one of the rubric's axes, and the `reform` axis (money in politics, structural reform) does not capture it.
3. **Spending:** Young cites processing grants to reduce the taxpayer burden. It is one line and not a spending position.
4. **Taxes, housing, safety, climate, reform:** no evidence in this race.
