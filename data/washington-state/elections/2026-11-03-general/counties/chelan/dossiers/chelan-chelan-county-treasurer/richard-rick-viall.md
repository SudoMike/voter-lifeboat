## Background

Richard (Rick) Viall prefers the GOP Party and lists no elected experience [S1][S3]. He is a Chelan County wealth advisor and business owner. About two years ago he left salaried employment to start his own advisory firm, and he says he now oversees four businesses with six associates [S1][S5]. He holds a bachelor's degree from Washington State University and an associate degree from Wenatchee Valley College [S1]. He is a past president of the Wenatchee Valley College Foundation and took part in the Exchange Club of Wenatchee, including in leadership roles [S1][S5]. He has helped teach the Financial Fitness sessions of the National Council on Aging's Aging Mastery Program for seniors [S5]. He lives in Chelan County with his wife and two sons [S5]. His PDC registration gives a Chelan address [S8].

He led the certified August primary with 11,809 votes (52.61%) to Doug Miller's 10,636 (47.39%) [S4].

## Positions

### Office administration and accountability
- Says the office needs "strong fiduciary leadership, operational accountability, and a modern approach to managing public funds" [S1].
- Priorities: closer coordination between the Treasurer, the Auditor and other county departments, stronger audit preparation, better workflows and updated financial software [S1][S5].
- Says "safety comes first": strictly following the county's investment policy and statutory guidelines [S5].
- Says "The Treasurer does not decide what taxes should be. The duty is to collect them accurately, safeguard them completely, and account for every dollar publicly" [S5].

### Taxpayer education
- His campaign site offers a property-tax lookup that splits a bill into levies required by state law, districts voters created and voter-approved bonds and levies, plus a "Money Map" of county funds and a Spanish-language version [S5].

### Port authority and Malaga tax increment plan
- His "Port File" maps 115 parcels held by the Chelan-Douglas Regional Port Authority and its predecessors. It notes that the port collected about $4.8 million a year in property tax across both counties in 2024 while asking other districts to give up future revenue in the Malaga tax-increment area [S7].
- Asks "whether the Port needs new revenue tools or better use of the assets it has," and offers an interactive "stress test" of the Malaga bond plan showing the shortfall taxpayers would cover if assessed-value forecasts miss [S7].
