## What the office does
A Washington State Senator is the single Senate member from each of the 49 legislative districts, elected to a four-year term. Senators write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including the state tax structure, K-12 funding, criminal-justice statutes and housing rules, and the Senate confirms gubernatorial appointments. The 44th District lies entirely in Snohomish County.

## District and race dynamics
Democrat John Lovick, appointed to the Senate in 2021 after careers as a State Patrol trooper, Snohomish County Sheriff and County Executive, faces Republican Sherri Larkin, a Snohomish School Board director since 2023 and former teacher running for the Legislature for the first time (john-lovick.md S1, S4; sherri-larkin.md S1). Lovick runs on public-safety and transit work, including SB 5974 (2026) setting eligibility and certification standards for police chiefs and sheriffs (john-lovick.md S1, S5). Larkin runs on "Stronger Schools, Safer Communities, Smarter Spending" and says the state has left families "overtaxed and overburdened" (sherri-larkin.md S1, S2).

Lovick voted for the 2026 tax on income over $1 million (ESSB 6346), which I-645 would repeal, and for the 2025 capital gains, excise and B&O tax increases (john-lovick.md S8-S11). Neither candidate has stated a position on the three statewide initiatives. Lovick reports $186k raised to Larkin's $47k (john-lovick.md S14; sherri-larkin.md S4).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Lovick's votes for the 2025 and 2026 tax increases vs. Larkin's pledge to reduce the tax burden.
2. **Spending:** Larkin centers spending restraint and "measurable results"; Lovick states no spending position beyond affordability.
3. **Record vs. renewal:** long legislative and law-enforcement career vs. a first-time legislative candidate with three years on a school board.
4. **Parents and schools:** Lovick voted for ESHB 1296 (2025 amendments to the I-2081 parents' rights list); Larkin's endorsers describe her school-board work in parental-rights terms, but she has no published position of her own.
5. **Housing:** Lovick voted for the 2025 rent-increase cap (EHB 1217); Larkin names affordable housing as a goal without a mechanism.
