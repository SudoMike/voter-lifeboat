## Background
Sherri Larkin is the Republican candidate for State Senate in the 44th District. Her pamphlet statement lists service as a Snohomish School Board Director since 2023 and work as an elementary and substitute teacher; she holds a B.A. in psychology from the University of Puget Sound [S1]. She describes herself as a military spouse who arrived in Washington 35 years ago, a mother of five and a grandmother [S1][S2]. Her community service includes the U.S. Naval Academy state parent club, FBLA advising, school and youth-sports volunteering and a food bank backpack program [S1]. This is her first run for the Legislature [S1].

## Positions
- **Taxes and spending:** says "a rapidly expanding state government has left us overtaxed and overburdened, with higher bills and programs that too often deliver no measurable results"; promises to work "on Day One restoring fiscal integrity" and to "reduce the heavy burden on families and businesses, treating every taxpayer dollar with the respect it deserves" [S1]. Her campaign organizes its issues as "Stronger Schools, Safer Communities, Smarter Spending" [S2]. The issue pages on her site carried no readable text when retrieved on 2026-10-08 [S2].
- **Public safety:** wants "safe neighborhoods where law enforcement is supported" [S1].
- **Housing and economy:** names "affordable housing, and an economy that rewards hard work" as goals, without a stated mechanism [S1].
- **Bipartisanship:** says progress comes "through hard work across the aisle" [S1].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no statement found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Parents and schools:** wants "schools that truly prepare our children for success" [S1]. She has no published position of her own on parental notification, record access or opt-outs. Two endorsers quoted on her site describe her that way: Snohomish City Councilmember Gabe Jacobs says "she believes parents should have a voice in their children's education", and former Snohomish Mayor John Kartak says that on the school board she "helped move the conversation away from controversial ideologies and back toward common sense, parental rights, and the proper role of parents in their children's education" [S3].
