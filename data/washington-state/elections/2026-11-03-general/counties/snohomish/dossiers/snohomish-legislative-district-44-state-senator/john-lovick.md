## Background
John Lovick is the incumbent Democratic State Senator for the 44th Legislative District. His pamphlet statement lists past service as Snohomish County Sheriff, Snohomish County Executive, State Representative and Acting Speaker of the House, and Mill Creek City Council member, along with Coast Guard service and 31 years as a Washington State Patrol trooper and sergeant [S1]. The Senate Democratic Caucus biography says he was elected to the Mill Creek City Council in 1993, served nine years in the House from 1998 (five as Speaker Pro Tem), and was appointed to the Senate in 2021 [S4]. His 2026 committees are Transportation (Vice Chair), Rules, Law & Justice, and Business, Trade & Economic Development [S6][S7]. He holds an AA in criminal justice [S1].

## Positions
- **Public safety:** says people should "not only be safe" but "feel safe" at home, on the streets and in schools; cites work with the Criminal Justice Training Commission to open three regional peace-officer academies that have graduated more than 700 officers, and work "to build a robust accountability system" [S1]. His campaign site cites seat-belt (HB 1460) and road-safety legislation and SB 5272 on harassment of school sports officials [S2].
- **Transportation:** a frequent transit user committed to "a complete expansion of light rail to Everett and beyond" [S1]; says he has worked with Sound Transit on light rail and Swift bus lines [S2].
- **Affordability:** "I will always work to ensure that Washington is an affordable place to live" [S2]; no specific affordability mechanism beyond SB 5420 (military-spouse employment preference) is named [S2].
- **Taxes:** no campaign statement on taxes found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted for the 2026 Millionaires Tax and three 2025 revenue bills (see Record) [S8][S9][S10][S11]. The 44th District's 2026 session mailer, which carries his update alongside Berg's and Donaghy's, describes the Millionaires Tax as asking "the wealthiest to pay their fair share"; that section appears in Rep. Berg's column, not his [S7].
- **Housing:** no campaign statement found; voted for EHB 1217 (2025 limits on rent and fee increases) [S12].
- **Federal policy:** says Washington needs leadership that will "fight back" against "reckless cuts and decisions from our federal government" [S2].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no statement of his own found; his pamphlet lists a Planned Parenthood endorsement [S1], and Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates lists him [S17].
- **Parents and schools:** says he will "expand measures to ensure we lead in equitable, accessible, and safe education" [S2]. Voted for ESHB 1296 (2025), which amended the I-2081 list of parent and guardian rights (see Record) [S13][S18]. No campaign statement on parental notification or opt-outs found.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no public position on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted for ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S8].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted for ESHB 1296 (2025), the amendments I-1 would repeal [S13][S18].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- Prime sponsor of SB 5974 (2026), signed April 1, 2026: sets eligibility requirements for police chiefs, marshals and sheriffs (including CJTC certification, five years of law-enforcement experience, no felony convictions) and vacates the office of a leader who loses certification. He called it "about accountability" [S5]; the session mailer describes it the same way [S7].
- Sponsored SB 5272 (school sports officials) and SB 5420 (military-spouse employment preference), per the session mailer and campaign site [S2][S7].
- ESSB 6346 (tax on income over $1 million): yea on Senate final passage as amended by the House, 2026-03-11 (27-21), and on the 2026-02-16 Senate passage (27-22) [S8].
- ESSB 5813 (2025, higher capital gains and estate tax rates): yea, 2025-04-24 (27-21) [S9].
- ESSB 5814 (2025, excise taxes, including sales tax on more services): yea on final passage as amended by the House, 2025-04-24 (26-22) [S10].
- ESHB 2081 (2025, business and occupation tax increases): yea, 2025-04-26 (26-22) [S11].
- EHB 1217 (2025, limits on residential rent and fee increases): yea on final passage as recommended by the conference committee, 2025-04-27 (27-20) [S12].
- ESHB 1296 (2025, amendments to the I-2081 list of parent and guardian rights): yea on Senate passage, 2025-04-11 (30-19) [S13][S18].
- PDC (data updated 2026-10-05): $185,971.27 raised and $140,034.73 spent for 2026 [S14].
