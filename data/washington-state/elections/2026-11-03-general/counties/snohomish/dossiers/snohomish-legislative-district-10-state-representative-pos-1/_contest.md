## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets: K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The 10th District spans Island County and parts of Skagit and Snohomish counties; Shavers cites housing funding he secured for Langley, Coupeville, Stanwood and Arlington (clyde-shavers.md S2).

## District and race dynamics
Democratic incumbent Clyde Shavers, Vice Chair of House Education and a Navy veteran and attorney from Whidbey Island, faces Republican Robert (Chili) Hicks, a retired Navy Master Chief elected to the Stanwood City Council in 2024 (clyde-shavers.md S1; robert-chili-hicks.md S1). With only two candidates filed, both advanced automatically from the primary (clyde-shavers.md S17).

Both run on affordability, but differently. Shavers campaigns as a Democrat who does not support raising taxes and backs that with floor votes against the 2026 income tax over $1M, the 2025 gas-tax package, the services sales tax, the B&O increases and the 2025-27 operating budget (clyde-shavers.md S3, S7, S9-S11, S14). Hicks proposes cutting the sales tax, repealing the estate tax, stopping new taxes and fees and auditing spending (robert-chili-hicks.md S3). The sharper differences are on parents and schools (Hicks would restore the original parents' bill of rights, require curriculum posting and prior notice of non-emergency health services; Shavers voted for the 2025 amendments, ESHB 1296) and on abortion (Shavers "100 percent pro-choice"; no Hicks position found) (robert-chili-hicks.md S2, S3; clyde-shavers.md S3, S8). PDC: Shavers $105.4k raised, Hicks $81.6k (clyde-shavers.md S6; robert-chili-hicks.md S5).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Parents and schools:** Hicks's specific notification and curriculum-posting proposals vs. Shavers's vote for ESHB 1296.
2. **Spending:** Hicks centers audits and performance budgeting; Shavers voted against the 2025-27 operating budget without a stated spending philosophy.
3. **Record vs. renewal:** sitting representative and committee vice chair vs. a first-term city councilmember.
4. **Safety:** Hicks's enforcement-first platform vs. Shavers's general support for law enforcement.
5. **Taxes:** both lean toward limiting taxes; Shavers by record, Hicks by platform, so this axis differentiates little.
