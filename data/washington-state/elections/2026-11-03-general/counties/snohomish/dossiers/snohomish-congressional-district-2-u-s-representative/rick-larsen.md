## Background
Rick Larsen is the Democratic incumbent U.S. Representative for Washington's 2nd Congressional District, in his thirteenth term, and the lead Democrat on the House Transportation & Infrastructure Committee [S5]. The district includes portions of Snohomish County and all of Skagit, Whatcom, Island and San Juan counties [S5]. He began in public service on the Snohomish County Council and previously worked for the Port of Everett and the Washington State Dental Association [S1]. He graduated from Pacific Lutheran University and holds a master's degree from the University of Minnesota [S1]. He grew up in Arlington, the son of a union power-line worker [S1].

## Positions
- **Cost of living:** names lowering health care, gas, grocery, housing, child care and energy costs as his top priority [S1]; opposes "sweeping tariffs and reckless foreign wars" as policies that raise prices [S2].
- **Taxes:** supports preserving Social Security "by scrapping the cap on income subject to Social Security taxes" [S2]. Voted no on the 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act (see Record) [S6].
- **Spending and economy:** supports raising the federal minimum wage to $17 per hour, expanding affordable child care, and transportation, manufacturing and apprenticeship investment [S2]; cites infrastructure funding brought to Washington [S5].
- **Health care:** "I believe health care is a right, and I support universal, affordable coverage" [S2]. His listed record and proposals work through existing programs: Medicare drug-price negotiation, the $35 insulin cap, restoring ACA tax credits, closing the Medicaid coverage gap, adding hearing benefits to Medicare, and opposing privatization of Medicare and Social Security [S2]. No Medicare for All or single-payer proposal is stated [S2].
- **Immigration:** "I will not vote for any full-year funding for the Department of Homeland Security and ICE that fails to create oversight and accountability"; wants ICE officers to meet higher training standards and wear visible ID and body cameras; supports ending child and family detention and barring private for-profit detention facilities [S2]. Pamphlet: ICE agents should "follow the same rules as all other law enforcement agencies" [S1].
- **Public safety / fentanyl:** "addiction is a disease"; seeks funding for local prevention and recovery services while strengthening law enforcement's ability to hold traffickers accountable [S2]; supports local law enforcement against fentanyl [S1].
- **Climate and energy:** "mitigating climate change must be a top priority"; supports lowering utility bills "through clean energy and efficiency"; helped draft and pass the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act [S2].
- **Technology / data centers:** says data centers need "strong guardrails" so their costs are not passed to ratepayers through higher electricity bills and strain on water systems, and that data-center development "should strengthen these systems, not strain them" [S4].
- **Gender and reproductive policy:** supports suspending the filibuster to pass the Women's Health Protection Act to restore a nationwide abortion right, protecting telehealth medication abortion and travel for abortion care; cites "a 100% voting record on choice" [S2]. Endorsed by Planned Parenthood Action Fund, Reproductive Freedom For All, Pro-Choice Washington and Washington State Stonewall Democrats [S3].
- **Parents and schools:** no position found.
- **Foreign policy / defense:** the pamphlet criticizes the administration for "more wars" [S1]; voted for the 2024 Israel and Ukraine security supplementals (see Record) [S9][S10].
- **Elections and money in politics:** has voted for legislation to "end partisan gerrymandering, require disclosure of dark money, expand opportunities to vote" [S2].

## Record
- Voted No on the House motion to concur in the Senate amendment to H.R. 1, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 3, 2025; passed 218-214) [S6].
- Voted Nay on S. 5, the Laken Riley Act, which requires detention of certain noncitizens charged with theft-related crimes (January 22, 2025; passed 263-156) [S7].
- Voted Yea on H.R. 5376, the Inflation Reduction Act (August 12, 2022; passed 220-207) [S8].
- Voted Yea on H.R. 8034, Israel security supplemental appropriations (April 20, 2024; passed 366-58) [S9], and on H.R. 8035, Ukraine security supplemental appropriations (April 20, 2024; passed 311-112) [S10].
- Lead Democrat on Transportation & Infrastructure; his office reports $8.2 billion in Bipartisan Infrastructure Law funding invested in Washington as of January 2024 [S5].
