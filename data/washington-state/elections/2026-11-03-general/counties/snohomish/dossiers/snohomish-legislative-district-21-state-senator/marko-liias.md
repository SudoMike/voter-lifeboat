## Background
Marko Liias is the incumbent Democratic State Senator for the 21st Legislative District, in the Senate since 2014 and currently chair of the Senate Transportation Committee; he previously served as Majority Floor Leader [S1]. He was a State Representative 2008-2014 and a Mukilteo City Councilmember 2006-2008 [S1]. He lists work as communications manager for the Snohomish County Executive and as an adjunct professor at Everett Community College, and a BA from Georgetown and an MPA from the University of Washington [S1]. He grew up in the district (Kamiak High School) [S1][S2].

## Positions
- **Housing:** calls himself "a passionate voice in Olympia for affordable housing," says he won resources for "hundreds of units" for working families, vulnerable adults and seniors, and has "demanded more housing near growing light rail and transit" [S1].
- **Taxes:** says that this year he "voted to reduce taxes for small businesses" and to provide relief through the working family tax credit [S1]. Record: voted for ESSB 6346, the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million [S5], and for ESSB 5813 (2025), which made the capital gains and estate taxes more progressive [S7].
- **Spending / investment:** chairs the committee "creating thousands of jobs fixing our roads, bridges, and infrastructure" [S1]; his site lists the Move Ahead Washington transportation package and school funding bills (special education, materials and operating costs) among his achievements [S3].
- **Climate:** his site lists the Low Carbon Fuel Standard (HB 1091, 2021), building-emissions standards (SB 5722, 2022), greenhouse-gas tracking (SB 5036, 2025) and the 2025 clean fuels bill (HB 1409) as achievements, and says the state must keep "reducing carbon emissions" [S3]. He voted for 2SHB 1409 [S10].
- **Transportation and safety:** says traffic safety is his main transportation focus, with fatalities "at a 30-year high"; lists a 2025 catalytic-converter theft law [S3].
- **Technology:** says he is "working to pass legislation to support local journalism by taxing large social media platforms and search engines" and lists media-literacy and digital-citizenship laws [S3]; prime sponsor of SB 5346 (2026), restricting student mobile device use in public schools [S11][S3].
- **Immigration:** says he is "committed to protecting our economy and families from dangerous federal policies" and cites advocacy for "immigrant neighbors" [S1].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** cites his advocacy for "LGBTQ+ ... neighbors" [S1]; his site promises to "tear down barriers and guarantee every Washingtonian is welcome and respected" [S2].
- **Parents and schools:** voted for ESHB 1296 (2025), which amended the I-2081 list of parental rights [S6]; prime sponsor of SB 5346 (student cellphone limits) [S11]. No campaign statement on parental notification or opt-outs found.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted for ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal, on Senate passage and final passage [S5].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** no statement found. Record: voted for ESHB 1296 (2025), the amendments I-1 would repeal [S6].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** no statement found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record

- Voted yea on ESSB 5813 (capital gains and estate tax rate structure; Senate final passage 2025-04-24, 27-21) [S7].
- Voted yea on ESHB 1296 (Senate passage 2025-04-11, 30-19) [S6].
- Voted yea on EHB 1217, limiting rent and fee increases (Senate final passage on the conference report 2025-04-27, 27-20) [S8].
- Voted yea on ESHB 2266 (2026), which encourages permanent supportive, transitional and emergency housing and shelters (Senate 2026-03-04, 29-20) [S9].
- Voted yea on 2SHB 1409, the clean fuels program bill (Senate 2025-04-15, 25-23) [S10].
- Prime sponsor of SB 5346 (2026), restricting mobile device use by public school students [S11].
