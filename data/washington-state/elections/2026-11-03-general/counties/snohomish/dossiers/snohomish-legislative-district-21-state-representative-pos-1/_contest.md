## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members elected from each legislative district to the 98-member House, for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the state budgets, including K-12 funding, the tax structure, housing and land-use rules for cities, and criminal-justice statutes (local-voters-pamphlet page 30).

## District and race dynamics
Two Democrats advanced from the primary. Incumbent Strom Peterson chairs the House Housing Committee and also holds a seat on the Snohomish County Council (strom-peterson.md S1). Challenger Jason Moon is Mukilteo City Council President and pledges to resign that seat if elected; he makes Peterson's two offices a central issue (jason-moon.md S1, S2).

The race turns on state versus local control of housing and on taxes. Peterson was prime sponsor of ESHB 2266 (2026), which requires local governments to allow supportive housing and shelters, co-sponsored 2025 rent stabilization, and co-sponsored and voted for the Millionaire's Tax (strom-peterson.md S2, S5, S7, S8). Moon says "state mandated density threatens the character of our communities," wants more local control, and says the state has "a spending problem," not a revenue problem (jason-moon.md S1, S2). Peterson reports $116.8k raised to Moon's $52.3k (strom-peterson.md S10; jason-moon.md S3). Neither candidate has stated a position on the three statewide initiatives.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Local control:** Peterson sponsored a state siting mandate for supportive housing; Moon wants local governments to decide density.
2. **Taxes:** Peterson co-sponsored and voted for the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million; Moon pledges to protect taxpayers from higher taxes.
3. **Spending:** Peterson cites Housing Trust Fund investment; Moon calls for balanced, accountable budgeting.
4. **Record vs. renewal:** long-tenured legislator and committee chair vs. a city council president running on "fresh perspectives."
5. **Housing:** Peterson's record is rent stabilization plus public housing investment and supply bills; Moon frames housing as a local planning matter.
