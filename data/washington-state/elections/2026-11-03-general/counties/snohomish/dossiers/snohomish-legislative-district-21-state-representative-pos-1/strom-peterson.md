## Background
Strom Peterson is the incumbent Democratic State Representative for the 21st District, Position 1, and chairs the House Housing Committee; he also sits on Appropriations and Civil Rights & Judiciary [S1]. He is also a sitting Snohomish County Councilmember (chair of Community Safety and Justice, Board of Health and Community Transit board member) and a former Edmonds City Councilmember and council president [S1]. He is a small-business owner (restaurants and a cheese shop in Edmonds) and holds a BA from the University of New Mexico [S1].

## Positions
- **Housing:** describes "the three S's of housing– stabilization, supply, and support": says "we passed rent stabilization, capping rental increases at 7% plus CPI (not to exceed 10%)," middle-housing and ADU legislation for supply, the Covenant Homeownership Act, and "nearly a billion dollars" for the Housing Trust Fund [S2]. As Housing chair, says he has "led efforts to improve affordability, reduce homelessness, and keep people in their homes" [S1].
- **Taxes:** "This year I was proud to cosponsor and vote for the Millionaire's Tax to fix our outdated tax code," which he says will fund K-12, health care, higher education, the working families tax credit and free school meals [S2]. Record: voted for ESSB 6346 [S5].
- **Public safety:** cites gun laws passed (assault-weapon and high-capacity-magazine sales bans, ghost guns, permit to purchase) and says he will "continue to lead on fighting substance use disorder, investing in more behavioral and mental health services" [S2]; pamphlet cites investments in "behavioral health, public safety" [S1].
- **Climate and environment:** pamphlet lists "protecting our environment" and Conservation Action's endorsement and a board seat with the Leadership Alliance Against Coal [S1]; voted for the 2025 clean fuels bill (2SHB 1409) [S9].
- **Local control:** prime sponsor of ESHB 2266 (2026), "Encouraging permanent supportive housing, transitional housing, indoor emergency housing, and indoor emergency shelters" [S8]; his campaign site links a Washington State Standard report that the new law "will require local governments to allow housing for people experiencing homelessness in residential areas" [S11]. Also cites state middle-housing legislation [S2].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** "defended reproductive freedom" [S1]; his site says abortion protection is "something we must continue fighting for" and pledges "to fight for our LGBTQ friends and neighbors" [S3]; endorsed by Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates [S1].
- **Parents and schools:** pamphlet lists "stronger schools" [S1]. Record: co-sponsor of and yea vote on ESHB 1296 (2025), which amended the I-2081 parental-rights list [S6]. No statement on notification or opt-outs found.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (income tax repeal):** no statement naming the initiative found as of 2026-10-08; co-sponsored, voted for and campaigns on the Millionaire's Tax that I-645 would repeal [S2][S5].
- **I-1 (parental rights):** no statement found; co-sponsored and voted for ESHB 1296, which I-1 would repeal [S6].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no statement found.

## Record
- Voted yea on ESSB 6346 (House final passage 2026-03-09, 51-46) [S5].
- Co-sponsor of EHB 1217 (2025), limiting rent and fee increases; voted yea on House passage and on the conference report (2025-04-27, 54-44) [S7].
- Prime sponsor of ESHB 2266 (2026); House final passage 2026-03-09, 56-39 [S8].
- Co-sponsor of ESHB 1296 (2025); voted yea on final passage 2025-04-24, 59-39 [S6].
- Voted yea on 2SHB 1409 (2025-04-21, 54-43) [S9].
- PDC (data updated 2026-10-05): $116,764.49 in contributions and $48,742.40 in expenditures [S10].
