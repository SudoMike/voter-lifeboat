## Background
Tim Hazelo is a Republican candidate from Oak Harbor. His pamphlet statement cites 38 years of Navy and government-contracting work (USS Nimitz, VP-40/69, NAS Whidbey), including work as an intelligence analyst and combat simulation technician, as a functional test technician on the Boeing 787, teaching aviation maintenance, and as a small business owner and budget consultant [S1]. His campaign site says he joined the Navy in July 1987 and has lived on Whidbey Island for 30 years, where he and his wife run a small farm [S4][S2]. His elected experience is listed as union steward (International Association of Machinists), 2020 congressional candidate and precinct committee officer [S1]. The Everett Herald reported that he works as a truck driver, was previously Island County Republican Party chairman and is now its state committeeman [S6].

The Herald also reported that after he refused to wear a mask while observing 2024 ballot counting, prosecutors charged him with disorderly conduct (later dropped), first-degree criminal trespass and unauthorized access to a voting center; a jury found him guilty in July 2025, a judge vacated the trespass conviction, he was sentenced to 40 hours of community service and $700 in fees, and the case is on appeal [S6].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** "No income taxes on Washington State residents!" and will "fight to protect working families from higher taxes" [S3]. The Herald reported he is running "against any taxes the population doesn't vote on" [S6]. His statement calls for stopping "unsustainable spending that has led to ever-increasing taxation" [S1].
- **Spending:** will "advocate for audits, accountable spending and transparency of every dollar spent" [S3]; "Why is it that we spend all this money on all these social programs, and yet we can't help anybody?" [S6].
- **Housing and regulation:** will "eliminate the red tape and over-bearing government regulations" to lower housing costs [S3]; statement calls to "reduce unnecessary and burdensome regulations that drive up the cost of housing" [S1].
- **Public safety:** "end cashless bail" [S1]; the Herald reported he favors judicial reform and ending "catch and release" [S6].
- **Ferries and energy:** opposes the state's electric ferries ("They're going to be less dependable, and they're going to cost more money") and favors diesel-electric ferries built locally [S6]; supports "smart, financially responsible solutions to restore our ferry system, roads, and bridges" [S3].
- **Economy:** "strengthen our partnership with NAS Whidbey Island and the Navy" [S3].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no position found.
- **Parents and schools:** his site lists "protecting our children from dangerous policies" without naming the policies [S2]. No specific position on parental notification, record access or opt-outs found.
