## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House, for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including taxes, K-12 funding, housing and land-use rules and health policy.

## District and race dynamics
The 39th District covers rural and small-city east and north Snohomish County (Lake Stevens, Arlington, Sultan) and eastern Skagit County (Sedro-Woolley) (sam-low.md S1, S3). Republican Sam Low, a two-term incumbent and former Snohomish County councilmember who ranks on the House Housing and Transportation committees, faces Democrat Kathryn Lewandowsky, a registered nurse and first-time candidate (sam-low.md S1, S5; kathryn-lewandowsky.md S1). Low has raised about $152k to Lewandowsky's $9.4k (sam-low.md S11; kathryn-lewandowsky.md S5).

Low runs on tax relief, transportation, housing supply and public safety; he voted against the 2026 tax on income over $1 million and led on rural ADU legislation (sam-low.md S1, S2, S3). Lewandowsky runs on single-payer health care, housing as a human right and a pause on data centers (kathryn-lewandowsky.md S1, S2, S3). Neither has stated a position on I-645, I-1 or I-638.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Healthcare:** Lewandowsky supports a state single-payer system; Low has no stated single-payer position.
2. **Taxes and spending:** Low voted against ESSB 6346 and ESSB 5814 and says the state has "a spending problem"; Lewandowsky calls for "fair taxation" without specifics.
3. **Housing:** Low's agenda is supply and deregulation and he voted against 2025 rent-increase limits; Lewandowsky calls housing a human right and backs tenant protections.
4. **Climate and energy:** Low criticizes CCA and fuel costs and voted against the clean-fuels expansion; Lewandowsky supports accelerating the clean-energy transition.
5. **Tech:** Lewandowsky wants data-center growth stopped until protections are law.
6. **Record vs. renewal:** incumbent with enacted bills vs. a first-time candidate.
