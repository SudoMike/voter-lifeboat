## Background
Sam Low (R-Lake Stevens) is the incumbent State Representative for the 39th Legislative District, Position 1, finishing his second term [S1][S5]. He previously served three terms on the Snohomish County Council and on the Lake Stevens City Council (Council President/Mayor Pro Tem), chaired the Transportation Improvement Board (2024-2026) and the Traffic Safety Commission (2025-2026), and lists work as a small-business owner and elementary school teacher [S1]. He is ranking Republican on the House Housing Committee and assistant ranking Republican on Transportation, and sits on Health Care & Wellness [S5][S12]. The legislator, the former county councilmember and the pamphlet candidate are the same person: the pamphlet lists both roles and the legislature's member page lists District 39 Position 1 [S1][S5].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** pamphlet names "providing tax relief" as a priority [S1]. His 2026 session recap calls ESSB 6346 "the state income tax bill," says income taxes are always sold as narrow before thresholds fall, and describes House Republicans' 24-hour floor fight and amendments to require voter approval, cut the rate to 1% and exempt retirement and military income [S3][S4].
- **Spending:** says the 2026 supplemental budget "spends $4.9 billion more than the state is expected to collect," relies on reserves and one-time money, and that "Washington does not have a revenue problem. It has a spending problem" [S3]. Opposed HB 2034's use of LEOFF Plan 1 surplus to ease budget pressure [S3].
- **Housing:** frames the housing crisis as caused by policies "that have made housing harder to build, slower to permit, and more expensive"; "If Washington wants lower housing costs, it needs more housing" [S3]. Caucus page cites regulation as 24% of new-home costs and pledges to "remove barriers, speed up permitting" [S12].
- **Transportation and climate:** says drivers pay "an estimated 52 cents more per gallon because of the CCA" and that Climate Commitment Act revenue should go to road and bridge preservation; cites "$1.29 per gallon in gas taxes, carbon taxes and fees" as part of the affordability crisis [S3].
- **Public safety:** pamphlet lists "prioritizing public safety" and endorsements from law enforcement [S1]. Sponsored HB 2441 (health-insurance reimbursement for survivors of officers and firefighters killed in the line of duty) and HB 2192 (traffic-fatality review by the Traffic Safety Commission) [S3].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no statement found.
- **Parents and schools:** no campaign statement found. Record: voted no on ESHB 1296 (see Record) [S6].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08 (his caucus site is frozen by election-year restrictions [S12]). Record: voted no on ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S2], and publicly opposed it [S3][S4].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** no public position found. Record: voted no on ESHB 1296 (2025), the amendments to the I-2081 list that I-1 would repeal [S6].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** no public position found.

## Record
- Voted nay on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million (2026-03-09, passed 51-46) [S2].
- Voted nay on ESSB 5814 (2025, excise taxes on select services; House final passage 2025-04-23, 50-47) [S9].
- Voted nay on EHB 1217 (2025 rent and fee increase limits) on House passage (2025-03-10) and on the conference report (2025-04-27, passed 54-44) [S7].
- Voted nay on 2SHB 1409 (2025 clean fuels program expansion), final passage 2025-04-21, 54-43 [S8].
- Voted nay on ESHB 1296 (2025, amendments to the I-2081 parental-rights list), final passage 2025-04-24, 59-39 [S6].
- Prime sponsor of EHB 1345 (detached ADUs outside urban growth areas), passed the House 86-5 on 2026-01-22 and signed into law [S10][S3]; also HB 2192 and HB 2441, both signed [S3]. Pamphlet says 12 of his bills became law in 2025-2026 [S1].
- PDC (updated 2026-09-22): $151,958.30 raised and $123,699.84 spent for 2026 [S11].

## Endorsements
- Pamphlet: over 30 Republican House members, law enforcement, labor, Sen. Keith Wagoner, Rep. Carolyn Eslick and former Lake Stevens councilmember Steve Ewing [S1].
