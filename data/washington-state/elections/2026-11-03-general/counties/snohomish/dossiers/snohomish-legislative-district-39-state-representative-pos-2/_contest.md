## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House, for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets, including taxes, K-12 funding, housing and land-use rules and health policy.

## District and race dynamics
This is an open seat: Republican Rep. Carolyn Eslick is not seeking reelection (steve-ewing.md S2). Democrat Ida Keeley, a former juvenile court administrator and DCYF administrator running for office for the first time, faces Republican Steve Ewing, a Department of Corrections hearings officer and former Lake Stevens councilmember (2019-2025) endorsed by the district's Republican delegation (ida-keeley.md S1; steve-ewing.md S1, S2). Ewing has raised about $123k and Keeley about $68k (steve-ewing.md S3; ida-keeley.md S6).

Ewing runs on "normal" government: low taxes, balanced budgets, public safety and schools that "focus on teaching" (steve-ewing.md S1, S2). Keeley runs on affordability, school and special-education funding, public safety that combines accountability and prevention, and a pause on large data centers (ida-keeley.md S2). Neither has stated a position on I-645, I-1 or I-638.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes and spending:** Ewing pledges low taxes and says he opposed tax increases; Keeley has no tax position and emphasizes protecting programs and funding schools.
2. **Public safety:** Ewing calls statewide limits on police "extremism"; Keeley pairs law-enforcement staffing with prevention and treatment.
3. **Tech:** Keeley supports a pause on high-capacity data centers and enforceable AI standards; Ewing has no stated position.
4. **Experience:** a former city councilmember vs. a first-time candidate with a long public-administration career.
