## Background
June Robinson is the incumbent Democratic State Senator for the 38th Legislative District and chair of the Senate Ways & Means Committee [S1][S5]. She served in the House from 2013 until May 2020, when she was appointed to the Senate, and also sits on the Senate Health & Long-Term Care Committee [S5]. She works at the Snohomish County Health Department; earlier she worked at Public Health Seattle and King County, was executive director of the Housing Consortium of Everett and Snohomish County, and was a planning director at the Community Health Center of Snohomish County [S1]. She holds a Master of Public Health from the University of Michigan [S1]. The district includes Everett, Marysville and the Tulalip Reservation [S5]. In the August 4 primary she took 66.2% to Republican Brad Bender's 33.6% in the count the Everett Herald reported [S12].

Note: her campaign website's issue text partly dates from earlier campaigns (it calls her Vice Chair of Ways & Means and carries a stale judicial endorsement banner) [S2][S3]; the pamphlet [S1] and Senate page [S5] are the current descriptions.

## Positions
- **Taxes:** as "the Senate lead on budget and tax reform," says she "helped push generational change to our regressive tax code, asking millionaires to pay a fair share while winning needed relief for workers and small businesses" [S1]. Her campaign site says she will "continue to reform our regressive tax system and close unfair loopholes" [S2], and describes her prime-sponsored SB 5096 as a tax on the sale of stocks and bonds that helps "rebuild our regressive tax code" [S3].
- **Spending/budget:** cites "record investments in affordable housing and behavioral health services, childcare for working families, and access to health care" [S1]; says a fair tax system and budget can sustain investments in education, job training, homelessness and addiction and mental health services [S2].
- **Housing:** longtime affordable-housing advocate who says she will fight for "stable, quality homes for low wage workers, struggling families, and anyone at risk of losing shelter" [S2]; former director of the Housing Consortium of Everett and Snohomish County [S1].
- **Health care:** relies on her public-health background and cites access to health care and prescription drug price transparency [S1][S3]; sponsored SB 5751 on hospital staffing [S3].
- **Public safety/guns:** campaign site lists "passing common sense gun safety laws" [S2].
- **Immigration:** says "we need principled voices to defend our democracy, immigrant and LGBTQ+ neighbors" [S1].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** pamphlet pledges to "safeguard reproductive rights" and defend LGBTQ+ neighbors [S1]. Her campaign site says "Healthcare – including reproductive care – is a human right," that she will "protect the right to an abortion in our state," and objects to attacks on "care for transgender people" [S3]. Endorsed by Planned Parenthood and NARAL Pro-Choice WA [S1][S4].
- **Parents and schools:** campaign site says she "was proud to help pass Senate Bill 5395 which brought medically accurate, consent focused sex education to Washington public schools" [S3]; supports smaller classes, higher teacher pay and school nurses and counselors [S3]. Voted for ESHB 1296 in the Senate (see Record) [S9].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Record: co-sponsored and voted for SB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal [S6][S7], and her pamphlet statement presents it as her tax-reform achievement [S1].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted yea in the Senate on ESHB 1296 (2025), the amendments to the I-2081 parental-rights law that I-1 would repeal [S9].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** no position on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- Chair, Senate Ways & Means; the Senate passed its 2025-27 supplemental operating budget proposal (about $79.3 billion) on Feb. 27, 2026 [S5].
- Secondary sponsor of SB 6346 (2026 tax on income over $1 million) [S7]; voted yea on Senate passage (2026-02-16, 27-22) and on final passage as amended by the House (2026-03-11, 27-21) [S6].
- Prime sponsor of SB 5096 (2021), the capital gains excise tax [S8][S3].
- Voted yea on Senate passage of ESHB 1296 (2025-04-11, 30-19) [S9].
- Voted yea on EHB 1217 (2025 cap on residential rent increases): Senate passage as amended (2025-04-10, 29-20) and the conference report (2025-04-27, 27-20) [S10].
- PDC (data updated 2026-10-05): $225,478.96 in contributions and $167,453.90 in expenditures for 2026 [S11].
