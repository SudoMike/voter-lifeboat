## What it does

The ballot title asks whether Bothell should "be annexed into and become a part of the Shoreline Fire Department Regional Fire Authority, effective March 1, 2027, pursuant to the plan amendment, as provided in Resolution No. 1784" [S1]. This entry covers Snohomish County voters in Bothell; King County voters in Bothell vote on the same question from King County's pamphlet.

- If approved, the Bothell Fire Department would become part of the larger regional fire authority (RFA) instead of operating as a standalone department. The explanatory statement says annexation "will not change the delivery or the level of services to Bothell residents" [S1].
- Bothell voters would be "entitled to vote on all ballot measures proposed by the RFA-including those pertaining to taxation and benefit charges," and Bothell would have representation on the RFA board [S1].
- The RFA FAQ says the nine-member board would have three Shoreline fire commissioners, three Lake Forest Park and Kenmore commissioners and three Bothell City Councilmembers [S2].
- Firefighters would move into the RFA with equivalent positions, pay and benefits, and residents would keep being served from existing stations [S2].
- Each annexation measure (Bothell, and Snohomish County Fire District 10) needs a simple majority in its own jurisdiction; current Shoreline RFA voters do not vote [S2].
- If rejected, "Bothell would need to reseek voter approval, negotiate a new agreement with RFA or another agency, or reconstitute the Bothell Fire Department" [S1].

## Cost

The ballot title and explanatory statement state no tax rate or dollar figure [S1].

- The RFA FAQ says financial impacts "would begin in 2028" and will be finalized in the RFA's 2028 budget; Bothell now funds fire service through its general fund and "has indicated that it intends to reduce its general-fund property tax rate to reflect the change in fire service delivery," with details to come from the city [S2].
- The FAQ's preliminary estimates (2026 dollars) give savings figures for Shoreline, Lake Forest Park, Kenmore and Sno10 taxpayers but none for Bothell [S2].
- The city's study page says "The exact financial impacts of moving to a regional fire services model are still being evaluated" (as read on 2026-10-08) [S3].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- Bothell's fire service is now a city department funded from the general fund [S2]. Joining moves it into an RFA that levies its own property tax and fire benefit charge, which Bothell voters would vote on as RFA voters [S1][S2].
- The FAQ says planning calls for one additional Bothell station whether or not Bothell joins; if it joins, the RFA board would decide funding, from fire impact fees plus a voter-approved bond estimated at $0.03 to $0.058 per $1,000, with a bond measure under consideration for 2027 [S2].
- The FAQ identifies about $3 million a year in potential efficiencies from combining organizations [S2].

## Arguments for

From the statement for, by Wesley Wang, Brandon Keith and Mason Thompson [S1]:

- "As Bothell grows, our fire and emergency medical response needs to grow with us." The Bothell Professional Firefighters Association, IAFF Local 2099, endorses the measure.
- "Regional fire service is the standard model in our area"; a larger organization can share resources and avoid duplicating administration and equipment.
- Sharing future facility and equipment costs "across a larger regional tax base lightens the financial impact on our city."
- "With a Yes vote, we retain a significant amount of control," with "three of the nine seats on the RFA's governing board."

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed; the pamphlet says no person in the jurisdiction contacted the Auditor's office to provide one [S1]. The city reports "There were no applicants for the Con Committee" [S3].

## Context / Coverage

- On July 21, 2026 the Bothell City Council "unanimously approved a resolution to place a measure on the November 3, 2026, General Election ballot" [S3].
- The city says Bothell is "one of only eight cities in King and Snohomish Counties that still operates its own fire department" [S3].
- The Shoreline RFA already provides executive fire administration, operations and support management and fleet maintenance for Bothell and Sno10, and its chief serves as their fire chief [S2].

## Axis notes

- **local-control:** YES moves fire and EMS governance from the Bothell City Council to a nine-member regional board on which Bothell appoints three seats [S1][S2]. That is a regional-consolidation case. The statement for frames it as keeping "a significant amount of control" [S1].
- **taxes:** YES puts the RFA levy and fire benefit charge on Bothell property from 2028; the city intends to cut its own general levy, but no net figure is stated in the sources read here [S2][S3]. The direction (more property-tax authority outside the city) is clear; the size is not.
- **spending:** framed as maintaining current service levels [S1], not expanding programs.
