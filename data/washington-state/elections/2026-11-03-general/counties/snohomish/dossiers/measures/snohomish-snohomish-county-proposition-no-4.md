## What it does

Proposition No. 4 adds a section to Article 6 of the charter requiring Snohomish County "to make available, where reasonably practicable through existing systems or future modernization efforts, all financial information legally available within the county" [S1].

- **Scope of data.** New Section 6.130 requires "publicly accessible, searchable and downloadable financial transparency tools" built from information the county already collects in budgeting, procurement, contracting, grant administration, financial management and auditing [S3]. Where legally permissible and reasonably practicable, the information includes the purpose of funded activities, amounts and expenditures, recipient names and locations, agreement terms, administering department, the county fund or budget line, and county audits, monitoring reports and oversight findings [S1][S3].
- **No fee.** Information must be searchable and accessible "without fee" [S1][S3].
- **Implementation limits.** Implementation "shall prioritize the use of existing systems and processes, avoid unnecessary duplication of reporting requirements, minimize administrative burden on contractors and funding recipients, and allow phased implementation" as systems are modernized [S3].

## Cost

No tax or appropriation in the ballot title [S1]. The statement against says the county's chief budget officer "testified that a public portal was not in the project's scope and that adding one would be expensive and technically complex"; the county is mid-way through "a fifteen-million-dollar financial system overhaul" [S2]. No cost estimate is in the official text [S1][S3].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

No existing charter section on financial transparency [S3]. Obligations are qualified by "reasonably practicable" and "to the greatest extent practicable," with phased implementation [S3].

## Arguments for

From the statement written by Danny Perkins and Janelle M. Cass [S2]:

- "Public dollars belong to the people, and every resident deserves clear, straightforward access to how those dollars are spent—without needing special expertise or filing formal requests."
- It "ensures future technology and financial systems are built with the public in mind" and "can also reduce the time and cost of responding to individual records requests."
- Rebuttal: "Ordinances can change. A Charter commitment lasts."

The Herald reports Commissioner Janelle Cass sponsored it, citing "open checkbook" portals elsewhere, and that it was drafted with the county's chief budget officer [S4].

## Arguments against

From the statement written by Doris Fulton and Janice R. Greene [S2]:

- "Proposition 4 promises a financial transparency portal and the goal is worth supporting. But this measure does not deliver transparency."
- "It asks the county to improve public access to financial data only where 'reasonably practicable.' Those are gentle words with no force behind them."
- "If the Council wants a transparency portal, it can pass an ordinance and fund it in the open."
