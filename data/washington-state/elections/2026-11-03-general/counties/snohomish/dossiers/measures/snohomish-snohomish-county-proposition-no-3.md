## What it does

Proposition No. 3 "would require that all candidates for a Snohomish County elected office appear on the ballot without a political party designation and that all elected county offices be non-partisan offices," amending charter Sections 4.15 and 4.80 [S1].

- **Which offices change.** The explanatory statement says the County Council, Executive and Prosecutor are partisan today [S1]. The current Section 4.15 already makes the treasurer, auditor, assessor, sheriff and clerk nonpartisan [S3].
- **Phase-in.** Under new transitional Section 11.090, council districts 2 and 3 and the Executive would first run without party labels in 2027, council districts 1, 4 and 5 in 2029, and the Prosecuting Attorney in 2030 [S3]. The transitional sections expire January 1, 2030 [S3].
- **Vacancies.** Section 4.80 now has the council fill a partisan vacancy from three names submitted by the county central committee of the departing official's party. The amendment deletes that procedure, so the council would fill all vacancies [S3].

## Cost

No tax, rate or appropriation [S1][S3].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

Replaces partisan ballot designations for three offices and the party-nominee vacancy procedure (Sections 4.15 and 4.80) [S3].

## Arguments for

From the statement written by Todd Welch, Mark James and Shawn O'Donnell [S2]:

- "County government exists to deliver essential local services - roads, parks, land use, and more. These are not partisan functions."
- It "extends the nonpartisan approach already used successfully by the Snohomish County Treasurer, Auditor, Assessor, Sheriff, and Clerk, as well as by every Mayor and City Councilmember in Snohomish County."
- "Partisan labels discourage well-qualified community leaders who are not closely aligned with a particular party from running." Parties "remain free to recruit, endorse, and support candidates publicly."

The Herald reports Commissioner Mark James sponsored it and said removing party letters lets voters "look past the letter to find out who this person is" [S4].

## Arguments against

From the statement written by Anna Maria Jackson Laurence, Janice R. Greene and James Kenny [S2]:

- "This proposition is anti-transparency. In Initiative 872 in 2004, the People of Washington chose to allow disclosure of a candidate's political party for all county elected offices."
- "Removing party affiliation does not eliminate influence, it hides it. Name recognition, incumbency, and campaign spending fill the vacuum."
- Rebuttal: "Snohomish County elected offices have disclosed political affiliation since 1892."
