## What it does

Proposition 265 amends charter Sections 3.4, 3.5, 4.13, 11.6, 13.9, 15.5, 16.2 and 16.3. The ballot title says it would allow the city "to publish those notices, appointments, ordinances and proposed ordinances, proposed initiatives and referenda, bid advertisements, and franchise on City's website instead of requiring this information to be published in a newspaper, while also having this information available in the office of the City Clerk for examination by the public" [S1].

- **Mechanism.** The amended sections add "or on the City's website" alongside the City official newspaper; for proposed charter amendments, publication once a week for two weeks in the newspaper "or for two (2) successive weeks on the City's official website" [S2]. Newspaper publication remains allowed, not required [S2].

## Cost

No tax or rate [S1]. The statement for says it "reduces unnecessary print costs" [S1]; no dollar estimate is given.

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

Replaces newspaper-only publication requirements dating from the 1968 charter [S1][S2].

## Arguments for

From the statement written by Alice Hedges, Mason Rutledge and Todd Welch [S1]:

- "Everett's City Charter was written for a print-only era."
- Notices online "are available instantly, 24/7, to anyone with a phone or computer." "This change does not shrink transparency — it expands it."
- The statement says it updates "nine sections"; the ballot title lists eight [S1].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed. The pamphlet says no person in the jurisdiction contacted the Auditor's office to provide a statement [S1].

## Campaign committees and money (PDC)

As of 2026-10-08 the PDC summary listed no 2026 committee for or against any Everett charter proposition; the only 2026 committee with an Everett jurisdiction was Citizens for Everett Public Schools [S4].

## Context / Coverage

- **How it got here.** Everett's charter (adopted 1968) is reviewed every 10 years by a Charter Review Committee; the City Council adopted the committee's recommendations, with its own changes, in Ordinance No. 4189-26, passed July 15, 2026, sending Propositions 261-265 to the ballot [S1][S2]. The Herald reports the council forwarded most of the 15-member committee's recommendations, rejected a proposal to review the charter every five years instead of ten, and voted 5-2 against adding term limits, which the committee had not advanced [S3].
- **Passage.** Simple majority of Everett voters; ratified amendments take effect on the date of the county's certification of the election [S2].
- **City intent.** The Herald reports city officials said they would likely keep publishing notices in the newspaper, but the change gives flexibility [S3].
