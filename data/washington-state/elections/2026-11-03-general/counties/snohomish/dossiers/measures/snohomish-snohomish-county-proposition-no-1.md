## What it does

Proposition No. 1 is a county charter amendment proposed by the Snohomish County Charter Review Commission. The ballot title says it "would require the county legislature and executive branch to prioritize and protect funding for the offices of county Assessor, Auditor, Clerk, Executive, Prosecuting Attorney, Sheriff, Treasurer, and the District and Superior Court Judges," by adding a new section to Article 6 of the charter [S1].

- **The policy.** New Section 6.140 requires the county to "adopt, maintain, and periodically update a Foundational Government Services Policy" that (1) prioritizes funding of state-mandated services, including the listed offices; (2) "Prioritizes the funding and delivery of foundational services before the expansion of discretionary programs"; (3) promotes structural budget balance and long-term fiscal sustainability; and (4) establishes policies for reserves, revenue stabilization and financial planning [S3].
- **Implementation.** The County Council must adopt the implementing policies by ordinance [S1][S3].
- **Definition.** Foundational government services "include but are not limited to" public safety, prosecutor, law enforcement, courts, clerk and corrections; public works and infrastructure; public health and human services; emergency management and disaster response; and core administrative and financial functions [S3].
- **Passage.** A charter amendment needs a simple majority of county voters.

## Cost

The ballot title and explanatory statement name no tax, rate or dollar amount [S1]. The statement for says the measure "does not raise taxes and does not dictate annual spending levels" [S2].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

The charter has no comparable section today; the amendment text lists no existing sections amended or repealed [S3]. It sets a funding priority and requires a council policy; it does not appropriate money or set amounts for any office [S2][S3].

## Arguments for

From the statement written by Brian Sullivan [S2]:

- The measure "establishes a common-sense principle: fund essential county services before expanding discretionary programs."
- It promotes "balanced budgets, reserve policies, and fiscal sustainability."
- Rebuttal: "Essential services are mandated, but they are often underfunded." It "neither guarantees any office a specific amount nor removes the County Council's authority to adopt budgets in public."

The Herald reports the proposal was sponsored by Commissioner Shawn O'Donnell and brought to the commission by County Treasurer Brian Sullivan, who said: "During the good times, you have to prepare for the bad times. But you also have to fund basic county services" [S4].

## Arguments against

From the statement written by Christine Eck and Janice Green [S2]:

- The named offices "are already required by the state constitution, state law, and county code"; the measure "restates existing duties in language nobody can define."
- "The commission's own attorney warned that one person's idea of full funding will not match another's, and those disagreements become costly and end up in court."
- "Commissioners on the sponsoring body said the wording was too broad and would strip the Council of its budget authority. One commissioner who originally sponsored the measure withdrew support before the final vote."
- Rebuttal: the measure "never defines 'foundational' versus 'discretionary'" [S2].

## Axis notes

- **spending:** The amendment writes into the charter a priority for funding state-mandated "foundational services before the expansion of discretionary programs" and for "structural budget balance" and reserves [S3]. The pro statement frames it as "necessities come before extras" [S2]. That is a restraint-before-expansion principle (pole A), though it sets no spending level [S2].
- **taxes:** No tax effect; the pro statement says it "does not raise taxes" [S2].
- **local-control:** Not applicable in the rubric's sense: it allocates budget priority within county government, not between the state and local governments.
