## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would authorize South County Fire (SCF) "to replace, renovate and/or make seismic upgrades to aging fire stations, build additional fire stations, and construct, improve, acquire and/or equip facilities"; "issue up to $420,000,000 of general obligation bonds maturing within a maximum of 25 years; and levy annual excess property taxes to repay the bonds" [S1].

- The explanatory statement says SCF's capital facilities plan includes "replacing six aging fire stations; constructing four new fire stations to meet demand; seismic upgrades and improvements to four fire stations; and constructing or improving vehicle maintenance facilities, warehouses, classrooms, headquarters, and other facilities" [S1].
- The Herald lists the replacements as Lake Serene, Silver Firs, Esperance, Mill Creek, Blue Ridge (Lynnwood) and Station 22; renovations at Mariner, Lynnwood Civic Center, Edmonds Downtown and Mountlake Terrace plus headquarters; and four new stations (Manor Way near Lake Stickney, Alderwood Mall Parkway at 170th Street SW, near Swedish Edmonds, and Cascadian Way near 146th Street SE in Martha Lake) [S3].
- SCF serves Brier, Edmonds, Lynnwood, Mill Creek, Mountlake Terrace and unincorporated south Snohomish County [S2].
- As a bond, it needs 60% approval with the usual validation requirement (Washington bond rule; the Herald notes bonds require 60% in its school coverage, not repeated here).

## Cost

- "levies are expected to average approximately $0.194 per $1,000 of assessed value for the term of the bonds"; the exact annual amount depends on principal and interest rates when sold [S1].
- The Herald reports "an estimated $136 per year increase in property taxes for the average home valued at $700,000" [S3].
- The board chose a "levy-level" repayment model that "includes about $50 million in interest" but lessens the immediate impact on taxpayers; one commissioner opposed it: "I can't support an extra $50 million over the life of the bond" [S4].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- New voter-approved excess levy on top of SCF's existing levies and benefit charge [S1][S2]. The con statement notes RFAs "levy their own property taxes and benefit charge tax" and collect transport fees [S2].
- The con statement cites the capital plan's top priority as replacing six "high-risk" stations for $136.7 million and says $91.4 million is for headquarters, warehouses and service buildings [S2].

## Arguments for

From the statement for, by Shannon Sessions, Alex Johnson and Steve Barnes [S2]:

- "An independent assessment found that two-thirds of South County Fire stations need major upgrades or replacement, and nearly half could become inoperable after a major earthquake" [S2]; the Herald reports the same assessment [S3].
- "Emergency calls have increased by more than 30% over the past decade."
- Modern stations protect firefighters from carcinogens like diesel exhaust; investing now avoids costlier emergency repairs.
- Rebuttal: SCF "is already among the lowest-cost fire and EMS providers in Snohomish County"; voting no delays repairs and raises future costs.

## Arguments against

From the statement against, by Melinda Goforth, Mary Jane Goss and Jim Landers [S2]:

- The bond "with interest, exceeds half a billion-dollars"; "There is a limit to what households can pay."
- RFAs "have little incentive to contain costs"; the shift to a regional structure "has triggered significant cost increases rather than savings."
- Prioritize: rebuild the six high-risk stations ($136.7 million) and return to voters for future stations if population requires.
- Rebuttal: buying additional fire houses now "is unwise and unaffordable."

## Context / Coverage

- On May 20, 2026, Edmonds Mayor Mike Rosen asked the board "to reduce the bond amount to address immediate priorities" [S4]. The Board of Commissioners then "unanimously advanced the measure" in June [S4].
