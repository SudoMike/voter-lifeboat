## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would authorize the district to "add to and replace portions of Mukilteo, Serene Lake and Olivia Park Elementaries and Explorer Middle School; expand the Kamiak High School gymnasium; replace and improve track, field and athletic facilities; update technology systems; make safety and security upgrades; and upgrade building components, by issuing $400,000,000 of general obligation bonds maturing within 21 years" and levying excess property taxes to repay them [S1].

- The explanatory statement adds network cabling and a disaster-recovery data center [S1].
- The Herald reports $134.7 million for replacing Explorer Middle School within the package [S3].
- It is the same $400 million bond that received 57.2% in February 2026, short of the 60% bonds require [S3][S2].

## Cost

- The district estimates the excess levy rate "to average $0.57 per $1,000 of assessed property value" over the bonds' life [S1].
- The Herald reports the district's estimate that property taxes "would increase by approximately 20 cents per $1,000 of assessed home value, or about $132 per year for the median homeowner" [S3]. (The $0.57 is the average rate for this bond; the 20 cents is the estimated increase over current taxes.)
- The rebuttal to the statement against says it costs "the average homeowner about $11 a month, including interest" [S2].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- New 21-year bond debt repaid by excess levies [S1]. The statement for says approval makes the district eligible for about $30 million in state matching funds [S2].
- The statement against says the district is paying $9.6 million this year in interest on bonds issued 2016-2024 and estimates $10-15 million a year in interest on the new bonds [S2].

## Arguments for

From the statement for, by Judith M. Schwab, Nhi Pham and Angela Gottula [S2]:

- "Many of our schools were built before the Vietnam War"; an emergency pipe repair closed part of a school and a failed fire system cost $1.5 million.
- The bond replaces worn-out infrastructure "before emergencies force our hand" and unlocks about $30 million in state match.

## Arguments against

From the statement against, by Howard Damoff [S2]:

- "This exact proposition to take on additional debt already failed in February 2026."
- Spending is outpacing inflation while enrollment declined; seniors and young families face rising taxes.
- Use "interest-free capital-project levies" instead of bonds; interest sends money to institutional investors.
- Rebuttal: "Replacing four schools at once is too much. Enrollment has dropped."

## Context / Coverage

- District staff recommended running the bond again, hoping higher turnout and more time to explain it would help [S3].
