## What it does

Proposition 261 asks whether to amend charter Sections 2.2 and 4.10. The ballot title says it "would require candidates for Mayor and City Council to have registered to vote in the City of Everett and resided in the City (and district if applicable) for at least one year before the deadline for filing for office; and would prohibit elected officials from holding other elected offices simultaneously," and that Section 4.10 would require only elected officials to take an oath of office [S1].

- **Residency and registration.** Today a person must have been a registered voter and resident of the city for one year "next preceding his or her election," and a district councilmember a district resident for six months before the primary [S2]. The amendment requires one year as a registered city voter, and one year of city (Mayor, at-large) or district (district seats) residence, before the last day to file a declaration of candidacy; "residence" takes its meaning from RCW 29A.04.151 [S2].
- **No dual elected office.** New Section 2.2(c) bars the Mayor or a councilmember from simultaneously holding "any other elected public office at any level of government," naming county, state, other city, school, fire, port, PUD and special-district and federal offices. Holding both means the Everett office "is immediately deemed to have [been] vacated." Running for another office while serving is still allowed [S2].
- **Oath.** Section 4.10's oath requirement changes from "Every officer" to "Every elected City official" [S2].

## Cost

No tax or rate [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

Replaces the existing eligibility wording in Section 2.2 and the oath wording in Section 4.10 [S2].

## Arguments for

From the statement written by Todd Welch, Randy Bolerjack and Jonathan Peebles [S1]:

- "By ensuring the mayor and city council members hold only one elected office at a time, Proposition 261 protects the integrity of local service."
- "A year of residency shows real investment in Everett's people and challenges ... and it prevents last-minute moves made only to chase a title."
- "It remains fully inclusive: unhoused residents can still run as long as they live and are registered to vote where they file."

## Context / Coverage

- **How it got here.** Everett's charter (adopted 1968) is reviewed every 10 years by a Charter Review Committee; the City Council adopted the committee's recommendations, with its own changes, in Ordinance No. 4189-26, passed July 15, 2026, sending Propositions 261-265 to the ballot [S1][S2]. The Herald reports the council forwarded most of the 15-member committee's recommendations, rejected a proposal to review the charter every five years instead of ten, and voted 5-2 against adding term limits, which the committee had not advanced [S3].
- **Passage.** Simple majority of Everett voters; ratified amendments take effect on the date of the county's certification of the election [S2].
- **Why now.** The Herald reports the committee sought to clear up ambiguous eligibility language after a court in 2025 found council candidate Niko Battle ineligible over residency after he had won the primary; the council amended the committee's version to tie both requirements to the end of filing week [S3]. It notes that former councilmember (now State Rep.) Mary Fosse held council and legislative seats at the same time, and that county councilmembers Strom Peterson and Sam Low hold county and state seats simultaneously [S3].
- **Citation note.** The amended Section 2.2 text cites "RCW 28A.24.050" for the filing deadline [S2]; candidate filing periods are in Title 29A, so this appears to be a typographical reference in the ordinance.
