## What it does

Proposition No. 5 "would require a 4/5 supermajority council vote to assess, levy, or increase any councilmanic tax," amending charter Section 2.20 [S1].

- **The rule.** The amended text gives the council power to levy taxes, "provided that after January 1, 2027, any new councilmanic tax may be assessed, levied or increased only by a 4/5 supermajority affirmative vote of the Council" [S3].
- **What counts.** A "new councilmanic tax" is "a tax for which the county council has the authority to levy but has not or is granted to the county council after January 1, 2027." It excludes "fees, rates and charges, or special assessments" [S3].
- **What is exempt.** The section "does not apply to existing councilmanic taxes levied by the county council as of January 1, 2027, or any renewal or reauthorization of those taxes that does not increase the tax rate" [S3]. The explanatory statement summarizes it as requiring "any new tax that the Council can impose without a vote of the electorate be passed by a supermajority of the Council (4 out of 5 councilmembers)" [S1].
- **Voter-approved taxes** are not affected: it applies to taxes the council imposes without a public vote [S1][S4].

## Cost

No tax or rate change. The measure changes the council vote needed for certain future tax actions [S1][S3].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

Today Section 2.20 gives the council power "To levy taxes, appropriate revenue and adopt budgets" by ordinance with no supermajority requirement; the amendment adds the four-of-five proviso [S3]. Whether a rate increase on a tax already levied before 2027 is covered depends on reading the "new councilmanic tax" definition together with the exemption for renewals "that [do] not increase the tax rate" [S3]; the statements differ on how far it reaches (below).

## Arguments for

From the statement written by Todd Welch, Dan Perkins and Janelle Cass [S2]:

- "New and increased taxes hit hardest for our most vulnerable residents." The measure "strengthens accountability by requiring broad agreement among County Councilmembers before a new tax is created or an existing tax is increased without voter approval."
- "Proposition 5 preserves the County's ability to fund essential services and meet community needs. It simply raises the consensus standard."
- Rebuttal: it "doesn't reduce a single service."

The Herald reports Commissioner Janelle Cass sponsored it, saying council members should "build consensus before dipping back into the pockets of the taxpayer" [S4].

## Arguments against

From the statement written by Paula Rhyne, John Lovick and Mike Sells [S2]:

- "Proposition 5 creates minority veto power within the County Council... just two of the five councilmembers could overrule the majority."
- The council "regularly votes on levy decisions that fund the basic services ... including law enforcement, our court system, roads, veterans services, environmental conservation"; blocking them "could mean fewer sheriff deputies, reduced court operations, delayed road projects."
- Rebuttal: it "will jeopardize routine levy approvals." (The amendment text exempts existing taxes and their renewals at the same rate [S3].)

## Context / Coverage

- Advanced by the Charter Review Commission and forwarded unanimously by the County Council on June 3, 2026 [S4]. The Herald describes it as applying to councilmanic tax increases after January 1, 2027 [S4].

## Axis notes

- **taxes:** YES makes new non-voted county taxes, and (per the pro statement) increases, harder to enact by requiring four of five council votes [S1][S2][S3]. That is a structural limit on raising taxes (pole A). Its reach is narrower than the ballot title's "any councilmanic tax": existing taxes and same-rate renewals are exempt [S3].
- **spending:** Opponents say it could block funding for services; proponents say it cuts no service [S2]. Not a direct spending measure.
- **local-control:** Concerns the county council's internal voting rule, not state-local authority.
