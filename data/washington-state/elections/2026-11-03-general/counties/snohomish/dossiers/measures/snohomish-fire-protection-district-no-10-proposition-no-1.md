## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would authorize Snohomish County Fire Protection District No. 10 (Sno10, unincorporated area around Bothell) "to annex into Shoreline Fire Department Regional Fire Authority to provide fire and emergency medical services effective March 1, 2027," as described in the RFA plan amendment attached to District Resolution No. 2026-02 [S1].

- The district now receives fire and EMS service under a contract with the City of Bothell, which is itself asking its voters to annex into the RFA (City of Bothell Proposition No. 1) [S1].
- "The Plan amendment does not provide District residents with representation on the Governing Board" [S1]. The RFA FAQ says that "Under the proposed plan, Sno10 is scheduled to dissolve at the end of 2027," so its area would become part of the RFA rather than keep its own board [S2].
- Residents would keep being served from existing stations; the change is organizational [S2].
- A simple majority of Sno10 voters decides; current RFA voters do not vote [S2].

## Cost

- "The Plan amendment estimates a tax rate of $.45 per thousand to be assessed in 2027 for collection in 2028 which will replace the District's regular tax levy rate (currently approximately $.90) and the imposition of a fire benefit service charge that will be calculated in 2028 based on the formula set forth in the RFA Plan" [S1].
- The RFA FAQ's preliminary estimate, in 2026 dollars, is "potential savings of approximately $0.20 per $1,000 of assessed value - about $160 per year for an $800,000 home" for Sno10 taxpayers; it stresses the estimates are preliminary and actual 2028 rates depend on the adopted budget [S2].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- YES replaces the district's own regular levy (about $0.90 per $1,000) with the RFA levy (estimated $0.45) plus a fire benefit charge set by formula in 2028 [S1]. The net effect therefore depends on the benefit charge, which the pamphlet does not quantify [S1]; the RFA's net estimate is the $0.20 per $1,000 saving above [S2].
- The FAQ says a future RFA capital bond for new stations (one in Bothell, one in Shoreline) is being considered for 2027, estimated at $0.03 to $0.058 per $1,000 [S2].

## Arguments for

From the statement for, by Morris Parrish, Jack Barnes and Craig Bishop [S1]:

- FD10 and Bothell have grown, but the department "remained relatively static in the size of the department and number of stations."
- "Regional fire service is the standard model throughout King and Snohomish counties"; a larger organization can share resources, training and specialized capabilities.
- A YES vote secures "permanent, reliable funding for emergency services"; "This measure is fully endorsed by the Bothell Professional Firefighters union."

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed; the pamphlet says no person in the jurisdiction contacted the Auditor's office to provide one [S1].

## Endorsements

- Bothell Professional Firefighters union (per the statement for) [S1].

## Axis notes

- **local-control:** YES dissolves the district's own elected board (end of 2027) and moves governance to a regional board on which district residents get no seats [S1][S2]: a regional-consolidation case.
- **taxes:** the pamphlet describes a levy swap ($0.45 RFA levy replacing about $0.90) plus a new benefit charge [S1], and the RFA estimates a net saving of about $0.20 per $1,000 [S2]. The net direction is a preliminary estimate, not a guarantee, so a taxes lean is weak.
- **spending:** framed as maintaining or improving current service levels [S1].
