## What it does

Proposition 263 amends charter Section 8.3 "so that the Civil Service Commission's responsibilities are consistent with the requirements of state law" [S1].

- **Current text.** The Civil Service Commission "shall provide for the classification of all employees except day laborers and the appointive officers," with open competitive examinations, "an eligibility list from which vacancies shall be filled," probation, and merit promotion [S2].
- **Amended text.** The Commission "shall provide for the classification and hiring examination processes as required by chapter 41.08 RCW and chapter 41.12 RCW or successor statute(s)" (the state civil service laws for firefighters and police) [S2].
- **Effect.** The explanatory statement: the charter now requires civil service procedures "for the majority of City positions"; the amendment would "only require the City to use Civil Service rules when hiring police officers and fire fighters. All other hiring would still be required to comply with all other provisions of state law and any requirements found in union contracts" [S1][S2].

## Cost

No tax or rate [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

Replaces the city-wide civil service classification and eligibility-list requirement in Section 8.3(a) with the state-law minimum for police and fire [S2].

## Arguments for

From the statement written by Randy Bolerjack and Brent Terry [S1]:

- Eligibility lists "may remain in effect for up to three years," so "departments may be required to fill vacancies from applicant pools established many months or even years earlier," and "qualified applicants cannot be added to an eligibility list once it is established."
- "Rather than relying on eligibility lists established well in advance of vacancies, applicants would apply for specific positions as they become available."

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed. The pamphlet says no person in the jurisdiction contacted the Auditor's office to provide a statement [S1].

## Context / Coverage

- **How it got here.** Everett's charter (adopted 1968) is reviewed every 10 years by a Charter Review Committee; the City Council adopted the committee's recommendations, with its own changes, in Ordinance No. 4189-26, passed July 15, 2026, sending Propositions 261-265 to the ballot [S1][S2]. The Herald reports the council forwarded most of the 15-member committee's recommendations, rejected a proposal to review the charter every five years instead of ten, and voted 5-2 against adding term limits, which the committee had not advanced [S3].
- **Passage.** Simple majority of Everett voters; ratified amendments take effect on the date of the county's certification of the election [S2].
- **Committee rationale.** The Herald reports the committee's report called the broader charter requirement redundant because the city's recruitment procedures already require merit-based hiring and promotion, and said the change would let the city hire faster [S3].
