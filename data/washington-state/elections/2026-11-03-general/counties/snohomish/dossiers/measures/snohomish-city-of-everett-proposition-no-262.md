## What it does

Proposition 262 amends charter Section 3.2. The ballot title: it "would require the City to have at least two council meetings per month and at least 36 meetings per year; require the City to publish a schedule of those meetings every year in January and revise the procedures for determining who will preside over meetings when the Council President is absent" [S1].

- **Meeting minimum.** Today the council must meet "at least 48 times per calendar year." The amendment requires "at least twice per month and at least 36 times" a year, and publication of the year's schedule each January [S2]. The charter's stated "goal to have weekly meetings" remains [S2].
- **Evening meetings.** At least one regular meeting a month stays in the evening; the amended text says "with scheduled start no later than 6:30 p.m." where the current text says "after 6:30 p.m." [S2].
- **Presiding officer.** In the President's absence the Vice-President presides; if both are absent, the President designates a councilmember. Today the members present choose by majority [S1][S2].

## Cost

No tax or rate [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

Replaces the 48-meeting minimum and the presiding-officer rule in Section 3.2 [S2].

## Arguments for

From the statement written by Alan Rubio, Alice Hedges and Marcus Nuñez [S1]:

- "Everett's current requirement of forty-eight City Council meetings each year was set decades ago. Today, that requirement can result in meetings with limited agendas held mainly to meet a numerical target, which can waste taxpayer dollars and resources."
- The change "gives the City Council flexibility to meet when city business requires it, while providing predictable opportunities for residents to participate."

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed. The pamphlet says no person in the jurisdiction contacted the Auditor's office to provide a statement [S1].

## Context / Coverage

- **How it got here.** Everett's charter (adopted 1968) is reviewed every 10 years by a Charter Review Committee; the City Council adopted the committee's recommendations, with its own changes, in Ordinance No. 4189-26, passed July 15, 2026, sending Propositions 261-265 to the ballot [S1][S2]. The Herald reports the council forwarded most of the 15-member committee's recommendations, rejected a proposal to review the charter every five years instead of ten, and voted 5-2 against adding term limits, which the committee had not advanced [S3].
- **Passage.** Simple majority of Everett voters; ratified amendments take effect on the date of the county's certification of the election [S2].
- **Origin.** The explanatory statement says the meeting change came from the Charter Review Committee and the presiding-officer change was added by the Council [S1].
