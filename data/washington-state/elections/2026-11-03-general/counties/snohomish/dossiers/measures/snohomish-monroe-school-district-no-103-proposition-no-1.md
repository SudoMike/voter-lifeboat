## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would authorize the district "to levy the following excess taxes to fund major maintenance projects, safety and security improvements, and construction projects at select schools" for collection years 2027 through 2030 [S1].

- The explanatory statement lists "school safety and security improvements, major maintenance projects, facility improvements, and the relocation of Sky Valley Education Center to the updated Wagner Center site" [S1].
- The statement for lists secure school entrances, fire and security system upgrades, roof and HVAC replacements and infrastructure repairs [S1]. The Herald adds sporting-facility repairs and a new gym at Frank Wagner Elementary, and reports the Sky Valley relocation at about $30 million of the total [S2].
- A capital levy needs a simple majority; bonds need 60% [S2].

## Cost

- $12,282,731 a year in each of 2027-2030; estimated rates $0.95, $0.90, $0.86 and $0.82 per $1,000 of assessed value [S1].
- The district puts the average at 88 cents per $1,000 over the collection period [S2].
- Senior and disability exemptions may be available [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

- A new four-year capital levy (about $49 million total); the statement for says it replaces a February 2026 plan for a roughly $152 million, 20-year bond [S1].
- That February bond received 48.9% and failed [S2].
- The statement for says 73% of Snohomish County school districts have voter-approved capital levies and Monroe is "one of only four districts without one" [S1].

## Arguments for

From the statement for, by Kyle Fisher and Molly Barnes [S1]:

- "After the February election, voters asked for a different approach"; the levy cuts the investment "from approximately $152 million to $49 million while shifting from a 20-year bond to a four-year levy."
- The projects are "essential repairs and safety improvements"; addressing them now avoids costlier emergency repairs.
- Superintendent Shawn Woodward: "Following the February election, we heard clearly from our community that they wanted a shorter tax commitment and more focused approach" [S2].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed; the pamphlet says no person in the jurisdiction contacted the Auditor's office to provide one [S1].
