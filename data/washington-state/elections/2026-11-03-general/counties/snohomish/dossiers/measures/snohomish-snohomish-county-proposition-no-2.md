## What it does

Proposition No. 2 is a county charter amendment from the Charter Review Commission that "would require the county legislature and executive branch to prioritize the funding of the county budget stabilization funds," whose purpose is "to help mitigate the impacts of catastrophic economic downturns and revenue shortfalls and provide resources for emergencies and catastrophic events" [S1]. It adds Section 6.150 to Article 6 [S3].

- **Two funds.** The county must keep a Primary Stabilization Fund (administered by the Finance Department) "for fiscal stabilization, revenue volatility, and general budget continuity," and a separate Secondary Catastrophic Stabilization Fund (administered by the Treasurer) for declared emergencies, catastrophic natural disasters or severe fiscal disruption [S1][S3].
- **Funding the catastrophic fund.** The council must adopt ordinances providing contributions from investment earnings of the primary fund, one-time or extraordinary revenues, emergency reimbursements, dedicated revenue sources and other sources [S1][S3]. The fund may "Not … be funded by taxes or fees imposed or increased for the exclusive purpose of this fund" [S3].
- **Initial capitalization.** On establishment, the county transfers 10% of the primary fund's balance into the catastrophic fund [S3].
- **Spending rules.** Appropriation or expenditure from either fund requires approval by at least four council members [S3]. Catastrophic-fund withdrawals need a replenishment plan, and related federal or state reimbursements go back to the fund "whenever legally permissible" [S1][S3]. The catastrophic fund "shall not be used for routine structural budget balancing absent a declared emergency" [S3].
- **Reporting.** The Treasurer reports annually on the catastrophic fund's balance, investments, contributions, withdrawals and adequacy [S1][S3].

## Cost

No new tax or rate in the ballot title [S1]. The statement for says it "does not create a new tax or require higher taxes" [S2]; the amendment bars funding the catastrophic fund with taxes or fees imposed for that purpose alone [S3].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

There is no existing charter section on stabilization funds [S3]. The amendment redirects existing revenue streams (investment earnings, one-time revenues, emergency reimbursements) into reserves [S1][S3]. The statement against says the money would be pulled "away from the services residents rely on every day," and its rebuttal says redirected FEMA reimbursements "repay bills the County already incurred," "forcing cuts to other essential services" [S2].

## Arguments for

From the statement written by Brian Sullivan [S2]:

- "Proposition No. 2 helps prepare Snohomish County before, not after, the next crisis by strengthening the County's emergency savings."
- It "limits the use of reserve funds to genuine emergencies, requires a replenishment plan after withdrawals, and maintains a supermajority vote before reserve funds may be spent."
- Rebuttal: "After the Oso disaster, Snohomish County spent $22 million in six weeks from its General Fund." Disaster aid "usually arrives as reimbursement."

The Herald reports the proposal was sponsored by Commissioner Demi Chatters and brought by Treasurer Sullivan, who said a Treasurer could "be the champion for that fund" [S4].

## Arguments against

From the statement written by Hans Dunshee and Janice R. Greene [S2]:

- "This is unnecessary and bureaucratic. The County Council can do all of this without the rigid bureaucracy. This proposal was last minute and untested."
- "Roughly seventy percent of the County's budget supports law enforcement and courts." Disaster response is primarily a state and federal responsibility, and "A modest county fund would not be enough for a true disaster."
- "Spending would need four of five Council members. It reduces the weight of each resident's vote by increasing the power of a small minority."

## Axis notes

- **spending:** YES builds reserves from existing revenue and requires a four-of-five council vote to spend from either stabilization fund, with replenishment plans [S3]. That favours saving and restraint over current spending (pole A); opponents say it takes from current services [S2].
- **taxes:** No tax change; new taxes or fees for the fund alone are barred [S3].
