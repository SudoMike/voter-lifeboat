## Background
Julio Cortes is the incumbent Democratic State Representative for the 38th Legislative District, Position 1, in office since 2022 [S1], seeking a third term [S11]. He is Manager of Economic Development for the City of Everett and previously worked at Cocoon House with at-risk and homeless youth [S1][S2]. His family immigrated from Mexico when he was five and settled in Wapato; he graduated from Western Washington University in 2009 [S2]. In 2026 he was vice chair of Early Learning & Human Services and served on Appropriations and Technology, Economic Development & Veterans [S6]. In the August 4 primary he took 51.1% to Cascade Party candidate Jeff Kelly's 28.4% and Democrat Annie Fitzgerald's 19.3% [S12].

Identity note: the Senate roll calls also list a "Cortes" (a state senator, not this candidate); only House votes are attributed to Rep. Julio Cortes here [S7][S8][S9].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** the 2026 district session report he co-signed says "we made history by passing the Millionaires' Tax (SB 6346)" to create "a fairer, more equitable system" [S6]. In a June interview he "touted" the tax as a way to improve affordability, and said the 1% cap on annual property-tax revenue growth is "killing cities" and he would consider reviewing it and possibly putting an increase to voters [S11]. His campaign priorities page features a "Balance Our Tax Code Rally" (January 26, 2026) [S4].
- **Spending/budget:** said the state "could have done better to plan ahead" with one-time federal COVID funds, that "we need to make sure that we are not investing in programs that we're not going to be able to continuously fund," and that he would cut programs with limited effects while protecting ones like early learning and child care [S11].
- **Housing:** co-sponsored the 2025 bill capping annual rent increases (HB 1217) [S10][S11] and voted for it [S9]; also says he wants to cut red tape and speed permitting so more housing is built [S11]. Pamphlet cites "expanding affordable housing opportunities" [S1].
- **Public safety:** pamphlet cites "standardizing juvenile diversion programs" [S1]; would increase funding for diversion programs for young people and for support programs for people leaving jail or prison ("if we can instead invest those monies into support systems, we're going to not only save money but save that person from the streets"), wants police hiring "more reflective of the communities they serve," and supports more investment in social-worker programs [S11].
- **Climate:** said the state "should be more aggressive at targeting major polluters and invest into green technology" [S11].
- **Health care:** was among the sponsors of a 2025 bill to create the Washington Health Trust, a universal public health-care system; it died in committee [S11].
- **Immigration:** the session report he co-signed says "We are deeply concerned about the aggressive and violent actions we have witnessed from ICE agents" and lists bills passed banning face-concealing masks for officers, including ICE agents, and limiting immigration enforcement's use of license plate readers [S6].
- **Labor:** pamphlet cites "strengthening wage theft protections for workers" [S1].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** pamphlet says he will "keep fighting for ... reproductive rights"; endorsed by Planned Parenthood [S1].
- **Parents and schools:** voted for ESHB 1296 (see Record) [S8]. The co-signed session report lists bills limiting isolation and restraint in schools and requiring timely access to special-education evaluation reports for families [S6].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted for ESSB 6346 [S7] and co-signed a session report celebrating it [S6].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted for ESHB 1296 (2025), the amendments to the I-2081 list that I-1 would repeal [S8].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- Voted yea on House final passage of ESSB 6346 (2026-03-09, passed 51-46) [S7].
- Voted yea on House passage of ESHB 1296 (2025-03-12, 56-37) and on final passage as amended by the Senate (2025-04-24, 59-39) [S8].
- Sponsor of HB 1217 (rent increase limits); voted yea on House passage (2025-03-10, 53-42) and on the conference report (2025-04-27, 54-44), and nay on concurring in the Senate amendments (2025-04-23, 48-50) [S9][S10].
- PDC (data updated 2026-10-05): $84,080.98 in contributions and $62,811.20 in expenditures for 2026 [S13].

- Emphasizes diversion and prevention on public safety [S1][S11]; his opponent emphasizes funding law enforcement (see thomas-jeff-kelly.md).
