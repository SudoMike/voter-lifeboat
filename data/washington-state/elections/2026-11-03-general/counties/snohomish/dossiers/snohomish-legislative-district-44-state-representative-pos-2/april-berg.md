## Background
April Berg is the incumbent Democratic State Representative for the 44th District, Position 2, in office since 2021. She chairs the House Finance Committee and serves on Appropriations, the Joint Legislative Audit & Review Committee, the Economic & Revenue Forecast Council and the Income Tax Implementation and Administration Advisory Group [S1][S4]. She is a former Everett and Edmonds School Board director and Mill Creek planning commissioner, and worked as a Boeing program manager, in tech business development and as an Edmonds bed-and-breakfast owner [S1]. She holds a B.A. in history from Oregon State University [S1].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** "This year I helped lead efforts to reform our tax code, reducing tax burdens on working families and small businesses, while making investments in K-12 and childcare" [S1]. Her campaign site calls the Millionaires' Tax "the first step in addressing our regressive tax structure" and says "I am proud to have been a part of the team leading this effort" [S3]. Her April 2026 newsletter says that as Finance chair "this work is deeply personal to me" and that "the wealthiest few paid far less than their fair share" [S5]. In her section of the 2026 delegation mailer, the tax measure is described as "asking the wealthiest to pay their fair share" [S6].
- **Spending:** cites "real investments in affordable housing, behavioral health, great schools, and community safety" and says "more needs to be done" [S1]. Her platform calls for universal childcare, treating childcare as infrastructure, updating the school funding model and more early-learning support [S2].
- **Housing:** platform supports the Housing Trust Fund to build affordable housing, funding for permanent supportive housing and fewer barriers for shelters [S2]. Voted for EHB 1217 (2025 rent and fee increase limits) [S11].
- **Local government:** sponsored HB 2442, which "gives local governments the flexibility to use their local tax revenues" for housing, childcare and behavioral health [S3][S6].
- **Climate:** "Support a science-based approach for combating climate change," "Support Clean Fuel Standards," environmental justice and the Clean Air Authority [S2].
- **Public safety:** her site says she voted to "increase law enforcement pay, hire new officers, and expand proven programs" [S3]; the pamphlet lists endorsements from "public safety advocates" and firefighters [S1].
- **Healthcare:** "Accessible and affordable healthcare for all," pandemic preparation and rural healthcare funding [S2].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no statement of her own found; her pamphlet lists a Planned Parenthood endorsement [S1], and Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates lists her [S16].
- **Parents and schools:** her platform calls for more social-emotional learning support and reducing disparities in graduation and discipline rates [S2]; she wrote HB 1273 (period products in school bathrooms) and legislation eliminating the school lunch co-pay [S3]. Voted for ESHB 1296 (2025), which amended the I-2081 list of parent and guardian rights [S12][S17]. No campaign statement on parental notification or opt-outs found.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (IP26-645, repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. She voted for ESSB 6346, which I-645 would repeal, and says she helped lead the effort to pass it [S7][S3][S5].
- **I-1 (IL26-001, parental rights):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted for ESHB 1296, the amendments I-1 would repeal [S12][S17].
- **I-638 (IL26-638, girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- Chair of the House Finance Committee [S1][S4].
- Prime sponsor of HB 2442 (2026, local revenue flexibility) and HB 2334 (2026, rounding cash transactions after the end of the penny), both enacted [S3][S5][S6]; primary sponsor of HB 2401 (boys and men's commission) and author of HB 1273 (period products in schools) [S3].
- ESSB 6346 (tax on income over $1 million): yea on House final passage, 2026-03-09 (51-46) [S7].
- ESSB 5813 (2025, higher capital gains and estate tax rates): yea, 2025-04-23 (53-45) [S8].
- ESSB 5814 (2025, excise taxes, including sales tax on more services): yea, 2025-04-23 (50-47) [S9].
- ESHB 2081 (2025, business and occupation tax increases): yea, 2025-04-22 (50-48) [S10].
- EHB 1217 (2025, rent and fee increase limits): yea on 3rd reading 2025-03-10 (53-42) and on final passage as recommended by the conference committee 2025-04-27 (54-44); nay on concurring in the Senate amendments 2025-04-23 (48-50) [S11].
- ESHB 1296 (2025, amendments to the I-2081 parents' rights list): yea on final passage as amended by the Senate, 2025-04-24 (59-39) [S12][S17].
- PDC (data updated 2026-10-05): $249,932.10 raised and $82,213.42 spent for 2026 [S13].
