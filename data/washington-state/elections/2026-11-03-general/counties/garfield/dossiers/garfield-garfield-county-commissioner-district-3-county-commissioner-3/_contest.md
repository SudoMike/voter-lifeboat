## What the office does

The three-member Board of County Commissioners is the county's legislative authority: it adopts the county budget, sets county policy and ordinances, and oversees county roads, property and services [S2]. The seat carries a four-year term [S1].

## Race dynamics

- **Incumbent and challenger.** Commissioner Justin E. Dixon (Prefers Republican Party), on the board since 2015 [S1], faces Gary W. Bowles (States No Party Preference), a Fire District 1 commissioner and volunteer EMT [S1]. Dixon won the seat in 2018, 848 to 470 [S5], and was re-elected unopposed in 2022 [S6].
- **Primary by district, general county-wide.** Only District 3 voters voted in the primary: Bowles 171, Dixon 165, with 6 write-ins, in 4 of 4 units [S4]. Every county voter votes in the general [S3][S8]; in 2022 the District 3 seat was on all 11 precincts [S6].
- **Forum.** At a June 28 forum both were asked about alternative energy, putting citizens over party, county-city cooperation, the county's top issues and its budget; the paper did not report their answers [S7].
- **Money.** Dixon files full PDC reports and reports $0 raised and spent; Bowles chose the mini-reporting option [S9].

## Differentiating issue axes

- `experience`: Dixon runs on more than ten years as a commissioner and on state and regional boards; Bowles, a first-time county candidate, pairs his fire-district and EMT service with a promise of "honest leadership" and moving the county "forward in a positive direction" [S1].
- `spending`: Dixon names "fiscal responsibility, transparency, and accountability" as essential to keeping services and infrastructure; Bowles makes no budget statement [S1].
