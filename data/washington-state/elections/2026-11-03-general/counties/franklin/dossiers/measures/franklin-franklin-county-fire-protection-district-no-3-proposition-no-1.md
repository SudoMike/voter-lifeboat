## What it does
The fire district's board adopted Resolution No. 491 to raise property taxes "to adequately fund District operations including emergency medical services". The proposition lets the district levy $1.24 per $1,000 of assessed value for assessment in 2026 and collection in 2027 (S1). It is a "single-year permanent" lid lift. The amount collected in 2027 becomes the base for calculating the levy limit in later years under chapter 84.55 RCW, so the increase carries forward rather than expiring (S1, S5). The explanatory statement says the money keeps "maintaining and adequately funding district operations, including emergency medical services (ambulance)" (S1).

The district's station at 2108 N Road 84, Pasco is one of the county's ballot drop-box sites (S1, printed page 45). The tax applies only to property inside Fire District 3, and only its voters decide the measure (S3, S5).

## Cost
- New rate: $1.24 per $1,000 of assessed value for 2027 collection (S1). Current rate: $0.86 per $1,000, according to the district via NonStop Local (S2). The increase is $0.38 per $1,000 (S2).
- On a $400,000 home: about $496 a year at $1.24 against about $344 at $0.86, roughly $152 more (computed from S1 and S2).
- A lid lift approved by a simple majority may exceed the 1% annual growth limit on regular levies (S5). A fire district's regular levy authority runs up to the statutory dollar-rate limits (S6).

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces
No levy expires. The measure raises the existing regular levy rate and resets the base. The district asked for a similar lift in the August 2024 primary, from $0.88 to $1.28 per $1,000 (S3). Voters rejected it, 1,023 (56.9%) to 775 (43.1%) (S4).

## Arguments for
No committee came forward to write a statement for (S1). The district says call volumes have risen significantly over five years. It says overlapping calls now happen 30% of the time and are "leading to longer response times". It says the money would fund one more full-time firefighter-paramedic, two seasonal wildland firefighters, protective gear and ambulance replacements, and backup power at the main station (S2). In 2024 the chief also argued that better response capacity can lower homeowners' insurance premiums through ISO ratings (S3).

## Arguments against
No committee came forward to write a statement against (S1), and no committee for or against is registered with the PDC (S7). The 2024 attempt failed with 56.9% voting no (S4).

## Endorsements and campaign
No endorsements, committees or campaign spending were found (S1, S7).

## Lean notes
- `taxes`: a yes vote raises a regular property tax levy above the statutory growth limit (a lid lift), from $0.86 to $1.24 per $1,000, and makes the higher amount the permanent base (S1, S2, S5). This is the "levy-cap lift" the rubric names at the pole_b end.
- `spending`: the district ties the money to new staff and equipment (S2). That is an expansion of service capacity, though the explanatory statement frames it as maintaining operations (S1).
