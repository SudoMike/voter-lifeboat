## Background
Huber is the Franklin County undersheriff, with more than 23 years in the sheriff's office as reserve deputy, patrol deputy, sergeant, captain, commander and undersheriff. He has been a defensive tactics, firearms and police training officer, captain of investigations, special investigations commander and SWAT commander (S1). Before that he worked two years as a corrections officer at Coyote Ridge Corrections Center and served 12 years as a U.S. Army Ranger (S1). He served five years on the Connell City Council, including as mayor pro tem (S1). He holds an A.A. and B.S. in criminal justice from Liberty University (S1). He lives in Eltopia (S2) and prefers the Republican Party (S1).

In the August 4 primary he finished first with 5,932 votes (47.8%) (S6).

## Positions

### Public safety and policing
- "Experience matters." He says his time at both the patrol and administration level makes him "uniquely qualified to lead our office - keeping our community safe and supporting the people doing the job" (S1).
- Campaign priorities: "Safer Communities, County-Wide" through proactive patrols and resources across the county; "Accountability & Integrity" through high standards, training and transparency; and community partnerships with residents, agriculture and local leaders (S2).
- On short- versus long-term needs: "the best investment is your people. Turnover is expensive. So you invest in your folks by training and officer wellness" (S3).

### Mental health and addiction
- "Mental health and addiction are huge issues in our community, and I am proud to be part of the group who helped bring about the new behavioral health center" (S1).
- Named funding the Behavioral Health Recovery Center as the issue he would spend political capital on: deputies "are not mental health professionals ... Jail is a short term solution. The emergency room is a short term solution, and the only other option was to leave them in place ... having this recovery center benefits not only the community, but it benefits law enforcement" (S3).
- Serves on the Benton/Franklin Behavioral Health Advisory Committee (S1).

- No public position found in the sources reviewed. His campaign site describes the office's Big Buddy program, in which deputies mentor elementary students (S2).

## Record
- Helped lead the sheriff's office to full accreditation and oversaw continuous deputy training (S1).
- Upgraded the Mesa sub-station with the city and served as the first Mesa community liaison deputy (S1).
- Organized recruitment for the Bi-County Sheriff's Posse and wrote its policies and procedures (S1).
- Connell City Council, five years, mayor pro tem (S1).

## Endorsements
His campaign site lists the Franklin County Sheriff's Office Command Staff Guild, retired Sheriff Richard Lathim, several current Washington sheriffs, Jim Jacobs (retired North Franklin School District superintendent), Stephen L. Kent and sheriff's office personnel (S2).

## Campaign finance
PDC summary: $32,054.70 raised, $20,056.04 spent (S4). About $5,930 is his own money. Outside contributors of $2,400 each include Marti Noble, Consolidated Telecom Inc. and Clay Easterday (S5).

## Scoring notes
- Experience: long career inside the office he seeks to lead, now second in command; the continuity candidate.
- Safety: enforcement career, but his signature issue is a behavioral health recovery center as an alternative to jail and the ER for people in crisis; that points toward the services side more than his opponent does.
- Spending: investment in staff training and wellness, framed as cutting turnover costs.
