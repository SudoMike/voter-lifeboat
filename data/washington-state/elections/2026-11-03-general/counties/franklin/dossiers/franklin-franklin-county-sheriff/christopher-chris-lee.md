## Background
Lee is chief of police for the City of Connell. He reports more than 25 years in law enforcement, with service at the Yakima County Sheriff's Office (reserve deputy), Hanford Patrol and the Pasco, Richland and Connell police departments, after serving in the U.S. Marine Corps (S1). He says he has worked in patrol, investigations, training, supervision and command (S1). He holds a bachelor's degree in interdisciplinary studies (business and government), magna cum laude, from Liberty University, an associate degree in exercise science, and is a graduate of the FBI National Academy (session 273) (S1). He has lived in Franklin County for more than 20 years and prefers the Republican Party (S1).

In the August 4 primary he finished second with 3,791 votes (30.6%) (S6). His campaign website was a "Launching Soon" placeholder when checked (S5).

## Positions

### Public safety and policing
- "Franklin County deserves a Sheriff who will support deputies, enforce the law, defend victims, and keep dangerous criminals off our streets" (S1).
- "I believe public safety comes first, taxpayers deserve accountability, and government should operate with transparency and fiscal discipline" (S1).
- Promises to "protect constitutional rights, diligently strengthen partnerships, and ensure the Sheriff's Office remains focused on law and order" (S1).
- Would judge his first year by whether the agency had "realigned ourselves with our mission" and cut "the noise that has been distracting" it (S2).
- On planning: starts by identifying a problem and its root causes, then builds a strategy that can adapt (S2).

### Immigration
- Named DACA as an issue he would spend political capital on: recipients he knows are "god-loving, patriotic people, but there is no pathway to citizenship for them"; he believes they should have a way to pursue citizenship (S2). (Recorded only: `immigration` is not scored for county contests.)
