## Background
Didier is the incumbent Franklin County Commissioner for District 3 (S2). He was chair pro tem of the board in February 2025 and its chairman by September 2025 (S5, S3). He describes himself as a farmer (campaign email "farmerdidier") and lists no other experience in his pamphlet entry (S1). He prefers the Republican Party (S1). In the August 4 primary he received 2,764 votes (57.5%) to Prantle's 1,924 (40.0%) (S10).

## Positions

### Taxes and spending
- "I oppose any tax increases. We need a government that lives within its means, not one that demands more from the hardworking people of Franklin County" (S1).
- Asks for support "to continue the fight for more freedom, lower taxes, and fewer regulations" and calls a vote for him "a vote for a smart, limited government" (S1).
- On ending the county's human services contract with Benton County in 2025: "The financial demands from Benton County were simply unreasonable." He cited declining sales tax revenue. Benton County disputed that new fees had been proposed (S3).
- On the Attorney General's 2025 opinion that former commissioners had raised their own pay mid-term unconstitutionally: "We were elected to safeguard the taxpayers and their hard-earned money; this can never be allowed to happen again in Franklin County." The current board had sought the opinion (S4).

### Property, agriculture and state government
- Says he has been "a steadfast champion for private property rights and our vital agricultural interests" and has "consistently stood up to state government overreach to ensure Olympia doesn't dictate how we live or work" (S1).

### Transparency
- Says he initiated "the policy to record and preserve all commissioner meetings" (S1).

### Public safety and roads
- Joined Commissioner Bauman, Sen. Torres and Rep. Klicker in December 2025 to call a public work session on U.S. 395 safety after two fatal crashes north of Pasco. The joint release said, "Our number one priority is public safety" (S7).

### Public health
- Spoke for the board's February 2025 resolution urging the Benton-Franklin Health District to stop providing and promoting what it called "gene-therapy" (COVID-19 mRNA) vaccines until it presented research on adverse effects: "It's your free will. It's your decision ... Yet fear took over this nation and they use fear to push this mandate" (S5).

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Backed the board's April 2025 resolution against "men competing in women's sports": "As a county, we got to stand against this. And we got to make it known that we will not go along with men competing in women's sports" (S6). (Recorded only: `social` is not scored for county contests.)

### Parents and schools
- No public position found in the sources reviewed.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
No direct statement on I-645, I-1 (IL26-001) or I-638 (IL26-638) was found. The April 2025 board resolution on school and youth sports concerns the same subject as I-638 (S6), but it is not a statement on the initiative.

## Record
- County Commissioner, District 3; board chairman in 2025 (S2, S3).
- 2025: board resolutions on COVID-19 vaccines (February) and women's sports (April) (S5, S6); sought the Attorney General opinion on former commissioners' pay (S4); ended the human services interlocal agreement with Benton County effective September 30, 2025, moving some services to the state Department of Commerce (S3); called the U.S. 395 safety work session (S7).
