## What the office does
The three-member Board of County Commissioners is Franklin County's legislative and executive authority. It adopts the budget and fixes county taxes, maintains county roads and buildings, passes county ordinances, sets county policy, and runs the departments that are not headed by an elected official (S2, S3). Commissioners also sit on regional boards, among them the Benton-Franklin Health District, Ben Franklin Transit and emergency management (S3).

## Who votes
Only District 3 voters vote in this race, in the primary and the general. Franklin County's commissioner districts were redrawn by Resolution 2022-082 (S7). State law lets a county move to district-only elections under the Washington Voting Rights Act (S6). In the 2024 general, the District 1 race appeared on 48 precincts and the District 2 race on 31 of the county's 123 (S4). The 2026 District 3 primary was counted in 44 of 123 reporting units (S5). District 3 includes west Pasco around Road 68 and Burden Boulevard (S7).

## Race dynamics
Both candidates prefer the Republican Party (S1). Incumbent Clint Didier, who chaired the board in 2025, faces Joel Prantle, vice president of a family railway-supply business and a volunteer member of the county planning commission (S1). Didier led the primary with 2,764 votes (57.5%) to Prantle's 1,924 (40.0%) (S5). Neither candidate answered NonStop Local's primary interview requests (S8).

Didier's statement emphasizes opposing all tax increases, limited government, private property and agricultural interests, resisting state "overreach", and recording commissioner meetings. Prantle's emphasizes responsible growth, listening to constituents, servant leadership and his support for President Trump (S1). Prantle raised about $7,300, nearly all from himself and family members. Didier raised $3,050 (see the candidate dossiers).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience/record:** a sitting commissioner with a public record on the board, against a planning commissioner with no elected record.
2. **Taxes:** Didier explicitly opposes any tax increase. Prantle states no position on taxes.
3. **Spending:** Didier stresses a government that "lives within its means". His board record includes ending the human services agreement with Benton County over cost and seeking an Attorney General opinion on past commissioners' pay. Prantle gives no budget positions.
4. **Growth/housing:** Prantle names "responsible growth" without specifics. Didier names private property rights and agriculture.
5. Gender and school sports: Didier backed a 2025 board resolution against "men in women's sports". It is recorded in his dossier, but `social` is not scored for county contests.
