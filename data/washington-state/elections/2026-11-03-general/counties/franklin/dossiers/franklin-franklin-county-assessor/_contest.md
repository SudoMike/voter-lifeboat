## What the office does
The assessor values every taxable property in the county. State law requires property to be valued at 100% of its true and fair value, based mainly on recent sales of the property or similar properties (S2). Rosenau's campaign site explains the division of labor: taxing districts (cities, schools, the county, fire, hospital and other districts) set the dollar amounts they collect, and the assessor sets values, calculates levy rates from those budgets, administers exemptions and keeps records. It also says the assessor does not set the total tax (S4).

## Race dynamics
Both candidates prefer the Republican Party (S1). Incumbent John A. Rosenau was first elected in 2021 and re-elected in 2022 (S1). Challenger Chris Hollingsworth is a state-certified residential appraiser with more than 20 years in private practice and a Republican precinct committee officer and state committeeman (S1). In the August 4 primary Hollingsworth led with 6,032 votes (54.1%) to Rosenau's 4,979 (44.7%) (S3).

Hollingsworth says assessment problems he saw while helping residents at Board of Equalization hearings led him to run, and he promises to "restore confidence" in the office through appraisal training and standards (S1). Rosenau points to training, consistency and process changes since 2021. He also cites advocacy at the state assessors' association for property tax relief and broader exemptions (S1). Rosenau lists endorsements from more than 30 county assessors and the appraisers' union local (AFSCME Local 2658 F). Hollingsworth lists the Tri-Cities Association of Realtors (S1). Hollingsworth reports $10,703 raised. Rosenau has registered with the PDC but reports no contributions (S5).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience/record:** a sitting assessor with a two-term record and statewide association roles, against a private appraiser who has not held the office.
2. **Taxes:** both frame their goal as fair, accurate assessments rather than tax levels. Hollingsworth says no resident should pay more than a "fair share". Rosenau has advocated for broader exemptions for seniors, people with disabilities, farmers and small businesses. Neither proposes a change in tax rates, which the assessor does not set (S4).
3. **Spending/accountability:** Hollingsworth's pitch is better appraisal practice and accountability in the office. Rosenau's is that standards and training are already in place.
4. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives: none found for either candidate.
