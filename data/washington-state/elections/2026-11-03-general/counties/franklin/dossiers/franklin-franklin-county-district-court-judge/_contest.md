## What the office does
The District Court is Franklin County's court of limited jurisdiction. It hears misdemeanors and gross misdemeanors (up to one year in jail and a $5,000 fine), traffic infractions and peace-keeping proceedings. It holds preliminary hearings and sets bail for defendants held on warrants (S2). The seat is nonpartisan and elected countywide for four years (S1, S4). With only two candidates, the race was not on the August primary ballot and appears for the first time in the general (S4).

## Race dynamics
Incumbent Judge Trinity Orosco, the court's presiding judge, faces Michael Nguyen, a public defense attorney (S1). Orosco was a defense attorney for 14 years before taking the bench. She cites a Therapeutic Court program she created in her first term and more than $1 million in state funding and grants she brought to the county (S1). Nguyen has practiced for five years as a public defender in Benton and Franklin County courts and as Franklin County's preliminary appearance attorney, arguing bail at first appearances (S1). He promises to be a neutral officiant, to take part in therapeutic courts, and to bring court costs in line with Benton County and Pasco Municipal Court (S1). Orosco has reported $5,818 raised, all her own money. Nguyen has registered but reports no contributions (S3).

## Differentiating issue axes
Judges are scored only on `judicial`, `safety` and `experience`.
1. **Experience:** an incumbent presiding judge with 14 years of prior defense practice, against an attorney admitted after a 2018 J.D. who has five years of public-defense practice and no judicial service.
2. **Safety (formation and diversion):** both come from criminal defense. Both back therapeutic courts. Orosco built the program and added a work-crew alternative to jail. Nguyen pledges rehabilitation with accountability and says his work has included hundreds of bail arguments.
3. **Judicial philosophy:** Nguyen pledges to apply the law "rather than imposing my beliefs from the bench". Orosco pledges hearings "free from bias or personal agendas". Neither describes an interpretive approach beyond that.
