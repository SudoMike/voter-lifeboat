## Background

- Adams County Assessor. She joined the Assessor's Office in 2015 as an administrative assistant, was selected in 2019 to fill a vacant Assessor's term and served through 2021, and has been Assessor since 2022 [S2]. Her statement counts 11 years in the office, five as elected Assessor [S1]. She was elected unopposed in November 2022 (3,293 votes) [S3].
- Earlier business manager for Ag Tech Services, a division of Ritzville Warehouse [S2]; her statement also cites public administration, office management and community relations experience and education in business [S1].
- Age 43, of Ritzville [S2]. Prefers the Republican Party [S1].
- Trailed in the 2026 primary, 1,039 votes to Templin's 1,295 [S4]. The Journal's election-night story called Templin the incumbent, which conflicts with both candidates' own statements; she could not be reached for comment that night [S1][S2][S6].
- Files under the PDC mini-reporting option [S7].

## Positions

### Running the office

- Commits to "fair, accurate, and transparent property assessments" and "professional and respectful service" [S1].
- Says her office has focused on "timely assessments, improved communication with property owners, and maintaining compliance with Washington State assessment standards" [S1].
- Says taxpayers deserve an office that is "accessible, dependable, and dedicated to serving the community with integrity" [S1].

## Record

- Assessor since her 2019 appointment, elected 2022 [S2][S3].
- The State Auditor's accountability audit of Adams County for 2024 examined property tax valuations among other high-risk areas and reported no findings [S5]. The audit covers the county as a whole, not the Assessor's Office alone.
