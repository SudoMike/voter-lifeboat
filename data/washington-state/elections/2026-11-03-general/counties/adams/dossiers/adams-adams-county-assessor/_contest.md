## What the office does

The assessor values property for taxation so that assessments are fair and equitable, and explains to property owners how values are set and how to appeal [S1]. The office is partisan with a four-year term [S1].

## Race dynamics

- **Incumbent and a career appraiser.** Assessor Veronica Rodriguez, in the office since 2015 and Assessor since a 2019 appointment, seeks a second term [S1][S3]. Curtis Templin, a career appraiser and deputy assessor who farms near Ritzville, is the challenger [S1]. Both prefer the Republican Party [S1].
- **Primary.** Templin led the certified primary 1,295 to 1,039 (55% to 44%) [S2].
- **The argument.** Templin says "recent years have illustrated the difficulties of operating without a foundation of seasoned professional knowledge" and that he would return the system "to a commonsense footing"; Rodriguez cites timely assessments, improved communication and compliance with state standards [S1]. The State Auditor's 2024 accountability audit of the county, which examined property tax valuations among other areas, reported no findings [S4].

## Differentiating issue axes

- `experience`: the sitting assessor's record in office against a challenger who has never held elected office and argues for a change in how the office is run on the strength of his appraisal career.
- No candidate states a position on tax levels, spending, housing or climate; the office applies state valuation law.
