## Background

- Law enforcement officer for 29 years: 15 years in the Military Police, two years with the Killeen, Texas, Police Department and 12 years with the Ritzville Police Department [S2]. Small business owner in Ritzville [S1].
- Training listed includes the U.S. Army Military Police Academy, military working dog (K-9), the Texas law enforcement academy, the Washington State equivalency academy, advanced roadside impaired-driving enforcement and police training officer courses [S1]. High school diploma [S2].
- Age 50, of Ritzville [S2]. Prefers the Republican Party [S1]. Has not held elected office [S1].
- Ran for sheriff in 2022 while a Ritzville police officer and lost to Wagner, 1,710 votes to 2,180 [S3][S6]. Applied in August 2024 to become Ritzville's police chief [S4].
- Lost the 2026 primary count 1,060 to 1,329 [S7]; said afterwards he needed to get his message out more vigorously before November [S5].
- Files full PDC reports: $0 raised and $2,300 spent as of Sept. 22, 2026 [S8].

## Positions

### Public safety and the sheriff's office

- Says "leadership starts with showing up, listening to people, and staying connected to the community" and that "strong communities are built through trust, relationships, and being present" [S1].
- Lists advocating for "public safety, accountability, and community trust" and working alongside first responders as community service [S1].
- No specific policies on staffing, the jail, budgets or programs were found in his statement or Q&A [S1][S2].

### Immigration enforcement (recorded; not a scored axis for county offices)

- Says local law enforcement should not support national immigration enforcement "because that's a federal job," and that if local agencies enforced immigration law "we should also be enforcing all the federal laws" [S2].
- Reads the Keep Washington Working Act as allowing help to ICE but not local investigations of immigration status or handing information to ICE, and says it has no local significance [S2].
