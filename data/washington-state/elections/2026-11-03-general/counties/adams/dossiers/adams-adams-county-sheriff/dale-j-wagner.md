## Background

- Adams County Sheriff; at the Adams County Sheriff's Office since 1997 as D.A.R.E. instructor, taser instructor, detective, field training officer, sergeant and undersheriff [S1][S2]. The sources differ on when he was first elected: 2015 in his 2026 announcement and Q&A [S2][S3], 2016 in the Journal's 2022 profile [S5].
- Earlier a City of Kennewick reserve police officer from 1995; 2005 Adams County Medal of Valor recipient [S5].
- Age 54, of Othello for 29 years [S2]. Prefers the Republican Party [S1].
- Won re-election in 2022 over Matthew Carlson, 2,180 votes to 1,710 [S7]; led the 2026 primary 1,329 to 1,060 [S8].
- Files full PDC reports: $7,977.27 raised and $4,679.65 spent as of Sept. 22, 2026 [S9].

## Positions

### Public safety and the sheriff's office

- Says a top priority is to "recruit and retain quality employees" and to give deputies, corrections staff and dispatchers "the training, leadership, and modern equipment they need" [S1].
- Says he will "continue working to secure funding for needed improvements in the jail, training opportunities, and resources" [S1]; he is coordinating construction of a new jail and recruiting staff to run it [S4].
- Wants to keep investing "in responsible technology, transparency, and accountability" and in programs that connect deputies with youth and families, because "building trust starts long before emergencies happen" [S1].
- Cites jail upgrades, updated equipment and training, and work with youth as achievements [S3].

### Immigration enforcement (recorded; not a scored axis for county offices)

- Supports cooperating with federal immigration authorities "when lawful cooperation ... helps protect the public, especially in cases involving violent crime, trafficking, or repeat offenders," while saying deputies' primary job is public safety and people must be able to call 911 "without fearing that asking for help will make them a target" [S2].
- Has "concerns about limits on cooperation when serious criminal activity is involved" under the Keep Washington Working Act [S2]. In April 2025 he testified before the U.S. House Judiciary Subcommittee on Immigration Integrity, Security and Enforcement on the conflict between that act and federal law [S6].

## Record

- Sheriff since 2015 or 2016 (see Background); re-elected in 2022 [S2][S5][S7].
