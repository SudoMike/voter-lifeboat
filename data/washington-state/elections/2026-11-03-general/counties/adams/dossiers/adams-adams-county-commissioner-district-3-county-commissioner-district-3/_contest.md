## What the office does

The three county commissioners hold "the power of the purse": they adopt the county budget and set policy for county government, alongside the other elected department heads [S2]. Commissioners are nominated by district in the primary and elected by the voters of the whole county in the general [S5]; in 2022 this seat was on every precinct's general ballot [S4]. The office is partisan with a four-year term [S1].

## Race dynamics

- **Incumbent and a former city councilmember.** Commissioner Miguel "Mike" Garza of Othello (prefers Republican), first elected in 2022 by 58 votes over the incumbent, faces Eleanor Brodahl of Othello (prefers Democrat), a 16-year Othello City Council member with 30 years in banking who calls herself "a conservative Democrat" [S1][S4].
- **Primary.** Garza led the District 3 primary, 334 votes to 227 [S3]. The general electorate is the whole county.
- **Budget pressure.** At an Oct. 6 forum both described a tight county budget. Garza said expenditures have been outpacing revenues and every office, the commissioners included, was told to cut operating costs; Brodahl said the county must "live within our means" and "can't just keep taxing and taxing our citizenry" [S2].
- **Growth and cooperation.** Both called for better communication with the cities; Garza wants job-creating employers rather than housing growth, and Brodahl stressed persistent business recruitment [S2].

## Differentiating issue axes

- `experience`: a first-term commissioner against a challenger with 16 years on the Othello council but new to county office.
- `taxes` and `spending`: both stress restraint; Brodahl explicitly opposes further taxation, Garza describes cutting office budgets. Neither proposes new revenue.
