## Background

- **Roots and work.** Lifelong Columbia County resident; Dayton High School graduate (1983) and Washington State University graduate (1988, B.S. in agriculture with minors in agricultural economics and agronomy); farmer, orchardist, rancher and private business owner [S1][S3].
- **Public roles.** Trustee of Walla Walla Community College since 2017 and current board chair; past Republican precinct committee officer; Columbia County Weed Board; WSU Center for Sustaining Agriculture and Natural Resources advisory committee; Columbia/Blue Mountain Counties Farm Bureau; Dayton School career and technical education advisory committee [S1][S3].
- **Community.** Assistant scoutmaster of Boy Scout Troop 332; Blue Mountain Snowmobile Club [S1].
- **Party.** Prefers the Republican Party [S1].
- **Primary.** Led the District 3-only primary with 215 votes to Miller's 198 [S5].

## Positions

### Budget, services and growth
- "Maintaining essential services and functions of county government is a high priority." With rising operating costs, he calls supporting job growth in a county of small businesses and agribusinesses "critical" [S1].
- "Our community needs funds to address issues for youth and young families. We need to complete the pool project, maintain reliable child care, and promote access to state and federal lands and the abundant natural resources and scenery in our county" [S3].
- Would "support and maintain investments in critical infrastructure" and focus on "identifying risks and modernizing systems to improve efficiency" [S1].
- Pledges to support the missions of the Port, the local health care system, schools and businesses, "speaking with a common voice to state and federal officials" [S3].

### Immigration (state sanctuary law)
- "I would abide by the state's sanctuary state law," calling it "a good step to build trust for people trying to manage their legal obligations without fear of detention and separation of their families" [S3].

### Governing approach
- Stresses transparent communication "to build trust, improve collaboration for decision-making, and reduce conflict," drawing on labor relations, policy and budget work as a college trustee [S1]. Says he will serve based on the issues people bring forward rather than their politics [S3]. Says "being a commissioner is more than having a seat at the table" and "I believe I can bring a stronger voice to the community" [S3].
