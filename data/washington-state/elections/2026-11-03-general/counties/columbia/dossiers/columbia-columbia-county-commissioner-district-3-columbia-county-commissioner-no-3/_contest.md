## What the office does

The three county commissioners are Columbia County's legislative authority: they fix county taxes, build and maintain county buildings and roads, and run county business [S4]. Commissioners are nominated by district: only District 3 voters chose the finalists in August [S3][S9], and the whole county elects the commissioner in November [S9]. The SOS precinct results put the 2022 Commissioner #3 race and the 2024 #1 and #2 races on all 13 voting precincts [S6][S7].

## Race dynamics

Incumbent Jack Miller, a retired farmer elected in 2022, faces Bill Warren, a Dayton-area farmer and orchardist; both prefer the Republican Party [S1][S8]. Because only two candidates filed, both advanced from the primary [S8]. In the District 3 primary Warren had 215 votes and Miller 198, with 4 write-ins [S5]. Fundraising is small: as of July 24 Miller had raised $400 and Warren $2,000, all his own money [S10].

The 2025 board, Miller included, declined to join Franklin County's resolution declaring itself a non-sanctuary county; Miller called county business "dollars and cents" [S11]. In the 2026 Q&A the two split on the state's sanctuary law: Miller said such laws "need to be abolished," Warren said he would abide by it [S8].

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Taxes and spending:** Miller names "over taxation" as the problem facing residents and stresses careful spending and positive cash flow; Warren says the community "needs funds" for youth and young families, including completing the pool project and child care, and would maintain infrastructure investments [S1][S8].
2. **Experience:** Miller runs on his four years as commissioner, saying the "learning curve is now behind me"; Warren is a first-time county candidate whose public role is trustee and board chair at Walla Walla Community College [S1][S8].
3. **Immigration (not a county-scored axis):** opposite answers on the state sanctuary law [S8].
4. **Growth:** both want new business and jobs; Miller adds reducing regulations and red tape, Warren modernizing systems and access to public lands [S1][S8].
5. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives: none found for either candidate.
