## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would authorize the Columbia County Park and Recreation Pool District to levy $200,000 of excess property taxes in 2026 for collection in 2027, about $0.20 per $1,000 of assessed value, for "operation and maintenance of a pool project within the District," as provided in the board's Resolution 2026-01 [S1][S2][S3]. It is an excess levy above the regular property tax levy under RCW 84.52.052 and 84.52.054 [S1][S11].

- **The district.** Voters formed it in 2023 "for the sole purpose of building, maintaining, and operating a swimming pool," with boundaries that are Columbia County minus the Town of Starbuck and the area already in the Prescott Joint Park and Recreation District, and capped its levy authority at about 20 cents per $1,000 [S1]. The 2023 formation vote was 986 to 455 [S8]. The district has five elected commissioners [S5].
- **The pool.** Dayton's old pool closed in 2017 and was filled with dirt in 2022 for safety reasons; the nearest community pool is in Prescott [S5][S6]. The district plans to build a new pool with grants and fundraising; the levy pays for operations such as election fees, insurance and legal services now and pool maintenance once it opens [S5].
- **History.** The same $200,000 levy passed in November 2024 (61.7% yes in election-night results) [S6] and added 20.6 cents per $1,000 to 2025 tax bills [S7]. It passed again in November 2025, 854 to 564 (60.2%) [S10]. The argument for says the levy "is voted on annually" [S1].
- **Funding raised so far.** Per the argument for: $5,000 (Warren Community Action Fund), a $225,000 reimbursement grant from the state Recreation and Conservation Office, $7,500 (Dayton/Columbia County Washington Fund) and $20,000 in fundraising for construction, plus $9,360 (Innovia Foundation) for administration [S1]. The Union-Bulletin totals $266,860 [S4].

## Cost

- $200,000 collected in 2027, approximately $0.20 per $1,000 of assessed value [S1][S3]; the Union-Bulletin estimates about $60 a year on a $300,000 home, in addition to current property taxes [S4].
- One year (2026 levy, 2027 collection) [S1].
- An excess levy needs voter approval under RCW 84.52.052 [S11].

## Arguments for

Written by Kevin Rust: the planned Dayton pool would offer swim lessons that "help curb drowning," structured programs and part-time seasonal jobs for kids, a pool "large enough for state competition," low-impact aerobics and physical therapy, and sensory-sensitivity swims; commissioners have pursued "a modest pool, focusing on essential rather than extravagant amenities"; "'YES' secures funding for operation and maintenance, which is voted on annually" [S1][S2].

## Arguments against

No statement submitted [S1][S2][S4]. In the 2025 vote, 39.8% of voters said no [S10].

## Where it is on the ballot

Every Columbia County precinct except the Town of Starbuck votes on it (2024: 12 of 13 voting precincts, all but STARBUCK CITY) [S9]; the Brooklyn precinct sample ballot carries it [S3]. WA DOR's park-district layer (PKR2025) holds the district as polygon `CPR`.

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote continues an excess property tax of about $0.20 per $1,000 that voters approved for 2025 and 2026 collection; with the 2025 levy in place for 2026, this is a renewal at the same amount, so +1 (renewal tier) [S1][S7][S10].
- `spending`: funds operation of a pool that is not yet built, a new public facility. A case for +1 (invest and expand) exists, but construction itself is grant-funded and the levy covers operations; not mapped.
