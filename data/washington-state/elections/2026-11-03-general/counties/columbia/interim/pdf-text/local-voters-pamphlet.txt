2 Please vote for only one candidate per race. When finished, place your ballot in the security sleeve. Place the security sleeve in the return envelope, then read, sign, and date the Voter’s Declaration on the return envelope. The envelope must be signed in order for the ballot to be counted. If you are unable to sign, make an identifying mark (such as an “X”) and have two people sign as witnesses. A power of attorney is not acceptable for the voter declaration. You may vote and return your ballot as soon as you receive it. You can return your ballot to a ballot drop box or send it by mail (no stamp required) to the Auditor’s Office. The drop boxes will be opened on October 16, 2026 and will close at 8:00 p.m. on Election Day, November 3, 2026. (See next page for drop box locations.) If you choose to return your ballot by mail, the USPS recommends that you mail your ballot at least one week before Election Day. Ballots postmarked after Election Day, November 3, 2026, will not be counted. Voting and Returning Your Ballot Voters needing assistance may use the accessible voting unit available eighteen days prior to an election through the close of voting on Election Day at 8:00 pm. Please call the Auditor’s Office to schedule an appointment to utilize the accessible voting unit. Accessible Voting If you did not receive a ballot or need a replacement, call 509-382-4541 or visit the Columbia County Auditor’s Office. A replacement or reissued ballot cancels the first ballot, and the replacement ballot becomes your current ballot. Replacement Ballots Thank you for participating in the 2026 General Election. Election Office Memo You may update your address by visiting www.votewa. gov or contacting the Columbia County Auditor’s Office at 509-382-4541. Updating Your Address Washington State uses signatures to verify voters’ identification. In order for your vote to count, the signature on your ballot return envelope must match the signature in your voter registration record. Signatures change over time, and your current signature may be very different from the one you used when you registered to vote. If you think your signature may have changed, contact the Auditor’s Office to learn how to update it. Updating Your Signature There are several ways you can register to vote in Washington State: 1. Register in person at any County Auditor’s Office. Your local office is located at 115 E Main Street, Suite 3, Dayton, WA 99328. The Columbia County Auditor’s office is open Monday - Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. and until 8:00 p.m. on Election night. 2. Register online at www.votewa.gov. Your registration will be routed to your local office where it will be reviewed and a voter registration card will be mailed to you to acknowledge your registration process is complete. 3. Register by mail by calling the Columbia County Auditor’s Office at 509-382-4541 and asking that a voter registration application be mailed to you. Complete the application and return it by mail. NOTE: Online and mail-in registrations must be received no later than eight days prior to the election. After that time, you must register in person. Should this excess levy be approved? Levy Year/Collection Year Estimated Levy Rate/$1,000 Assessed Value Levy Amount 2026/2027 $0.20 $200,000 In 2023, the Voters approved the formation of the Columbia County Park and Recreation Pool District (“the District”) by ballot measure: the ballot measure formed the District for the sole purpose of building, maintaining, and operating a swimming pool, set boundaries of the District contiguous with the boundaries of Columbia County (excluding the Town of Starbuck and the areas already covered by the Prescott Joint Parks and Recreation District), and capped levy authority at approximately 20-cents per $1000 of assessed property value. As authorized in 2023, the District has now proposed that an excess levy - a tax on real and personal property above the regular property tax levy as provided for in RCW 84.52.052 and 84.52.054 - in the amount of approximately 20-cents per $1000 of assessed value on all real and personal property within the District, as provided by law. The total amount to be raised by this excess levy is $200,000.00. If approved, the levy would be applied to 2026 property taxes, and collected in 2027. The District intends to use this money for operation and maintenance of a pool project within the District. Voting LEVY YES means you approve of the tax being applied and used for the stated purposes; LEVY NO means you do not approve of the tax being applied and used for the stated purpose. EXPLANATORY STATEMENT Columbia County Park and Recreation Pool District Proposition No. 1 Local Measure In 2017, the Dayton pool closed. Moving here in 2019, we never got to use it. We came from a community bordering a lake with a beachfront park overflowing with families, cluttered with trikes, bikes, and strollers. Lots of healthy outdoor activity, a place where kids learned to swim, dive, splash, and were reminded not to run on the dock. It was a great place to get together for birthdays, holidays, graduation and team parties, and special occasions. The park was a community institution, a huge draw for homebuyers. It made for a healthier community and elevated the quality of life. The planned Dayton pool will deliver similar perks, with endless recreational and character-building opportunities. Swim lessons will help curb drowning. Kids will have structured programs and part-time seasonal jobs, anchoring them to our community. It’ll be a place for both the athletic and not so, providing swim team opportunities with a pool large enough for state competition, a place for low impact aerobics and physical therapy, and sensory sensitivity swims for those with special needs. Park and Recreation Pool District commissioners have secured $5,000 (Warren Community Action Fund), $225,000 reimbursement grant (Washington State Recreation and Conservation Office), $7,500 (Dayton/Columbia County Washington Fund), and $20,000 (fundraising), all for the construction of a modest pool, focusing on essential rather than extravagant amenities. Also received was $9,360 (lnnovia Foundation) for administrative expenses. “YES” secures funding for operation and maintenance, which is voted on annually. Effect change, vote “YES”. 1 0 The Board of Commissioners of the Prescott Joint Park and Recreation District adopted Resolution No. 7-14-2026 B, concerning a proposition to finance maintenance and operation expenses. This proposition would authorize the District to levy the following excess taxes upon all taxable property within the District, for support of the District’s maintenance and operation expenses during 2027: MAINTENANCE AND OPERATION EXCESS LEVY Levy Yes [ ] Levy No [ ] all as provided in Resolution No. 7-14-2026 B. Should this proposition be approved? Levy Year/Collection Year Estimated Levy Rate/$1,000 Assessed Value Levy Amount 2026/2027 $00.35 $175,000 The Prescott Joint Park and Recreation District requests that the voters approve the proposed maintenance and operation excess levy ballot proposition. Under current Washington law, park and recreation districts and park and recreation service areas may seek voter approval for what is commonly referred to as an “operations and maintenance levy.” (See RCW 84.52.052.) This levy is in addition to, or in excess of, regular property taxes, only endures for one-year, and is intended to assist the district or service area reach its contractual obligations. A vote of “Yes” by sufficient voters will result in imposition of the excess tax levy in the amount of $175,000. A vote of “No,” will result in no additional tax levy. The additional tax levy is the primary source of funding used by the Prescott Joint Park and Recreation District to operate and maintain its facilities. EXPLANATORY STATEMENT Prescott Joint Park and Recreation District Proposition No. 1 Local Measure The Prescott Pool has always been more than just a place to cool off on a hot summer day. It’s where kids learn to swim, families make memories, teenagers find summer jobs, and neighbors gather. In a small town like Prescott, where recreational opportunities are limited, the pool has been one of the few places that truly brings our community together. This summer reminded us how much we’ve taken that for granted. Without the pool, our children lost a safe place to spend their days, families had to drive to other towns for swim lessons, and many kids simply missed out because those extra trips aren’t possible for everyone. We felt the absence of something that has been part of Prescott for generations. Voting YES on the Parks & Recreation Levy is about making sure our kids don’t have to experience another summer without these opportunities. The pool is also an investment in our community. It serves families not only from Prescott but from neighboring communities as well, strengthens local connections, and gives young people positive ways to spend their summers. Once a community pool is gone, it’s incredibly difficult to bring it back. We’ve proven what this community can accomplish when we come together. We fought to protect our school because we believe our children deserve every opportunity to succeed. Now we have another chance to invest in their future. Please vote YES for the Prescott Pool Levy.
