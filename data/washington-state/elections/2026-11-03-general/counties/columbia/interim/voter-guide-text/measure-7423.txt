## ArgumentFor
In 2017, the Dayton pool closed. Moving here in 2019, we never got to use it. We came from a community bordering a lake with a beachfront park overflowing with families, cluttered with trikes, bikes, and strollers. Lots of healthy outdoor activity, a place where kids learned to swim, dive, splash, and were reminded not to run on the dock. It was a great place to get together for birthdays, holidays, graduation and team parties, and special occasions. The park was a community institution, a huge draw for homebuyers. It made for a healthier community and elevated the quality of life.	
The planned Dayton pool will deliver similar perks, with endless recreational and character-building opportunities. Swim lessons will help curb drowning. Kids will have structured programs and part-time seasonal jobs, anchoring them to our community. It’ll be a place for both the athletic and not so, providing swim team opportunities with a pool large enough for state competition, a place for low impact aerobics and physical therapy, and sensory sensitivity swims for those with special needs.	
Park and Recreation Pool District commissioners have secured $5,000 (Warren Community Action Fund), $225,000 reimbursement grant (Washington State Recreation and Conservation Office), $7,500 (Dayton/Columbia County Washington Fund), and $20,000 (fundraising), all for the construction of a modest pool, focusing on essential rather than extravagant amenities. Also received was $9,360 (lnnovia Foundation) for administrative expenses.
“YES” secures funding for operation and maintenance, which is voted on annually. Effect change, vote “YES”.
Kevin Rust

## ExplanatoryStatement
In 2023, the Voters approved the formation of the Columbia County Park and Recreation Pool District (“the District”) by ballot measure: the ballot measure formed the District for the sole purpose of building, maintaining, and operating a swimming pool, set boundaries of the District contiguous with the boundaries of Columbia County (excluding the Town of Starbuck and the areas already covered by the Prescott Joint Parks and Recreation District), and capped levy authority at approximately 20-cents per $1000 of assessed property value.
As authorized in 2023, the District has now proposed that an excess levy - a tax on real and personal property above the regular property tax levy as provided for in RCW 84.52.052 and 84.52.054 - in the amount of approximately 20-cents per $1000 of assessed value on all real and personal property within the District, as provided by law. The total amount to be raised by this excess levy is $200,000.00. If approved, the levy would be applied to 2026 property taxes, and collected in 2027. The District intends to use this money for operation and maintenance of a pool project within the District. 
	Voting LEVY YES means you approve of the tax being applied and used for the stated purposes; LEVY NO means you do not approve of the tax being applied and used for the stated purpose.
