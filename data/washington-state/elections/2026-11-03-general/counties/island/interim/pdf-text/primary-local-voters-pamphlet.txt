Message from the Elections Office Your ballot is your voice. Make sure it can be counted! Mark your ballot clearly. Be sure to mark the box next to your choice with a dark colored ink. If you want to make a change to your vote, use the instructions on your ballot to make the change. Put your ballot packet together to return it. Your ballot goes into the security sleeve and then into your ballot return envelope. It provides an added layer of secrecy for your ballot. If you forget to put your ballot in the security sleeve, don't worry - your ballot can still be counted without it. Sign your ballot envelope. Be sure to sign inside the box on your return envelope. Your envelope signature will be verified against the signature in your voter registration record. Power of attorney is not valid for voting. If you are unable to sign your ballot envelope, make a mark and have it signed by two witnesses in the area provided. Return your ballot on time. Returning your ballot by U.S. Postal Mail? - Due to changes in postal service processing times, the U.S. Postal Service strongly recommends mailing your ballot a minimum of seven (7) days before election day to be certain that your ballot will receive a postmark in time for the election. Returning your ballot to a drop box? - There are six Official Ballot Drop Boxes in Island County. Drop boxes are open 24-hours a day from the day ballots are mailed until 8:00 p.m. on Election Day. Contact 316 SE Pioneer Way #368 | Oak Harbor, WA 98277 (360) 720-0602 https://votehazelo.com VoteHazelo@gmail.com Getting My Ballot When are ballots sent to voters? Registered voters in Washington have ballots automatically mailed to them no later than 18 days before any election. Your ballot will be mailed to the address you provided on your voter registration. No action is required on your part to have your ballot sent to you. What do I do when I receive a ballot for someone who no longer lives here? Simply write "Does Not Live Here" or "Deceased" and return it to the U.S. Postal Service. A voter's registration cannot be cancelled without verification from the voter. This protects voters from having someone cancel their registration without the voters' knowledge. By returning the ballot to the election office, the process can begin for verifying the whereabouts of the voter to update or inactivate the registration. Why did I get a second ballot? If a voter updates their voter registration, a replacement ballot is automatically generated to their new address. The most common ways for this to happen is a voter updating their driver's license or going online to update their mailing address. The original ballot is suspended and the replacement ballot is now the valid ballot. The voting system records incoming ballots and will only allow one ballot to be counted per voter.

Before an Election • Voting systems certification and testing: Ballot counting equipment is tested and certified by an independent authority and by the state. • Voting systems are not connected to the internet or to any network. • Pre-election audit: Logic and Accuracy Test - Before every election, counties test their voting systems to verify that the systems were correctly set up and accurately counting votes. • Voter Registration Database: Washington's statewide voter registration database is continually maintained and updated as voters become registered, move, change their name, pass away, or are no longer eligible to vote. Precinct Committee Officer (PCO) | All Precincts | 2-year Regular Term Each major political party in Washington state has a Precinct Committee Officer (PCO) position in each precinct. The PCO is the elected official who represents the voters of that precinct with that political party. PCO races appear on the Primary Election ballot in even-numbered years, and only if two or more candidates within the same party are running for the same precinct. In precincts where only one candidate has filed for their party, the candidate is considered to be elected and that race does not appear on the ballot. When no candidate has filed, the race does not appear on the ballot. (RCW 29A.52.171) State law does not allow writein votes for PCO. As a result, not every voter will have a PCO race on their ballot. PCO candidate information is not printed in the Voters’ Pamphlet. For more information about Precinct Committee Officers, contact the political party of your choice. Marking My Ballot Make your choice: Using blue or black ink, fill in the box to the left of the candidate name or measure choice you want to select. Changed your mind? Cross out the line and box you marked before and you can then make a new choice. Want to skip over a contest? Simply don't fill in a box. The rest of your ballot will still count. Want to vote for a write-in candidate? In each contest on your ballot, below the candidate names, there is a dashed line with a box to the left of it. Mark the box and print the name of your write- in candidate choice on the line. Why don't the names of write-in candidates show in the results reports? Under Washington law, results for individual declared write-in candidates are tabulated only when there are enough write-in votes to impact the outcome of a race. Until then, all of the write-ins are reported as a single total called "Unresolved write-ins". Write-in votes for persons who have not filed as a write-in candidate are not individually tabulated. 33 Local Voters’ Pamphlet The Island County Auditor is not responsible for the content of statements or arguments (WAC 434-381-180). Vote-By-Mail (VBM) How does VBM work? All eligible voters are sent a ballot at least 18 days before any election. Military and overseas ballots are sent at least 45 days before. Your residential address must be in Island County, but your mailing address can be elsewhere. After marking your choices, your ballot is put in the secrecy sleeve. The secrecy sleeve is put in the return envelope and signed. Ballots have pre-paid postage and can be returned through the mail or at ballot drop boxes. If returning your ballot by mail, the U.S. Postal Service recommends mailing your ballot 7-10 days before election day. Drop boxes are open until 8:00 p.m. on election day. Voters can check their ballot status online or sign up for text notifications at VoteWA.gov. It may take the system 3–4 days for status updates to post. New ballots can be requested or printed, but will suspend any previously issued ballot. The new ballot will be the valid one. How does a VBM ballot get processed? Envelopes are scanned and marked as “Received” in the VoteWA system, then sorted into batches. Each signature is checked by trained election staff. The voter is contacted if the signature is missing or doesn’t match the voter registration record. If the signature is a match, the voter is given credit for voting. Text notifications are sent and voters can see their updated ballot status at VoteWA.gov. Teams of election workers open the outer envelopes and the secrecy sleeve is removed. The outer envelope and the secrecy sleeve are each placed in separate areas. At this point, the ballots cannot be traced back to the voters. Ballots are removed from the secrecy sleeve and inspected. Ballots are reviewed for scanning issues like torn corners or other damage. Ballots are scanned and securely stored. Scanners are not connected to any form of network or internet. Scanned ballots are tabulated. Ballots continue to be verified, processed and scanned. Votes are tabulated until the election results are officially certified.
