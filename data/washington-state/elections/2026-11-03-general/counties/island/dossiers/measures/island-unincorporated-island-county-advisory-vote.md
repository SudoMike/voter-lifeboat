## What it does

This is a non-binding advisory vote. It asks voters in unincorporated Island County whether the Board of County Commissioners should amend Island County Code chapter 9.08A to ban the use of consumer fireworks in unincorporated Island County [S1]. The commissioners called the vote in Resolution C-62-25 [S1][S2].

- "This vote will not change the law." The board "may or may not act on the results" [S1].
- Permitted public displays are not affected [S1].
- Only residents of unincorporated areas vote. Residents of Oak Harbor, Coupeville and Langley cannot vote on it [S8][S9]. The cities and town set their own fireworks rules [S1]. Langley adopted its own ban in 2020 [S9].
- The South Whidbey Record reported it is the first advisory vote in county history [S8].

**County authority.** State law lets a county "limit or prohibit the sale, purchase, possession, or use of consumer fireworks" [S5]. A local ordinance more restrictive than state law cannot take effect sooner than one year after adoption [S6]. The county code repeats this rule: a more restrictive amendment to chapter 9.08A takes effect one year after adoption unless the adopting resolution sets a different date [S3]. So if the board adopted a ban after this vote, it could not take effect for at least a year.

**Current county rules.** Under chapter 9.08A [S3][S4]:

- Consumer fireworks may be discharged in unincorporated areas only on July 3 (9 a.m. to 11 p.m.), July 4 (9 a.m. to midnight) and December 31 (6 p.m. to 1 a.m. January 1). State law alone would allow discharge from June 28 through July 5 and on New Year's Eve [S5].
- Mortar fireworks are banned. That amendment (Ordinance C-87-23) was adopted January 23, 2024 and took effect January 23, 2025 [S3].
- The county fire marshal can ban fireworks during severe dry weather by issuing a Type II burn ban [S3].
- Fireworks are banned in county and state parks [S3][S4].
- A violation is a Class I infraction with a fine of up to $250 [S3].

## Cost

- The measure sets no tax or fee [S1].
- Placing the measure on the ballot cost about $3,000 to $3,500. The commissioners said the money was not in the county budget [S7][S9]. Safe and Humane Fireworks of Island County, a group that wants a ban, raised the money at the board's request [S9].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

**How the vote got on the ballot.**

- Commissioner Melanie Bacon (D, South and Central Whidbey) proposed the advisory vote at an October 2025 work session. She has pushed for fireworks limits for several years and earlier won the mortar ban and the removal of July 5 as a legal day [S7].
- Resolution C-62-25 was adopted November 4, 2025 [S2]. It passed 2-1 [S8].
- Commissioner Jill Johnson (R, Oak Harbor area) voted no. She said an advisory vote measures voters rather than residents and leaves out Navy members registered elsewhere, people under 18, and city residents who own property in the county [S7][S8]. She called the city exclusion "creating different classes of citizens" [S8]. She also said the board had already decided not to ban fireworks [S7].
- Commissioner Janet St. Clair (D, North Whidbey and Camano) voted yes. She said she backed it to support Bacon and had proposed an advisory vote herself two years earlier, when she thought a ban adopted by the board alone would be "too heavy handed" [S7][S8].
- Bacon said a clear result "would make that easier for me to be able to take some kind of action as a commissioner" [S7].

**Fire record.** Over the 2026 Fourth of July weekend, fire chiefs said fireworks caused or were suspected in most of the incidents on Whidbey Island: a severe hand injury in Oak Harbor, a sailboat fire, a burned historic building's remains in Coupeville, and at least five vegetation fires [S10]. One Oak Harbor fire was started on July 5, a day county rules do not allow [S10]. In late June 2026, a house fire and explosion involving about 700 pounds of fireworks on Smugglers Cove Road injured three firefighters [S10].

## Arguments for

From the statement for, filed by the Committee for a Safer, Healthier, and more Environmentally Friendly July 4th [S1]:

- A ban would "reduce preventable danger before one spark becomes a tragedy" [S1].
- As nearby areas restrict fireworks, Island County "has increasingly become a destination for 'fireworks tourism'" [S1].
- Washington is in its fourth straight drought-emergency year. Camano and Whidbey have limited evacuation routes, dense forests and mostly volunteer fire departments [S1].
- The statement cites the state Insurance Commissioner on wildfire risk raising the cost of homeowners' insurance [S1].
- Fireworks frighten pets, livestock and wildlife, disturb veterans and others, and leave debris [S1].
- Permitted professional displays would remain [S1].

Jackie Lasater, co-founder of Safe and Humane Fireworks of Island County, cited fire risk, pollution and stress on animals and people. She said the county "has historically put minimal effort into enforcement" and that a full ban would be simpler for the sheriff's office to enforce [S9]. At the November 2025 meeting, five residents spoke for the advisory vote, including members of Citizens for Safe and Humane Fireworks and the president of the Whidbey Audubon Society [S8].

## Arguments against

From the statement against, filed by the Committee to Keep Fireworks [S1]. The Record reported that Commissioner Johnson was responsible for writing it [S9].

- Fireworks traditions are something "every generation deserves the opportunity to create" [S1].
- The county has already reached "reasonable compromise": limited days, the mortar ban, limits on where fireworks may be used, and extra limits in dry conditions [S1].
- "We should enforce those rules and insist on responsible behavior—not replace reasonable regulation with prohibition" [S1].
- "Our responsibility is not to eliminate every freedom that carries risk or inconvenience" [S1].

Johnson's separate objections were about the vote itself: who may vote, and whether the board should poll voters instead of deciding. "It's a representative form of government," she said [S7][S8].
