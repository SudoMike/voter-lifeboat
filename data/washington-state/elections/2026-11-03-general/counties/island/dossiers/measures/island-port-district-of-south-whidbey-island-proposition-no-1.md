## What it does

Proposition No. 1 is a levy lid lift for the Port District of South Whidbey Island. It would raise the port's regular property tax rate by six cents, from $0.109 to $0.169 per $1,000 of assessed value, for collection in 2027 [S1]. The port commission adopted Resolution No. 2026-06 to place it on the ballot [S1].

- **Permanent base.** After 2027, "such dollar amount will be used as the base to compute limitations for subsequent years" [S1]. Later growth returns to the 1%-a-year limit plus new construction [S1][S15].
- **Purpose.** The explanatory statement lists "renovation and modernization of port facilities including at the historic fairgrounds and waterfront," port economic development, and "expansion of the small business incubator programs" [S1].
- **District.** The port serves about 16,000 residents and businesses in Freeland, Clinton and Langley [S1][S3]. It owns the harbor at Langley, the Whidbey Island Fairgrounds & Events Center and campground, the Humphrey Road ferry parking lot, the Bush Point and Possession Beach boat ramps, and the Clinton beach park, pier and dock [S5].
- **Legal cap.** State law lets port districts levy up to $0.45 per $1,000 for general port purposes [S4]. The port says the new rate stays below the maximum [S2].

**Adoption.** Resolution 26-06 was on the agenda of the commission's June 9, 2026 special meeting [S4]. The packet's draft, which names commissioners Greg Easton, Curt Gordon and Jack Ng, cites the port's 2023-2028 Comprehensive Scheme of Harbor Improvements. It says the 1% limit "would not be sufficient" for the costs of renovating and modernizing port facilities [S4]. The port announced on June 10 that the board had approved the resolution [S3]. The signed resolution and the vote count were not found. The June 9 minutes posted on the port's site are an image scan.

## Cost

- **Rate:** +$0.06 per $1,000, from $0.109 to $0.169 [S1]. That is about a 55% increase in the port's rate (computed for this dossier).
- **Average home:** the port says about $4.33 a month, or about $52 a year, for the average South Whidbey home assessed at $866,000 [S2][S3][S5]. Computed for this dossier: $866,000 × 0.06 / 1,000 = $51.96. The port portion of the bill would go from about $94 to about $146.
- **$500,000 home:** about $30 a year more (computed for this dossier).
- **Revenue:** no revenue estimate was found. At the district's 2025 assessed value of $8,054,482,542, six cents would raise about $483,000 a year (computed for this dossier from DOR data [S8]; 2027 values will differ). The port's 2025 regular levy was $881,596 [S8].
- The rate applies equally to residential and commercial property in the district [S2].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

**Levy history.** DOR levy detail shows a jump in the port's rate for 2017, then a steady fall [S8][S9][S10][S11][S12][S13][S14]:

| Taxes due | Assessed value | Rate per $1,000 | Port regular levy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2005 | $2,708,602,119 | $0.15137 | $410,001 [S14] |
| 2015 | $3,888,004,669 | $0.13396 | $520,836 [S13] |
| 2016 | $3,975,264,819 | $0.13276 | $527,772 [S12] |
| 2017 | $4,105,163,223 | $0.18300 | $751,245 [S11] |
| 2020 | $5,064,779,971 | $0.15778 | $799,132 [S10] |
| 2024 | $7,872,171,268 | $0.10988 | $864,963 [S9] |
| 2025 | $8,054,482,542 | $0.10945 | $881,596 [S8] |

The port says the measure would "restore the levy to approximately its 2017 rate" [S2]. The proposed $0.169 is below the 2017 rate of $0.183 [S11]. The Record reported there has been no levy increase since the port took over the Whidbey Island Fairgrounds from Island County in 2017 [S6][S7]. The years in the table are samples.

**Why now.**

- Executive Director Jim Pivarnik said the levy "has not kept pace with inflation." Projects identified for the six-year capital facilities plan total about $19 million and focus on keeping existing facilities. "We're not talking about new stuff. We're talking about preserving what we have" [S5].
- Grants could cover most project costs, but the port needs local matching money to qualify [S5][S2]. The port says it has secured more than $3.2 million in grants [S2]. In February 2025 Commissioner Curt Gordon said many grants require a 50% port match, and that a 2018 county economic development grant of $688,610 was running out [S6].
- The port says it has cut staff by 25% through attrition to seven full-time employees. It has raised fairgrounds rental and event fees 10% and harbor rates 18%, and deferred some projects that need matching funds [S2].

**Planned projects.**

- **Fairgrounds:** about $6 million proposed, including a new horse barn, riding area upgrades and pole barn improvements [S5]. The port FAQ lists "preservation of the Whidbey Island Fairgrounds & Events Center" [S2].
- **Langley harbor:** electrical upgrades and breakwater stabilization. Pivarnik said "every day someone is breaking" [S5]. The FAQ lists harbor float, gangway and marina improvements [S2]. In 2025 the port had an $80,000 estimate to repair dock electrical outlets [S6].
- **Passenger ferry:** dock improvements for a planned port-run passenger-only ferry. The FAQ lists infrastructure for a future passenger-only ferry facility in Clinton [S2][S5].
- **Other:** ADA improvements, the Possession Beach boat ramp, repaving and a pay system at the Humphrey Road lot, and sediment control at the Bush Point dock [S2][S5].
- **Small business incubator:** the ballot statement and resolution name "expansion of small business incubator programs" [S1][S4]. The FAQ lists "programs supporting small businesses and entrepreneurs" [S2]. No cost or program detail was found.

**Earlier hesitation.** In April 2025 commissioners delayed the request until a capital facilities plan was done. Gordon said "the public deserves to know more specifically what they're paying for" [S7]. Commissioner Jack Ng said 2025, with market losses, was "probably not the best year to ask" [S7].

## Arguments for

From the statement for (the voters' guide does not name an author) [S1]:

- The measure would "help preserve South Whidbey's public waterfronts, fairgrounds, and small business spaces" [S1].
- It would help the port "address growing maintenance needs, compete for grant funding, and invest in infrastructure" [S1].
- Grant dollars "help stretch local tax dollars further" [S1].
- The port "has a long history of responsible financial management, operating under balanced budgets, and successfully completing independent state audits" [S1].

## Arguments against

The voters' guide says: "No community members have come forward to provide this statement" [S1]. No committee opposing the measure was found in PDC data [S16]. In 2025 Gordon expected the request might be "ugly" because people may not understand how small the port's tax is [S7].

## Endorsements and campaign

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote raises the port's rate about 55%, from $0.109 to $0.169 per $1,000, about $52 a year on the average home, and keeps the higher base [S1][S2]. A levy lid lift is the cap-lift example at the rubric's pole_b. The dollar amount is small, but the direction is clear.
- `spending`: mixed, no mapping. The port frames the money as preservation and grant matching, "not talking about new stuff" [S5]. The ballot statement also names expanding the small business incubator, and the project list includes infrastructure for a new passenger ferry [S1][S2][S5].
- `local-control`: none. The measure does not move authority between governments. The port notes it gets no county funding apart from grants [S5].
- `social` / `parental-rights`: not addressed.
