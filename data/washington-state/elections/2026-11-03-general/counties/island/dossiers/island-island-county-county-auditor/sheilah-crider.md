## Background

Sheilah Crider has been Island County Auditor for 18 years (S1). She was first appointed in 2008 when her predecessor left the state (S4). Before that she served 10 years on the Oak Harbor City Council and 2 years as a county freeholder (S1). Her statement lists 14 years on the Island County Planning Commission; the 2022 South Whidbey Record article says 15 (S1, S4). She was a Realtor until she became auditor, when she gave up her license (S4). She is a Secretary of State certified election administrator and has taken Humphrey School cybersecurity courses (S1, S3). She lives in Oak Harbor (S3).

She ran as a Republican in 2022 (S4), and the News-Times described her as a Republican as late as April 2026 (S9). She is running in 2026 as an Independent (S1). She says "We conduct partisan work, but we do it in a nonpartisan manner, and that's why I chose to run as an Independent this year" (S2). Her primary-pamphlet statement matched the general guide statement (S13).

In the August 4 primary she placed first with 11,341 votes (39.7%), ahead of Barbara Fuller (8,014, 28.0%), Andrea Downs (5,254, 18.4%) and Terry Lacey (3,892, 13.6%) (S10).

## Positions

### Election security and administration
- "I will continue implementation of strong cybersecurity policies to ensure citizens their votes are secure" (S1).
- Says she has improved cybersecurity and election processing technology over the past four years: "We're always on the watch for anything that is unusual" (S3).
- Priorities for a new term include better communication and stronger elections security ahead of the 2028 presidential election (S2).
- "Our policies, practices and procedures have stood the test of time" (S2).
- Pledges "fair, non-partisan service to all citizens" (S1).
- In 2022 she said misinformation and disinformation about elections "puts tremendous fear in [voters'] minds," and that she wanted a methodology to address it (S4).

### Fiscal accountability and staffing
- "Fiscal accountability of our county is one of our most essential responsibilities." Her office prepares the county's annual financial statement and facilitates the annual SAO audit (S1).
- On the chief accountant post, vacant for nearly a year: "salary makes all the difference in the world." She notes the previous chief accountant left for a higher-paying job and says the county has limited budget flexibility (S3).
- Wants funding to hire and train another employee so the office has backup when key staff leave (S3).
- Says the voter registration coordinator, a certified elections administrator, was hired as elections supervisor to replace the retiring Michele Reagan: "We don't lose anything in that transition" (S2).
- Says a dedicated space for public observers would help the elections office, but funding is an obstacle (S2).
- In 2022 she said she welcomes new technology that would save the county manpower and dollars (S4).

### Transparency and outreach
- Says the office promotes transparency through open houses and print and online advertising, and wants more community involvement (S3).

## Record

- **2024 mask mandate.** In 2024, 10 of 24 election workers fell ill with COVID during the primary recount. Crider then required masks for everyone in the ballot-processing rooms during the general election, under a canvassing board rule (S5). Two Republican Party observers refused and were cited (S5). A visiting judge dismissed the observers' lawsuit with prejudice in April 2025, ruling that Crider "clearly has the authority to control her work environment" (S5). A jury convicted former county GOP chair Tim Hazelo of felony unauthorized access to an election office and of criminal trespass (S6). Crider says of the mandate: "It happened. It's over, and we're done with it" (S2).
- **Related public records dispute.** In the criminal case of a second observer, a Superior Court judge found a limited conflict involving Crider and ordered her to get independent legal counsel. In a separate public records lawsuit over heavily redacted emails between Crider and Prosecutor Greg Banks, a visiting judge disqualified the prosecutor's office in August 2026 and ordered a special prosecutor (S7).
- **State audit of 2025 finances.** The SAO gave the county an unmodified (clean) opinion on its financial statements with no material weaknesses in financial-reporting controls. It made two findings on federal COVID relief funds: a material weakness in quarterly reports to the U.S. Treasury and a significant deficiency in subrecipient monitoring for a broadband contract. No questioned costs were identified (S8). The article does not say which county office prepared the federal reports (S8).
- **Earlier audit finding.** In 2022 Fuller pointed to a prior SAO finding that the county had filed duplicate claims for COVID relief grants in 2020 (S4).
