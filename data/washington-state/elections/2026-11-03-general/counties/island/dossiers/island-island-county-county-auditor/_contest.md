## What the office does
The county auditor records deeds and other instruments, keeps the county's accounts with the treasurer, sends the state auditor a statement of the state fund account, and publishes the county's prior-year finances to the State Auditor's standards (S2). The auditor also supervises all primaries and elections in the county (S3). Crider's statement lists the office's divisions as accounting, payroll, licensing, recording, marriage licenses, and elections and voter registration. The office prepares the county's annual financial statement and facilitates the annual State Auditor's Office (SAO) audit (S1).

## Race dynamics
This is a 2022 rematch. Sheilah Crider was appointed auditor in 2008 and beat Barbara Fuller in 2022, running as a Republican. In that year's primary Crider had about 54% to Fuller's 46% (S7). Crider is running this year as an Independent. She says "We conduct partisan work, but we do it in a nonpartisan manner, and that's why I chose to run as an Independent this year" (S5). Fuller is a Democrat (S1).

In the four-way August 4 primary Crider received 11,341 votes (39.7%) and Fuller 8,014 (28.0%). Democrat Andrea Downs got 5,254 (18.4%) and Terry Lacey 3,892 (13.6%) (S4). The Island County Democrats endorsed both Fuller and Downs before the primary (S10), and Downs has since backed Fuller (S5). The News-Times framed the general as "change against continuity" (S5). Both campaigns are small: Crider reports $1,900 raised, all her own money, and Fuller $2,200 (S11, S12).

Issues in the race include staffing: the chief accountant post was vacant for nearly a year, and the elections supervisor retires at year end (S6). Another is Crider's 2024 mask mandate in the ballot-counting room after a COVID outbreak among election workers. A judge upheld her authority to impose it (S9). Fuller says "I don't disagree with doing a mask mandate" (S5). The state audit of the county's 2025 finances gave a clean opinion on the financial statements and made two findings on federal COVID-fund reporting (S8).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience:** an 18-year incumbent and certified election administrator, against a challenger with 25+ years in regulatory compliance and auditing who has not held public office (S1, S6).
2. **Reform:** Fuller says she is not satisfied with the status quo. She wants to modernize the office, assess why posts stay vacant, and improve outreach, and says she will not "clean house" (S5, S6). Crider cites the office's record and says its "policies, practices and procedures have stood the test of time" (S5).
3. **Spending:** Crider says salary is what limits hiring and wants funding for a backup employee. She says a dedicated observer space would help but funding is an obstacle (S5, S6). Fuller says she will look for "more cost-effective and sustainable ways" to run the office and that a new elections office will probably be needed at some point (S5, S6).
4. Both stress election security and nonpartisan administration, so that does not separate them much (S1).
5. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives (I-645, I-1, I-638): none found for either candidate.
