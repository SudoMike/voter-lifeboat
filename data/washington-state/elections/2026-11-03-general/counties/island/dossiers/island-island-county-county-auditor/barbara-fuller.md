## Background

Barbara Fuller is a Coupeville resident who prefers the Democratic Party (S1, S3). She lists 25+ years in regulatory compliance, auditing and operational leadership, including leading audits and managing oversight programs, and says she taught safety, accountability and ethics as a college professor (S1). She is a former police officer (S1, S3). She holds an MBA, a master's in engineering and a BS in occupational safety and health (S1). She sits on the Island County Civil Service Commission, which recruits sheriff's office employees and deputies (S1, S3). In 2022 the South Whidbey Record described her as a health, safety and environmental consultant and part-time community-college faculty member with 22 years of risk-management experience (S4).

She ran for auditor against Crider in 2022, her first run for any office. She received nearly 46% in that primary to Crider's 54% and lost the general (S1, S4). In the August 4, 2026 primary she placed second with 8,014 votes (28.0%) to Crider's 11,341 (39.7%) (S6). Her primary-pamphlet statement matched the general guide statement (S9). She lists no campaign website (S1).

## Positions

### Office management and staffing
- Wants to modernize the auditor's office and "isn't satisfied with maintaining the status quo" (S3).
- Wants to find out why the chief accountant post stayed vacant for nearly a year, and suggests the cause may be more than salary (S3). Says an assessment of the office, with staff input, could identify improvements: "They know what opportunities we have for improvement" (S2).
- Says she does not plan to "clean house" if elected: "That is definitely not my plan, and I think anybody that does that is foolish" (S2).
- Will pursue certification as an elections administrator if elected (S2).
- "I think we can do more to make the auditor's office better, to make it a better service to the community" (S2).

### Spending and financial oversight
- Says she would look for "more cost-effective and sustainable ways" to operate the office (S3).
- Pledges to "maintain strong financial oversight, safeguard public records" (S1).
- Says a new elections office will probably be necessary at some point (S2).
- In 2022 she criticized the county's response to an SAO finding on duplicate COVID relief claims, saying the corrective spreadsheet could "fail massively," and proposed more secure IT solutions (S4).

### Elections and transparency
- "Trust in our elections is essential. I support secure systems, consistent procedures, and transparent communication" (S1).
- Wants more visibility for the office, more community involvement in the election cycle, and staff available for feedback (S3).
- On the 2024 mask mandate in the counting room: "I don't disagree with doing a mask mandate." She notes Tim Hazelo was given multiple chances to mask or leave (S2).
