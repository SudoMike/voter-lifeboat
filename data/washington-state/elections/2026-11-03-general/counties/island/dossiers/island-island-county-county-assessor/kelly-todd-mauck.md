## Background

Kelly Todd Mauck has been Island County Assessor since 2023 (S1). He won the 2022 election as a Republican from Camano Island by 12 votes after a hand recount: 19,993 to T.J. Kubisiak's 19,981 (S9). This year he is running with no party preference (S1). The News-Times described him as "formerly a Republican candidate" (S10).

He is a U.S. Navy veteran. He moved to Oak Harbor in 1993 and was a reserve deputy with the Island County Sheriff's Office and then a Skagit County deputy. He returned to Island County as a patrol deputy in 1996 (S3). He became undersheriff at 35 and says he was the longest-serving undersheriff in county history, overseeing personnel, budgets, labor relations and policy (S3). His statement lists 22 years in law enforcement (S1). He is an Accredited Washington State Real Property Appraiser and a licensed real estate broker. He says he has also been a licensed home inspector, a state-certified building inspector and a general contractor (S1, S3). He serves on the Washington State Association of County Assessors (WSACA) education and appraisal committees (S1). His primary-pamphlet statement matched the general guide statement (S15).

In the August 4 primary he received 13,470 votes (47.1%) to Stults's 15,078 (52.7%) (S12).

## Positions

### Taxes and the state school levy correction
- Says Island County taxpayers were overcharged on the state school levy and that he "corrected it, and ... made our taxpayers whole" by reducing the 2025 levy by a combined $5.6 million (S7).
- The overcollections came from a 2022 error under the previous assessor (about $1.2 million) and a 2024 error in which the prior year's rate was carried forward in the assessor's software (about $4.4 million). He says he added safeguards and contacted the software vendor after finding the 2024 error (S7).
- The Department of Revenue's audit treats the first 5% of an overlevy as an allowable surplus owed to the state. Under that reading, all of the 2022 excess and about $1.8 million of the 2024 excess were not refundable. Mauck disputes this, citing DOR's 2013 handling of a Douglas County overlevy (S7).
- Says he would refuse a DOR requirement to raise a future levy to undo the correction, which he estimates would cost the average homeowner $50 to $75: "I'll go to court if I have to." Prosecutor Greg Banks says his office will defend the assessor (S7).
- His campaign describes this as having "successfully challenged and corrected significant tax issues that returned millions of dollars to Island County taxpayers" (S4).

### Exemptions and taxpayer relief
- Supported the 2026 legislation raising income thresholds for the senior and disabled-veteran exemption, a WSACA legislative priority. He said 2,092 people were enrolled and that enrollment could double (S8).
- His office brought online applications, outreach through senior organizations and in-person exemption help on Camano Island (S1, S6).

### Valuation and office management
- Says the office's Price Related Differential, a measure of uniformity across value ranges, improved from about 1.40 to about 1.20 during his term (S4).
- Says the office met 4 of 8 Department of Revenue standards when he took office in 2023 and now meets all of them (S6).
- Lists modernized public property tools, improved land valuation, stronger appraiser training, expanded services on Camano Island, "significantly reduced appeals," and operating under budget (S1).
- Says the office has "consistently operated under budget, reduced unnecessary staffing costs, and created efficiencies" (S4).
- "Since my time in Office I've focused on improving the Office, not my personal credentials" (S4).

### Agriculture, forestry and land
- Set up Island County's first Farm & Agriculture Advisory Committee (S1, S6).
- Says he administers the Designated Forest Land program to protect working forests and helps landowners who no longer qualify move into other programs such as PBRS (S6).
- Says conservation "can go hand-in-hand with private property rights." He mentions commuting in a personally owned electric vehicle (S6).

## Scoring notes

- Taxes: as assessor, made a $5.6 million levy reduction to return school-levy overcollections and has said he will refuse to raise a later levy if the state demands it. One of the two overcollections happened during his term.
- Experience: a first-term incumbent with long county law-enforcement management experience and DOR appraiser accreditation, backed by the county commissioners and most of the state's elected assessors.
- Reform: cites changes already made (all DOR standards met, PRD improvement, fewer appeals, agriculture advisory committee) rather than new proposals.
- Spending: says the office runs under budget with reduced staffing costs.
