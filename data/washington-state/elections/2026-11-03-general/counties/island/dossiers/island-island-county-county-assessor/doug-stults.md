## Background

Doug Stults has worked for more than 4.5 years as a residential appraiser in the Island County Assessor's Office and has 6+ years of appraisal experience in total. He says he has appraised more than 6,500 residences (S1). He holds the Assessment Administration Specialist (AAS) and Residential Evaluation Specialist (RES) designations from the International Association of Assessing Officers (IAAO) and is accredited by the Washington Department of Revenue (S1). He is serving his second term on the IAAO Ethics Committee, sits on the task force updating the IAAO Ethics Manual, and was a FEMA disaster housing inspector after Hurricanes Gustav and Ike (S4, S6). He lists no elected experience (S1). He lives in Oak Harbor, works in Mauck's office, and filed as a Democrat during filing week in May 2026 (S8). He has lived on Whidbey Island for nearly 15 years, where he and his wife keep a small farm (S3). His primary-pamphlet statement matched the general guide statement (S15).

In the August 4 primary he received 15,078 votes (52.7%) to Mauck's 13,470 (47.1%) (S12). The News-Times noted that Mauck was the only local incumbent trailing in the primary (S10).

On his campaign site he says he chose to preserve funds during an extended family emergency, which meant he owed penalties and interest on his federal taxes. He says these are now paid in full (S3).

## Positions

### Taxes and the levy error
- Says "Island County property owners were overcharged millions. It should never have happened" (S2).
- His Taxpayer Protection Plan (Part 1, Levies) proposes two tests on every levy before he signs the roll: whether any rate is identical to last year's, and whether the tax billed matches the certified amount. He says the first test would have caught the state school levy error (S5).
- Says "every dollar ought to go back to the people who paid it — including refund checks to those who have since sold their homes, and not credits on someone else's tax bill," citing RCW 84.69.090 (S5).
- Proposes a standing policy that no correction above a set threshold is made without a written DOR determination first. He calls this "the direct lesson of 2026: a unilateral multimillion-dollar correction without guidance exposes taxpayers to reversal" (S5). The correction he refers to is Mauck's $5.6 million 2025 levy reduction, which DOR disputes (S11).
- Other plan items: a named levy chain of custody, removing software auto-population of prior-year rates, confirmation letters to taxing districts, a three-year levy look-back, inviting a DOR levy audit, software regression testing, and an annual public levy report to the commissioners (S5).

### Exemptions and taxpayer relief
- Promises expanded outreach to help seniors, veterans, people with disabilities, farmers and forestland owners claim exemptions and current-use programs (S1).
- On SB 6162 (2026), which raised income thresholds for the senior, disabled and veteran exemption from January 1, 2027, he proposes plain-language mailers, enrollment clinics at senior centers and libraries across Whidbey and Camano, partnerships with VFW and American Legion posts, and asking about eligibility during field visits (S7).

### Valuation, fairness and office management
- "The Assessor's role isn't political. It's technical, ethical, and deeply consequential" (S1).
- Commitments with deadlines: mentoring so land values are set only by accredited appraisers with at least a year's experience; pursuing the IAAO Certificate of Excellence in Assessment Administration; cross-training staff; posting staff accomplishments and his own work hours; a public conflict-of-interest log; expanded neighborhood stratification before April 2027; and a written uniformity-review process (S4).
- Says he will "invest in retaining and developing our appraisal team" and "lead from office and field" (S1).
- Says waterfront valuations need repeatable statistical methods and that commercial valuation methods "could benefit significantly from a thorough evaluation" (S3).
- Says current-use classification should be applied consistently "and not leaning toward what a future developer may see in it" (S3).
- Argues that selling real estate, fee appraisal and mass appraisal are different professions and that a real estate license does not qualify someone for mass appraisal (S16). Mauck lists a real estate broker's license among his qualifications in the same voters' guide (S1).

### Ethics
- Says he has recused himself from properties where he had potential conflicts, including one involving a former supervisor of an immediate family member, by reassigning them to colleagues (S6).

## Scoring notes

- Taxes: has a detailed, dated plan for levy verification and supports refunds by check to those who paid the state school levy overcollection. He criticizes the incumbent's way of correcting it, not the goal of returning the money.
- Experience: a staff appraiser with national IAAO designations and no elected or management record, challenging his own office's elected head.
- Reform: proposes many transparency mechanisms with deadlines (conflict log, posted hours, annual levy report, IAAO departmental certification).
- Campaign almost entirely self-funded; won the primary with 52.7%.
