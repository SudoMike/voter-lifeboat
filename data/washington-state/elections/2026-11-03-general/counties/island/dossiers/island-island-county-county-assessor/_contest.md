## What the office does
The assessor values every parcel in the county for property-tax purposes and administers exemption and current-use programs. Mauck's statement lists running valuation systems, keeping the tax rolls and making assessments fair and uniform (S1). The assessor also computes levy rates from the amounts taxing districts and the state certify, and extends the taxes onto the tax roll (S2). Anyone who values real property for taxation must be accredited by the Department of Revenue (S3). The assessor does not set how much revenue a district collects. Stults calls the job "technical, ethical, and deeply consequential" (S1).

## Race dynamics
Incumbent Kelly Todd Mauck was first elected in 2022 as a Republican. A hand recount confirmed his 12-vote win over T.J. Kubisiak (19,993 to 19,981), and the outgoing assessor, Bernie Upchurch, had not sought reelection (S8). Mauck filed for reelection with no party preference (S1, S6). Doug Stults is a residential appraiser on Mauck's own staff. He filed as a Democrat during filing week (S6). Upchurch, who recruited him to the office, endorses him (S1).

In the August 4 primary Stults received 15,078 votes (52.7%) and Mauck 13,470 (47.1%) (S4). The Island County Democrats endorsed Stults. The Island County Republican Party endorsed Mauck (S7). Stults reports $43,250 raised, of which $43,009.55 is his own money (cash and in-kind). Mauck reports $6,291 raised (S11, S12, S13).

The race centers on a state school levy error. In 2022, under the previous assessor, the county collected about $1.2 million more than the state certified. In 2024 the prior year's rate was carried forward in the software, which Mauck says caused a further overcollection. Mauck reduced the 2025 levy by a combined $5.6 million to return both amounts. The Department of Revenue says much of that correction was not authorized, and Mauck says he will go to court rather than raise a later levy (S5). Mauck presents the correction as returning "millions of dollars" to taxpayers (S10). Stults's Taxpayer Protection Plan says the error was preventable. It proposes levy checks before he signs the roll and refund checks to the people who actually paid, and it criticizes a "unilateral multimillion-dollar correction without guidance" (S9).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** both promise to return the levy overcollection to taxpayers. Mauck made the correction through a lower 2025 levy and says he will refuse to raise a later levy if DOR requires it (S5). Stults wants refund checks rather than credits, a written DOR determination before any large correction, and published annual levy reconciliation (S9). Both statements cite outreach on the senior, disabled and veteran exemption program (S1).
2. **Experience:** a first-term incumbent and former undersheriff who holds DOR appraiser accreditation and a real-estate license (S1), against a staff appraiser with about 4.5 years in the office and two IAAO professional designations, who has not held elected office (S1).
3. **Reform:** Stults proposes dated commitments: a public conflict-of-interest log, posted work hours, a levy exception report, IAAO departmental certification and expanded neighborhood stratification (S9). Mauck cites changes he has already made: modernized tools, the office meeting all eight DOR standards, fewer appeals and a Farm & Agriculture Advisory Committee (S1, S10).
4. **Spending:** Mauck says the office has run under budget and cut unnecessary staffing costs (S10). Stults's plan stresses staff retention, cross-training and mentoring (S1).
5. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives (I-645, I-1, I-638): none found for either candidate.
