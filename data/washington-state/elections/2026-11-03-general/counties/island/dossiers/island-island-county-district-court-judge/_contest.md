## What the office does

District court is a court of limited jurisdiction. It hears misdemeanor and gross-misdemeanor cases (sentences up to one year in jail and a $5,000 fine), traffic infractions and preliminary hearings [S2], and civil claims of up to $100,000 [S3]. Judge Costeck's campaign says more than half of the court's cases are civil and that the court also serves as the municipal court for Coupeville, Oak Harbor and Langley [S7]. Island County has one elected district court judge, elected countywide to a four-year term [S1][S5].

## Race dynamics

- **Uncontested.** Appointed incumbent Ronald A. Costeck is the only candidate. The office is nonpartisan [S1].
- **Why there is a short term.** Judge Bill Hawkins was re-elected without opposition in November 2022 [S6] and retired at the end of May 2024 [S5]. State law has the county commissioners fill a district court vacancy by appointment, and the appointee serves until the next general election [S4]. The commissioners unanimously appointed Costeck, then the district court commissioner, effective June 1, 2024; the appointment runs until the 2026 general election is certified [S5]. The ballot therefore carries a "Short and Full Term": the short term covers the rest of Hawkins's unexpired term and the full term is the next four years [S1].
- **Appointment process.** Six lawyers applied and four were interviewed, including two from the prosecutor's office and two Whidbey defense attorneys [S5].
- **No primary.** He was the only filer, so the race did not appear on the August primary ballot [S8].
- **Bar ratings.** No bar association rating or poll for this race was found in the sources reviewed.
