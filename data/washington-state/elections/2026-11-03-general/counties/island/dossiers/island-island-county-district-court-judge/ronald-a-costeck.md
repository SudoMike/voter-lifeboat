## Background

- **Current role.** Presiding Judge of Island County District Court since June 2024. Island County commissioners appointed him unanimously after Judge Bill Hawkins retired [S1][S2][S4]. Under state law the appointment lasts until the next general election [S6]. He filed in May 2026 to keep the seat [S3].
- **Court commissioner, 2018-2024.** Before the appointment he served about six years as Island County District Court and Oak Harbor Municipal Court commissioner [S2]. He describes this as full-time judicial service since 2018 [S1].
- **Before the bench.** Admitted to the Washington bar in 1998 and began as a deputy prosecutor; the 2024 report places that work in Skagit County [S1][S2]. In 2005 he founded a private practice handling criminal defense, family law and complex civil cases in Island, San Juan, Skagit, Snohomish and Whatcom counties [S1][S4]. He also served as a judge pro tem [S2]. His campaign says he has handled more than one hundred trials [S4].
- **Teaching.** Adjunct professor at Temple University and later Lummi Tribal College [S2]. He has taught at Skagit Valley College and Whatcom Community College and served on the education committee of the District and Municipal Court Judges' Association [S4]. Washington State Judicial College and eight years of continuing judicial education [S1].
- **Education.** J.D., 1995 (listed as "UofM"; the guide does not name the school further); honors B.Comm., McMaster University, 1991 [S1].
- **Community.** Rotarian; Oak Harbor High School mock trial coach; vestry member at St. Augustine's [S1].

## Positions

Judicial candidates are recorded only on experience, judicial approach and court administration.

- **Judicial approach.** He describes a philosophy "rooted in fairness, impartiality, and respect for every person who enters the courtroom." He says justice "requires both accountability and understanding" and commits to making sure "all parties are heard and that decisions are clearly explained" [S1].
- **Court administration.** As presiding judge he says he has improved online resources, modernized court operations and increased access to court services [S1].
- **Rehabilitative processes.** He says he has "designed rehabilitative processes that reduce recidivism while maintaining accountability and protecting public safety" [S1]. The sources reviewed give no detail or data on these programs.
- **Nonpartisanship.** His campaign site says the office "must remain free from political affiliation and bias" [S4].
