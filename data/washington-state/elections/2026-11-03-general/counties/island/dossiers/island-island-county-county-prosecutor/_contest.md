## What the office does
The prosecuting attorney is legal adviser to the county commissioners and to all county and precinct officers and school directors. The office represents the state and county in all criminal and civil proceedings, prosecutes criminal and civil actions, and defends suits brought against the county (S3). In Island County the office does both jobs: felony and misdemeanor prosecution, and civil counsel to the county's elected officials (S1, S7).

## How Andrew Scott got on the general ballot
Greg Banks was the only candidate to file during the May 4-8 filing week, and the primary pamphlet listed him as "Unopposed" (S2, S12). Andrew Scott, a Freeland attorney, announced a Democratic write-in campaign in early July 2026 because filing had closed. He said he had put off filing earlier in the year because of medical issues (S7). Scott registered with the PDC on July 28, with a declared date of July 13 (andrew-f-scott.md S9).

He qualified through the primary write-in route. Under state law, a write-in candidate must file a declaration of candidacy by 8 p.m. on primary day, and votes count only for declared write-ins (S5). For any office with a primary, no name may be printed on the general ballot unless that candidate received at least 1% of the total primary votes cast for the office (S4). In the certified August 4 primary, Banks received 19,627 votes and write-ins 1,875, out of 21,502 cast (S6). On election night, 1,014 unresolved write-ins had already been counted, far above the roughly 117 needed at that point (S8). The county elections supervisor reported that 1,437 of the write-ins were for Scott, about 6.7% of the total and well over the 1% threshold. The other 438 were unassigned write-ins for undeclared names (S10). The canvassing board certified the primary on Tuesday, August 18 (S10). VoteWA's general-election candidate list shows Scott's filing date as 8/18/2026, the certification date (S11). No vacancy or special filing period (RCW 29A.24.171/181) was involved.

## Race dynamics
Banks has been prosecutor since 1999. He has won seven times and was unopposed in his last three elections. He first ran as a Democrat and now states no party preference (S1; greg-banks.md S7, S8). Scott was chief prosecuting attorney for the City of Missoula, Montana, from 2010 to 2016 and has practiced law on Whidbey since 2016. He prefers the Democratic Party (S1).

The race is closely tied to Banks's prosecution of Tim Hazelo, a former Island County Republican Party chair. Hazelo was convicted by a jury in July 2025 of felony unauthorized access to an election office and misdemeanor trespass after he refused to wear a mask in the ballot-counting room (S14). After the primary, Banks said Scott "seems to have benefited from the very vocal support" of Hazelo. Scott said Hazelo supports his candidacy but is not involved in his campaign (S9). In August 2026 a visiting judge disqualified Banks's office from a related public-records lawsuit and ordered a special prosecutor. Banks pointed to earlier rulings by two other judges that declined to remove him (S13). The 10th Legislative District Democrats endorsed Banks over Scott (greg-banks.md S5). The Clinton Community Council holds a prosecutor forum on October 19 (S15).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience:** Banks has 28 years as elected county prosecutor, a felony trial record and statewide and national prosecutor-association leadership. Scott spent about 15 years in a city prosecutor's office, mainly on misdemeanors, rising to chief prosecutor, plus private practice and service as a municipal judge pro tem (S1, S7). Banks's supporters say Scott has no felony prosecution experience. Scott's supporters dispute that this matters (andrew-f-scott.md S8; greg-banks.md S11).
2. **Safety:** Scott proposes a written "complete initial charging" policy against filing a misdemeanor and later refiling it as a felony when plea talks fail. He also wants faster charging decisions, more victim communication and coordination with the sheriff, and more specialty courts, such as mental-health courts alongside the existing drug court (andrew-f-scott.md S3, S5). Banks stresses prosecuting serious offenses, victims' rights and recent law-enforcement training. The Hazelo case followed the pattern Scott criticizes: it was refiled in superior court with a felony added after negotiations failed (S14; greg-banks.md S3).
3. **Reform:** Scott's charging policy and treatment-court expansion are his reform proposals. Banks presents independence and nonpartisanship ("without fear or favor") and the rule of law against politicized prosecution (S1; greg-banks.md S4).
4. **Spending:** no clear contrast in the sources. Banks cites cost-effective management and a paperless office; Scott cites "responsible use of public resources" (greg-banks.md S2; andrew-f-scott.md S3).
5. The 2026 statewide initiatives (I-645, I-1, I-638): no public position found for either candidate in the sources reviewed.
