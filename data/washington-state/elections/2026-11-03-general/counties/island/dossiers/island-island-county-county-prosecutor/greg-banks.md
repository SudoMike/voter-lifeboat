## Background
Greg Banks has been Island County Prosecuting Attorney since 1999 and states no party preference (S1). He holds a mechanical engineering degree from the University of Connecticut (1985) and a J.D. from the University of Washington (1993). He worked at Boeing before law school (S1, S4). He was an Island County deputy prosecutor from 1993 to 1995 and in private practice from 1995 to 1998, and was first elected in 1998 (S1, S8). He first ran as a Democrat with party financial support. He later "got disillusioned" and says partisanship has no place in the prosecutor's office (S7, S8). He has won seven times and was unopposed in his last three elections (S2, S8). He announced in February 2026 that he would seek an eighth term (S7). He was again the only candidate to file, and the primary pamphlet listed him as unopposed (S6). He received 19,627 of 21,502 primary votes; write-ins took the rest (S9).

He was president of the Washington Association of Prosecuting Attorneys (2008 per his statement, 2009 per his site), a National District Attorneys Association director from 2016 to 2024, and has been NDAA vice president since 2024 or 2025 (his statement and site differ on the dates) (S1, S3). Governor Ferguson appointed him to the Forensic Investigations Council in 2026. He has served on the Council on Public Defense and the Judicial Information Systems Committee, and chaired the Island County Law and Justice Council (S1, S3).

## Positions

### Prosecution, public safety and victims
- "A prosecutor must always seek justice and uphold the rule of law." He cites "successfully prosecuting serious offenses and standing up for crime victims" while upholding defendants' constitutional rights (S1).
- Describes his approach as "without fear or favor": decisions "based on the evidence and the law, even when the cases are difficult or unpopular," and equal treatment "regardless of race, gender, religion, or beliefs" (S4).
- Says the crime-victim coordinator unit is understaffed but does "an outstanding job." He says his office works with all law-enforcement agencies and recently finished several rounds of officer training (S10). His site says he and his deputies trained all Oak Harbor and Island County officers in 2026 on investigations and building cases for trial (S3).
- On partisanship and sentencing: "Democrats might be a little more likely to recommend alternative sentencing, but then again, Republicans use drug courts too" (S8).
- Explained that in a juvenile case later tied to a murder, the law required diversion for a misdemeanor and "juveniles get treated differently." He also described declining to prosecute when evidence would not support conviction (S8).

### Government legal adviser and democratic norms
- "I never flinch from giving public officials candid legal advice, even when it is not the answer they hoped for" (S1).
- His statement says "attorneys beholden to the President are abusing the immense power of their offices for private gain and vengeance against political opponents," and that he stands "against that kind of corruption in our small corner of the country" (S1).
- Calls local prosecutors "a bulwark against corruption and the erosion of democratic norms" (S7).

### Office administration
- Says he has run Island County's largest law office "cost effectively" and made it one of the first paperless prosecutor's offices in the state (S2).
- Drafted a first version of the county's artificial-intelligence policy (S4).

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No public position found in the sources reviewed. His statement of values lists treating everyone equally "regardless of race, gender, religion, or beliefs" (S4).

### Parents and schools
- No public position found in the sources reviewed. His site lists past school outreach: teaching legal writing at Coupeville High School and impaired-driving mock trials at South Whidbey High School (S3).

## Record
- **State ex rel. Banks v. Drummond:** challenged the county commissioners' 2015 hiring of outside lawyers and won a unanimous state Supreme Court decision (S3).
- **State v. Gamble:** argued consolidated murder cases after the Andress decision; his site says the result let many of those cases be retried (S3).
- **State v. Miley:** reopened a murder case his predecessor had declined and won a conviction (S3).
- **Hazelo prosecution:** personally prosecuted Tim Hazelo, former Island County Republican chair, for refusing to wear a mask in the ballot-counting room in 2024. The case was first filed in district court, then refiled in superior court with a felony added after negotiations failed. A jury convicted Hazelo in July 2025 of felony unauthorized access to an election office and first-degree criminal trespass; a disorderly-conduct charge was dismissed (S14). Banks said Hazelo "basically demanded to be charged with a crime" and "was given multiple off ramps." He denied that the prosecution was politically motivated (S14). He reports threats and an intense social-media campaign during the case (S3, S8).
- After the primary, Banks said Scott "seems to have benefited from the very vocal support of another candidate who is unhappy with his conviction by an Island County jury for violating election laws" (S12).
- **Disqualification ruling:** in August 2026, visiting Judge Kathryn Loring disqualified the prosecutor's office from defending the county in a public-records suit tied to the Hazelo and Abuhl cases and ordered a special prosecutor. Two other judges had earlier declined to disqualify him in the criminal cases, and Banks says those rulings were correct (S13).
- Co-chaired the WAPA appellate committee from 2019 or 2020 to 2026 and the criminal training committee from 2014 or 2015 to 2025 (his statement and site differ by a year). Helped develop a statewide model "Brady" disclosure policy for officer misconduct in 2022 (S1, S3).

## Scoring notes
- Experience: a 28-year incumbent with felony trial and appellate work, Supreme Court cases and statewide and national prosecutor-association leadership (S1, S3).
- Safety: emphasizes prosecuting serious crimes and victims' rights. He mentions drug courts and alternative sentencing only in general terms and has proposed no new programs (S1, S8).
- Reform: frames his candidacy around nonpartisan independence and resisting politicized prosecution, not policy changes (S1, S4).
- The Hazelo prosecution and the August 2026 disqualification ruling are the main points of contention in the race (S13, S14).
