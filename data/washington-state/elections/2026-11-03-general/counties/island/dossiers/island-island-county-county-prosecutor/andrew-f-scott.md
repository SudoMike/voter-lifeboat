## Background
Andrew F. Scott is a Freeland attorney who prefers the Democratic Party (S1, S5). He holds a B.A. in political science from Virginia Tech (1991), a J.D. from the University of Montana (1994) and an MPA from the University of Montana (2012) (S1, S4). He was in private practice in Missoula from 1994 to 1999 and served as Missoula Municipal Judge pro tem from 1995 to 1999. He was Missoula deputy city attorney from 1999 to 2010 and chief prosecutor from 2010 to 2016, supervising the criminal division (S1, S2). The News-Times describes his Missoula work as a high-volume misdemeanor caseload over 15 years, rising from deputy city prosecutor to chief (S5). He ran for the Montana State Senate in 2000 (S1). He moved to Whidbey in 2016 (his statement) or 2017 (per the News-Times) and runs a general-practice law office in Freeland handling criminal defense, family law and other matters (S1, S5).

**How he reached the ballot.** Filing week had closed with Banks unopposed. Scott announced a write-in candidacy in early July 2026 and said he had put off filing because of medical issues that are now resolved (S5). His PDC registration shows a declared date of July 13 and registration on July 28 (S9). In the August 4 primary, 1,437 voters wrote in his name, about 6.7% of the 21,502 votes for prosecutor and above the 1% needed to appear on the general ballot (S7, S10). VoteWA lists his general-election filing date as August 18, 2026, the day the primary was certified (S7, S11).

## Positions

### Charging policy
- Has published a "Complete Initial Charging" policy: "If the evidence supports a felony and the prosecutor believes the conduct warrants felony prosecution, charge the felony." He opposes filing a misdemeanor and later "recharg[ing] as a felony even though the facts supporting the felony were known" after plea negotiations fail: "A criminal charge is more than a negotiating position." He also commits to "Don't overcharge. Don't file counts simply because they are available. Don't use the threat of unnecessary charges to force a plea." He would allow new charges when new evidence appears (S3).
- "I support plea bargaining. It is an essential part of our criminal justice system" (S3).
- Supports "making charging decisions in a timely manner, filing charges fully based on the facts and the law" (S1). He says clients have told him charging decisions can take up to a year (S5).

### Public safety, victims and treatment courts
- Would "prioritize serious and repeat offenses while working closely with law enforcement, courts, and community partners" (S3).
- Would improve services to crime victims and coordination and training with the sheriff's office (S1). He says victims often are not kept informed about their cases and that deputies have raised concerns about legal training. Sheriff Rick Felici said he was unaware of those concerns (S5).
- Wants to expand specialty courts: keep the existing drug court and add mental-health and other treatment courts. He says prosecution now puts more emphasis on rehabilitation and partnerships (S5). In Missoula he was part of the mental-health, veterans and co-occurring courts working group (S1).
- A retired Missoula police sergeant says he worked with victims and advocates in domestic-violence cases and held offenders accountable "through a variety of appropriate court-ordered treatment programs and robust supervision" (S2).

### Office management and trust
- Would build "a professional office culture with clear expectations, effective training, and responsible use of public resources" (S3).
- Says there is a lack of trust in the prosecutor's office "in a lot of circles" (S5). He commits to "building trust with all members of the community, regardless of their individual politics" (S1).

## Record
- Chief prosecuting attorney, City of Missoula, 2010-2016. He supervised the criminal division and advised the police, fire, business licensing, parks and building departments (S1, S2).
- He has not held elected office (S1).
- Opponents' letters say he has no felony prosecution experience. A supporting letter from the retired Missoula police chief calls that a "false claim" and says the rules of evidence are the same at both levels (S8, S12).

## Endorsements
- His campaign site quotes Tiffany L. Harrison Cook, a former Missoula prosecutor colleague, and Bob Campbell, a retired Missoula police sergeant and current Missoula city councilor (S2).
- Mark G. Muir, retired Missoula police chief, wrote a supporting letter to the editor (S8).
- Scott said Tim Hazelo, a Republican legislative candidate whom Banks prosecuted, supports his candidacy but is not involved in the campaign (S6).
- No party or organizational endorsements were found. A pro-Banks letter says the 10th LD Democrats endorsed Banks (S12).

## Campaign finance
PDC summary as of the September 17, 2026 update: $1,281.67 in contributions, including $1,136.67 in loans, and $1,281.67 spent. He is his own treasurer (S9).

## Scoring notes
- Reform and safety: the only candidate with a written charging policy limiting later charge escalation, plus a plan to add mental-health and other treatment courts (S3, S5).
- Experience: about 16 years of prosecution, mostly a municipal misdemeanor caseload in Montana, plus private practice. He has not held elected office or prosecuted in Washington, as far as the sources show (S1, S3, S5).
- Safety: also pledges to prioritize serious and repeat offenses and to improve victim services and sheriff coordination (S1, S3).
- Reached the ballot as a declared primary write-in with 1,437 votes; his campaign is minimally funded (S7, S9).
