## Background

Rick Felici has been Island County sheriff since he was first elected in 2018 (S1). He has 38 years in law enforcement, including service with the Island County Sheriff's Office since 1994. He has worked as patrol deputy, detective, sergeant and chief criminal deputy. Before that he served in the U.S. Navy Security Police and as a reserve officer with the Oak Harbor Police Department (S1). His leadership training includes Northwest Command College and the FBI Law Enforcement Executive Development Association's Executive Leadership Institute (S1). His campaign address is in Coupeville, and his PDC name is Ricky J. Felici (S14).

In 2018 he beat deputy Lane Campbell by 13 percentage points. In 2022 both men ran as Republicans and Felici led Campbell 62.4% to 37.6% in the first count (S11, S12). In 2026 he is running as an independent. He said he is tired of partisan politics and believes the sheriff's position should be apolitical (S1, S2). At a July forum he also said trafficking victims had been "afraid to talk to me because of the letter I had by my name" (S3). In the August 4 primary he received 15,123 votes (51.7%) to Tavier Wasser's 14,065 (48.1%) (S13). Afterward he said, "It's halftime" (S17).

## Positions

### Public safety and policing
- Calls mental health and narcotics the county's biggest public safety challenges and says the response should combine "education, enforcement and accountability." He says people in the system "have to receive some level of treatment that they are willing to do" but "also need to be held accountable for their actions" (S3).
- Agrees deputy coverage of the county is insufficient. He blames the tight budget and a lack of staffing grants, saying the office has "been making it work for years" but that this is not sustainable as population and deputy costs grow (S3).
- In 2022 he said the office was funded for 43 deputies but had 36, and that his goal was 50 to 55 deputies so it could bring back a proactive team for problems such as burglaries and drug houses (S11).
- Describes the office as "absolutely in the service business" and lists service, professionalism and accountability as its values (S1, S11, S16).

### Federal immigration enforcement and sheriff's authority
- Says the Keep Washington Working Act bars local agencies from assisting ICE with detentions or raids and says he will follow it (S3, S6).
- At a January 2026 commissioners' session he advised residents to comply with ICE agents in encounters and pursue legal remedies later ("Survive the encounter"). He also said he had asked ICE's regional supervisor to notify him of operations in the county and received no commitment (S6).
- After a January 2025 ICE arrest in Langley he wrote that local law enforcement focuses on criminal violations, not immigration status, and that "the federal government has no direct authority over local law enforcement" (S7).
- Says he does not support the "constitutional sheriff" movement. He says "the people are the ultimate authority" and that a sheriff refusing to enforce laws is beyond the job's authority (S3). He took the same position in 2022 against Campbell (S11).

### Budget and spending
- Attributes staffing limits to the county budget (S3). When asked about criticism over the Sheriff's Office budgeting error, he declined to engage, saying, "I want to stay out of the mud" (S5).

### Parents and schools
- No public position found in the sources reviewed. He says education about the dangers of drugs should start "early on in their lives" (S3), which is a drug-prevention position, not a schools-governance or parental-rights one.

## Record

- Lists results as sheriff: body cameras for every deputy, expanded mental health and opioid outreach, advanced de-escalation and leadership training, and planning for a new jail and dispatch systems (S1). In 2022 the News-Times reported grant-funded programs in which opioid and mental health responders go out with deputies, and that he worked with state lawmakers to fund body-worn cameras (S11).
- Jail replacement: in a 2026 press release he called the county's public meetings on three possible sites for a new jail "about transparency and community engagement." The county and the Sheriff's Office began the process more than two years earlier (S8). In May 2026 commissioners paused a decision to rebuild on the current Coupeville site. A consultant cited a $100 million figure, and the current jail has 58 beds (S9).
- Budget error: the Sheriff's Office failed to account for at least $400,000 in overtime costs, an error that was later corrected. At an April 15 commissioners' meeting Felici said, "I'm not an accountant" and "it is what it is." Commissioner Jill Johnson said the omission did not create a deficit and that blaming the sheriff for county cuts is "purely political" (S5).
- Animal control: commissioners froze the second animal control officer position for budget reasons. Animal welfare groups have pressed Felici and the commissioners over it and in August suspended informal animal services to the county. Felici declined to comment on their letter (S5, S10). An Animal Advocates leader says Felici frequently calls animal calls "nuisance calls." That claim comes from a group that endorsed his opponent (S5).
- Also serves as county fire marshal and sets burn-ban levels (S16).

## Endorsements

Island County Republican Party, Island County Deputy Sheriffs Guild, Island County Corrections Association and Indivisible Whidbey (S4). He said it was the first time he could recall the deputies' and corrections unions endorsing a sheriff candidate (S5).

## Campaign finance

PDC summary as of the October 4, 2026 update: $25,275.61 raised, $11,343.70 spent, and a $1,500 loan, under full reporting (S14). Itemized contributors include the Washington Association of Realtors Political Affairs Council ($1,200 and $1,000) and the Washington Teamsters Legislative League ($500) (S15).

## Scoring notes

- Experience: incumbent with nearly eight years as sheriff and 32 years in the office, endorsed by both deputy unions (S1, S4, S5).
- Safety: an enforcement-and-accountability approach paired with existing co-responder mental health and opioid outreach. He says the office is understaffed (S1, S3, S11).
- Spending: says budget limits drive understaffing. The overtime budgeting error and animal control coverage are the main criticisms of his management (S3, S5).
- Reform: points to reforms already made (body cameras, de-escalation training) and jail and dispatch modernization planning rather than new proposals (S1, S11).
