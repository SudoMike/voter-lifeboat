## What the office does
The sheriff is the county's chief executive law-enforcement officer and conservator of the peace. The sheriff and deputies keep the peace, make arrests and serve court process (S2). The Island County Sheriff's Office runs a civil unit that serves protection orders, writs and other court papers (S3). It also runs the county jail through its Corrections Division. The jail holds up to 58 sentenced and pre-trial inmates, and its facility dates from 1972 (S4, S13). The sheriff also serves as county fire marshal and sets burn-ban levels (S3). This is a regular four-year term with no short term (S1, S17).

County budget context matters for this race. Commissioners closed a 2026 general-fund gap partly by adopting a 0.1% criminal justice sales tax, expected to raise nearly $2 million a year (S11). They froze most new hiring in 2026, including the Camano Island animal control officer position, and expect 2027 to be one of the toughest budget years since 2008 (S10). The county is planning a replacement jail, which it calls a "corrections rehabilitation center." A consultant has cited a $100 million figure, and commissioners have discussed asking voters for funding (S12, S13).

## Race dynamics
Sheriff Rick Felici, first elected in 2018 and reelected in 2022 as a Republican, is running for a third term without party affiliation ("Prefers Independent Party") (S1, S16). The challenger is Tavier Wasser, Langley police chief since 2022 and an Island County sheriff's deputy from 2015 to 2022, running as a Democrat (S1). In the certified August 4 primary Felici received 15,123 votes (51.7%) and Wasser 14,065 (48.1%) (S5).

The endorsements cross party lines. Felici has the Island County Republican Party, the Island County Deputy Sheriffs Guild, the Island County Corrections Association and the progressive group Indivisible Whidbey. Wasser has the Island County Democrats and the 10th Legislative District Democrats (S7). Wasser's Democratic credentials were challenged inside the party. Those challenges cited his description of himself as a "constitutional sheriff" at a Republican event, which he now calls a "rookie candidate mistake" and repudiates. They also cited his ballot in the 2024 Republican presidential primary (S9). Felici's critics, including Animal Advocates of Island County (which endorsed Wasser), focus on a Sheriff's Office budgeting error that left out at least $400,000 in overtime and on the county's animal control coverage. Commissioner Jill Johnson called blaming the sheriff for the county's budget cuts "purely political" (S8). Wasser reports $45,426 raised and $32,777 spent; Felici reports $25,276 raised and $11,344 spent (S14, S15).

At the July Clinton forum both candidates said deputy coverage is insufficient. Both said they will follow the Keep Washington Working Act's limits on cooperating with ICE, and both said they reject the constitutional sheriff movement (S6).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience:** a Sheriff's Office veteran since 1994 with nearly eight years as sheriff against a first-time candidate who heads a small city police department and spent seven years as a county deputy (S1).
2. **Safety:** Felici frames drugs and mental health as the biggest challenges and calls for "education, enforcement and accountability." Wasser emphasizes deputy availability, diverting some calls such as behavioral health crises to social workers, more SWAT and crisis training, and a new victim advocacy unit (S6; tavier-wasser.md S4).
3. **Spending:** Felici attributes understaffing to the county's tight budget and lack of staffing grants, and his office's overtime budgeting error is a campaign issue. Wasser cites a "proven record of responsible public budgeting" but gives no county budget figures (S1, S6, S8).
4. **Reform:** Wasser proposes a public information officer, officer wellness programs, more training and a victim advocacy unit. Felici points to body cameras, de-escalation training, co-responder mental health and opioid outreach, and jail and dispatch modernization already under way (S1; rick-felici.md S11).
5. Taxes, housing and climate: no differentiating positions found.
6. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives (I-645, I-1, I-638): none found for either candidate.
