## Background

Tavier Wasser has been Langley police chief since 2022. He was an Island County sheriff's deputy and acting sergeant from 2015 to 2022, and served in the U.S. Marine Corps from 2009 to 2015 as a military policeman and presidential security Marine (S1). His site says he was selected for Marine Helicopter Squadron One (HMX-1), held a Top Secret clearance, and led Marines as a non-commissioned officer in charge (S3). As a deputy he earned master-level certification in crisis and hostage negotiation, trained as a SWAT operator, and served as a field training officer for multiple agencies (S1, S3). He graduated from South Whidbey High School and attended Skagit Valley College and Southern New Hampshire University. He also holds executive leadership certificates from the Washington State Criminal Justice Training Commission and FBI-LEEDA (S1). He lists awards as 2024 Police Officer of the Year and Island County employee of the month in 2016. He also co-chairs the Washington Association of Sheriffs and Police Chiefs' Public Trust Committee (S1). His community service includes work as an ordained minister, the SWC.A.R.E.S opioid prevention group, school volunteering, the PATCH juvenile assistance program, the Recovery Navigation Program and National Drug Take Back (S1). When Langley hired him in 2022 he became the city's first Black police chief (S8).

This is his first campaign for office (S1). He announced in February 2026 and is running as a Democrat (S6, S11). He says he was an independent voter when he cast a ballot in the 2024 Republican presidential primary, and he denies voting for Donald Trump (S8, S10). In the August 4 primary he received 14,065 votes (48.1%) to Felici's 15,123 (51.7%) (S12). He called that "really positive" for a first-time candidate against an incumbent (S18).

## Positions

### Public safety and policing
- Runs on a four-point plan: "increase deputy training, have better communication with residents, improve our mental health support systems and increase our victim advocacy" (S6).
- Training: wants more deputies trained in SWAT tactics, hostage and crisis negotiation, and other advanced techniques (K9, drones). He cites the New Year's Day multi-county crime spree as an event "we could have been better prepared" for (S4).
- Victim advocacy: proposes a victim advocacy unit working with other agencies. Victim advocacy currently runs through the Prosecutor's Office and, he says, begins "much later" (S4).
- Mental health: says it is important "to find the difference between a crime and a call for help." He plans to expand the Sheriff's Office's mental health capacity (S4). At the July forum he suggested that some calls now handled by deputies could go to other professionals, such as a social worker responding to a behavioral health crisis (S7).
- Availability: wants deputies more available for individual needs, such as "checking on the homeless person at the bus stop," a domestic violence victim who has not been heard from, and children left unattended (S7). He agreed with Felici that current deputy coverage is insufficient (S7).
- Communications: will add a public information officer function and increase communication with Whidbey and Camano residents, especially during crises (S1, S4). His site says that when crises happen, "we need leadership that knows how to step up, engage, and keep residents informed" (S2).
- Animal control: according to Animal Advocates of Island County, which endorsed him, he has proposed ways to provide trained animal control coverage on both islands while the second officer position stays frozen (S10).

### Budget and spending
- Says he has "a proven record of responsible public budgeting" (S1). No county budget proposals or cost figures were found in the sources reviewed.

### Federal immigration enforcement and sheriff's authority
- Says he will follow the Keep Washington Working Act's limits on helping ICE. He says the Sheriff's Office should support and offer resources to community members affected by ICE raids (S7).
- News coverage reports that critics quote him telling a Republican audience that ICE agents were "doing their job, as far as I'm concerned," and later telling Democrats he did not support the administration's "hateful rhetoric." He calls the critics' material "disinformation" and "misleading statements" (S10).
- Called himself a "constitutional sheriff" before a Republican group in early 2026. He later called that a "rookie candidate mistake" and wrote that he does "not support, endorse or align myself with the extremist 'constitutional sheriff' movement" and rejects "any ideology claiming sheriffs can override state and federal law" (S8). At the July forum he said, "No one is above the law" (S7).

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No policy position found in the sources reviewed. The News-Times reports he has been "an energetic presence" at Langley's Pride parade, and he wrote that he will "stand as a voice for the voiceless and for underserved communities" (S8).

### Parents and schools
- No public position on parental notification, record access or curriculum opt-outs found in the sources reviewed. He lists school volunteering and the PATCH juvenile assistance program as community service (S1). He supported Gwendy Gabelein's South Whidbey School Board campaign, citing their work together on an opioid prevention committee. A Democratic critic described Gabelein as a "MAGA candidate" (S8).

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives

No public position found in the sources reviewed on I-645 (IP26-645), I-1 (IL26-001) or I-638 (IL26-638).

## Record

- As Langley police chief, his campaign says he created the department's first policy manual "to make them more accountable to the public," adopted VR training, and created access to mental health support (S3, S6). These are the campaign's own claims. No independent confirmation was found.
- Langley has left a fourth police officer position unfilled amid city budget constraints, according to the mayor (S17).
- He was among the local officials who said they learned of a January 2025 ICE arrest at a Langley laundromat only afterward (S16).

## Endorsements

Island County Democratic Party; 10th Legislative District Democrats; Animal Advocates of Island County; Alliance for Gun Responsibility; POWER Whidbey (S5, S9, S15). Langley council member and Democratic precinct committee officer Craig Cyr urged the party not to endorse him (S8, S9). Wasser says deputies who support him are afraid to say so publicly for fear of repercussions (S10).

## Campaign finance

PDC summary as of the October 5, 2026 update: $45,426.19 raised and $32,776.59 spent, under full reporting (S13). Itemized receipts include a $1,750 in-kind contribution from the Washington State Democratic Central Committee and several $1,000 to $1,200 contributions from individuals in Island County and elsewhere in Washington (S14).

## Scoring notes

- Experience: municipal police chief of a small department since 2022 and a former county deputy and acting sergeant. His listed experience does not include running a sheriff's office or a jail (S1).
- Safety: proposes more advanced tactical and crisis training, a victim advocacy unit, more mental-health capacity, and routing some behavioral-health calls to social workers (S4, S7).
- Reform: emphasizes public communications (a PIO), officer wellness and public trust. He co-chairs WASPC's Public Trust Committee (S1, S4).
- Spending: claims budgeting experience but has given no county budget detail. He has outraised the incumbent (S1, S13).
