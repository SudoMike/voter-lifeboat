## What the office does
The county clerk is the clerk of the superior court. By statute the clerk keeps the court's seal, records, files and minutes, files papers in court cases, attends court sessions, receives jury verdicts, enters the court's orders and judgments, and authenticates court records (S2). Island County describes the clerk as an independently elected official who acts as "the record keeping and financial officer of the Superior Court" (S3). Former Clerk Debra Van Pelt described the job the same way: a staff member sits in court with each judge, the office tracks trial exhibits, and members of the public come to the counter to file court documents. She said the office has moved to electronic records and e-filing (S6).

## Why the ballot says "short and full term"
Clerk Debra Van Pelt, a Democrat, retired from the elected office on May 1, 2026, before candidate filing week (S6). The News-Times reported she had spent 21 years in the office; her own letter says she served as clerk for 15 1/2 years (S6, S11). The commissioners first made Chief Deputy Clerk Michelle Schisler acting clerk and scheduled interviews for an interim clerk who would serve until the November general election is certified (S6). By July, Schisler had been appointed interim clerk (S7). She is not on the ballot (S1). State law has a county appointee serve "until the next general election, and until their successors are elected and qualified" (S4). When a vacancy occurs after the last election at which the office could have been voted on, the ballot carries a "short term" alongside the full term. The short term runs from certification of the general election until the next full term begins (S5). The November winner therefore takes office at certification, finishes Van Pelt's term, and then serves the four-year term (S1, S5). Both the primary pamphlet and the general guide list the race as a short and full term (S1, S14).

## Race dynamics
It is an open seat. Four candidates ran in the August 4 primary, and all four were county employees or court professionals (S7). Official results: Dierdre Butler (Democrat) 9,639 (33.6%), Maria Hazelo (Republican) 7,551 (26.3%), Amber Sewell (Democrat, Van Pelt's daughter) 7,366 (25.7%), and Toni Adams (Republican) 4,125 (14.4%) (S8). Hazelo's margin over Sewell for the second general-election spot was too large to trigger an automatic recount (S9).

Both finalists come from inside county government. Butler worked in the Clerk's Office from 2014 to 2020, rising from deputy clerk to chief deputy, and has worked in accounts payable in the Auditor's Office since 2020 (S1). Hazelo is the Juvenile Court Services office manager and has worked for the county for 20 years (S1, S7). The Island County Democrats endorsed Butler and the Island County Republican Party endorsed Hazelo (S10). In October, retired Clerk Van Pelt, whose daughter lost in the primary, endorsed Hazelo (S11). Both platforms center on modernizing records, cutting wait times and treating the public with respect. Butler adds budget management drawn from her Auditor's Office work. Hazelo adds staff training and retention through a "coaching-style" approach (S1, S7). Butler reports $4,654 raised and $4,196 spent. Hazelo files under PDC mini reporting, so no contribution totals are reported (S12, S13).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience:** Butler has six years inside the Clerk's Office, including as chief deputy, plus six years in county accounts payable. Hazelo has about two decades managing Juvenile Court Services operations, records, grants, payroll and purchasing, and serving as liaison to the state Administrative Office of the Courts (S1, S7).
2. **Reform/operations:** both promise modernization and shorter waits. Hazelo stresses staff training, de-escalation training and retention, while Butler stresses borrowing processes from other counties and public education about the office (S7).
3. **Spending:** Butler cites county financial experience and promises careful management of the clerk's budget. Hazelo cites her experience managing grants, payroll and purchasing. Neither gives specific budget proposals (S1, S7).
4. Taxes, housing, climate and safety: no differentiating positions found. The clerk's office does not set policy in these areas.
5. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives (I-645, I-1, I-638): none found for either candidate.
