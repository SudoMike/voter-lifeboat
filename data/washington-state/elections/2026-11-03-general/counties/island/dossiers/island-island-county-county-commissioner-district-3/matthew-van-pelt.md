## Background
Matthew Van Pelt prefers the Republican Party. He reports 23 years of service in the U.S. Navy and more than 20 years living in Island County. He owns three Island County small businesses, studied business and political science at Chapman College and completed Naval schools (S1, S5). The News-Times describes the businesses as a construction company, a cabinet company, and hot dog and grilled cheese carts he co-owns with his brother (S8). He has been a project captain for Hearts and Hammers and scoutmaster for Troop 4059, and he helps organize a yearly Thanksgiving meal program serving more than 1,400 free meals (S1, S5). He lives in Oak Harbor on North Whidbey (S2, S7). He has not held elected office ("No information submitted" under elected experience) (S1).

His wife, Debra Van Pelt, retired in May 2026 as Island County clerk after 21 years in that office. She is a Democrat and said she retired partly so she would not hold county office at the same time as her husband (S6). He was the first candidate to file for District 3 (S7). The Island County Republican Party endorsed him (S8). He won the District 3 primary with 4,469 votes (44.7%), the only Republican in a three-way race (S9, S10).

## Positions

### Budget, spending and taxes
- Says he will "bring practical construction and budget experience to the board" and that business growth and tourism help "reduce pressure for continued tax increases" (S1).
- Focuses on controlling county costs. Raised concerns about policies and programs that bring more people to Island County and strain county services and the budget. Named SPiN Cafe, which serves low-income people, and an Oak Harbor treatment center, both county-funded, as drawing people to the island "for free services." Quoted: "We need to take care of who's already here before we take care of new people" (S8).
- "Tax increases should be the last thing we look at" (S8).
- On the jail: would first look at remodeling the existing jail and expanding into the juvenile detention center next door, which he called underused, possibly contracting with another county for juvenile detention. Questioned whether in-jail treatment programs work for inmates not ready to change. If a new jail is needed, suggested volunteers review the plans instead of paid experts (S8).
- Calls Island County "running on slim financial margins" and suggests looking at grants to fund public safety (S4).

### Housing and growth
- Wants to "address rising housing costs and support sensible growth that protects our natural resources and quality of life" (S1).
- Says "non-essential building codes are driving up construction costs" and would "reassess and trim non-essential building codes," keep "the safety and environmental codes that matter," and "cut permitting delays" (S4).
- Disagrees with the state requirement that the county plan for future population growth in its comprehensive plan (S8).
- Calls the state's five-year low-income housing plan "commendable," but says it will add residents with limited means and raise demand on Island County Hospital. Wants county-state partnership on health-care funding linked to housing growth (S4).

### Public safety
- Says Island County has historically had the fewest sheriff's deputies per 1,000 residents in Washington. Would advocate funding to fill the deputy shortage, expand law-enforcement presence on Camano and Whidbey, and invest in investigative training (S3, S4).
- Says there is a "growing strain on local medical services, emergency response resources, and public safety" that needs "practical, compassionate solutions" (S1).

### County workforce, economy and tourism
- Would work to keep county employees through flexible work arrangements, professional development and open communication (S4).
- Supports local businesses, tourism and downtown revitalization, and calls tourism "the lifeblood of Island County" (S1, S4).

### Climate and environment
- His statement backs growth "that protects our natural resources" (S1). His site lists protecting "the natural beauty and local culture" as a tourism goal (S4). No climate-specific position found.

## Scoring notes
- Taxes and spending: the most cost-cutting candidate in the race. He puts tax increases last, questions county-funded social services that he says attract newcomers, and prefers remodeling the jail to replacing it (S8).
- Housing: proposes cutting "non-essential" building codes and permitting delays, a supply-side approach focused on lowering costs (S4).
- Growth and environment: opposes the state requirement to plan for population growth (S8), while his statement backs growth that protects natural resources (S1).
- Safety: calls for more sheriff's deputies, but is skeptical of in-jail treatment programs (S4, S8).
- Experience: Navy veteran and business owner with no elected-office record (S1).
