## Background
Grant Shaw prefers the Democratic Party. He has lived on Camano Island for 30 years and says this is his first run for public office (S1, S2). He reports a background in construction and architecture and a career managing multi-million-dollar retail budgets. He studied architecture, humanities and political science (S1, S5). He is vice president of the Freedom Park Association board, president of the Utsalady Elementary Booster Club and president of a well association. He received the Jeanie Ovenell Community Spirit Award, and is known on Camano for playing Santa Claus ("Granta Claus") during the holidays (S1, S7). He filed with the PDC in February 2026 after Commissioner Janet St. Clair announced she would not seek reelection (S7). He calls himself a moderate (S9). His father worked in the fishing industry. St. Clair's letter says that after his father was diagnosed with dementia, Shaw became a caregiver and later managed the family assets (S8, S9).

In the District 3 primary he finished second, 168 votes ahead of fellow Democrat Satin Arnett: 2,845 votes (28.5%) to her 2,677 (26.8%). Matthew Van Pelt received 4,469 (44.7%) (S10, S11).

## Positions

### Budget, spending and taxes
- Pledges "setting priorities, watching taxpayer dollars carefully, improving how services are delivered" (S4).
- Wants a deeper review of county operations for efficiency while keeping the current level of service and planning for infrastructure. He would modernize processes and adopt technology even where that takes up-front investment: "Why have we always done it this way and is there now a better way?" (S9).
- Supports "maintaining what taxpayers have already invested in, planning ahead for future needs, pursuing outside funding" (S4).
- Supports building an improved jail with space for treatment, behavioral-health and rehabilitation programs, and says the county should pursue grants and other funding options (S9). His site calls the current jail aging and past its expected lifetime (S5).

### Housing and growth
- Wants "more housing choices without sacrificing the rural character of our islands," with "ADUs, smaller homes, and workforce housing, particularly where infrastructure can support them," and "clear and predictable" permitting (S4).
- Says the Growth Management Plan is "so important right now as our population grows." He would put growth "where it makes sense, respecting property rights," and protect farmland, forests and open space: "Our islands shouldn't become just another suburban corridor" (S4, S5).

### Water and environment
- Treats water protection as a priority. He cites growth in septic systems, saltwater intrusion and nearly 10,000 drinking-water wells in Island County, and says new development "needs to account for available water and septic capacity" (S4, S5).
- No climate-specific position found.

### Public safety and infrastructure
- Says the ICOM 911 system "is aged and needs an overhaul" and that "for our communities to be safe we must support our first responders" (S5).
- "If we're serious about improving public safety and reducing recidivism, we need a facility that provides adequate space for treatment, behavioral health services and rehabilitation programs" (S9).
- Supports veterans and military families, including health and mental-health care (S5).

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No public position found in the sources reviewed.

### Parents and schools
- No public position found in the sources reviewed. He has been a school and booster-club volunteer and says he considered running for school board (S1, S5).

## Scoring notes
- Spending: supports a new jail with treatment and program space, plus efficiency reviews that keep service levels. He has not proposed cuts (S5, S9).
- Housing: names ADUs, smaller homes and workforce housing where infrastructure allows, with predictable permitting (S4).
- Growth and environment: backs Growth Management Act planning and puts water, septic capacity and rural-land protection first (S4, S5).
- Taxes: no explicit position on new taxes or levies. He pledges careful spending and outside funding (S4, S9).
- Experience: a first-time candidate whose experience is in retail budgets and nonprofit and water-association leadership. He has the endorsement of the outgoing District 3 commissioner (S1, S8).
