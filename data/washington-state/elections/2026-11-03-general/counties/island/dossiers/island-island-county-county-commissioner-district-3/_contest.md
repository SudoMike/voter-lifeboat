## What the office does
The three-member Board of Island County Commissioners is the county's legislative authority. It builds and repairs courthouses, jails and other public buildings, manages county roads, fixes county taxes, audits county accounts and manages county property and funds (S5). Recent board decisions show what the job covers in practice. In December 2025 the board adopted a one-tenth of 1% criminal-justice sales tax and the 2026 budget, both on 2-1 votes; the general fund was $37.6 million and the county faced rising insurance and public-defense costs (S11). In May 2026 it paused the choice of a site for a new jail, which a consultant put at around $100 million, and discussed asking voters for funding (S12). On June 23, 2026 it adopted the 2025 Comprehensive Plan update under the Growth Management Act; two groups have since appealed the plan to the Growth Management Hearings Board (S13).

District 3 covers much of North Whidbey and all of Camano Island (S7, S9). Commissioners are nominated in the primary only by voters in their home district, and the general election is county-wide (S2, S3, S4).

## Race dynamics
Incumbent Janet St. Clair, a Democrat from Camano Island first elected in 2018, did not seek reelection (S7, S10). Before the election the board had two Democrats and one Republican, so this seat decides which party holds the majority (S8). St. Clair has endorsed Shaw (grant-shaw.md S6, S8).

In the certified District 3 primary, Republican Matthew Van Pelt of North Whidbey (Oak Harbor) received 4,469 votes (44.7%), Democrat Grant Shaw of Camano Island 2,845 (28.5%) and Democrat Satin Arnett of Camano Island 2,677 (26.8%), out of 9,997 votes (S6, S10, S15). Shaw took second place over Arnett by 168 votes (S10). The two Democrats together outpolled Van Pelt in the district primary (S6). The Island County Republican Party endorsed Van Pelt. Before the primary the Island County Democratic Party endorsed Arnett; Shaw now lists the Island County Democrats and the 10th LD Democrats among his endorsers (S9; grant-shaw.md S6). Shaw calls himself a moderate (S9). The Clinton Community Council holds a forum for this race on October 13 (S14).

Both statements stress cooperation and practical leadership (S1). Their differences show up in coverage and on their websites. Van Pelt opposes the state requirement to plan for population growth, questions county-funded services that he says draw people to the island, and is skeptical of building a new jail. Shaw backs growth management with water and septic safeguards, more housing types, and a new jail with treatment space (S9; matthew-van-pelt.md S4; grant-shaw.md S4, S5).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Van Pelt says "tax increases should be the last thing we look at" and presents business growth and tourism as a way to "reduce pressure for continued tax increases" (S1, S9). Shaw says he will watch taxpayer dollars carefully and seek outside funding (grant-shaw.md S4). Neither candidate has stated a position on the 2025 criminal-justice sales tax or on a jail funding measure (S9, S11).
2. **Spending:** Van Pelt would cut costs, including county-funded programs (SPiN Cafe, an Oak Harbor treatment center) that he says attract newcomers. He would also consider remodeling the jail instead of replacing it. Shaw supports a new jail with program space and a review of county operations for efficiency, without cutting service levels (S9).
3. **Housing:** Van Pelt would cut "non-essential" building codes to lower construction costs. Shaw names ADUs, smaller homes and workforce housing where infrastructure allows, along with clear, predictable permitting (matthew-van-pelt.md S4; grant-shaw.md S4).
4. **Climate / environment and growth:** Shaw stresses protecting aquifers, septic capacity, rural character and farmland under the Growth Management Plan (grant-shaw.md S4, S5). Van Pelt disagrees with the state requirement to plan for population growth, though his statement backs "sensible growth that protects our natural resources" (S1, S9). Neither candidate addresses climate policy directly.
5. **Safety:** Van Pelt would push to fund more sheriff's deputies. Shaw would back first responders, a new jail with behavioral-health space, and an overhaul of the ICOM 911 system (matthew-van-pelt.md S4; grant-shaw.md S5, S9).
6. **Experience:** neither has held elected office. Van Pelt brings 23 years in the Navy and owns three businesses, including a construction company. Shaw brings a retail-management budget background plus nonprofit and water-association leadership (S1).
7. **Reform:** no differentiating evidence on government reform beyond general accessibility and transparency pledges from both (S1).
8. The 2026 statewide initiatives (I-645, I-1, I-638): no public position found for either candidate in the sources reviewed.
