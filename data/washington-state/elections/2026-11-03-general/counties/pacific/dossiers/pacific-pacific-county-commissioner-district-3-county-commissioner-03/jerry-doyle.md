## Background

Jerry Doyle, of Raymond, is the incumbent District 3 commissioner and prefers the Independent Party [S1][S3]. He was first elected in November 2022 after a write-in primary campaign, beating former commissioner Michael "Hawk" Runyon 60.39% to 39.1% in the county-wide general [S5][S4]. He has chaired the Board of County Commissioners since 2024 and represents the county on the Economic Development Council, the Pacific Mountain Workforce Board, the Legislative Steering Committee and the Pacific Transit board; he also sits on the Raymond Planning Commission [S1][S3]. He has been a general contractor in Pacific County for about 30 years (Ocosta High School 1986; associate degree in carpentry technology, 1988) [S1][S3].

He finished second in the District 3 primary with 600 of 2,680 votes, behind Pattie Gray's 750 [S9].

## Positions

### Budget and spending
- Says every budget cycle he has been part of "has been balanced and core services have been preserved" [S2].
- On declining timber revenue: departments such as Community Development and Health and Human Services have used grants and fees so they need no general-fund contributions, and the county's finance team has earned interest income in the Local Government Investment Pool [S2].

### Taxes
- Says "tax reform" will take a large effort "to get the state level government to control spending and not rely on us, the citizens, to bridge the gap" [S2].
- In a March 2026 report on timberland owners' tax breaks and county road costs, he "said it's time for the county to revisit the rates" [S10]. The article does not say which rate or in which direction he would change it.

### Land use and local control
- Names land-use management as District 3's critical issue: the state has "increased regulations on landowners' use and rights" under the Growth Management Act, and he has "been a voice against more state regulations" [S2].
- On the comprehensive plan: "we don't let the state dictate what is in the best interest for our community based on their 'best available science' methodology" [S2].
- With commissioners Lisa Olsen and David Tobin he signed a 2025 letter opposing the Forest Practices Board's riparian buffer rule change [S6].

### Housing
- Says as "a builder by trade" that housing costs "have always been driven by supply and demand," and lists permitting reform through the comprehensive plan and affordable housing among the county's work [S2].

## Record

- Chair of the Board of County Commissioners since 2024 [S1].
