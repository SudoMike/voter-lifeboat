## What the office does
The three-member Pacific County Commission is "the county's top legislative board" [S2]. Its members work on the county budget, land use and the comprehensive plan [S1], and the board fills vacancies in county offices: in October 2025 it appointed the current auditor 3-0 [S7]. Commissioner District 3 is the north-county seat (Raymond, Tokeland, Menlo, Frances, Lebam and nearby communities). Only District 3 voters vote in the primary; all Pacific County voters elect the commissioner in November [S2][S3]. The 2022 District 3 primary was held in District 3's precincts only [S5], and the 2022 general for the same seat drew 10,443 votes county-wide [S4].

## Race dynamics
Incumbent Jerry Doyle (Prefers Independent Party), first elected in 2022 after a write-in primary campaign and now chair of the commission, faces Pattie Gray (Prefers Republican Party), chair of the Pacific County Republican Party since 2025 [S2][S3][S1]. In the five-way August primary, held in District 3 only, Gray led with 750 votes to Doyle's 600 of 2,680 (28.0% and 22.4%); Ron Mullins (528), Michael "Hawk" Runyon (476) and Ron Black (322) were eliminated [S6][S3]. Doyle beat Runyon 60.39% to 39.1% in 2022 [S4].

Gray runs on limited government, transparency, lower taxes and fewer regulations, term limits, a salary commission and cutting commissioner pay [S1]. Doyle runs on his record: balanced budgets, opposing state land-use and forestry rules, and county finance work [S1].

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience:** a four-year incumbent and board chair against a first-time candidate whose elected experience is in party office.
2. **Spending:** both stress fiscal responsibility. Gray calls for line-by-line department reviews, audits and cuts to "wasteful spending"; Doyle points to balanced budgets with core services kept and new grant and fee revenue.
3. **Taxes:** Gray explicitly calls for less taxation. Doyle talks about state tax reform and state spending control but has no local tax pledge.
4. **Local control:** both oppose state mandates (Doyle on Growth Management Act regulation and the state's "best available science"; Gray on "state and federal interference"). Little difference.
5. **Housing:** both favor supply-side answers (Gray: cut fees, permitting delays and red tape; Doyle: costs driven by supply and demand, permitting reform in the comprehensive plan).
