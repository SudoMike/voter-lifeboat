## Background

Pattie Gray, of Raymond, prefers the Republican Party [S1][S5]. She has chaired the Pacific County Republican Party since June 2025, after serving as a 2024 convention delegate, precinct committee officer and state committeewoman [S1][S5]. She has worked in the legal field for about 16 years as a paralegal (B.S. in paralegal studies, 2008) and is a notary [S1]. She has also worked as a human resources manager and administrative assistant for Pacific Transit System and as an assistant marketing manager for the Shoalwater Bay Indian Tribe, and served on the Willapa Harbor Chamber of Commerce board [S5][S6]. She says she is bilingual in Spanish [S1]. She has not held county office.

She led the District 3 primary with 750 of 2,680 votes [S8]. Her campaign site says she was fired from two jobs after filing to run, which she calls illegal retaliation for protected political activity [S2].

## Positions

### Taxes and spending
- Supports "less taxation" and cutting "excessive regulations and red tape" [S1]; her platform lists "Lower Taxes" and lower tax burdens on local entrepreneurs [S2][S3].
- Wants to "stop wasteful spending," audit departments regularly and account for "every penny" [S1][S3].
- On declining timber revenue: the county must "live within its means"; she would review each department's budget "line by line" and decide which positions and programs to restructure, "not to make cuts just for the sake of cutting" [S4].

### Government accountability
- Calls for term limits for county officials, a ban on lobbying by former county officials, limits on executive sessions and full financial transparency [S1][S3].
- Wants a salary commission because "Commissioners should not set their own salaries," and would cut commissioner salaries to the county's median income [S1][S3].

### Housing, growth and land use
- Says the county can make housing more affordable by addressing "regulations, fees and permitting delays" and supports "pro-growth policies" and rural development [S4][S3].
- For the comprehensive plan update, prioritizes roads, bridges, drainage and broadband, fixing existing infrastructure before new long-term obligations, and protecting property rights; the plan "should not become another layer of red tape" [S4].

### Local control
- Would "protect local control from state and federal interference" and "oppose state mandates that undermine local policing efforts" [S3]; calls citizens' rights and limited government the critical District 3 issue [S4].

### Public safety and immigration
- Platform: fully fund law enforcement, "strong and real consequences for violence and theft" [S3].
- Supports policies that "uphold the rule of law" on immigration, opposes unfunded mandates shifting federal immigration burdens onto local taxpayers [S3]. Her site urges supporters to sign an initiative to "end the sanctuary state" [S2].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No statements found. Her platform lists "medical freedom," defending "your right to make personal medical decisions without government interference" [S3]; it does not say whether this concerns vaccines, abortion or something else.

### Parents and schools
- Platform: "Champion parents' rights to be fully involved in their children's education," curriculum transparency at the school-district level, and education "not political indoctrination" [S3]. (Recorded; not scored for a county office.)

### Environment
- "Promote responsible resource use — protect our environment without shutting down jobs" [S3].

## Endorsements

The Pacific County Republican Party; 19th District Rep. Joel McEntire; State Sen. Jeff Wilson; The Affordable Housing Council of the Olympia Master Builders; and Roy Runyon, per her campaign site [S2].

## Campaign notes

- The Chinook Observer reported that Gray sold $20 raffle tickets for her campaign at the Ocean Park Rod Run and promoted similar drawings at the county fair, and that state law limits raffles to qualifying charitable and nonprofit groups; she did not respond to a request for comment [S7].
- PDC (updated 2026-09-17): $3,585 raised, $8,016.57 spent [S9].
