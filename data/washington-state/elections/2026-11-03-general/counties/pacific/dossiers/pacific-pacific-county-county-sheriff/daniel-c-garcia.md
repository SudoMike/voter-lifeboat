## Background

Daniel Garcia, of Naselle, is the incumbent Pacific County sheriff (since January 2023) and prefers the Republican Party [S1][S6]. He is a retired U.S. Navy Reserve senior chief petty officer with 22 years of service and nine deployments or mobilizations, a small business owner, a volunteer firefighter/EMT since 2014 and a youth wrestling, baseball and soccer coach [S1][S2]. He had no previous policing experience when he took office; he graduated from the state Basic Law Enforcement Academy in December 2023 [S6]. He won the 2022 general after a write-in primary campaign, 6,498 votes (55.81%) to Robin Souvenir's 5,079 (43.62%) [S7][S6].

He led the August 2026 primary with 4,341 of 9,166 votes [S8].

## Positions

### Public safety
- Says his first term re-established a detective division, a volunteer search and rescue team, a training cadre, an internal affairs division and fleet management, and cites the recovery of Eric Vieth's remains and the convictions in the 2015 Jeffrey Beach homicide [S3][S4].
- Let the Pacific County Joint Drug Task Force end in 2025 by not seeking further state funding; says the state budget made renewal unlikely and that "the sheriff's office is the drug task force" now [S4].
- Wants to restart the office's marine unit [S4].
- On mental health crises: people should get help from trained mental health professionals "whenever possible"; credits a locally embedded co-responding designated crisis responder and crisis-intervention training for deputies [S3].

### Staffing, budget and taxes
- Says the office needs more deputies but is using its staff "to the best of our ability" with a schedule built on data, "without raising taxes — of which I am opposed." He says he was asked to support a one-tenth of one percent sales tax increase and did not [S5].

### Immigration enforcement
- Will cooperate with federal requests "that [are] lawful and consistent with Washington law" while keeping community trust so victims and witnesses report crimes [S3]. At the Raymond forum he said he would support ICE operations "should they be legal and lawful," and that he met agency heads in Washington, D.C. at his own expense [S5].

### Firearms
- Opposes any state-mandated gun buy-back or confiscation on constitutional grounds [S5].

### State standards for sheriffs
- Opposed SB 5974, which would have required five years of law enforcement service; he called it a reaction to Pacific County voters' choice and said it "says that the Legislature in Olympia knows better than the voters" [S6].

## Record

- Sheriff since Jan. 1, 2023 [S6]. In 2025 the jail was transferred out of the sheriff's office to a separate county department; at a November 2025 town hall Garcia said his office had to "bailout" the jail multiple times that year [S12].
- August 2026: fired deputy Sean Eastham, his primary opponent, after an investigation that followed a 42-minute response to an Ocean Park call, rather than accepting Eastham's medical retirement; the commission voted to acknowledge the retirement, and Garcia objected at the meeting [S9].
- Says there is "a problem between myself and the police chiefs, which we'll continue to work on" [S4].

## Campaign finance

As of July 2, $10,983 raised, almost all known donors from Pacific County [S10]. PDC (updated 2026-10-04): $20,509 raised, $12,904.32 spent [S11].
