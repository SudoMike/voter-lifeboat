## What the office does
The sheriff is the county's chief law enforcement officer and runs the Pacific County Sheriff's Office, which patrols a rural county of more than 1,200 square miles split into north and south zones [S2]. Since 2025 the county jail has been run by a separate Pacific County Jail Services department, not the sheriff [S5]. The term is four years [S1].

## Race dynamics
Incumbent Daniel Garcia (Prefers Republican Party), a retired Navy Reserve senior chief who won the office in 2022 as a write-in primary candidate with no prior policing experience, faces Rich Byrd (Prefers Independent Party), who spent about 20 years in the sheriff's office and retired as a lieutenant [S1][S6][S2]. In the four-way August primary Garcia had 4,341 votes and Byrd 3,091 of 9,166; deputy Sean Eastham (1,061) and retired LAPD sergeant David Podesta (651) were eliminated [S4].

The 2026 law setting sheriff eligibility standards (SB 5974), which could have removed Garcia because he lacks five years of law enforcement service, was blocked in court before it took effect, so the election went ahead without it; on Oct. 2 a judge permanently blocked the eligibility provisions, and the state plans to appeal [S6][S7].

The main disputes are the drug task force (Garcia let it end in 2025 and says the office now handles its cases; Byrd would restore it "day one"), round-the-clock coverage and the budget (Garcia says more deputies are needed but opposes a tax increase; Byrd says the office's budget doubled under Garcia and it must "do better with what we have"), and relations with other county departments and police chiefs [S2][S3].

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience:** a first-term incumbent from outside law enforcement, against a 20-year veteran of the same office who has been out of policing since 2010-2011.
2. **Spending:** Byrd argues for doing more with the current budget and cutting overtime; Garcia says the office needs more deputies but will not support a tax increase to pay for them.
3. **Safety:** both use enforcement framing; Byrd stresses a dedicated drug-enforcement unit alongside crisis-responder expansion and treatment, Garcia stresses rebuilt investigations and co-responder crisis work.
4. **Taxes:** Garcia says he opposes raising taxes and did not support a proposed 0.1% sales tax increase; Byrd has no tax position on record.
