## Background

Rich Byrd prefers the Independent Party and was born and raised in South Bend [S1][S3]. He served in the U.S. Army from 1986 to 1990 [S3], then spent about 20 years in the Pacific County Sheriff's Office as a corrections officer, deputy, sergeant, narcotics investigator, search and rescue coordinator and lieutenant [S1][S4]. His campaign site says he retired in 2010; the Observer reports 2011, after then-Sheriff Bill Didion lost re-election [S3][S6][S7]. Since then he has managed operations and budgets in the private sector, including for Astoria Ford and Dr. Roof Inc. [S3][S1]. He has named Heath Layman, a former Cosmopolis police chief and firearms instructor, as his undersheriff [S3][S6]. He has not held elected office [S1].

He finished second in the August primary with 3,091 of 9,166 votes [S8].

## Positions

### Public safety and drugs
- Calls fentanyl and methamphetamine the county's greatest public safety problem and would rebuild "a dedicated drug enforcement effort" to arrest traffickers [S4]. Would bring back the drug task force "day one," saying about $126,000 for it remains unspent and that the Legislature funded it through 2026 [S5].
- Also supports working with mental health providers, treatment programs and community organizations so people with addiction "have access to recovery" [S4].
- Strongly supports expanding designated crisis responders and co-responder programs pairing deputies with mental health professionals, toward seven-day coverage, using state grants and regional partnerships [S4].
- Supports community-oriented policing and wants deputies known personally in the communities they patrol [S5].

### Budget and staffing
- Says the budget "has doubled from $1.6 million to $3.2 million in the last three years" under Garcia: "to say we need more money is ludicrous… We have to do better with what we have" [S6].
- Would review staffing, scheduling and administrative costs, cut what he calls an "unheard of" use of overtime and flex time by salaried administrators, and put those administrators on shifts to keep 24-hour patrol coverage at both ends of the county [S4][S5][S6].

### Immigration enforcement
- Would cooperate with federal partners "within the limits of the law" on violent offenders and traffickers and follow Washington law; "we take care of our local area before we worry about a federal entity or a federal job" [S4][S6].

### Firearms
- Opposes any gun buy-back or confiscation and would lobby against legislation that violates the Second Amendment [S6].
