## BallotTitle
Concerning the Requirement of Supermajority Approval by Council for County Taxes

The Clark County Charter Review Commission adopted Resolution No. 26-08 proposing an amendment to the Clark County Home Rule Charter, concerning the requirement of supermajority approval by Council for County taxes. If approved, after January 1, 2027, this amendment would require any new councilmanic tax assessed, levied, or increased to have a two-third affirmative vote by the Council. Also, if approved, this amendment would not apply to fees, rates and charges, special assessments, or existing taxes levied prior to adoption of the amendment or any renewal or reauthorization of those taxes not seeking an increased tax rate.

## ExplanatoryStatement
The Clark County Charter Review Commission proposes requiring a two‑thirds supermajority vote of the County Council to approve any new councilmanic tax or increase to such a tax beginning January 1, 2027. The amendment defines “new councilmanic tax” and excludes existing taxes, fees, rates, and special assessments. The measure aims to strengthen fiscal oversight and limit tax increases without broad council support.

## ArgumentFor
Tax Increases Should Require Broad Support
When families and businesses are already paying more for everyday expenses, government should think carefully before asking taxpayers to pay even more. This amendment raises the standard for future council-approved tax increases by requiring a minimum two-thirds affirmative vote of the County Council before a new councilmanic tax may be assessed, levied, or increased.
A supermajority requires Councilmembers to work across political lines and build greater consensus before raising taxes. Requiring broad support also provides greater tax predictability for households and businesses, encouraging long-term investment and responsible planning.
This does not change any current tax or fee. It does not reduce or eliminate any current county service or program. Clark County will continue providing essential services, including public safety, roads, and parks.
Instead, this amendment protects taxpayers from future tax increases approved by only a narrow majority while encouraging future County Councils to build broader support before asking taxpayers to pay more. At a time when families and businesses face rising costs, this added safeguard helps ensure tax increases are carefully considered before they are approved. An “Approved” vote supports greater consensus before raising taxes. Tax increases deserve broader support.

## RebuttalFor
Don't be fooled. The pro statement is right about one thing: this won't lower your taxes or cost of living. It only limits a small fraction of your taxes — those that include funding parks and public safety. A minority's veto could starve that funding while costs keep rising. Real predictability means services that keep pace with inflation — not fiscal paralysis. Council seats are nonpartisan — there's no reason to limit its authority for a fake "consensus."

## ArgumentAgainst
Not a Tax Cut
Please vote No on the proposed supermajority amendment. This amendment will not cut your taxes because most taxes are not set by the County Council. Counties already have tight limits on raising taxes, and voters must approve extra taxes such as bonds and levies. County Council has not taken tax increases lightly. In 7 of the last 14 years, they did not approve the 1% property tax increase allowed by law.
Threatens Services You Rely On
By not increasing taxes by 1%, Clark County has had a hard time keeping up with services people expect and that the state requires. A supermajority requirement makes it harder for the Council to fund law and justice which is 73% of the county's budget. Approximately 1/3 of this goes to the Sheriff's Office. Maintaining roads, providing timely permits, funding parks, and insuring water quality might also decline without the 1% increase.
Gives Veto Power to a Super-Minority
The supermajority requirement sounds like a simple 2/3 rule, but with only 5 councilors, 2/3 means 4 out of 5 or 80% of councilors must agree. This would make Clark County the most restrictive in Washington. Allowing 2 councilors to control county funding puts public services at risk. Vote No on proposed Amendment 25!

## RebuttalAgainst
Amendment 25 does not claim to cut taxes, nor does it cut funding or services. Funding can still be approved by a Council supermajority or voters, including for public safety on this ballot. The County faces rising costs, but so do families and businesses. Calling this “veto power” ignores the alternative: just three councilors can impose a new tax on 550,000 Clark County residents. Amendment 25 simply requires broader support for Council-imposed tax increases.
