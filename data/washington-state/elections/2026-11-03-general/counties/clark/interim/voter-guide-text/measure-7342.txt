## BallotTitle
Bonds for Public Safety and Criminal Justice Capital Infrastructure

The Clark County Council adopted Resolution 2026-07-08, concerning public safety and criminal justice infrastructure. This proposition would allow Clark County to acquire, construct, remodel, and equip public safety and criminal justice infrastructure in Clark County, including the remodel and expansion of the Clark County Jail and courtrooms and a new or remodeled Sheriff’s Office Headquarters; issue no more than $366,204,000 of general obligation bonds maturing within 31 years; and levy annual excess property taxes on all taxable property within Clark County to pay the bonds, pursuant to Resolution 2026-07-08.

Should this proposition be approved?

## ExplanatoryStatement
Clark County proposes a bond measure seeking approval to issue up to $366,204,000 in general obligation bonds to upgrade public safety and criminal justice facilities. Funding would support remodeling and increasing jail capacity, adding additional courtrooms and building or renovating the Sheriff’s Office Headquarters. The bonds would mature within 31 years and be repaid through excess property tax levies. The Budget Office and Finance Team have reviewed fiscal impacts.

## ArgumentFor
Public safety begins with a justice system that works. Clark County's jail, the hub of the system, was built 40 plus years ago for a much smaller community. Today, it is overcrowded, outdated and unsafe. It no longer has the capacity to meet the needs of our growing county. Action to address this long-standing need is long overdue.
This measure would provide funding to expand capacity and update the Jail and related public safety infrastructure. It will create the space needed now and for the future to safely hold offenders accountable and improve staff safety. It will reduce the need to house inmates in other counties at higher cost. It will add much needed medical and addiction treatment and reentry services that reduce repeat crime and ultimately save money. It would also support improvements to court facilities and a new Sheriff's Office headquarters displaced by the jail expansion.
Voters are concerned about affordability. The 30-year bond structure will reduce today’s costs to about $86/year per median home. Why is this a good investment now? When there is not enough room in the jail, offenders may be released sooner than intended, reducing public confidence in the justice system. A properly sized jail helps ensure that people arrested for crimes can be held while their cases move through the courts, while also providing access to mental health, addiction treatment, and other humane services.
Accountability, safer neighborhoods, treatment and ultimately reduced costs are the strongest reasons to approve this investment.
