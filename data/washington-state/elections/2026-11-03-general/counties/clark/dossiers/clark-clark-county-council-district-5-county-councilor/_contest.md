## What the office does
The five-member Clark County Council is the county's legislative body under its home rule charter: it adopts the budget and property-tax levies, sets councilmanic taxes and fees, adopts the comprehensive plan and land-use rules for unincorporated areas, and decides which county propositions go to voters. The District 5 seat is nonpartisan [S1]. Silliman describes District 5 as encompassing nearly half of Clark County (peter-silliman.md S3); both finalists live in the north county, in the Battle Ground and La Center areas (troy-mccoy.md S1; peter-silliman.md S1).

## Race dynamics
The seat is open: Council Chair Sue Marshall is not seeking re-election [S2]. Battle Ground City Councilor and former mayor Troy McCoy won 52.54% of the election-night primary vote to Charter Review Commissioner Peter Silliman's 47.46% [S2]. Both call for public-safety investment and caution on taxes, but they differ in degree. Silliman campaigns on "protection from tax increases," criticizes the council's maximum levy increases and sales tax, and supports a charter amendment requiring four of five councilors to approve a tax increase; McCoy says there is not "a lot of room for tax increases" but called that threshold too high, and as mayor voted for a councilmanic police sales tax [S3] (peter-silliman.md S5; troy-mccoy.md S8). Silliman authored the budget-transparency (Charter Amendment 20) and performance-audit (Charter Amendment 27) amendments on this ballot and opposes Charter Amendment 22 (peter-silliman.md S11, S12, S13). Silliman would roll back county GMA, permitting and building-code requirements; McCoy stresses keeping industry off farmland and rural Brush Prairie (peter-silliman.md S3; troy-mccoy.md S3). McCoy is endorsed by Marshall, The Columbian, and officials of both parties; Silliman is a Republican precinct committee officer (troy-mccoy.md S6, S11; peter-silliman.md S1). Neither candidate's position on Propositions 12 and 13 was found.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** a "protection from tax increases" platform and support for a tax supermajority vs. caution on taxes alongside a record vote for a city public-safety sales tax.
2. **Spending and accountability:** charter-mandated budget timelines and performance audits vs. "responsible budgets" and outside funding before taxpayers.
3. **Housing and growth:** regulatory rollback (GMA, permitting, codes) vs. managed growth that keeps industry off farmland.
4. **Public safety:** a charter mandate for deputy staffing and "unapologetically pro law enforcement" framing vs. a "holistic" staffing-and-facilities plan centered on jail capacity.
5. **Record vs. renewal:** a city councilor and former mayor vs. a charter commissioner and former council staff analyst.
