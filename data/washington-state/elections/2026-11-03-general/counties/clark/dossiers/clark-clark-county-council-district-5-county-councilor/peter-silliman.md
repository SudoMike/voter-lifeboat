## Background
Peter Silliman, of La Center, is a 56-year Clark County resident and an elected District 5 member of the 2026-2027 Clark County Charter Review Commission [S1][S14]. He served on the 2013 Clark County Charter Freeholder Board and was a policy analyst for the Board of Clark County Councilors from 2014 to 2016, working for Councilor Tom Mielke [S1][S8]. He was a telecommunications professional for 22 years, leaving as a senior engineer in 2014, and has run a communications consulting business since 2017 [S1]. He is a Republican precinct committee officer and legislative district coordinator, and founded Torah Tree of Life Ministries [S1]; his site describes "professional representation of conservative values" [S3].

He finished second in the August 4 primary with 47.46% in the election-night count to Troy McCoy's 52.54% [S18].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** "People in government think that your money is their money"; criticizes the council for raising property-tax levies "by the maximum amount possible without a vote of the people" and raising the countywide sales tax, and says the general-fund, road and conservation-futures levies rose 5%, 8.5% and 16% in four years [S5]. Says residents are "fiscally exhausted" [S17]. At the Oct. 1 forum supported the charter amendment requiring four of five councilors to approve tax increases, saying he helped sponsor it [S17].
- **Spending and accountability:** would "dig into the budget to find real non-essential services that can be cut" [S5]. Sponsored charter amendments for an earlier, more public budget process and for council-initiated performance audits of county programs, to "check and balance the power of the executive branch" [S4][S15][S16].
- **Housing:** says GMA restrictions, impact fees, zoning limits, permitting expenses and building codes add an estimated $164,000-$200,000 to the cost of a new Washington home; would revisit county GMA restrictions that exceed state law, propose limits on county permitting timelines and review county building codes stricter than cities' [S3][S8].
- **Public safety:** says county deputy staffing is far below a reasonable level and blames officials for not prioritizing it; "citizens must elect unapologetically pro law enforcement representatives" [S6]. Co-sponsored a charter amendment requiring the county to prioritize deputy funding [S4][S9]; says rural residents should receive the same level of law-enforcement staffing as city residents [S8].
- **Growth and land:** would protect important habitat without "over burdening landowners," provide land for industry, and preserve agriculture and forestry [S7]. Says the comprehensive plan is delayed because agricultural-land questions are being addressed too late [S17].
- **Parks:** wants the county to develop trails and parks on land it bought years ago [S3][S7][S17].
- **Fireworks:** opposed additional county restrictions [S17].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Parents and schools:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. Related: says "Washington state has passed the biggest tax increase in state history" [S5].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **County charter amendments:** chairs the committee for Charter Amendment 20 (budget transparency process) [S11] and Charter Amendment 27 (legislative-branch performance audits) [S12]; serves on the committee against Charter Amendment 22 (skipping the primary for county races with two or fewer candidates) [S13]; supported Charter Amendment 25 (tax supermajority) at the Oct. 1 forum [S17].
- **Propositions 12 and 13:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- **Charter Review Commission (elected Nov. 2025):** sponsored amendment 26-10, revised budget transparency process, which passed the commission 12-3 and is on the ballot as Charter Amendment 20 [S16][S11]; sponsored 26-14, performance audits, which passed 13-2 and is on the ballot as Charter Amendment 27 [S15][S12]. Co-sponsored 26-26, a public-safety amendment setting deputy staffing at 85% of the statewide per-capita average, which the commission deferred to 2027 [S9][S10].
