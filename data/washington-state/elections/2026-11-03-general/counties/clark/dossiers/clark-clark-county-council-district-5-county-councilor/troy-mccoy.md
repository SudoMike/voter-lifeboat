## Background
Troy McCoy is a Battle Ground city councilor and former mayor running for the open District 5 seat; Council Chair Sue Marshall is not seeking re-election [S10]. His pamphlet statement lists Battle Ground City Council since 2021, mayor 2023-2025, and the Battle Ground School Board 2017-2021, two years as its president [S1]; his campaign site gives his council service as 2022 to present and his mayoral term as 2024-2025 [S5]. He has owned a small business in Battle Ground for more than 20 years, was an account manager for Buckman Specialty Chemicals, and holds a B.S. in chemical engineering from Oregon State University [S1][S4]. He has served on the C-TRAN board, chaired the Southwest Washington Regional Transportation Council, and sits on the Discovery Clean Water Alliance [S5].

He led the two-candidate August 4 primary with 52.54% in the election-night count to Peter Silliman's 47.46% [S10].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** at an Oct. 1 forum said "I don't think there's a lot of room for tax increases in this. People can't do it" [S9]. On jail capacity, would "secure state and federal funding first and make sure every outside dollar is pursued before asking taxpayers for additional support" [S3]. Says taxpayers must not pay for light rail unless voters approve [S1]. Opposed the proposed charter amendment requiring four of five councilors to approve a tax increase, calling the threshold too high [S9].
- **Spending:** pledges "responsible budgets" and investments in safety that are "targeted, accountable and effective"; says he delivered balanced budgets in Battle Ground [S1][S3][S5].
- **Public safety:** says law enforcement and emergency services need "the tools and facilities needed to protect the community" and calls current jail capacity, which "limits enforcement options in serious situations," unacceptable; wants "a realistic, nonpartisan plan" for jail capacity and public-safety infrastructure [S3]. Describes his approach as "holistic": staffing, equipment, facilities, training and coordination [S3].
- **Growth and land use:** supports "carefully managed growth"; opposes industrial development on productive farmland or next to established neighborhoods, including industrialization of rural Brush Prairie [S1][S3]. Says the comprehensive plan is not moving in the right direction because of repeated delays, "a lack of leadership" [S9].
- **Housing and permitting:** wants timely, predictable permitting, including for accessory dwellings [S3].
- **Parks:** says population growth has increased demand for parks while staffing and maintenance remain concerns [S9].
- **Fireworks:** would ask residents what regulations they want before acting [S9].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Parents and schools:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08. (As a school director he cites restoring middle-school sports [S2].)

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **County measures:** opposed Charter Amendment 25 (council supermajority for taxes) at the Oct. 1 forum [S9]. No explicit position on Propositions 12 and 13 found; his site calls for a jail-capacity plan that pursues outside funding before taxpayers [S3]. No position on the other charter amendments found.

## Record
- **Battle Ground public-safety sales tax (Nov. 17, 2025):** as mayor, part of the unanimous council vote adopting a 0.1% councilmanic public-safety sales tax (about $667,000 a year) to add police officers: "Our community has been consistently clear about the importance of maintaining a respectable number of police officers" [S8].
- Lists as city accomplishments: balanced budgets, body cameras, three additional officers and eight police vehicles, $7.5 million in road resurfacing, $1.5 million for a new park, and senior-center funding [S5].
- Endorsers credit him with helping pass SB 5820, which they say limits industrialization of Brush Prairie and protects resource land [S6].
- Voted against the Aug. 5, 2026 termination without cause of Battle Ground's city manager (passed 4-3) [S13].
