## What the office does
The assessor values property for taxation as of a January 1 valuation date, and owners can appeal those values to the Board of Equalization (S1). The office also runs the property-tax exemption program for seniors and people with disabilities and values new construction. Its staffing is set through the county budget (S2).

## Race dynamics
Four-term incumbent Peter Van Nortwick, assessor since 2011, faces Tyler Thoune, a real estate investor and remodeler who has never held office (S1). Van Nortwick runs on his appraiser credentials, his work in Olympia to expand the senior exemption and push a homestead exemption, and an office with fewer employees than when he took over. Thoune argues the office's valuations miss the market. He cites a $13.5 million sale assessed at about 44% of price and a 56% reduction-or-reversal rate in 2024 Board of Equalization appeals, and says under-assessment of expensive homes shifts taxes onto other owners (S1). Neither campaign shows significant fundraising: Van Nortwick files under PDC mini reporting and Thoune reported $1,619.72 raised (peter-van-nortwick.md S2; tyler-thoune.md S2). Thoune's listed website is a parked domain (tyler-thoune.md S3). No news coverage of this race was found as of 2026-10-08.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** a 15-year incumbent against a first-time candidate who challenges the office's accuracy.
2. **Taxes:** Van Nortwick advocates property-tax relief through exemptions. Thoune's argument is about fair distribution between owners, not the overall tax level, so taxes separates them only weakly.
3. **Spending/accountability:** Van Nortwick points to a leaner office. Thoune's "accountability for mistakes" concerns valuation accuracy, not government spending.
4. Housing, safety, climate and reform do not arise in this administrative race.
