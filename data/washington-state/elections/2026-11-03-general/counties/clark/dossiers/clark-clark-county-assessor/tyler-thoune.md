## Background

Tyler Thoune says he has more than 20 years of experience in the Clark County real estate market, having bought, remodeled, flipped, evaluated and invested in properties around the county [S1]. He graduated from Fort Vancouver High School in 1999 and earned EMT certification in 2018 [S1]. He lists no elected experience [S1]. Since 2016 he has volunteered as a coach for Little League Baseball and Clark County Youth Football. He also volunteers with the Pleasant Valley Middle School band and at Luke's Legacy Carnival at King's Way School [S1]. The office is nonpartisan and the ballot lists no party preference [S1].

The pamphlet gives www.votethoune.com as his website. When fetched on 2026-10-08 the domain showed only a hosting company's parked-domain page [S1][S3].

## Positions

### Assessment accuracy and fairness
- Says homeowners deserve assessments "that reflect the real market — not a system that misses the mark," and that he understands the market from hands-on experience, "not just computer models and formulas" [S1].
- Cites a luxury home that sold for $13.5 million on December 22, 2023, ten days before the January 1, 2024 valuation date, and was assessed at $5,955,707, about 44% of the sale price. He estimates the gap at roughly $75,000 a year in property taxes and says under-assessing high-value properties can shift the tax burden to other taxpayers [S1]. This dossier did not independently verify the example.
- Says 56% of the valuation cases that reached the Board of Equalization in 2024 were reduced or overturned, which he says raises concerns that many residents were over-assessed [S1].
- Promises "fair valuations, transparency, accountability for mistakes, better customer service, and common-sense assessment practices" so that property owners do not overpay [S1].
