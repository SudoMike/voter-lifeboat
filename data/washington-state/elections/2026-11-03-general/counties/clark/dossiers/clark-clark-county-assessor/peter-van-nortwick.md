## Background

Peter Van Nortwick has been Clark County Assessor since 2011 [S1]. He was reelected in November 2022 to a four-year term that began in January 2023 [S5]. He is a certified general appraiser with residential, agricultural and commercial appraisal experience, a member of the Clark County Association of Realtors, and was its 2020 Affiliate of the Year [S1]. He studied at the University of Washington, did graduate work at the University of Colorado and Seattle University, and graduated from Leadership Clark County in 2010 [S1]. He has been a volunteer football coach in the Vancouver School District and is a youth leader at Northwest Gospel Church Westside [S1]. The office is nonpartisan and the ballot lists no party preference [S1].

## Positions

### Property taxes and exemptions
- Says he has worked to educate taxpayers on how the property tax system works and why taxes increase [S1].
- Says he "successfully fought" in Olympia to expand the senior exemption program, with the income threshold rising to $85,000 this year [S1]. In 2019, when the Legislature raised the threshold to $50,348, he said, "We have fought for years to align income levels to the median incomes of our counties" [S3].
- Says he has worked to advance a homestead exemption in Washington and will keep working with assessors and legislators "to provide meaningful property tax relief for hardworking homeowners" [S1].

### Office operations and cost
- Says the office uses technology to keep assessments fair and equitable "while keeping costs low for taxpayers," and that it now has fewer full-time employees than when he was first elected [S1].

## Record

- Clark County Assessor since 2011; reelected in 2022 [S1][S5].
- In November 2019 he asked the County Council to add two temporary project positions (about $116,000) to handle the expanded senior exemption program and new construction. He told the council his office had given up three full-time positions in 2013 when construction was slow. The council did not add the positions to the supplemental budget; the county manager instead recommended one permanent appraiser in the 2020 budget [S4].
- In July 2025 he spoke in public comment against a late-added council proposal to put a charter change making the County Clerk an appointed office on the ballot, saying it "violates the spirit of the charter." The council took no action and left the question to the Charter Review Commission [S6].
