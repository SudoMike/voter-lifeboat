## Background
Kim D. Harless (Prefers Democratic Party) is a Vancouver City Council member, first elected in 2021 and serving a second term that runs through 2029 [S1][S7]. She co-chaired the 2021 Clark County Charter Review Commission [S1][S3]. She is director of community engagement and policy at the Native American Youth and Family Center and previously worked for Clark County Public Health and Environmental Services (recycling and waste system planning) and PaintCare [S1][S3]. She holds a B.S. in environmental science from Washington State University Vancouver and sits on the C-TRAN and Southwest Washington Clean Air Agency boards [S1]. The Columbian reports she is 39 [S5].

She is running for the open Position 1 seat held by Rep. Sharon Wylie, a Democrat who ran for Clark County auditor instead of re-election [S7]. Her campaign site says she advanced from the August primary to the November ballot [S4].

The primary dossier carried forward here cited a City of Vancouver council page that now returns "page not found"; the council service it described is confirmed by the pamphlet and the Columbian [S1][S7].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** wants to defend the capital gains tax and the 2026 tax on income over $1 million "from ballot and legal challenges," expand taxes on concentrated wealth, close tax breaks for large corporations and lower the tax burden on families and small businesses; calls Washington's tax structure the second most regressive in the country [S2]. Pamphlet lists "a fair tax system" among her priorities [S1].
- **Spending:** would direct new revenue to housing, education, healthcare, clean air and water and the public workforce, and calls the budget "a moral document" [S2]. Calls for reversing cuts to community and technical colleges, moving toward free community college, restoring Working Connections Child Care and fully funding special education [S2].
- **Housing:** calls housing "a human right, not a commodity"; would strengthen rent stabilization "with a lower cap than what passed in 2025," add just-cause eviction and a tenant right of first purchase, expand publicly owned social housing, raise Housing Trust Fund and Right to Counsel funding, and fund permanent supportive housing [S2].
- **Public safety:** would expand mental-health co-responder programs, route 911 calls that do not need an armed response to crisis responders, and "civilianize traffic enforcement" [S2]. Supports firearm secure-storage requirements, an excise tax on firearms and ammunition, and limits on bulk purchases [S2].
- **Climate and energy:** would defend the Climate Commitment Act, have the Legislature repay money borrowed from it, and adopt a "climate superfund" approach charging the largest carbon polluters [S2]. Supports wind, solar, storage and transmission buildout, and urges caution on small modular nuclear [S2].
- **Data centers and technology:** wants ratepayers protected from paying for infrastructure that serves data centers and other large industrial users, with "real transparency on their energy and water use" [S2]. Supports building on new limits on surveillance tools such as Flock cameras [S2].
- **Healthcare:** would "champion the Washington Health Trust to move our state toward a unified system covering all residents" [S2]; pamphlet lists "universal healthcare" [S1]. Would reopen Apple Health to low-income residents regardless of immigration status [S2].
- **Immigration:** would pass the SAFE Act to keep federal immigration enforcement out of schools, healthcare facilities and other sensitive places, strengthen enforcement of the Keep Washington Working Act, and fund deportation legal defense [S2]. Pamphlet lists "immigrant rights" [S1].
- **Transportation:** supports the Interstate 5 bridge replacement with light rail, transit, sidewalks and bike routes "instead of highway expansion," and fareless transit funded through progressive revenue [S2].
- **Elections and reform:** supports ranked choice and proportional ranked choice voting and a local option for any community to adopt it [S2][S3].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** would "protect and defend reproductive freedom and abortion access," protect access to gender-affirming care and the state's shield law [S2]. Urges voters to reject I-638, which she says "would ban transgender girls from school sports" [S2]. Endorsed by Pro-Choice Washington, Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates and Washington State Stonewall Democrats [S4].
- **Parents and schools:** urges voters to reject I-1, which she says would force schools to "out" LGBTQIA+ students and strip their privacy protections [S2]. Would defend comprehensive sex education "from any effort to roll it back," defend DEI in schools, replace zero-tolerance discipline with restorative practices and fully implement the Since Time Immemorial tribal history curriculum [S2]. Wants the school funding formula tailored to student needs [S2].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** opposes repeal; her platform pledges to defend the tax "from ballot and legal challenges" [S2].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** opposes; urges voters to reject IL26-001 [S2]. On Oct. 5, 2026, the Vancouver City Council, of which she is a member, voted unanimously for a resolution opposing Initiatives 001 and 638 [S8].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** opposes; urges voters to reject IL26-638 [S2][S8].

## Record
- Vancouver City Council (2022-present), as described by her campaign: championed Proposition 3 to expand the Affordable Housing Fund to $10 million a year; voted to expand the Multi-Family Tax Exemption and to waive developer fees for MFTE projects; supported removing parking minimums; helped establish Safe Stay communities and declare a homelessness emergency [S3].
- Says she "passed Vancouver's first B&O tax" to raise revenue for a shelter while protecting small businesses [S3].
- Says she supported co-responder models and diversion of non-violent calls to civilian teams, and also investments in body cameras, drones and non-lethal options and Propositions 2 and 4 for fire and police funding [S3].
- Says she championed Vancouver's Climate Action Framework (carbon neutrality by 2040) and led moratoriums on new warehouses and bulk fossil-fuel storage in city limits [S3].
- Charter Review Commission (2021): says she led the effort to put ranked choice voting and nonpartisan county elections on the ballot [S3].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $66,211.04 raised, $35,810.31 spent [S9].

## Scoring notes
- Defends the tax on income over $1 million and wants more wealth taxes; her opponent's campaign describes concerns about that tax [S2].
- Opposes I-1 and I-638; her opponent publicly supports I-638 [S2].
- Platform favors rent stabilization, social housing and tenant protections; city record also includes supply measures (MFTE expansion, parking minimums) [S2][S3].
- Supports a unified state health system and the SAFE Act on immigration enforcement [S2].
- Two-term city council member running for the Legislature for the first time; opponent has not held elected office [S1][S5].
