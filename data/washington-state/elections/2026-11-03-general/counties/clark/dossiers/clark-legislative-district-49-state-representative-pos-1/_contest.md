## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House, for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets: K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The 49th District is in Vancouver [S1][S2].

## Race dynamics
The seat is open for the first time in 15 years [S2]. Democratic Rep. Sharon Wylie did not seek a ninth term and ran for Clark County auditor instead [S3][S4][S5]; she voted yes on the 2026 tax on income over $1 million (ESSB 6346, passed the House 51-46) [S6]. The August primary also included Democrat Mike Pond (primary dossier); primary vote shares were not verified for this refresh.

Democrat Kim Harless is a two-term Vancouver City Council member; Republican Sarah Mittelman is a psychiatric nurse practitioner and political newcomer who chaired the Clark County Democratic Women until shortly before announcing as a Republican [S2][S4]. The Columbian reports both name affordability and public safety as priorities [S2], and its editorial board endorsed Harless [S7].

The policy gap is wide. Harless's platform defends the tax on income over $1 million and seeks more wealth taxes, a lower rent-stabilization cap, social housing, a unified state health system and the SAFE Act on immigration enforcement, and urges a no vote on I-1 and I-638 (kim-d-harless.md S2). Mittelman's campaign stresses measurable outcomes before new spending, describes concerns about the income tax, backs I-638, and opposed the 2026 state law expanding where supportive housing can be sited (sarah-mittelman.md S2, S3, S5, S6). Harless reports $66.2k raised to Mittelman's $27.3k (PDC, both dossiers).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Harless would defend and expand progressive revenue; Mittelman's campaign voices concerns about the new income tax.
2. **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** Harless opposes I-638 and backs reproductive and gender-affirming care protections; Mittelman supports I-638.
3. **Parents and schools:** Harless opposes I-1 and defends comprehensive sex education; no position found for Mittelman.
4. **Spending:** Harless directs new revenue to expanded programs; Mittelman's plank is "no blank checks without measurable outcomes."
5. **Housing and local control:** Harless favors rent stabilization, tenant protections and social housing; Mittelman opposes state siting mandates on supportive housing.
6. **Public safety:** Harless favors co-responders and civilian traffic enforcement; Mittelman pairs treatment capacity (including involuntary treatment) with accountability for repeat offenders.
7. **Record vs. renewal:** a sitting city council member vs. a first-time candidate.
