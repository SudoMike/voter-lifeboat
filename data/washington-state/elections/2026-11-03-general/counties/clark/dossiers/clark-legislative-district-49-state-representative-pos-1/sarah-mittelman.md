## Background
Sarah Mittelman (Prefers Republican Party) is a board-certified psychiatric mental health nurse practitioner with more than 20 years in emergency, behavioral health, addiction and homeless-outreach care [S1][S2]. She holds a Doctor of Nursing Practice and MSN from Frontier Nursing University, a BSN from Oregon Health & Science University and an associate degree in nursing from Clark College [S1]. She lists service as vice chair of the Oregon Nurses Foundation and in Oregon Nurses Association leadership [S1]. She has not held elected office [S1]; the Columbian describes her as a political newcomer [S11].

Until spring 2026 she chaired the Clark County Democratic Women; she announced her candidacy as a Republican shortly after leaving that group's executive team [S4]. She wrote that she left the Democratic Party over policy outcomes on homelessness and addiction and over antisemitism she saw "excused, minimized, or rationalized," and that she found Republicans who cared about "public safety, fiscal responsibility, individual liberty, strong families" [S7]. Her campaign site lists Rep. Peter Abbarno (R-20th) speaking in support of her [S3].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** her campaign site lists a video titled "It's Not a Millionaire's Tax," described as presenting "the campaign's concerns about the policy" of Washington's new income tax [S3]. Her priorities include protecting "working families and small businesses from unnecessary financial burdens" [S2].
- **Spending and accountability:** "No blank checks without measurable outcomes"; would "review programs for effectiveness before expanding costs" [S2]. Argues homelessness spending in Clark County lacks measurable results and that public dollars should be tied to clear expectations and outcomes [S9][S10]. Pamphlet calls for "practical, transparent, accountable government" [S1].
- **Public safety and behavioral health:** supports coordination among law enforcement, behavioral health and crisis response "while holding repeat offenders accountable" [S2]. Calls for more detox, psychiatric stabilization and recovery-housing capacity and "appropriate intervention pathways, including involuntary treatment when clinically necessary and legally justified," plus accountability for traffickers and drug dealers [S8]. Says "harm reduction was never meant to become harm acceptance" [S8].
- **Housing and growth:** opposed HB 2266 (2026), which she describes as expanding where permanent supportive housing, transitional housing and shelters can be sited while limiting local governments' ability to restrict them; wrote that no neighborhood "should be forced to absorb state-mandated solutions without meaningful local input" [S6]. Supports "thoughtful growth with strong local input and community-focused planning" [S2]. Says supportive housing "is not the same as treatment or recovery" [S6].
- **Local voice:** says "State policy should reflect local needs, local infrastructure, and local voices" [S2].
- **Transportation:** wants policy that reduces congestion and improves mobility [S2].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** supports I-638; wrote that "biological males should not compete in girls sports" while saying transgender people "deserve dignity, kindness and opportunity" and that the state should work harder to provide athletic opportunities for transgender students [S5]. No public position found on abortion or gender-affirming care.
- **Parents and schools:** no public position found on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs as of 2026-10-08.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no explicit statement on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. Related: her campaign site describes a video setting out her concerns about the income tax [S3].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** supports; "I will be voting yes on IL26 638" [S5].
