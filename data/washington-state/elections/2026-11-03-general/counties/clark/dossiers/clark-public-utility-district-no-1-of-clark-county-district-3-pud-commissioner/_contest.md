## What the office does
The three Clark Public Utilities commissioners set policy, rates and budgets for the county's public electric and water utility, and approve resource plans and major investments [S1].

## Electorate
Only District 3 voters nominated candidates in the August primary, but "voters of the entire public utility district may vote at a general election to elect a person as a commissioner of the commissioner district" [S2]. Clark Public Utilities is countywide, so every Clark County ballot carries this race; in 2024 the District 1 general drew 222,496 votes countywide [S3].

## Race dynamics
Incumbent Jane Van Dyke, an attorney and past president of the state PUD association, runs on the utility's record: conservation beyond regional goals, one electric rate increase in 15 years, assistance programs and customer-satisfaction awards [S1]. Gordon Matthews, a retired BPA engineer endorsed by Washington Conservation Action, calls the utility well run but says declining hydro supply and electrification require new resources, chiefly solar built with eastern Washington utilities and redesigned community solar [S1]. Kevin Roegner, a third candidate in the primary, is not on the general ballot [S1].

## Differentiating issue axes
No rubric axis applies to PUD races (category Local), so this race ships summaries without scores. The practical differences are continuity of current resource and rate planning versus a faster push for new solar generation [S1].
