## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House, for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets: K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the top two finishers from the August primary.

## Race dynamics
The 20th District includes parts of Cowlitz, Clark and Lewis counties [S3]; the pamphlet also lists Thurston as a ballot county [S1]. Republican Rep. Ed Orcutt of Kalama, a consulting forester first appointed to the House in 2002, is seeking a 13th term; he is ranking Republican on the House Finance Committee and calls himself his caucus's lead in trying to prevent tax increases [S3][S1]. He voted against the 2026 tax on income over $1 million (ESSB 6346, passed 51-46) [S2]. Democrat Evan Jones of Kelso, a Port of Longview commissioner, IBEW journeyman and Army veteran, says he ran after Orcutt voted against the 2025 contractor child-labor bill (HB 1644) and defended that vote to him [S3]. Orcutt led the primary 62.26% to 37.56% in the election-night count [S4].

Jones runs as a business-friendly, pro-Second-Amendment Democrat under the slogan "Less Olympia. More Paychecks," says the state does not need higher taxes, and argues that a member of the majority party would be heard more than a Republican in the minority (evan-jones.md S2, S3). Orcutt says his role is to make sure constituents who want lower taxes are heard in Olympia [S3].

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** 25-year House member and Finance ranking member vs. a first-term port commissioner in his first legislative race.
2. **Worker safety and contractor accountability:** the HB 1644 vote that prompted Jones's run; Orcutt favors bringing violators back into compliance over penalties that could close a company.
3. **Taxes:** both oppose new taxes; Orcutt has a long record of no votes, repeal bills and income-tax amendments, while Jones offers a platform plank ("More Taxpayers, Not More Taxes") without a record.
4. **Spending:** Orcutt voted against the 2025-27 operating budget and campaigns on living within existing revenue; no Jones position found.
5. **Parents and schools / housing / climate:** Orcutt's no votes on the 2025 parents'-rights amendments, rent-increase limits and clean-fuels changes; no Jones positions found on these.
