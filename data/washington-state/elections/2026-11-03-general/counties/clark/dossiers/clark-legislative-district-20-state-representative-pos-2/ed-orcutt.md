## Background
Ed Orcutt, R-Kalama, is the Republican incumbent in Position 2 of the 20th Legislative District and is serving his 12th two-year House term [S1]. He was first appointed to a vacancy in the 18th District in 2002 and has represented the 20th District since the 2012-13 redistricting [S16][S4]. He is 64 [S17], a consulting forester and owner of Cascade Forest Management (since 2009; a forester since 1990), with a B.S. in forest management from the University of Idaho; he lives in Kalama and is a Kalama Lions member [S1][S4][S16][S17]. He is ranking Republican on the House Finance Committee, sits on the Transportation and Agriculture and Natural Resources committees, is vice chair of the Joint Legislative Audit and Review Committee, and serves on the Economic and Revenue Forecast Council [S1][S16].

In the August 4 primary he led Democrat Evan Jones 62.26% to 37.56% in the election-night count [S17].

Note: his campaign website's pages still carry 2018 event listings and a 2012 copyright; the Issues page bill list dates from earlier sessions [S2][S3].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** pamphlet says he is "a leader in protecting taxpayers" and wants to fund education and services "using existing revenues and protecting taxpayers from tax increases" [S1]. As Finance ranking member, he said the role "puts me as a lead for our caucus in trying to prevent tax increases" [S16]. He called the 2026 tax on income over $1 million the session's biggest disappointment: "They're just overspending... And an income tax is not going to solve that problem. It's actually threatening to make the situation worse" [S18]. He said he plans to keep working to repeal the 2025 tax increases and that frequent tax changes make the state look "very, very unstable and extremely risky" to businesses [S18]. He counts blocking proposals to raise the 1% annual property-tax growth limit as a success [S16].
- **Spending:** says state government "must be required to live within its means" and should fund schools, public safety and services for vulnerable residents "out of existing revenue" [S3][S1].
- **Jobs and business:** says the state "should treat employers as partners, not as an enemy" [S3]; priorities include family-wage jobs and making agencies more responsive [S1].
- **Natural resources:** works to protect farming and timber harvest and small forest landowners, and wants to revisit Forest Practices Board bylaws after its vote to widen stream buffers [S3][S16][S18].
- **Worker safety (HB 1644):** said he did not recall Jones's email but believes companies should be held accountable for negligence; "The question is the extent you penalize them." He said enforcement should aim at bringing companies back into compliance and that he wants to preserve trades and vocational opportunities for young workers [S16].
- **Elections:** called federal requests for state voter data a reasonable way to ensure voters are citizens and said the state should work with the federal government "rather than suing them" [S21].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no campaign statement found as of 2026-10-08. See Record (HB 1971).
- **Parents and schools:** no campaign statement found as of 2026-10-08. See Record (HB 1296, SB 5181).

## Record
- **ESSB 6346 (2026 tax on income over $1 million):** voted no on House final passage, March 9, 2026 (passed 51-46) [S5]. Offered three floor amendments, none adopted: 2538, sending the act to voters at the next general election instead of the emergency clause [S6]; 2569, exempting small forestland owners' income from the tax [S7]; and 2568, rewriting the intent section to say an income tax on few filers would expose state services to "severe volatility" [S8].
- **ESSB 5814 (2025 excise taxes on select services):** voted no on House final passage, April 23, 2025 (passed 50-47) [S9].
- **ESSB 5167 (2025-27 operating budget):** voted no on House passage (March 31, 2025) and on the conference report (April 27, 2025) [S10].
- **Sponsored tax bills (2025-26):** prime sponsor of HB 2130 (repeal the new taxes in ESSB 5814), HB 2335 (repeal the 2025 B&O tax increases), HB 1374 (reduce the state sales tax rate), HB 1729 (property-tax relief by reducing the state school levies), HB 1375 (inflation-adjust the estate tax exclusion) and HB 2716 (restore a public utility tax credit for low-income assistance); none advanced past committee [S11]. HB 1376, allowing prepayment of the capital gains tax, became law in 2026 (chapter 191, Laws of 2026) [S11][S18].
- **Other sponsored bills:** HB 1372 on JLARC studies (chapter 198, Laws of 2025), HB 1365 (rental assistance for manufactured-home park tenants), and HB 2668 directing relocation of the Bob Oke Game Farm, whose language went into the 2026 supplemental capital budget [S11][S18].
- **EHB 1217 (2025 rent-increase limits):** voted no on House passage and on the conference report [S12].
- **ESHB 1296 (2025, amending the parents' rights law) and ESSB 5181 (2025, amending the parents' rights initiative):** voted no on final passage of both [S13][S14].
- **2SHB 1409 (2025 clean fuels program changes):** voted no on House passage and final passage [S15].
- **ESHB 1644 (2025 contractor child-labor penalties):** voted no on House passage, March 10, 2025 (passed 60-35) [S23][S16].
- **ESHB 1971 (2025, health-plan coverage of 12-month refills of prescription hormone therapy):** voted no on House passage, March 8, 2025 (passed 58-38) [S24].
- **Recognition:** NFIB "Guardian of Small Business" with a 100% score on its 10 House votes for the 69th Legislature [S20].
- **Campaign finance:** PDC reports $106,313 in contributions, a $51,606 carryforward, and $122,905 in expenditures (updated 2026-10-04) [S22].
