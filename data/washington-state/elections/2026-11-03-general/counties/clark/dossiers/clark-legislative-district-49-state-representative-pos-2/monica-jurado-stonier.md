## Background
Monica Jurado Stonier (Prefers Democratic Party) is the incumbent and House Majority Floor Leader, a member of the Appropriations, Education, Health Care and Wellness, and Rules committees, and chair of the Financial Education Public-Private Partnership [S1][S2]. The Columbian reports she has held the 49th District seat since 2016 and works as an access coach at Evergreen Public Schools, where she has worked for two decades [S3]. Her campaign says she earlier represented the 17th District and is the first person of color to represent either district [S2]. She was a classroom teacher at Pacific Middle School, holds a B.A. in psychology and a teaching certificate from Western Washington University and a master's in education from WSU Vancouver, and serves on the Council for the Homeless board [S1].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** at an April 2026 town hall, defended the tax on income over $1 million, saying it would affect fewer than 1,000 Southwest Washington households and is about taxing top earners to "better support the poorest people in the state"; said she would not vote to lower its threshold or for "a flat income tax" [S4].
- **Budget:** said House Democrats treat the operating budget as "a contract with the people of the state" [S4]. Pamphlet calls for "real funding for special education," childcare and housing [S1].
- **Education:** would return school-bond approval to a simple majority and reform pupil-transportation and special-education funding formulas [S2].
- **Healthcare:** cites affordable healthcare as a priority [S1]; supports HB 1730 to require insurance coverage of infertility diagnosis and treatment, and helped secure $1.95 million in capital funding to expand dental access [S2].
- **Housing and behavioral health:** says she is organizing regional responses to housing and behavioral-health shortages; pamphlet cites Clark County's eviction rate [S1][S2].
- **Federal government and immigrants:** pamphlet says "leaders in the other Washington are undercutting democracy" and "While they attack our neighbors, I stand with them" [S1]; campaign cites "new protections for immigrant workers" among her work [S2].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** record below (votes for the 2023 shield law for reproductive and gender-affirming care and for ESSB 5599) [S14][S15]. No public statement on I-638 found.
- **Parents and schools:** prime sponsor of the 2025 law that rewrote the parents' rights list enacted by Initiative 2081, and of the 2024 instructional-materials law her campaign describes as stopping school boards "from banning books through discrimination" (record below) [S10][S12][S2].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no explicit statement on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. Record: voted Yea on ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal, and defended it at a town hall [S5][S4].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no public statement on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. Related record: prime sponsor of ESHB 1296 (2025), which modified and eliminated some of the parents' rights in the 2024 Initiative 2081 law [S10].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- **Taxes:** voted Yea on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the 2026 tax on income over $1 million (March 9, 2026; passed 51-46) [S5]. In 2025 voted Yea on ESSB 5813, a more progressive capital gains and estate tax rate structure (passed 53-45) [S6]; ESSB 5814, changes to excise taxes (passed 50-47) [S7]; and ESHB 2081, business and occupation tax rate increases and a surcharge on large companies (passed 50-48) [S8].
- **Housing:** voted Yea on EHB 1217 (2025), limiting residential rent and fee increases, on House passage and the conference report (passed 54-44) [S9].
- **Parents and schools:** prime sponsor of ESHB 1296 (chapter 369, Laws of 2025) [S10]. Per the nonpartisan final bill report, it eliminated parents' rights to prior notification when medical services are offered to their child and to notification of medical services with insurance impact or follow-up care; changed records access from a copy within 10 business days to FERPA's 45 days; tied classroom-material review to local policies; added rights such as protection from discrimination and filing bullying complaints; declared the parents' rights create no private right of action; and set student safety, freedom from discrimination, basic education and privacy as priorities for local school policies, enforceable through an OSPI complaint process [S10]. She voted Yea on its House passage and on the Senate-amended version (59-39) [S11]. She also voted Yea on ESSB 5181 (2025), a Senate bill amending the same initiative, which passed the House 56-39 but was not enacted [S13].
- Prime sponsor of ESHB 2331 (chapter 316, Laws of 2024), modifying requirements for public school instructional and supplemental materials [S12]; her campaign describes it as stopping school boards from banning books through discrimination [S2].
- **Gender and reproductive policy:** voted Yea on ESHB 1469 (2023), on access to reproductive health care and gender-affirming treatment (passed 59-38) [S14], and on ESSB 5599 (2023), supporting youth seeking protected health care services (passed 57-39) [S15].
- **Other bills her campaign cites:** HB 1508 (Breakfast After the Bell), HB 1455 (minimum marriage age of 18), the 2020 Domestic Workers Protection Act, and floor work on HB 1607, the bottle-deposit recycling law; brought Dolly Parton's Imagination Library to Washington [S2].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-05): $139,972.25 raised, $91,041.45 spent [S16].
