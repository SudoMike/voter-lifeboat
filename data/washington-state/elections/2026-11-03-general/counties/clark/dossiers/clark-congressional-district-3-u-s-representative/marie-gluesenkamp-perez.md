## Background
Marie Gluesenkamp Perez (Prefers Democratic Party) has represented the 3rd District since 2023 and is seeking a third term [S1][S36]. She serves on the House Appropriations Committee, including its Agriculture and Rural Development Subcommittee. In the 118th Congress she sat on the Agriculture and Small Business committees [S1][S4]. She co-owns Dean's Car Care, an auto repair and machine shop, with her husband, holds a B.A. in economics from Reed College, and lives in rural Skamania County [S1][S4]. She has served as an Underwood Soil and Water Conservation District supervisor [S1]. She won the seat in 2022 by defeating Republican Joe Kent and beat him again in 2024 [S36].

She calls herself "a Democrat… Maybe I'm an old school one" and says "the party needs to change," not her [S35]. In November 2025 she authored a resolution reprimanding a fellow Democrat, Rep. Chuy Garcia, over the timing of his retirement; the Standard described it as crossing up her party leaders, and it passed 236-183 largely on Republican votes [S35]. In the initial August 4 primary count she led with 38.90% to Braun's 38.71%, with Democrat Brent Hennrich at 15.30% [S40]. Hennrich has said he will not support her [S37].

## Positions
- **Cost of living and the economy:** "I know what it costs to fill a tank, a prescription, and meet payroll." She pledges to stand up to "any politician, or any party" and says "The President's wars and tariffs have raised costs and hammered small businesses and manufacturers" [S1]. She opposes grocery-chain mergers and "shrinkflation" packaging, and favors "abundant and cheap energy from traditional and renewable sources" [S2].
- **Taxes:** voted against the 2025 tax and spending law (H.R. 1) on passage and final concurrence [S5][S6]. Supported Rep. Thomas Massie's No Capital Gains Tax on Family Farms Act [S39]. Supports reintroducing and expanding the Child Tax Credit [S2]. Has "stayed largely quiet" about Washington's tax on income over $1 million [S39].
- **Spending and deficits:** "Our nation's deficit is worsening and we urgently need to balance our budget." She supported a bipartisan Fiscal Commission Act and a resolution to keep the deficit at or below 3% of GDP [S39]. Says she has "protected funding for schools and roads" and introduced legislation to freeze congressional pay [S1]. Voted against the 2025 Rescissions Act [S30]. She was one of six House Democrats who voted for the November 2025 bill ending the 43-day shutdown, calling the shutdown "a case study in why most Americans can't stand Congress" [S29][S34].
- **Immigration and border:** campaign plank "Safeguarding Our Communities & Securing the Border" calls for resources to keep fentanyl and illegal guns off the streets [S2]. Voted for the Laken Riley Act and the SAVE Act (proof of citizenship to register to vote in federal elections) [S7][S8][S39]. She was one of four Democrats who voted for the 2026 Homeland Security appropriations bill [S9][S39]. Voted against the 2023 Secure the Border Act (H.R. 2) and the 2026 Deporting Fraudsters Act [S11][S10].
- **Public safety and drugs:** would fight the drug crisis "through increased border security, stricter punishments for sale and possession, expanded access to treatment and rehab, and better job opportunities." Favors federal funding for officer and firefighter pay over "unnecessary line items like night-vision goggles" [S2]. Voted for the Keeping Violent Offenders Off Our Streets Act and the D.C. Cash Bail Reform Act, each opposed by most House Democrats [S32][S33].
- **Health care:** pledges to fight for "Medicare, Medicaid, Social Security, affordable prescriptions," and for rural hospitals. Would stop drug companies from blocking cheaper generics [S1]. Wants to "cut out the middlemen who jack up the cost of healthcare" [S2]. Would strengthen Social Security "without raising the retirement age or reducing benefits" [S2]. No position found on single-payer or Medicare for All. The OPB poll coverage lists protecting rural health-care access among her campaign themes [S38].
- **Climate and energy:** says climate change "is real and is man-made," and champions tax rebates for energy-efficiency upgrades for working families [S2]. Voted with Republicans on several energy measures: disapproving California's Advanced Clean Cars II waiver, the Protecting American Energy Production Act and the Unlocking our Domestic LNG Potential Act [S26][S27][S28].
- **Foreign policy and defense:** names "the President's wars" as a cost driver [S1], and the OPB poll coverage lists ending the Iran war among her themes [S38]. Voted for War Powers resolutions directing removal of U.S. forces from hostilities with Iran, in March 2026 (failed) and September 2026 (passed) [S22][S23]. Voted against a June 2026 War Powers resolution on U.S. forces in Lebanon [S24]. Voted for the FY2026 and FY2027 National Defense Authorization Acts, as one of 17 and one of six House Democrats respectively, and for the 2026 Ukraine Support Act [S20][S21][S25]. Braun says she has "been wrong on funding for national defense" [S40].
- **Money in politics and congressional conduct:** her campaign does not take corporate PAC money [S1][S2]. Introduced legislation to ban congressional stock trading and freeze congressional pay [S1]. Voted for the Stop Insider Trading Act [S31]. Endorsed by End Citizens United [S3].
- **Right to repair, trades and housing:** would stop farm- and consumer-equipment makers from forcing owners to pay them for repairs [S2]. She supports apprenticeships, career and technical education and Pell Grants for job training [S2]. Says she is "cutting red tape to build more homes and lower rents" [S1].
- **Voting:** opposes efforts to end vote-by-mail [S1].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- Describes herself as "a pro-choice mom" and says she "will do whatever it takes to prevent a national abortion ban" [S2]. Pamphlet: "a woman's right to make her own medical decisions" [S1]. Her first general-election ad attacked Braun's abortion record [S37].
- Voted against the Born-Alive Abortion Survivors Protection Act (2025) [S14].
- Voted against the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act in 2023 (H.R. 734) and 2025 (H.R. 28) [S13][S12].
