## Background
John Braun (Prefers Republican Party) has been the state senator for the 20th Legislative District since 2013. He is Senate Republican Leader, a former chair of the Ways and Means Committee and a budget negotiator [S1][S5]. The 20th District covers most of Lewis and Cowlitz counties, the northern tip of Clark County and a southern section of Thurston County [S13]. He is a retired U.S. Navy captain with 31 years of service and six commands in the submarine force, and president of Braun Northwest, a Chehalis emergency-vehicle manufacturer with about 350 employees [S1][S3]. He holds a B.S. in electrical engineering from the University of Washington and an M.B.A. and M.S. in manufacturing engineering from the University of Michigan [S1]. He does not have to give up his Senate seat to run for Congress [S13].

He launched his campaign in August 2025 [S13]. President Donald Trump endorsed him in April 2026, as did House Speaker Mike Johnson and Majority Leader Steve Scalise [S14]. In the initial August 4 primary count he was second with 38.71%, just behind Gluesenkamp Perez's 38.90% [S18]. An OPB/DHM poll released October 1 found the race within the margin of error, at 48% Braun and 47% Gluesenkamp Perez once undecided voters who leaned one way were counted [S21].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** says he "opposed the new state income tax and higher federal taxes" and that in Congress he will "fight to lower taxes" [S1]. Calls the 2026 tax on income over $1 million an income tax that will not stay limited to millionaires, calls it unconstitutional, and hopes the courts overturn it [S16]. Says a 9.9% tax on income over $1 million would also hit many LLCs and S corporations and would be "devastating" for Washington businesses [S19]. Has defended the 2025 federal tax and spending law (H.R. 1) for its tax and benefit-program changes, and criticizes Gluesenkamp Perez for voting against it [S13][S17].
- **Spending:** pledges to "reduce unnecessary spending, cut waste and fraud, and hold government accountable for how it uses your money" [S1]. Says Olympia has "an unsustainable spending problem" and that "You can't solve a spending problem with new revenue" [S16]. Says he would cut wasteful spending that feeds inflation [S2].
- **Immigration and border:** says he entered the race focused on border security and fentanyl. He would complete high-priority barriers, hire and equip more agents, tighten asylum loopholes and go after cartel supply chains [S2]. Faults Gluesenkamp Perez for voting against the 2023 Secure the Border Act (H.R. 2) [S2]. Trump's endorsement cited his advocacy for border security [S14].
- **Public safety:** would "pair compassion with consequences": shut down open-air drug markets, support local camping bans when shelter is available, step up fentanyl interdiction and fund detox and mental-health beds [S2]. As Senate leader he backed criminal penalties when children are harmed by fentanyl exposure [S3].
- **Energy and climate:** would "champion market-based production including hydropower, solar, oil, natural gas and nuclear power." He cites AI data centers and population growth as drivers of energy demand and says the U.S. must never again depend on "foreign despots" for energy [S2]. Polling coverage lists expanding domestic energy production among his main themes [S21].
- **Technology and AI:** frames AI data-center power demand as a reason to expand energy production; no AI or platform-regulation position found [S2].
- **Defense and veterans:** would support "a lethal, modern force", faster care and benefits for veterans, and rebuilding the defense industrial base [S2]. Says Gluesenkamp Perez has "been wrong on funding for national defense" [S18].
- **Guns:** opposed Washington's 2023 rifle ban and pledges to defend the Second Amendment [S2].
- **Salmon:** would fund sea-lion removal on the Columbia, hatcheries, culverts and habitat [S2].
- **Health care and entitlements:** pledges to protect Social Security and Medicare [S1]. As Senate leader he backed charity-care protections for small and rural hospitals [S3]. No position found on ACA subsidies or Medicaid levels.
- **Politics and coalitions:** describes governing as "listening, negotiating, building coalitions" and says he has advanced "conservative principles while working across the aisle" [S1]. His campaign reports contributions from the Emmer Majority Fund, Rep. Steve Scalise's Eye of the Tiger PAC and Caterpillar Inc. PAC [S17]. No position found on PAC money, stock trading or term limits.

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- The Washington State Standard describes him as "a consistent anti-abortion vote since winning his Senate seat in 2012." It reports he voted against legislation on emergency care for pregnant women and on requiring health plans to cover birth control [S15].
- At a March 2026 Pacific County Republican meeting he said of Washington's abortion law, "Candidly, we are unlikely to change the law in Washington in the near future, but we should be focused on winning hearts and minds so that someday we get there." His campaign manager confirmed this reflects his position and said abortion is "rightly a state issue and not a federal one" [S15].
- Supports federal protections such as the House-passed Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act and says he will "vote to keep women's sports based on biological sex" [S2].

### Parents and schools
- Pledges to restore "parents' rights in education" [S1]. Supports a federal Parents Bill of Rights and notes that Gluesenkamp Perez voted against the House bill [S2].
- Voted for Initiative 2081, the parents' rights initiative to the Legislature, which passed the Senate 49-0 in March 2024 [S12]. In April 2025 he voted against ESHB 1296, "Promoting a safe and supportive public education system," which passed the Senate 30-19 [S11].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no explicit "vote yes" statement found as of 2026-10-08. He backed the repeal effort's route to the ballot: "I think it's important for the people to get their chance at direct democracy… they're anxious to get a chance to say no" [S20]. He voted against the tax that I-645 would repeal [S6].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no public position on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. Related: he supports a federal Parents Bill of Rights and voted for I-2081 in 2024 [S2][S12].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. Related: he supports the federal Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act [S2].

## Record
- **ESSB 6346 (2026 tax on income over $1 million):** voted nay on Senate final passage (2026-02-16, passed 27-22) and on final passage as amended by the House (2026-03-11, passed 27-21). He voted for three Republican floor amendments that failed [S6].
- **2025 tax package:** voted nay on final passage of ESSB 5814 (sales tax on select services and nicotine products, 2025-04-24, 26-22) and ESSB 5813 (higher capital gains and estate tax rates, 2025-04-24, 27-21) [S7][S8]. His campaign calls the 2025 package "the largest in state history" [S3].
- **2021 capital gains tax (ESSB 5096):** voted nay on Senate passage and on the conference report (both 25-24) [S9].
- **2021 Climate Commitment Act (E2SSB 5126):** voted nay on Senate passage (25-24) and on final passage as amended by the House (27-22) [S10].
- As Ways and Means chair he led the 2017-19 operating budget, which fully funded basic education under the McCleary ruling. His campaign says the budget's property-tax changes cut taxes for 73% of residents, and it credits him with the 2015 College Affordability Program, which cut public-university tuition [S3][S13].
- Elected Senate Republican Leader in December 2020 and unanimously re-elected in 2024 [S3]. As leader, the Senate Republican Campaign Committee under his guidance backed 2024 legislative candidates some MAGA-aligned Republicans opposed [S13][S14].
