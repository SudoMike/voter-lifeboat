## What the office does

The U.S. Representative for Washington's 3rd District votes on federal law, taxes, spending and oversight for Southwest Washington. The district appears on the ballot in Clark, Cowlitz, Lewis, Pacific, Skamania, Thurston and Wahkiakum counties [S1]. The term is two years [S2].

## Race dynamics

Two-term Democratic incumbent Marie Gluesenkamp Perez, an auto-shop co-owner from Skamania County, faces Republican John Braun, the state Senate Republican Leader from the 20th Legislative District [S1]. Gluesenkamp Perez won the seat in 2022 over Joe Kent and beat him again in 2024 [S5]. In the initial August 4 primary count she led narrowly, 38.90% to Braun's 38.71%, with Democrat Brett Hennrich third at 15.30% [S3]; Hennrich later said he would not support her [S6]. The district voted for Trump three times by single-digit margins, Cook Political Report rates it a toss-up, and an OPB/DHM poll in late September found the race within the margin of error, 48% Braun to 47% Gluesenkamp Perez with leaners counted [S4]. Her first general-election ad attacked Braun's abortion record [S6]. The candidates were scheduled to debate on KOIN on October 7 and KATU on October 13 [S5].

## Differentiating issue axes

- taxes: Braun's long record of votes against state tax increases (the 2021 capital gains tax, the 2025 package, the 2026 tax on income over $1 million) and his defense of the 2025 federal tax law (H.R. 1), versus Gluesenkamp Perez's votes against H.R. 1, her support for an expanded Child Tax Credit and a family-farm capital gains exemption, and her silence on the state income tax.
- spending: both campaign as deficit-conscious. Braun pledges to cut waste and spending; Gluesenkamp Perez backs a fiscal commission and a 3%-of-GDP deficit cap but voted against the 2025 rescissions bill. Not a clean contrast.
- immigration: Braun's border-enforcement platform versus Gluesenkamp Perez's mixed record: for the Laken Riley Act, SAVE Act and 2026 DHS appropriations, against H.R. 2 (2023) and the Deporting Fraudsters Act.
- social: Braun's anti-abortion state Senate record and support for sex-based sports eligibility, versus Gluesenkamp Perez's pro-choice stance and votes against federal sports and gender-care criminalization bills (with a vote for barring Medicaid funding of gender transition for minors).
- climate: Braun's all-of-the-above energy platform and 2021 vote against the Climate Commitment Act, versus Gluesenkamp Perez's statement that climate change is man-made alongside votes with Republicans on several fossil-fuel and vehicle-emissions measures.
- defense: both back defense spending (Braun's "lethal, modern force"; Gluesenkamp Perez's votes for both NDAAs and Ukraine aid), but she also voted for War Powers limits on the Iran war.
- safety: both mix enforcement and treatment ("compassion with consequences" versus "stricter punishments" plus treatment); she voted for several Republican crime bills.
- reform: Gluesenkamp Perez's refusal of corporate PAC money and her stock-trading ban and pay-freeze bills; no comparable Braun position found.
- experience: both have records. Braun has 13 years in the state Senate, including as caucus leader, plus 31 years of Navy service; Gluesenkamp Perez is a two-term incumbent who presents herself as an outsider to party leadership.
- Parental rights in schools (Braun's Parents Bill of Rights support and I-2081 vote; her vote against H.R. 5) is recorded in the dossiers but not scored for a Federal contest.
