## What the office does
The auditor runs elections and voter registration, records property documents, issues marriage licenses, handles vehicle licensing and keeps the county's financial information (S1). Auditor Greg Kimsey has held the office since 1999 and is not seeking reelection. In his pamphlet letter he notes that Clark now has more than 360,000 active voters, runs all-mail elections with 22 ballot boxes, and that all 22 recounts since 1998 confirmed the original result (S2).

## Race dynamics
This is an open seat. Deputy county auditor and reporting and analysis manager Mitchell Kelly (29.15%) and former County Council chair Eileen Quiring O'Brien (28.74%) edged Vancouver Councilor Ty Stober and state Rep. Sharon Wylie in the election-night primary count (S4). O'Brien, a longtime Republican endorsed by the Clark County Republican Party, pledges a Day-1 independent third-party audit of the whole office, including voter rolls, and proactive eligibility cross-checks (S3; eileen-quiring-o-brien.md S6, S9). Kelly is a 10-year office insider endorsed by The Columbian and labor groups, and Kimsey informally supports him. He stresses internal controls, explaining the process to counter misinformation, and the legal limits on removing voters (S3; mitchell-kelly.md S11, S12). O'Brien has raised about $49.6k to Kelly's $26.9k (eileen-quiring-o-brien.md S5; mitchell-kelly.md S6). A Clark County Today story examined Kelly's use of the "deputy county auditor" designation, which about 40 county employees hold (mitchell-kelly.md S10).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Spending/accountability:** O'Brien centers her campaign on an outside audit of every division and a search for "waste, fraud and abuse". Kelly frames stewardship through internal controls and financial reporting.
2. **Election administration:** O'Brien proposes structural changes to how the office checks itself and is open to moving away from vote-by-mail. Kelly defends the existing procedures and the legal limits on voter-roll removal. This is the race's sharpest divide, but it does not map onto the rubric's `reform` axis (money and power in politics), so it is not scored there.
3. **Record vs. renewal:** an inside successor against a former council chair and legislator running as "fresh eyes". Experience separates them less cleanly than the usual incumbent-versus-outsider race.
4. Taxes, housing, safety and climate do not arise in this administrative race.
