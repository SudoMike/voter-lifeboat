## Background

Mitchell Kelly, 44, of Hazel Dell, is a first-time candidate who has worked in the Clark County Auditor's Office for 10 years: three as controller and the past seven as reporting and analysis manager [S1][S12]. He was earlier a financial analyst at Southwest Washington Behavioral Health and an assistant state auditor with the Washington State Auditor's Office, auditing local governments in Southwest Washington [S1][S4]. He holds a B.S. in accounting from Brigham Young University and an MBA from Arizona State University, and is a lifelong Clark County resident [S1][S4]. He is a former president (2020-2022) of the Southwest Washington Finance Officers Association [S4].

His pamphlet lists "Deputy County Auditor" as his first credential [S1]. Clark County Today reported that the designation is conferred by oath from the elected auditor, that about 40 county employees hold it, and that Kelly's oath was dated February 9, 2026. County HR records list his classification since 2019 as administrative services manager [S10]. Auditor Greg Kimsey said Kelly's job title "has not changed." Kelly said he did not intend to imply he was the sole deputy and that he could have described the role more clearly at a July forum [S10].

In the election-night count of the August 4 primary he placed first with 29.15%, just ahead of Eileen Quiring O'Brien (28.74%) [S9]. Kimsey has not formally endorsed him but says he encourages anyone who asks to vote for Kelly [S12].

## Positions

### Elections
- Lists accurate voter registration, secure ballot processing, bipartisan observation and "transparent auditing and reconciliation processes" as the basis of election integrity [S3].
- Wants to counter "misinformation and disinformation" by explaining each step of the election process, for example through short videos, and to keep improving camera surveillance at ballot drop boxes [S12].
- Says federal and state law limit how voters can be removed from the rolls: a voter can be removed only after ballots are returned undeliverable twice, and marked inactive only after four years without voting [S12].
- Says the auditor's role "is not political — it is procedural" and that the job is to "faithfully administer the law" [S3].

### Finances and administration
- Says he has spent 15 years as "a nonpartisan government finance professional focused on responsible stewardship of taxpayer resources" [S1].
- Cites Government Finance Officers Association reporting awards for the county's 2024 annual and popular financial reports as work done under his leadership [S4].
- Promises "continuous improvement": staff training, regular procedure reviews and "investment in secure and modern election infrastructure" [S3].
- Says the office "should continue to be led by someone with the experience" the position requires, and that he would not need on-the-job training [S2][S3].

## Endorsements

The Columbian's Editorial Board recommended him for the general election, citing "his professionalism, demeanor and commitment to election integrity" [S11]. In the primary it recommended Kelly or Ty Stober [S7]. Other endorsers: the Columbia Pacific Building Trades, the Southwest Washington Central Labor Council and Vancouver Firefighters IAFF Local 452 [S2]. Individual endorsers include Clark County Council Chair Sue Marshall, state Rep. Paul Harris, Washougal Councilor Ernie Suggs, Clark Regional Wastewater District Commissioner Norm Harker and former County Commissioner Marc Boldt [S12]. His site also lists county Finance Director Mark Gassaway, Camas Mayor Steve Hogan and retired Judge John Hagensen [S5].

## Campaign finance

PDC summary as of its October 4, 2026 update: $26,856.00 raised, $15,722.16 spent, $500 in loans [S6]. The Columbian reported that about 78% of his contributions came from individuals, plus the Vancouver Firefighters Union and the Southwest Washington Central Labor Council [S12].

## Scoring notes

- Experience: the institutional-continuity candidate. He has 10 years inside the office and Kimsey's informal support, though he has never held elected office.
- Spending: frames the job around internal controls, auditing and stewardship of taxpayer money, without O'Brien's "waste, fraud and abuse" framing or her outside-audit pledge.
- Reform: defends existing election procedures and the legal limits on voter-roll removal. O'Brien proposes outside audits and proactive roll cross-checks.
- No taxes, housing, safety or climate evidence; this is an administrative office.
