## Background

Scott G. Weber, 54, was elected Clark County Clerk in 2010 and reelected in 2014, 2018 and 2022 [S1][S8]. He has lived in Clark County for more than 25 years [S1]. Before becoming clerk he worked in manufacturing, where he developed and implemented Lean systems to increase efficiency [S1]. He holds a degree in applied arts and sciences [S1]. He has served on the Veterans Court board and the Evergreen School District truancy board, and as treasurer of the Washington State Clerk's Association [S1]. The office is nonpartisan and the ballot lists no party preference [S1].

## Positions

### The clerk's office and the courts
- Describes the clerk as an elected constitutional office that is "the administrative and financial officer for the Superior Court," and says "the Clerk must be independent to maintain the integrity of the court records" [S1][S2].
- Says keeping court records open and accessible is an essential function of the office [S1].
- The Columbian reports he points to his administrative record and digital modernization as evidence of a stable office, in a race that has become a debate over taxpayer-funded court technology, office accessibility and help for self-represented filers [S8].

## Record

- Clerk since his 2010 election [S1].
- Says he deployed an independent imaging system that let the Clark County court keep working in 2024 while the statewide system was down and most counties were seriously affected [S1].
- Says his office improved restitution collection for crime victims and added real-time public access to documents and electronic filing, "saving citizens legal costs" [S1].
- Context: in July 2025, Presiding Superior Court Judge Derek Vanderwood asked the County Council to send voters a proposal making the clerk an office appointed by the judges, or possibly by the council, arguing it would make the court system more efficient. Weber was out of town. After public comment from other elected officials, the council took no action and referred the question to the Charter Review Commission [S9].

## Endorsements

The Columbian's Editorial Board recommended him for the general election [S7]. In the primary it recommended either Weber or Rachel Shapiro [S5]. His campaign endorsements page lists Auditor Greg Kimsey, Assessor Peter Van Nortwick, retired Superior Court Judge Ed Poyfair, former Clark County Clerk Jo Anne McBride-Holtmann, Rep. Paul Harris, current and retired clerks from Cowlitz, Lewis, Kittitas and Skamania counties, and others. Some endorsers appear under titles from earlier years, such as Jaime Herrera Beutler as U.S. Representative and Chuck Atkins as Clark County Sheriff [S3].

## Campaign finance

His PDC registration uses mini reporting, so no contribution totals are filed. The PDC summary records $2,404.30 in independent expenditures supporting him, as of its September 17, 2026 update [S4].

## Scoring notes

- Experience: 16-year incumbent running on his administrative and technology record [S1][S8]. Shapiro has never held public office.
- Court technology: Weber cites the independent imaging system as a resilience asset [S1]. Shapiro calls the county's separate OnBase system duplicative spending (see rachel-shapiro.md).
- He offers no new programs; his platform is continuity and the record above [S1][S2].
