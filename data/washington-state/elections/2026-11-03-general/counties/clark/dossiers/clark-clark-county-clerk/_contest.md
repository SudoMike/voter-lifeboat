## What the office does
The clerk is an elected office created by the state constitution and serves as the Superior Court's administrative and financial officer. It receives, processes and permanently keeps the records of every Superior Court case (criminal, civil, family law, probate, guardianship, adoption and juvenile) and manages case-related deposits and disbursements (S1). In 2025 the presiding Superior Court judge asked the County Council to let voters make the clerk an appointed office. The council left the question to the Charter Review Commission (S3).

## Race dynamics
Scott Weber, clerk since 2010, faces mediator and attorney Rachel Shapiro. A third primary candidate, Gerald Gray, did not advance (S1; data/washington-state/elections/2026-08-04-primary/counties/clark/dossiers/clark-clark-county-clerk/_contest.md). The Columbian describes the race as a debate over taxpayer-funded court technology, office accessibility and how much help residents should get when filing on their own (S2). Weber points to his record: an independent imaging system that kept the court running during the 2024 statewide outage, e-filing and online records, and better restitution collection. Shapiro would replace the county's OnBase imaging system with the state-provided Odyssey system to cut what she calls duplicative costs. She also proposes a courthouse legal aid department, a Lawyer of the Day program, a dispute-resolution center and monthly open office hours (scott-g-weber.md S1; rachel-shapiro.md S5, S6, S7, S8). The Columbian's editorial board recommended either candidate in the primary and Weber in the general (scott-g-weber.md S5, S7). The Clark County Democratic Party recommends Shapiro (rachel-shapiro.md S10). Neither campaign has raised much: Weber files under PDC mini reporting and Shapiro reported $3,484.66 raised (scott-g-weber.md S4; rachel-shapiro.md S11).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** a 16-year incumbent against a challenger who has held no public office beyond precinct committee officer.
2. **Spending/accountability:** Shapiro pairs cuts to duplicative technology spending with new grant-funded access programs. Weber stresses efficiency gains already made. Neither offers a clear restraint-versus-expansion position, so the axis separates them only weakly.
3. Taxes, housing, safety, climate and reform do not arise in this administrative race.
