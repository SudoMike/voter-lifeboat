## What the office does
The five-member Clark County Council is the county's legislative body under its home rule charter: it adopts the budget and property-tax levies, sets councilmanic taxes and fees, adopts the comprehensive plan and land-use rules for unincorporated areas, and decides which county propositions go to voters [S4]. The District 2 seat is nonpartisan [S1]; the district covers much of the unincorporated area north of Vancouver, including Felida, Lake Shore, Hazel Dell and Walnut Grove [S3].

## Race dynamics
Incumbent Michelle Belkot, first elected in 2022, won 51.18% of the election-night primary vote; teacher and Vancouver Education Association representative John Zingale was second with 34.87% [S2]. This is the race's clearest split on county revenue: Belkot cast the only vote against the 1% general-fund levy increase in the 2026 budget and the only vote against the 0.1% housing sales tax, and runs on "No New Taxes" (michelle-belkot.md S2, S4, S5). Zingale says skipping the allowed 1% property-tax increase contributed to the county deficit and that "we do need to find ways to increase revenue" (john-zingale.md S3). Belkot opposes Vancouver annexation and light-rail taxes or tolls and prioritizes law enforcement before other spending; Zingale prioritizes roads, parks, public safety, housing and social services and frames homelessness mainly as an affordability problem (michelle-belkot.md S1; john-zingale.md S1, S3). Belkot voted with the full council to place the Proposition 12 jail bond and Proposition 13 levy lid lift on the ballot (michelle-belkot.md S6). Zingale is endorsed by Council Chair Sue Marshall, Councilor Wil Fuentes, Democratic legislators and labor (john-zingale.md S4).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** recorded "no" votes and a "No New Taxes" platform vs. support for using the 1% levy increase and raising revenue.
2. **Spending:** core services and law enforcement before any program growth vs. investment in social services, parks and infrastructure.
3. **Public safety and homelessness:** law-enforcement-first framing vs. a "holistic," affordability-centered approach.
4. **Housing:** opposition to impact fees and new costs vs. "up, not out" density and affordable housing.
5. **Record vs. renewal:** a first-term incumbent vs. a first-time candidate.
