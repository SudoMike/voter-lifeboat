## Background
John Zingale, 45, is a middle-school history teacher of more than 13 years and the 2026 Southwest Washington Teacher of the Year [S1][S6]. He is a Vancouver Education Association union representative, a board member of the Northwest Council for Computer Education and the Washington State Council for the Social Studies, and a member of the Clark County Historic Preservation Commission [S1][S5]. Before teaching he spent 16 years in grocery retail, rising to assistant store director [S1]. He holds a Master's in Teaching from the University of Portland and a bachelor's in history from Northern Illinois University [S1]. He has not held elected public office [S1].

He finished second in the August 4 primary with 34.87% in the election-night count [S5]. District 2 covers much of the unincorporated area north of Vancouver, including Felida, Lake Shore, Hazel Dell and Walnut Grove [S6].

## Positions
- **Taxes and revenue:** says not using the 1% annual property-tax increase allowed by state law has contributed to the county's deficit, that the cost per household would be minimal, and that "We don't need to exercise this every year, but we do need to find ways to increase revenue" [S3]. Pamphlet calls for "not wasting taxpayer dollars" [S1].
- **Spending:** wants government to invest "in strong social services" and maintain infrastructure [S1]; names roads, parks, public safety and housing as his spending priorities and says continued budget cuts threaten essential services [S3]. Would explore a voter-approved countywide metropolitan park district to fund regional parks [S3].
- **Housing and growth:** wants to protect natural spaces "while building more housing people can afford" [S1]; favors building "up, not out," using existing infrastructure and a mix of housing sizes [S3]; wants people to be able to stay in their homes [S2].
- **Homelessness and behavioral health:** says the county should take a "holistic approach," that data show affordability is the main cause of homelessness rather than drug use, and that he would press state lawmakers to fund opening the mental-health facility on 134th Street [S3].
- **Public engagement:** would hold forums in cities around the county rather than expecting residents to travel to council meetings [S3].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** pamphlet pledges to defend "fundamental rights" without specifying; no specific position found as of 2026-10-08 [S1].
- **Parents and schools:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
