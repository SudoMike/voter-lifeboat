## Background
Michelle Belkot is the incumbent Clark County Councilor for District 2, first elected in November 2022 and in office since January 2023 [S3]. Her council assignments include the Board of Health, C-TRAN, the Law Enforcement and Fire Fighters Disability Board, the Public Safety Sales Tax Funding Board and the Jail Remodel Steering Committee [S1]. She served a combined 25 years in the U.S. Air Force (master sergeant) and U.S. Navy (lieutenant) and more than 20 years as a civil servant in procurement of construction and engineering contracts [S1]. She holds an M.A. from Webster University and a B.A. from George Fox University and is a Hudson's Bay High School graduate [S1].

She led the August 4 primary with 51.18% in the election-night count, ahead of John Zingale (34.87%) and Martin Pittioni (13.95%) [S15].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** "I have consistently opposed new and unnecessary taxes" [S1]; campaign platform is "No New Taxes" and "Stop new taxes" [S2][S3]. Her campaign says she voted against property-tax increases every year, "including standing alone against the 1.8% levy increase in the 2026 budget" [S3] (Clark County Today reported the 2026 general-fund vote as a 1% increase plus banked capacity [S4]).
- **Spending:** says county government must prioritize law enforcement, fire protection and infrastructure "before expanding programs or increasing spending" and must manage existing resources before asking taxpayers for more [S1][S2]. Before the housing sales-tax vote she asked how the money would be tracked and requested a work session first [S5].
- **Housing:** says she is committed to reducing housing costs [S1]. On impact fees: "I've never supported impact fees to begin with" [S11].
- **Public safety:** says she has a record of supporting law enforcement and fought to fund more deputies [S1][S2]. "I stand with law enforcement—local, state, and federal" [S14].
- **Annexation and transportation:** opposes unwanted annexation by Vancouver; opposes taxes and tolls to subsidize light rail on the Interstate Bridge replacement, noting voters "rejected it three times," and calls for "practical transportation solutions" [S1][S2].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Parents and schools:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **County measures:** moved the July 21, 2026 resolution placing Proposition 12 (the jail and justice-system bond) on the ballot and seconded the Proposition 13 levy lid lift resolution; both carried "All aye" [S6][S7]. No separate campaign statement on them or on the charter amendments found.

## Record
- **2026 property tax (Dec. 2, 2025):** the only vote against the 1% general-fund levy increase ("It's a bad day for Clark County"); abstained on the banked-capacity votes [S4].
- **0.1% housing and behavioral-health sales tax (Jan. 6, 2026):** the lone no vote in a 4-1 decision, saying the county lacked a spending plan [S5]: "You can't reverse a tax once you implement it" [S17].
- **Propositions 12 and 13 (July 21, 2026):** moved approval of separate-business item 1 and seconded item 2; per the county these were the $366,204,000 bond for jail expansion, courtrooms and a sheriff's headquarters, and the levy lid lift to $1.15 per $1,000 for criminal-justice staffing [S6][S7].
- **Comprehensive plan (Apr. 28, 2026):** voted with Yung and Little for the planning commission's preferred alternative keeping urban-growth-area expansions under study [S8].
- **Manufactured-home-park moratorium (Oct. 14, 2025):** voted to extend the redevelopment moratorium (4-0) [S9].
- **Impact fees (Sept. 15, 2026):** voted against raised school impact fees for three districts, against new fire-district impact fees and against higher land-use and habitat review fees [S11].
- **Light rail (July 1, 2026):** the only councilor opposing the resolution asking that Interstate Bridge light rail extend to Library Square: "I don't personally support this" [S10].
- **C-TRAN:** the council removed her from the C-TRAN board in early 2025; a federal judge dismissed her lawsuit against the county over the removal in March 2026 [S12][S10].
- **PDC complaint:** in June 2026 the chair of the 18th District Democrats filed a PDC complaint alleging incomplete financial-affairs and campaign disclosures; Belkot said she had responded to the PDC, and the outcome was pending as of the report [S13].
- **Forums:** declined the League of Women Voters forum of Sept. 23, 2026, citing the League's alignment with groups she said vilify federal law enforcement [S14].
