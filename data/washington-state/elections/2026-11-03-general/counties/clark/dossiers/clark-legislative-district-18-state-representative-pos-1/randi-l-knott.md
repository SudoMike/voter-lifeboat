## Background
Randi L. Knott (Prefers Democratic Party) is a first-time candidate [S1]. She reports more than 25 years in public, private and nonprofit work "evaluating policy's financial impact on working families," including work with cities and local governments on disaster response, public safety, land use, housing and community services [S1][S4]. The Columbian reports she is 58 and came to the Pacific Northwest from California in 2022 after a nearly three-decade career evaluating public policy impacts on working families [S7]. She has owned and operated a bed-and-breakfast and a horse ranch in Brush Prairie [S1][S4]. She serves on the Clark County Developmental Disabilities Advisory Board (a County Council appointment), previously served more than a decade on the State Council on Developmental Disabilities, and sits on the boards of Joint4ces and Transitional Living and Community Support [S1][S4]. She holds an MPA from the University of Southern California and a BA from Cal State Northridge [S1]. She describes herself as an "Autism Mom" and advocate for neurodivergent people and their families [S1][S4].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** at the Ridgefield forum she said "I believe the ultra wealthy need to be taxed, especially in a time where our schools are not doing as well as they should" [S6]. Her pamphlet statement pledges to oppose "policies that shift costs onto families while protecting the wealthy and big corporations" [S1]; her platform similarly opposes policies that shift costs onto families "while protecting the wealthiest few" [S3]. She called the millionaire tax bill "drafted very well" [S6].
- **Spending and services:** calls for "fully funded special education," support for educators and paraeducators, expanded career and technical education, and "infrastructure investments that keep pace with growth" [S3]. Describes her approach as "economically progressive policies that are fiscally responsible" [S3]. On transportation she called for prioritizing road projects and examining additional revenue sources; on federal health funding cuts she supported restoring Medicaid and Apple Health funding; on the budget she proposed alternative funding sources, mentioning Colorado's professional-sports funding model for school sports [S6].
- **Housing and land use:** wants to make housing "more attainable through thoughtful planning and responsible growth," with "land-use policies that reflect community needs, not speculation" [S3]; pamphlet pledges to "make housing more affordable" [S1].
- **Wages and jobs:** supports policies that raise wages, protect working people and attract higher-paying jobs [S1][S3].
- **Health care and disability:** says "healthcare is a human right" and supports "universal healthcare solutions that reduce cost and complexity" and protections for people with disabilities and caregivers [S3].
- **Elections:** will defend vote-by-mail and accessible voting and supports "electoral reforms that strengthen trust and participation" [S3].
- **Artificial intelligence:** said the state could act temporarily to protect children if federal action does not occur [S6].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** platform says she will "stand firm on inclusion, civil rights, and human dignity" [S3]; no specific position found on sports eligibility, gender-affirming care or abortion.
- **Parents and schools:** no position found on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs; her education platform focuses on special-education funding and trade pathways [S3].
