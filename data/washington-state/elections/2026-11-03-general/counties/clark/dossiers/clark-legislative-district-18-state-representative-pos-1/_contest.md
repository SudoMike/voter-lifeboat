## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House, for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets: K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The 18th District lies in Clark County; the general election decides the seat between the two candidates who advanced from the August primary [S1].

## Race dynamics
Two-term Republican Rep. Stephanie McClintock is defending the seat against Democrat Randi L. Knott, a first-time candidate [S1][S2]. McClintock, 56, is a former Battle Ground School Board member and ranking Republican on the House Consumer Protection and Business Committee; Knott, 58, spent nearly three decades evaluating the effects of public policy on working families before coming to the Pacific Northwest in 2022 [S1][S2]. The Columbian editorial board recommends re-electing McClintock [S4].

The clearest split is taxes. McClintock voted against ESSB 6346, the 2026 tax on income over $1 million (passed 51-46) [S5], and said at an October forum that she opposes "the income tax"; Knott said at the same forum that "the ultra wealthy need to be taxed" [S3]. McClintock's record also includes votes against the 2025 business-and-occupation and transportation revenue bills and both operating budgets of the biennium, and co-sponsorship of the 2025 bill allowing sex-based eligibility in girls' school sports (stephanie-mcclintock.md S6-S9, S14). Knott's platform calls health care a human right and backs universal health-care solutions and fully funded special education (randi-l-knott.md S3). Neither candidate has stated a position on Initiatives 645, 1 or 638 as of 2026-10-08. PDC summaries show McClintock with $268k raised and Knott with $173k (stephanie-mcclintock.md S18; randi-l-knott.md S8).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** recorded votes and a stated position against the tax on income over $1 million vs. support for taxing the ultra-wealthy.
2. **Spending:** votes against both operating budgets and a "reckless spending" message vs. calls for fully funded special education and infrastructure investment.
3. **Public safety:** McClintock runs on law-enforcement funding, police pursuit and sex-offender registration bills; Knott has not stated a public-safety position beyond her career work.
4. **Healthcare:** Knott supports universal health-care solutions; McClintock has no stated position on coverage models.
5. **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** McClintock co-sponsored a girls'-sports eligibility bill; Knott's platform speaks of inclusion and civil rights without a specific policy.
6. **Record vs. renewal:** two-term incumbent and former school director vs. first-time candidate.
