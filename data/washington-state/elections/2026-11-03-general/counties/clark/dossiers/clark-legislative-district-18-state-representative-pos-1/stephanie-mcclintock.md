## Background
Stephanie McClintock (Prefers Republican Party) is the incumbent 18th District representative in Position 1, first elected in 2022 [S1]. The Columbian describes her as a two-term representative, age 56, who came to the Pacific Northwest from California as a college student in the mid-1990s [S17]. She is ranking member of the House Consumer Protection and Business Committee, assistant ranking member of Capital Budget, and serves on Agriculture and Natural Resources and on the Joint Legislative Audit and Review Committee [S1][S4]. She served on the Battle Ground School Board from 2013 to 2017 [S1][S3] and chaired the Clark County Republican Party in 2012 [S3]. She works in public affairs for United Grain, is a former small-business owner and human-resources professional, and holds a business management degree from Concordia University [S1][S3].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** pamphlet pledges to "continue fighting policies that raise taxes" and "protect your hard-earned income" [S1]. At an October 2026 Ridgefield forum she said: "I oppose the income tax that we spent over 24 hours debating on the House floor when the Democrats brought that forward" [S15]. Her campaign site says the state has a surplus and "no plans to provide badly needed tax relief" and that she worked "to advance policies that will keep our taxes from increasing" [S2].
- **Spending:** pamphlet pledges to "oppose reckless spending" and calls for "accountability" [S1]; her site calls for "accountability for the tax dollars that fund our public schools" [S2]. Named schools, special education, Medicaid and transportation as budget priorities at the Ridgefield forum [S15].
- **Public safety:** describes herself as the daughter of a police detective who supports law enforcement and wants to "close dangerous loopholes in our sex offender system" [S1]. Her site says law enforcement is "being handcuffed by legislative overreach in Olympia" and cites restoring police pursuit [S2]; her About page says "we must fully fund law enforcement and enforce our laws" [S3].
- **Education:** pledges to "stop unfunded mandates on K–12 education and support reforms to improve outcomes" [S1]; wants students proficient in reading, writing and math and "robust career and technical education" [S2][S3].
- **Transportation:** "Our roads do not look good. And we've got a number of bridges that need work" [S15].
- **Artificial intelligence:** said differing state AI regulations create challenges for businesses [S15].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** co-sponsored HB 1699 (2025), which would let school districts and the state athletic association prohibit "biologically male students from competing with and against female students" in sex-separated athletics [S13][S14]. No statement found on abortion or gender-affirming care.
- **Parents and schools:** her About page says that as a school director she "was a champion for increasing parental involvement" in district decisions [S3]. She voted no on ESHB 1296 (2025), which amended the parents' and guardians' rights enacted in 2024 by Initiative 2081 [S11][S12]. Prime-sponsored HB 1122 (2025, restricting student mobile-device use) and HB 1637 (2025, non-firearm school safety measures) [S13].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Related: voted against ESSB 6346, the tax I-645 would repeal, and says she opposes it [S5][S15].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08. Related: co-sponsored HB 1699 on sex-separated school athletics [S14].

## Record
- Voted Nay on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the 2026 tax on household income over $1 million (March 9, 2026; passed 51-46); voted Yea on the motion to postpone the bill indefinitely (failed 38-58) and offered floor amendment 2581, which failed 42-52 [S5].
- Voted Nay on ESHB 2081 (2025 business and occupation tax increases; passed 50-48) and ESSB 5801 (2025 transportation revenue package; passed 51-47) [S6][S7].
- Voted Nay on the 2025-27 operating budget, ESSB 5167 (conference report passed 52-45), and the 2026 supplemental operating budget, ESSB 5998 (passed 54-43) [S8][S9].
- Voted Nay on final passage of EHB 1217, the 2025 limit on residential rent and fee increases (passed 54-44) [S10].
- Voted Nay on ESHB 1296 (2025; final passage 59-39) [S11].
- Prime sponsor of HB 2506 and HB 2507 (2026, sex offender registration compliance and information), HB 1289 (2025, public school survey information; reached House Rules) and SHB 1121 (work rules for 16- and 17-year-olds, enacted as chapter 79, Laws of 2025) [S13].
- Co-sponsor of HB 2130 (repeal of taxes enacted in ESSB 5814, 2025), HB 2335 (repeal of 2025 B&O tax increases), HB 2167 (sales tax reductions), HB 1225 (budgeting process) and HB 1255 (funding removal of encampments) [S13].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-06): $268,355.00 in contributions and $42,357.77 in expenditures [S18].
