## Background
John Ley (Prefers Republican Party) has represented the 18th District in Position 2 since 2025 [S1]. He serves on the House Transportation, Environment and Energy, and Postsecondary Education and Workforce committees and on the Joint Oregon-Washington Legislative Action Committee [S1][S6]. His pamphlet lists experience as a Delta Air Lines captain and federal flight deck officer, a U.S. Air Force instructor pilot, flight commander and public affairs officer, a reporter for Clark County Today, a small-business owner, chairman of Air Line Pilots Association Council 124, and state committeeman for the Clark County Republican Party [S1]. At the Ridgefield forum he cited 11.5 years in the Air Force [S5]. He holds a B.S. from Portland State University [S1]. The Columbian reports he is 70 [S19].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** pamphlet: "End state income tax" and "exempt $250,000 from taxation" of a home's value to address property taxes [S1]. His campaign platform says "I oppose an income tax in Washington state" and "We need tax cuts, not tax increases!" [S2]. In an October 2026 op-ed he argues the Legislature will lower the $1 million threshold of the new income tax and urges a yes vote on I-645: "That is the clean repeal" [S4]. At the Ridgefield forum he argued the millionaire tax could eventually reach more residents [S5]. At a May 2025 town hall he called the 2025 session's revenue package "the largest tax increase in the history of the state of Washington" [S20].
- **Spending:** proposes to cap state budget growth to median household income and population, audit state programs to eliminate redundant positions, and return excess revenue; says "Washington does not have a revenue problem—it has a spending problem" [S3]. Pamphlet: "Demand value for taxpayer dollars" and "Focus on improved learning, not administration" [S1].
- **Transportation and the I-5 bridge:** "No tolls. No $3.5 billion light rail. We need more Columbia River bridges"; says "$2 billion builds bridge without tolls" [S1]. The Columbian reports he opposes the current Interstate 5 Bridge replacement plans and wears a "no tolls" tie [S19]. At the Ridgefield forum he proposed directing sales tax on new vehicle purchases to transportation [S5].
- **Climate and energy:** pamphlet: "Lower cost of living by ending carbon tax" [S1]. His platform backed 2024 initiatives to repeal the carbon tax (I-2117) and to stop restrictions on natural gas (I-2066) [S2]. On data centers he spoke of balancing power and resource demands against job creation, citing Quincy [S5].
- **Housing and land use:** at the Ridgefield forum he said "the best investment the state could make would be modifying the Growth Management Act to allow small families, smaller acreages" [S5]; ties housing affordability to property taxes [S1].
- **Public safety:** "Make neighborhoods safer. Fund more police. Stop 'catch and release'" [S1]. His platform supports Washington's "three strikes" law, jail for drug dealers and long-term treatment for people with addiction [S2]. Pamphlet also calls for funding Brockmann Campus staffing for mental-health treatment [S1].
- **Pensions:** pamphlet calls to "reverse government employee pension theft" [S1]; his op-ed says the Legislature "stripped eleven longstanding protections from retirement income" [S4].
- **Direct democracy:** "Support citizen initiatives" [S1]; his platform says three initiatives he supported became law in 2024 [S2].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** co-sponsored HB 1038 (2025), which would prohibit puberty blockers, cross-sex hormones and gender transition surgeries for minors, and HB 1699 (2025), which would let school districts and the athletic association prohibit "biologically male students from competing with and against female students" in sex-separated athletics [S15][S16][S17].
- **Parents and schools:** pamphlet: "Restore parental control over children's education" [S1]. His platform lists establishing "a parents bill of rights" among initiatives he supported [S2]. Voted no on ESHB 1296 (2025), which amended the parents' and guardians' rights enacted in 2024 by Initiative 2081 [S13][S14]. Co-sponsored HB 1140 (2025, education savings account scholarships) and HJM 4013 (2026, urging the governor to opt Washington into the federal K-12 tax-credit scholarship program) [S15][S18]. At the forum he said "educating our kids and funding it is the Legislature's job" [S5].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** supports; urges a yes vote [S4].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Related: campaigns to "restore parental control over children's education" [S1].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Related: co-sponsored HB 1699 on sex-separated school athletics [S16].

## Record
- Voted Nay on House final passage of ESSB 6346, the 2026 tax on household income over $1 million (March 9, 2026; passed 51-46); voted Yea on the motion to postpone the bill indefinitely (failed 38-58) and offered floor amendments 2547 and 2550, which failed [S7].
- Voted Nay on ESHB 2081 (2025 business and occupation tax increases; passed 50-48) and ESSB 5801 (2025 transportation revenue package; passed 51-47) [S8][S9].
- Voted Nay on the 2025-27 operating budget, ESSB 5167 (conference report passed 52-45), and the 2026 supplemental operating budget, ESSB 5998 (passed 54-43) [S10][S11].
- Voted Nay on final passage of EHB 1217, the 2025 limit on residential rent and fee increases (passed 54-44) [S12].
- Voted Nay on ESHB 1296 (2025; final passage 59-39) [S13].
- Prime sponsor of HB 1559 (2025, tolling on I-5 Columbia River bridges), HB 2030 (2025, interstate bridge finances), HB 2669 (2026, performance audit of the Interstate Bridge Replacement project), HB 2710 (2026, requiring joint Oregon-Washington committee approval of certain bridge contracts), HB 2670 (2026, gross misdemeanor penalties) and HB 2180 (2026, coaches of youth sports); none advanced from committee [S15].
- Co-sponsor of HB 2130 (repeal of taxes enacted in ESSB 5814, 2025), HB 2335 (repeal of 2025 B&O tax increases), HB 2743 and HB 2746 (state property tax reduction), HB 1225 (budgeting process), HB 2076 (government efficiency department), HB 1255 (funding removal of encampments) and HB 1324 (transportation funding and the Climate Commitment Act) [S15].
- PDC (updated 2026-10-07): $97,185.00 in contributions and $28,022.59 in expenditures [S21].
