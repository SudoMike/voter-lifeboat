## Background
Deken Letinich (Prefers Democratic Party) is assistant political and legislative director of the Washington/Northern Idaho District Council of Laborers and a former construction laborer [S1]. He attended Clark College, completed a registered apprenticeship, and has served on the executive board of LiUNA Local 335 since 2020 [S1]. He has served on the Columbia River Mental Health Board since 2025 [S1][S5]. His campaign says his family has lived in Clark County for three generations and that he was named Southwest Washington Labor Council Union Member of the Year in 2018 [S2][S5]. He has not held elected office; his pamphlet lists "Working Laborer fighting for better policy for Clark County" under elected experience [S1].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** at the Ridgefield forum he said he supports taxing income above $1 million and opposes lowering that threshold [S6]. His priorities page says the state should improve its tax code, "ensuring that the extremely wealthy pay their fair share and the burden on middle-class families is reduced" [S3].
- **Spending and public investment:** wants smaller class sizes, more after-school programs, more counselors and psychologists, and more education funding for Southwest Washington to stop staff layoffs [S3]; wants to expand Pre-K and "restore and advance" the Early Childhood Education and Assistance Program [S3]; pledges to "secure much needed funding" for new infrastructure and maintenance [S3]. At the forum he said lawmakers must examine individual programs before deciding how to replace lost federal funding [S6].
- **Transportation and the I-5 bridge:** pamphlet: "It's time to build the bridge" [S1]. Says he is committed to being "a steward" of the Interstate 5 Bridge replacement as it breaks ground [S3]; The Columbian reports he cites the economic infusion the project would bring [S7].
- **Housing and childcare:** pamphlet: "build more housing and build more childcare facilities" [S1]. Endorsed by the WA Housing Alliance Action Fund [S4].
- **Labor:** "I 100% support the right of workers to unionize" [S3]; says he fought in 2024 for workplace safety and access to health-care products on worksites [S2][S5].
- **Health care:** supports funding clinics in underserved areas, health-care workforce training, and "stronger negotiations with the Big Pharmaceutical drug companies" to lower prescription prices [S3].
- **Public safety:** says there is "a clear need for necessary and appropriate funding for our police, firefighters, and emergency medical professionals" [S5].
- **Data centers:** said "there needs to be some type of reinvestment in the community" where data centers operate [S6].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** pamphlet: "I'm Pro-Choice" [S1]. No position found on sports eligibility or gender-affirming care.
- **Parents and schools:** no position found on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs; his education plank focuses on funding, class size and staffing [S3].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
