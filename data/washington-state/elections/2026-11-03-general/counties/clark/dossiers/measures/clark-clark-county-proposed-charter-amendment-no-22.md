## What it does
The ballot title says the amendment "clarifies if two or less county candidates file for a race in the Primary, that race will bypass the Primary and proceed directly to the General Election," that county nonpartisan elections follow state-law procedures for nonpartisan elections, and that they occur in even-numbered years [S2]. The explanatory statement names the offices: Council members, Assessor, Auditor, Clerk, Sheriff, Treasurer and Prosecuting Attorney [S1].

**Who proposed it.** The Clark County Charter Review Commission, fifteen commissioners elected in November 2025 (three per council district) for 2026-2027 terms, proposes charter amendments; any amendment must be approved by Clark County voters at a general election [S4]. The adopting resolution is No. 26-01, listed on the county's election page as amended [S3]; the commission held a special meeting on July 29, 2026 to amend it [S4].

## Cost
No tax or rate in the ballot title; no fiscal statement was published [S1][S2]. Supporters argue it saves printing and mailing costs; no dollar figure is given [S1].

## Arguments for
The statement in favor (Cathie Garber, chair; Jennifer Wendel; Dorothy Gasque) says [S1]:
- City, fire-district and school-board races already skip the primary with one or two candidates; state law does the same for nonpartisan races.
- "In 2021, more than 64% of voters chose to make all county offices nonpartisan," but every county candidate still appears on the primary ballot.
- "Five races on your current ballot had two or fewer candidates file but still appeared on the primary ballot."

## Arguments against
The statement against (Ann Donnelly, chair; Peter Silliman; Liz Cline) says [S1]:
- Voters would lose the primary pamphlet's months of candidate information for offices covering elections, public safety, justice and assessment.
- Skipping the primary favors incumbents with name recognition.
- Embedding even-year elections in the charter limits later reconsideration.

## Endorsements and campaign
