## What it does
The ballot title says Proposition No. 11 would authorize the district "to levy the following excess taxes, replacing an expired levy," for programs not funded by the state, "including student safety, smaller classes, special education, reading and math support, curriculum, student activities, and preparing students for postsecondary education, employment, or military" [S2]. The board adopted Resolution No. I-26 [S2][S3]. The explanatory statement says it replaces the educational programs and operations levy "that expired in 2025" [S1].

## Cost
- Estimated $1.76 per $1,000 of assessed value: $37,025,000 (2027), $38,690,000 (2028), $40,430,000 (2029) [S2], $116,145,000 over three years [S1].
- The statement against says a home assessed at $650,000 would pay about $1,144 a year [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces
- The district's previous EP&O levy expired at the end of 2025 [S1][S5].
- In April 2025 the district asked for a four-year replacement levy at an estimated $1.95 per $1,000 ($38.3 million in 2026 rising to $44.975 million in 2029) [S5]; it failed, 49.05% yes [S6]. A February 2025 district proposition (No. 8) also failed, 49.85% yes [S7].
- The statement against says the levy "has failed 3 times in the last 2 years" and that "the last failure had asked for $189,510,000" [S1]. This research did not locate the third result.
- The statement in favor says that if the levy fails "the district is at risk of undergoing financial oversight, giving the state control over all financial decisions" [S1].

## Arguments for
The statement in favor (Terry Dotson, chair; Sabrena Worthy) says [S1]:
- Strong schools produce a competent workforce; it cites research that better-funded schools reduce later arrests and that school spending raises home values.
- "The district listened to community priorities. It responded to calls for lowering the amount."
- "Local dollars = local control."

## Arguments against
The statement against (Richard Rylander, chair) says [S1]:
- This is the fourth attempt; test scores have not improved and enrollment appears to be declining.
- The levy language puts all money in the general fund without spending specifics.
- Local and state school taxes are 48-50% of total property taxes.
The rebuttal for says state law requires levy dollars to go to the audited general fund [S1].

## Endorsements and campaign
Committee for: Terry Dotson, Sabrena Worthy (votebgschools.org); committee against: Richard Rylander (swweducation.org) [S1].

## Lean notes
- `taxes`: a yes vote restores a lapsed operations levy at about $1.76 per $1,000 (lower than the $1.95 the district sought in April 2025). As with other restorations of a lapsed or expiring levy at a similar or lower rate, +1.
- `parental-rights`: the levy funds general operations; nothing in the dossier concerns notification, records or opt-outs. No mapping.
- `spending`: replaces general operating funds rather than expanding programs. No mapping.
