## What it does
The ballot title says the amendment "would limit the number of consecutive terms a County Council member could sit to three terms, with eligibility restored after one full term out of office" [S2]. The explanatory statement adds that all consecutive service, including prior service, counts toward the limit, and that serving more than half a term counts as a full term [S1]. Council terms are four years, so the limit is twelve consecutive years [S1].

**Who proposed it.** The Clark County Charter Review Commission, fifteen commissioners elected in November 2025 (three per council district) for 2026-2027 terms, proposes charter amendments; any amendment must be approved by Clark County voters at a general election [S4]. The adopting resolution is No. 26-20 [S2][S3].

## Cost
No tax or rate in the ballot title; no fiscal statement was published [S1][S2].

## Arguments for
The statement in favor (Brandon Erickson, chair; Dorothy Gasque; Liz Shaw) says [S1]:
- Periodic open seats "encourage a broader field of qualified candidates, giving voters more choices."
- "Twelve years gives Council members ample time to learn the job"; former members may run again after a full term away.
- Changing council positions does not restart the count.
Its rebuttal cites Commissioner Betty Sue Morris serving three consecutive terms (1996-2008) and says staggered terms preserve institutional knowledge [S1].

## Arguments against
The statement against (Morgan Holmgren, chair; Liz Cline; Chuck Green) says [S1]:
- "No Clark County Councilor or Commissioner has served more than two terms in 36 years"; voters already remove officials.
- Term limits reduce institutional knowledge and may push councilors to rely on outside interest groups or executive staff who "never face the voters."
- Limits restrict voter choice and create lame ducks.
