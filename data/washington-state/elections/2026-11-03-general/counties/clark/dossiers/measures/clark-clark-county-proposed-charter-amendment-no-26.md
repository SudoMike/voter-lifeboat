## What it does
The ballot title says the amendment "would reduce the number of signatures necessary for initiatives and referendums from 10% to 8%," remove the separate signature calculation for measures affecting only unincorporated areas, let initiatives without enough signatures possibly become mini-initiatives, and let the Auditor's Office make limited use of statistical sampling to verify signatures [S2][S1].

**Who proposed it.** The Clark County Charter Review Commission, fifteen commissioners elected in November 2025 (three per council district) for 2026-2027 terms, proposes charter amendments; any amendment must be approved by Clark County voters at a general election [S4]. The adopting resolution is No. 26-40 [S2][S3].

**Prior vote.** In November 2022 Clark voters considered Proposed Charter Amendment No. 14, which would also have required signatures of 8 percent and allowed transfer of signatures to a mini-initiative [S5]; it was rejected, 58.98% to 41.02% [S6].

## Cost
No tax or rate in the ballot title [S2]. The statement in favor says the Elections Office expects sampling to reduce staff time and county costs [S1].

## Arguments for
The statement in favor (Cathie Garber, chair; Liz Cline; Dorothy Gasque) says [S1]:
- "Since Clark County adopted home rule in 2014, no citizen petition has qualified for the ballot."
- Valid signatures from a failed initiative could transfer to a mini-initiative once the 3% threshold is met; sampling would cover at least 3,000 signatures, and no petition could be rejected on sampling alone.
The rebuttal says, unlike 2022, the proposal does not put the Prosecuting Attorney's opinion on petitions or ballots [S1].

## Arguments against
The statement against (Janet Landesberg, chair; Chuck Green) says [S1]:
- Voters rejected the same thresholds in 2022 "by 59% of the vote."
- "All signatures should be verified."
- Whatcom and Snohomish lowered thresholds and still had no initiatives reach the ballot.

## Endorsements and campaign
The commission's members are Patrick Adigweme, Ann Donnelly, Eric LaBrant (District 1); Brandon Erickson (chair), Cathie Garber, Kim Hamlik (District 2); Ben Christly, Janet Landesberg, John Jay (District 3); Liz Cline, Dorothy Gasque, Morgan Holmgren (District 4); Brad Benton, Duncan Hoss, Peter Silliman (District 5) [S4].
