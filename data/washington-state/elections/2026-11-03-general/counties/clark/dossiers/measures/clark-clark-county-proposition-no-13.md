## What it does
The ballot title says Proposition No. 13 "would provide funds for public safety and criminal justice services, including additional correction deputies, sheriff deputies, prosecuting attorney staff, and other services," authorizing "a maximum regular property tax levy for collection in 2027 of $1.15 per $1,000 of assessed value, an increase of approximately $0.44 from 2026," with the 2027 amount used to compute later levy limits under chapter 84.55 RCW; qualifying persons are exempt under RCW 84.36.381 [S2]. The Council adopted Resolution 2026-07-09 [S2][S3]. The explanatory statement ties the staffing to the planned jail expansion and names exemptions for qualifying low-income seniors and disabled persons [S1].

## Cost
- County regular levy up to $1.15 per $1,000 in 2027, about $0.44 more than 2026; the higher amount becomes the base for future limits [S2].
- The statement against puts the current rate at $0.71, "62% higher," and says a $600,000 property would pay about $690 in 2027 county property tax, about $264 more [S1]. The statement in favor puts the cost at "approximately $212 per year per median household" [S1].

## Arguments for
The statement in favor (Ann Donnelly, chair; Sue Marshall; John Horch) says [S1]:
- The Sheriff's Office "has one of the lowest deputy-to-population ratios in Washington"; response times have grown and some lower-priority crimes no longer get an in-person response.
- The 42-year-old jail needs more corrections officers to operate safely.
- The levy would fund deputies, corrections officers, prosecutors and support staff.

## Arguments against
The statement against (Margaret Tweet, chair) says [S1]:
- It is "premature to raise taxes to staff a jail expansion that's not yet approved by voters."
- The Council approved a separate 0.1% criminal justice sales tax in April 2026 for officers and court staff.
- Multiple taxing districts' increases add up, and rents rise with property taxes.
The committee's rebuttal for says the opposing statement was written before the committee saw the statement in favor [S1].

## Endorsements and campaign
The committee for the statement in favor includes Sheriff John Horch and Councilor Sue Marshall [S1].

## Lean notes
- `taxes`: a yes vote raises the county's regular levy rate by about 62% and resets the base for future years: a large, permanent increase, +2.
- `spending`: it funds additional deputies, corrections officers and prosecutors, an expansion of staffing beyond current service, +1.
