## What it does
The ballot title says Proposition No. 12 "would allow Clark County to acquire, construct, remodel, and equip public safety and criminal justice infrastructure," including "the remodel and expansion of the Clark County Jail and courtrooms and a new or remodeled Sheriff's Office Headquarters"; issue no more than $366,204,000 of general obligation bonds maturing within 31 years; and levy annual excess property taxes on all taxable property in the county to repay them [S2]. The Council adopted Resolution 2026-07-08 [S2][S3]. The explanatory statement says the money would remodel and increase jail capacity, add courtrooms and build or renovate the Sheriff's Office headquarters, and that the county Budget Office and finance team reviewed fiscal impacts [S1].

## Cost
- Up to $366,204,000 in bonds over up to 31 years, repaid by a new excess property tax levy countywide [S2].
- The statement in favor says "the 30-year bond structure will reduce today's costs to about $86/year per median home" [S1]. No levy rate is stated in the ballot title [S2].

## Arguments for
The statement in favor (Ann Donnelly, chair; Sue Marshall; John Horch) says [S1]:
- The jail, "built 40 plus years ago for a much smaller community," is "overcrowded, outdated and unsafe."
- Expansion would reduce housing inmates in other counties "at higher cost" and add medical, addiction-treatment and reentry services.
- When the jail is full, "offenders may be released sooner than intended."

## Arguments against
None filed: "After recruitment attempts, no person within the jurisdiction volunteered to write a statement against Proposition No. 12" [S1].

## Endorsements and campaign
The committee for the statement in favor includes Sheriff John Horch and Councilor Sue Marshall [S1]. The opposing statement on companion Proposition No. 13 calls it "premature to raise taxes to staff a jail expansion that's not yet approved by voters" (see that measure's dossier).

## Lean notes
- `taxes`: a yes vote adds a new 31-year excess property-tax levy to repay up to $366 million in bonds, a new tax: +2.
- `spending`: it funds a large capital expansion (jail capacity, courtrooms, a Sheriff's headquarters): +2, consistent with other large bond programs.
- `safety` does not apply to measures.
