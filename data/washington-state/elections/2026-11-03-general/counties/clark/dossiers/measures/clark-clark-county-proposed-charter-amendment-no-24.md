## What it does
The ballot title says that before adopting "any ordinance reasonably likely to have a direct and material impact upon residential housing capacity, density, permitting, subdivision requirements, parking requirements, applicable to residential development, or residential construction costs, the County shall publish a Housing Impact Analysis" [S2]. The explanatory statement says the analysis must evaluate housing supply, land availability, permitting efficiency and production costs using accepted planning or economic methods, and applies only to discretionary county-initiated ordinances, not ministerial updates required by state law [S1].

**Who proposed it.** The Clark County Charter Review Commission, fifteen commissioners elected in November 2025 (three per council district) for 2026-2027 terms, proposes charter amendments; any amendment must be approved by Clark County voters at a general election [S4]. The adopting resolution is No. 26-07 [S2][S3].

## Cost
No tax or rate in the ballot title; no fiscal statement was published [S1][S2]. Opponents say the reports would be paid by taxpayers; proponents say a similar Whatcom County requirement needed no added staff or money [S1].

## Arguments for
The statement in favor (John Jay, chair; David Stuebe; Justin Wood) says [S1]:
- It "does not dictate policy outcomes or prevent the County from adopting new regulations"; it ensures officials and the public see housing impacts first.
- It would help "avoid unintended consequences that could raise housing costs."

## Arguments against
The statement against (Chuck Green, chair; Irene Finley; Morgan Holmgren) says [S1]:
- Housing and land-use policy belong to the Council, not the charter; the Development Engineering Advisory Board and Planning Commission already advise on costs.
- Construction costs are driven by interest rates and state and federal rules the county cannot control; more analysis will not fix affordability.

## Endorsements and campaign
The commission's members are Patrick Adigweme, Ann Donnelly, Eric LaBrant (District 1); Brandon Erickson (chair), Cathie Garber, Kim Hamlik (District 2); Ben Christly, Janet Landesberg, John Jay (District 3); Liz Cline, Dorothy Gasque, Morgan Holmgren (District 4); Brad Benton, Duncan Hoss, Peter Silliman (District 5) [S4]. Jay and Holmgren sit on the commission [S4]. David Stuebe, on the committee for the statement in favor, is a candidate for State Representative, 17th District, Position 2 [S1].
