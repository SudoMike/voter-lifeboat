## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House, for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets: K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The November 3, 2026 general election decides the seat between the top two finishers from the August primary.

## Race dynamics
The 20th District covers most of Cowlitz and Lewis counties and parts of Clark and Thurston counties [S3]; on the Clark ballot it reaches the county's northern edge (the pamphlet lists Clark, Cowlitz, Lewis and Thurston as ballot counties) [S1]. Republican Rep. Peter Abbarno of Chehalis, a Centralia attorney and former Centralia City Council member, is seeking a fourth term; Democrat Andy Zahn, 35, a Toutle writer and photographer, is making his first bid for office [S3][S1]. Abbarno led the August primary with 65.04% in the election-night count [S4].

Abbarno says his legislative and business experience sets him apart and that affordability would be his top focus next session; he voted against the 2026 tax on income over $1 million (ESSB 6346, passed 51-46) [S3][S2]. He points to $81 million in 2025 capital-budget money for the district (peter-abbarno.md S15). Zahn runs on rural parks, industrial hemp, hourly I-5 passenger rail and stopping sprawl, wants a moratorium on data centers, calls the tax structure regressive, and refuses corporate contributions; The Daily News reported Abbarno had raised about $171,648 to Zahn's $2,275 at the end of July [S3] (andy-zahn.md S4, S5, S6).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Abbarno voted against the 2026 income tax and the 2025 tax package and campaigns on tax relief; Zahn calls the tax structure regressive and wants more revenue with a lighter burden on middle and lower earners.
2. **AI and tech:** Zahn proposes a data-center moratorium and limits on generative AI; no Abbarno position found.
3. **Record vs. renewal:** three-term House member and caucus leader vs. a first-time candidate.
4. **Money in politics:** Abbarno chairs Cascade PAC and accepts corporate contributions; Zahn refuses corporate money.
5. **Climate and transit:** Abbarno voted against the 2025 clean-fuels expansion; Zahn wants fossil fuels phased out and publicly owned rail.
6. **Parents and schools:** Abbarno voted against the 2025 bills amending the parents' rights law (HB 1296, SB 5181); no Zahn position found.
