## Background
Peter Abbarno is the Republican incumbent in Position 1 of the 20th Legislative District, a state representative since 2021 and a Centralia City Council member from 2015 to 2021 [S1]. He is an attorney and partner at Althauser Rayan Abbarno, LLP (since 2010), with a J.D. from Vermont Law School and a Master of Laws in Taxation from the University of Washington [S1][S4]. He lives in Chehalis, is 51, and is seeking a fourth term [S15]. He is the House Republican Caucus chair and, since July 2026, chairman of Cascade PAC, a political action committee that backs Republican candidates in state and local races [S17]. He serves as assistant ranking member on the House Capital Budget Committee [S15][S3].

In the August 4 primary he led Democrat Andy Zahn with 65.04% of the vote in the election-night count [S16].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** pamphlet pledges "meaningful tax relief" [S1]. His campaign site says he "opposed the largest tax increase in Washington State history in 2025" and in 2026 "opposed the unconstitutional, unpopular, and unnecessary income tax," and that he fights for "relief from unnecessary and burdensome taxes" [S2].
- **Affordability:** says affordability would be his No. 1 focus in the next session and that he will fight policies that drive up costs [S15]. His campaign site cites high gas, grocery, housing, childcare and energy costs [S2].
- **Spending and the capital budget:** describes his focus as "bringing taxpayer dollars back home"; says he helped secure $81 million for the 20th District in the 2025 capital budget, including Woodland and Kalama projects [S15]. His campaign site's issues page (written about the 2021 session) says "My priority is to give the taxpayers their money to spend it on their priorities" [S3].
- **Public safety:** says he supported more law-enforcement funding, stronger penalties for repeat offenders, more victim support and "additional pathways for rehabilitation" in 2025-26 [S2]. His issues page says he opposed 2021 police-reform legislation that in his view made streets less safe [S3].
- **Climate and energy:** his issues page calls 2021 environmental bills (HB 1287, HB 1084, SB 5126, the cap-and-trade bill) measures that "aimed to kill jobs" and describes an amendment he offered to steer cap-and-trade money to workforce programs [S3]. Recent campaign posts discuss home energy-efficiency improvements [S2].
- **Legislative strategy:** at an April 2026 Kelso-Longview Chamber forum he said Republicans worked "with pragmatic Democrats" to stall bills and that "even if we're not in the majority, we can lead" [S15].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Parents and schools:** see Record (votes on HB 1296 and SB 5181). His issues page lists the right "to parent" among constitutional rights he defends [S3].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Related: voted no on the tax (ESSB 6346) and calls it "unconstitutional, unpopular, and unnecessary" [S5][S2].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- **ESSB 6346 (2026 tax on income over $1 million):** voted no on House final passage, March 9, 2026 (passed 51-46) [S5]. Offered floor amendment 2579, requiring annual Department of Revenue reports on income-tax filers, business closures and formations, and charitable giving; not adopted [S14][S5].
- **SB 5814 (2025 excise taxes on select services):** voted no on House final passage, April 23, 2025 (passed 50-47) [S6].
- **SB 5167 (2025-27 operating budget):** voted no on House passage (March 31, 2025) and on the conference report (April 27, 2025) [S7].
- **EHB 1217 (2025 rent-increase limits for tenants):** voted no on House passage and on the conference report [S8].
- **HB 1296 (2025, student and parental rights in public schools) and SB 5181 (2025, amending the parents' rights initiative):** voted no on final passage of both [S9][S10].
- **HB 1409 (2025 clean fuels program changes):** voted no on House passage and final passage [S11].
- **SB 5855 (2026, face coverings for law enforcement officers):** voted no on House final passage, March 3, 2026 [S12].
- **HB 1644 (2025 contractor child-labor penalties):** voted no in the House [S20].
- **Sponsored bills (2025-26):** prime sponsor of HB 1016 (employer tax incentives for supporting veterans and military families), HB 1025 and HB 1026 (long-term-care trust exemption and benefit sharing), HB 1055 (public-records access), HB 1990 (utility securitization of disaster costs), HB 1039 and HB 1064 (enacted 2025), and HB 2430 (crime-victim penalty assessment) [S13][S2].
- **Recognition:** his campaign site lists the Public Works Board's 2025 "Infrastructure Champion Award" and Farm Bureau "Friend of the Farm" recognition (2022-2025) [S4].
- **Campaign finance:** PDC reports $147,378 in contributions and $167,384 in expenditures (updated 2026-10-04) [S19]. Donors include T-Mobile and Amazon.com [S15].
