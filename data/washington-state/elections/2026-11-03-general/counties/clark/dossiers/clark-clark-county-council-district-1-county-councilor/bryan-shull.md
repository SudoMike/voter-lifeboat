## Background
Bryan Shull, 56, of Vancouver, was born and raised in Clark County and graduated from Hudson's Bay High School [S1][S4]. He owns Trap Door Brewing, the Prairie View Station food cart park and Cruise the Couve, a 501(c)(6) nonprofit, and was a general contractor (Performance Energy) [S1][S3]. He was operations manager at REC Solar, managed solar projects totaling more than 12 megawatts, worked as a utility energy-program engineer and did renewable-energy research and development for the California Energy Commission [S1][S3]. He holds a B.S. in environmental and renewable energy engineering with a minor in economics from California State University, Sacramento [S1].

He is president of the Uptown Village Association, a member of the Washington Hospitality Association government affairs committee and a board member of the Camas-Washougal Chamber [S1]. The Columbian described him as a political newcomer [S5]. He finished second in the August 4 primary with 29.03% in the election-night count [S6].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** supports a "tax-last" approach, "focusing on responsible budgeting before asking taxpayers to pay more" [S1]. Says "Tax increases are too often a short sighted strategy and have become a financial burden on our community" and wants to reduce the impact of taxes on vulnerable and fixed-income households [S2]. At launch said "continuous tax increases and overbearing bureaucracies are making it harder for families and small businesses to thrive" [S3].
- **Spending:** "We must address out of control spending" [S2]; supports reprioritizing funding to core county services and reducing or eliminating unnecessary fees [S3].
- **Housing:** wants to address "runaway costs of building affordable housing" through fees and land-use changes, and to cut permit fees and wait times by consolidating or eliminating duplicate permit paths [S2][S3].
- **Growth:** supports "balanced growth policies" [S1]; wants to work with Vancouver on planned annexations so the county bears net-zero costs from growth and infrastructure transfers, and to protect rural and agricultural land where appropriate [S2][S3].
- **Public safety and homelessness:** supports law enforcement and first responders and coordinating resources with the Sheriff's Office and Vancouver Police [S2]. Would "confront the unsuccessful strategy of housing first and harm reduction with an increased focus on treatment and accountability" and prioritize resources for those who accept them [S2]; pamphlet calls for "compassionate but accountable approaches to homelessness and addiction" [S1].
- **Energy and environment:** supports "affordable, reliable renewable energy solutions" [S1]; "Nothing is more damaging to the environment than the apathy of poverty ... Affordable, clean energy is essential" [S3].
- **Transportation:** supports a "multiple bridge approach" to congestion and full transparency on who bears Interstate Bridge Replacement costs [S2][S3].
- **Small business:** says small businesses should not be treated "like ATM machines" [S2][S3].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Parents and schools:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
