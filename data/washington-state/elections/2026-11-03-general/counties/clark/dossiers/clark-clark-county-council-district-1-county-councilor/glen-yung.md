## Background
Glen Yung is the incumbent Clark County Councilor for District 1, finishing his first term [S1]. He serves on the Clark County Board of Health, the Ending Community Homelessness Organization (ECHO), the Mental Health Sales Tax Advisory Board, the Opioid Abatement Council, the Discovery Clean Water Alliance, the C-TRAN board and the Regional Disaster Preparedness Organization [S1]. After a career in banking and finance, he and his wife started a family remodeling business; he attended Mt. Hood Community College and has been a licensed foster and respite home for children [S1].

He led the four-way August 4 primary with 36.65% in the election-night count, ahead of Bryan Shull (29.03%), Dusti Arab (24.11%) and Lukas Bardue (10.22%) [S15]. The seat is nonpartisan [S1].

## Positions
- **Taxes and county revenue:** at the December 2025 budget vote, said "The same level of services is becoming more and more expensive and the revenue is not rising to the level the expenses are" [S5]. Supported passing the 0.1% housing sales tax immediately because "we are experiencing a housing crisis" and said he trusted policy on spending it would follow [S6][S7].
- **Spending and accountability:** says he helped spearhead a formal performance review process for the county manager and supports "more transparency, better long-term planning, and responsible budgeting" [S4][S3].
- **Housing:** lists housing affordability as a top issue; says he initiated a pause on mobile-home-park redevelopment, helped pass rules allowing duplexes, townhomes and accessory units in more neighborhoods, supported down-payment assistance, and pushed for faster, more predictable permitting [S3][S4]. Pamphlet says he "championed new protections to preserve naturally affordable housing" for mobile-home-park residents [S1]. On impact fees: "I don't like impact fees because they create burdens on housing costs for everyone" [S14].
- **Public safety:** says he invested in law enforcement and justice-system resources, approved body-worn cameras, and backs "a balanced approach that addresses both crime and the root causes behind it" [S3][S4].
- **Homelessness and addiction:** secured funding for a 120-bed shelter (under construction) with integrated addiction and mental-health treatment, in collaboration with the City of Vancouver; chairs a regional homelessness coordination group [S1][S4].
- **Growth and transportation:** lists "smart growth" and transportation "accessible for all" among campaign themes [S2]. Said that if light rail is built on the Interstate Bridge replacement it should run to a hub at Library Square rather than end at the waterfront [S13].
- **Environment:** supported clean-water infrastructure and Vancouver Lake restoration [S1][S4].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.
- **Parents and schools:** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- **2026 property tax (Dec. 2, 2025):** voted with the 4-1 majority for the 1% general-fund levy increase plus banked capacity, and for 1% increases to the road and conservation-futures levies; the 2026 budget passed 5-0 [S5].
- **0.1% housing and behavioral-health sales tax (Jan. 6, 2026):** voted with the 4-1 majority for the councilmanic tax under RCW 82.14.530, about $6.5 million a year in unincorporated Clark County; Belkot voted no [S6][S7].
- **Propositions 12 and 13 (July 21, 2026):** both resolutions carried "All aye," Yung present [S8]; per the county they authorize a bond up to $366,204,000 for jail remodel and expansion, courtrooms and a sheriff's headquarters, and a levy lid lift to $1.15 per $1,000 of assessed value for criminal-justice staffing [S9]. The Columbian reported the bond vote as unanimous [S10].
- **Comprehensive plan (Apr. 28, 2026):** voted with Belkot and Little for the planning commission's preferred alternative, which keeps urban-growth-area expansions under study; Fuentes and Marshall voted no, citing farmland and sprawl [S11].
- **Manufactured-home-park moratorium (Oct. 14, 2025):** recused from the discussion and 4-0 extension vote, citing a family member who owns a manufactured home [S12].
- **Impact fees (Sept. 15, 2026):** voted for the school-district impact-fee plans and against new impact fees for three of four fire districts, objecting to their methodology [S14].
- **Light rail (July 1, 2026):** chaired the Council Time meeting and brought a Vancouver-requested resolution asking that any Interstate Bridge light rail extend to Library Square; it advanced 4-1 with Belkot opposed [S13].
