## What the office does
A Washington State Representative is one of two House members (plus one Senator) elected from each legislative district to the 98-member State House, for a two-year term. Representatives write and vote on state law and the operating, capital and transportation budgets: K-12 funding, the state tax structure, housing and land-use rules, health funding and criminal-justice statutes. The 17th District ballot appears in Clark, Klickitat and Skamania counties [S1].

## Race dynamics
Republican Rep. David Stuebe, who is also mayor of Washougal, is seeking a second term [S4]. He won the open seat in 2024 with 50.4% to Democrat Terri Niles' 49.43%, a margin of under 900 votes [S3]. He voted against the 2026 tax on income over $1 million (ESSB 6346) [S2] and cites that vote in his pamphlet statement [S1]. His challenger is Democrat Diana H. Perez, a Vancouver city councilor since 2022 and retired Forest Service biologist and manager [S1][S4]. The Columbian's editorial board recommends Perez and calls the two candidates "disparate" [S5].

Stuebe has a House voting record: no votes on ESSB 6346, the 2025 capital gains, excise and B&O tax bills, the 2025-27 operating budget, rent-increase limits, the clean fuels expansion and ESHB 1296, and a floor amendment to cut the state sales tax from 6.5% to 5% (david-stuebe.md S3-S11). In a March 2026 op-ed he called for no new taxes, a two-thirds vote to raise them and tying budget growth to inflation and population (david-stuebe.md S15). Perez has no legislative record; on the Vancouver council she cast the only vote against a 0.1% public-safety sales tax in August 2026, citing the cumulative tax burden (diana-h-perez.md S8). Her platform combines more state investment in affordable housing with faster permitting, and supports the clean-energy transition with an affordability and grid-reliability caveat (diana-h-perez.md S3). Perez reports about $226k raised to Stuebe's $124k (diana-h-perez.md S10; david-stuebe.md S20).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Taxes:** Stuebe opposes new taxes, voted against every 2025-26 revenue bill reviewed and proposed a sales-tax cut; Perez has no stated position on the state income tax but opposed a local sales tax.
2. **Spending:** Stuebe says the state has a spending problem and wants budget growth capped; Perez pairs "living within our budgetary means" with more state investment in housing, schools and infrastructure.
3. **Climate and energy:** Perez backs the clean-energy transition with cost caveats; Stuebe voted against the clean fuels expansion and counts blocking a data-center energy tax as a win.
4. **Housing:** Perez mixes public investment and deregulation; Stuebe emphasizes regulatory relief and backs a county housing-impact-analysis charter amendment.
5. **Record vs. renewal:** a first-term legislator and sitting mayor vs. a city councilor and former state commissioner.
6. **Parents and schools / gender and reproductive policy:** Stuebe voted against ESHB 1296; Perez's council opposed I-1 and I-638 by resolution, but no individual statement by either candidate was found.
