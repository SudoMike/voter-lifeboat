## Background
David Stuebe is the Republican incumbent in Position 2 of the 17th Legislative District and also serves as mayor of Washougal [S1][S19]. He lists service as a U.S. Marine Corps colonel and a bachelor's degree in political science [S1]. Legislative communications identify him as R-Washougal [S14].

He won the seat in 2024 with 50.4% to Democrat Terri Niles' 49.43% [S18]; Republican Paul Harris had held Position 2 after the 2022 election [S21], so Stuebe is in his first term. In the House he is assistant ranking minority member of Local Government and sits on Environment & Energy, Health Care & Wellness, and Transportation [S2]. He said in January 2026 that he would seek reelection [S19].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** his pamphlet statement says "I voted no on a state income tax because hard working families should keep more of what they earn" and that the region needs leadership "focused on lowering costs, not raising taxes" [S1]. In a March 2026 op-ed he called for "no new taxes," a two-thirds legislative vote to raise taxes, and returning surplus revenue through a Tax Relief Account [S15].
- **Spending:** says "Washington does not have a revenue problem; we have a spending problem" and calls for tying budget growth to inflation and population growth [S15]. After ESSB 6346 passed he said "Washington doesn't have a revenue problem. It has a results problem" and that government "needs to learn to live within its means" [S14][S16]. Wants programs that fail to deliver a return on investment eliminated or reformed [S16].
- **Housing:** pamphlet lists "expanding affordable housing" among his commitments [S1]. His 2026 update faulted the majority for enacting "no meaningful regulatory relief to accelerate housing construction" [S16]. He is listed on the committee for Clark County Charter Amendment 24, which would require a Housing Impact Analysis before county ordinances affecting housing capacity, permitting or construction costs [S17].
- **Public safety:** pamphlet commits to "strengthening public safety" [S1]. His update counts as wins blocking bills to shorten sentences for serious crimes and to restrict local enforcement of homeless-camping laws [S16].
- **Health care:** calls for restoring Medicaid access and the $782 million cut in 2025, and more support for rural hospitals [S15]. Sponsored HB 2531 ("my bill"), signed in 2026, which updates the ambulance quality assurance fee program to maximize federal Medicaid support [S16].
- **Energy and utilities:** counts as wins blocking a utility surcharge for wildfire liability and a $114 million energy tax on data centers [S16].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** no public position found.
- **Parents and schools:** voted against ESHB 1296 (see Record) [S11]. His 2026 update criticized education-budget cuts, including to Running Start and Transition to Kindergarten [S16].

- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no statement on the initiative itself found as of 2026-10-08. Related: voted against the tax and cites that vote in his pamphlet statement [S1][S3].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no public position on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. Related: voted against ESHB 1296, which changed the parental rights enacted by Initiative 2081 [S11][S12].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- **ESSB 6346 (2026, 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** voted yes on the motion to postpone the bill indefinitely (failed 38-58) and no on final passage (passed 51-46) on 2026-03-09 [S3].
- **Amendment 2564 to ESSB 6346:** sponsored a floor amendment that would have reduced the state sales tax from 6.5% to 5% effective July 1, 2028; not adopted, 43-52 [S4][S3].
- **2025 revenue bills:** voted no on final passage of ESSB 5813 (capital gains and estate tax rates, 53-45) [S5], ESSB 5814 (excise taxes, 50-47) [S6] and ESHB 2081 (business and occupation tax, 50-48) [S7].
- **2025-27 operating budget (ESSB 5167):** voted no on House passage (54-44) and on final passage of the conference report (52-45) [S8].
- **EHB 1217 (2025, limits on rent and fee increases):** voted no on House passage (53-42) and on the conference report (54-44) [S9].
- **2SHB 1409 (2025, clean fuels program):** voted no on House passage (54-41) and on final passage as amended by the Senate (54-43) [S10].
- **ESHB 1296 (2025):** voted no on House passage (56-37) and on concurrence (59-39) [S11]. The bill changed the delineated rights of parents and guardians enacted by Initiative 2081 and established a statement of student rights [S12].
- **E2SHB 1163 (2025, permit to purchase firearms):** voted no on House passage (58-38) and on final passage (57-39) [S13].
- **Clark County Charter Amendment 24:** a "David Stuebe" is listed as a member of the committee that wrote the statement for the amendment, chaired by John Jay [S17]. Identity is matched by name and county only.
- **Campaign finance (PDC, updated 2026-10-06):** $124,131.98 raised, $45,763.50 spent [S20].
