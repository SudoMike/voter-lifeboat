## Background
Kevin Waters is the Republican incumbent in Position 1 of the 17th Legislative District (Clark, Klickitat and Skamania counties) [S1]. He was a Port of Skamania commissioner from 2010 to 2019, is executive director of the Skamania Economic Development Council, founded Backwoods Brewing Company and co-founded the Carson General Store, and is a former Skamania County corrections officer [S1]. He holds a bachelor's in communications from Eastern Washington University [S1]. His campaign site describes him as a fourth-generation Southwest Washingtonian who grew Backwoods Brewing to more than 70 employees [S16]. Legislative communications identify him as R-Stevenson [S14].

He won the seat in 2022 with 53.15% over Democrat Terri Niles [S18] and was reelected without an opponent on the ballot in 2024 (93.26%, the rest write-ins) [S19]. In the House he is ranking minority member of State Government & Tribal Relations and sits on Capital Budget, Rules, and Technology, Economic Development & Veterans [S2].

## Positions
- **Taxes:** his campaign site says he will make the region affordable "by cutting taxes and keeping government out of the way of small businesses" [S16]. After the House passed ESSB 6346 he said the bill "doesn't fix what's broken" and sends money into the general fund "without guaranteeing real tax reform or meaningful relief for working people," and warned it "can easily grow into an income tax that touches every family" [S14][S15].
- **Spending:** says "Washington doesn't have a revenue problem; it has a results problem" and criticized the 2026 supplemental operating budget for pushing state spending past $80 billion and relying on one-time fixes [S15]. In the same update he faulted the majority for cutting education by $1 billion after passing an income tax to fund education [S15].
- **Infrastructure and housing:** pamphlet priorities are roads, bridges and broadband, and "practical solutions to expand affordable housing and ease cost pressures" [S1]. His campaign site says he will fix infrastructure holding back the region [S16].
- **Public safety:** supports law enforcement and local safety programs [S1]; his campaign site calls for "fully funding the police" and giving police tools and training to respond to the fentanyl crisis [S16].
- **Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy:** his campaign site, under "Defending Women's Rights," says the majority party politicized voter support for "a woman's right to choose" and that he "will never go against a voter-approved initiative" [S16]. The site's text appears to date from his first campaign (it says he "plans to" represent the district) [S16].
- **Parents and schools:** in his 2026 post-session update he criticized the majority for leaving "$700+ million on the table through an annual federal scholarship fund" and cited declining test scores [S15]. Voted against ESHB 1296 (see Record) [S11].
- **Health care:** his campaign site supports making the state long-term-care program optional [S16].

## Positions on the 2026 initiatives
- **I-645 (repeal of the 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** no public position on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. Related: voted against the tax (ESSB 6346) and sponsored an amendment to it (see Record) [S3][S4].
- **I-1 (parental rights in schools):** no public position on the initiative found as of 2026-10-08. Related: voted against ESHB 1296, which changed the parental rights enacted by Initiative 2081 [S11][S12].
- **I-638 (girls' school sports):** no public position found as of 2026-10-08.

## Record
- **ESSB 6346 (2026, 9.9% tax on income over $1 million):** voted yes on the motion to postpone the bill indefinitely (failed 38-58) and no on final passage (passed 51-46) on 2026-03-09 [S3].
- **Amendment 2591 to ESSB 6346:** sponsored a floor amendment that would have expired all services taxes imposed by ESSB 5814 (chapter 422, Laws of 2025) on July 1, 2026; not adopted, 42-53 [S4][S3].
- **2025 revenue bills:** voted no on final passage of ESSB 5813 (capital gains and estate tax rates, 53-45) [S5], ESSB 5814 (excise taxes, 50-47) [S6] and ESHB 2081 (business and occupation tax, 50-48) [S7]. He offered floor amendment 1333 to ESHB 2081, which failed 45-53 [S7].
- **2025-27 operating budget (ESSB 5167):** voted no on House passage (54-44) and on final passage of the conference report (52-45) [S8].
- **EHB 1217 (2025, limits on rent and fee increases):** voted no on House passage (53-42) and on the conference report (54-44) [S9].
- **2SHB 1409 (2025, clean fuels program):** voted no on House passage (54-41) and on final passage as amended by the Senate (54-43) [S10].
- **ESHB 1296 (2025):** voted no on House passage (56-37) and on concurrence (59-39) [S11]. The bill changed the delineated rights of parents and guardians enacted by Initiative 2081 and established a statement of student rights [S12].
- **E2SHB 1163 (2025, permit to purchase firearms):** voted no on House passage (58-38) and on final passage (57-39) [S13].
- **Campaign finance (PDC, updated 2026-10-07):** $398,697.87 raised, $113,256.81 spent [S20].
