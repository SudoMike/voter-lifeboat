## Background

- Lori Zoller (Prefers Republican Party) is the incumbent Klickitat County Commissioner for District 2, in her first term [S1][S8]. She says former Commissioner David Sauter encouraged her to run for his seat [S2].
- Her statement lists a background in industrial timber, agriculture and cattle; work as a Department of Natural Resources timber sales clerk; administering salmon recovery and watershed planning for the county; and over 40 years owning a recreational business [S1]. She says she has worked "in and around the county" on projects for about 30 years, including organizing the 220 landowners along the 31-mile Lyle-to-Goldendale rails-to-trails route [S2].
- She chairs or has chaired the Southwest Washington Aging and Disability Board, the Mid-Columbia Economic Board, the Columbia Gorge Housing Authority, the Columbia Gorge Regional Airport Board, the Columbia River Policy Advisory Group and the Board of Health; she was a county planning commissioner for 10 years and is co-chair of the Klickitat Community Council [S1].
- She led the August 4, 2026 primary with 1,148 votes, ahead of Andy Kallinen (760) and Gene Klejeski (667), in the District 2 nominating primary [S6].

## Positions

### County budget and spending
- Says the county is "very, very stable in our budget" with good reserves, and that a reorganization done in-house through the county administrator, instead of a consultant estimated at up to $250,000, "made us leaner" by not backfilling positions that came open; no one was laid off [S2].
- Says the county is effectively limiting hiring except for crucial positions [S2]; the board adopted a selective county-wide hiring freeze in December 2025 [S5].
- Wants county government to "go out and find more money, not just sit around and wait," and to reduce dependence on Republic Services' Roosevelt landfill revenue by attracting other industry and small employers [S2].
- Wants the county to explore taking back management of its state timber trust lands, as Hood River County does, after the state raised its management charge to 25% [S2].
- Her statement credits her term with "a balanced and sustainable budget" and reorganizing departments "for cost savings and efficiency" [S1].

### Public safety and the jail
- Her statement credits her term with bringing the Klickitat County Jail into compliance [S1]. She defends the county's takeover of jail operations from the Sheriff's Office after the death of Ivan Howtopat and a $20 million tort claim (settled, she says, for $2 million), says Sheriff Bob Songer took part in the transition, and says the jail administrator has stayed within the original budget and saved over $100,000 on food service [S2].

### Data centers and land use
- Opposed Commissioner Todd Andrews' motion for a six-month data-center moratorium, saying a moratorium would need legal review and would only delay the ordinance the county was already writing [S2][S3]. She prefers clear rules on where data centers may locate before any application arrives, names the former Goldendale aluminum plant as a site that could be considered, and says the tax base "could shore up this county for years" [S2].
- On September 30, 2026 the board, by consensus, directed staff to draft an interim zoning ordinance barring industrial data centers within one mile of residential zones and five miles of incorporated cities [S4].
- Credits her term with moving the county comprehensive plan ("20 years behind"), the critical-areas ordinance and accessory dwelling unit and short-term rental ordinances forward [S1][S2].

### Housing and services
- Hopes the Columbia Gorge Housing Authority can revive a senior assisted-living project in White Salmon on property the county preserved [S2].
- Says the aging and disability board pushed back when the state "tried to take money from the seniors" [S2].

### Transportation, airport and wildfire
- Her statement credits her term with extending public transportation connections [S1]. She wants the Columbia Gorge Regional Airport to become self-sustaining and equipped to load fire retardant, and cites helicopters now stationed there for wildfire response [S2].

### Governance style
- Lists civility and respect as a top priority and describes "data-informed decisions" and collaboration with government and community partners [S1]. In December 2025 she said the board had brought its work "back to civility" [S5].
