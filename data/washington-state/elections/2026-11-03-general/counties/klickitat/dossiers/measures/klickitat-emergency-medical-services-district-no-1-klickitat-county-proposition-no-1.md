## What it does

Proposition No. 1 asks whether Emergency Medical Services District No. 1 may levy "a permanent regular emergency medical services property tax not to exceed $0.50 per $1,000 of assessed valuation" [S1][S2]. The first levy would be made in 2026 for collection in 2027 and then each year with no end date [S2].

- The money may be used only for emergency medical services: personnel, training, service contracts, equipment, supplies, vehicles, facilities [S1][S2][S6].
- The district runs the county's ambulance service: three primary ambulances, one on-call and two back-ups, and passes retired ambulances to the 15 fire departments in the district [S10]. In 2024 it described a service population of 21,000, about 1,400 square miles and about 2,417 911 responses a year [S4].
- The district covers almost the whole county: 16,105 of the county's 16,421 registered voters (2025) [S9]. The Bickleton area is outside it (see the package's COMPLETENESS.md).

## Cost

- Up to $0.50 per $1,000 of assessed value a year, permanently [S1][S2]. At the full rate that is $250 a year on a $500,000 property (computed for this dossier).
- The district's 2026 levy is $0.48306752 per $1,000 on $5.30 billion of assessed value, raising $2,559,987.93 [S5]. So the proposition does not raise the maximum rate; it removes the expiration date.

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

| Vote | What voters approved | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Nov. 2018 | Reauthorize the regular EMS levy (six years) | Yes 7,601, No 2,603 (74.5%) [S8] |
| Nov. 2024 | Renew the levy at $0.50 or less for six years, collected from 2025 [S4] | Yes 9,253, No 3,288 (73.78%) [S7] |
| Nov. 2026 (this measure) | Make the levy permanent at up to $0.50 [S1] | |

- Without this measure, the 2024 renewal runs through 2030 collection [S4]; a later renewal would need another vote.
- RCW 84.52.069 allows six-year, ten-year or permanent EMS levies up to $0.50 [S6].
- **Approval threshold.** RCW 84.52.069(2) says a permanent levy "must be specifically authorized by a majority of at least three-fifths" of voters, with a turnout validation requirement; only subsequent six- or ten-year renewals need a simple majority [S6]. The district's resolution says the measure "shall require approval of a simple majority of voters" under RCW 84.52.069(2) [S2]. This dossier records the discrepancy; the threshold applied will be the county canvassing board's call.
- **Referendum and accounting.** A permanent levy must come with a referendum procedure (a petition of 15% of registered voters can refer the levy resolution to the ballot) and separate accounting of the money, updated at least every two years [S6]. The resolution's text does not mention either [S2].

## Arguments for

No statement for was filed: the district sought committee volunteers and none came forward [S1][S2]. The explanatory statement says the levy would fund ambulances, facilities, equipment and trained emergency medical personnel [S1][S3]. In 2024 the district argued that the levy and patient billing fund its operations and planned a fourth ambulance for peak demand and permanent stations [S4]. A district column says a new four-wheel-drive ambulance costs about $389,000 to put in service [S10].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed and no committee is listed [S1][S3]. No news coverage of this measure was found as of 2026-10-08. The structural objection on the record is the statute's own: a permanent levy ends the six-year re-votes that this district has passed twice with about 74% [S6][S7][S8].

## Endorsements and campaign

None found. The 2024 renewal had a pro committee (Heiko Stopsack, Patty Elkins) [S4]; none is listed for 2026 [S3].

## Scoring notes

- Taxes: a yes vote keeps an existing EMS levy at the same maximum rate but with no end date. It is a continuation, not a new tax, and it removes future renewal votes.
- Spending: funds existing ambulance service; no expansion is described in the ballot material.
