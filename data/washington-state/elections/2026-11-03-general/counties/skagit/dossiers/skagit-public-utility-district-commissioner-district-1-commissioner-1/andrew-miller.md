## Background

Andrew Miller is the incumbent Skagit PUD commissioner for District 1. He has held the seat since 2020 [S1][S8] and won it in the 2020 general election [S7]. His campaign says he is president of the PUD board [S4]. He is a Skagit County native and Air Force veteran, and CEO of United Way of Skagit County. He is a board director of the Skagit Valley Chamber and runs Tulip Valley Farms with his wife, Holly [S1]. His campaign lists his Air Force work as combat aircrew and later cyber warfare, intelligence and Latin America foreign area officer. It also lists past roles leading global teams at Amazon, Expedia and Weyerhaeuser, director of business and expansion at the Economic Development Alliance of Skagit County, professor at Western Washington University, and attorney [S3][S4]. He holds a J.D. from Seattle University, an MBA from Pacific Lutheran University, and master's degrees from Gonzaga University and the Naval Postgraduate School [S1][S4]. The office is nonpartisan [S1].

## Positions

### Water rates and affordability
- "Safe, affordable water is the PUD's most important promise to its customers" [S1].
- Pledges to use "every funding tool available so the cost of modernization never falls solely on our ratepayers," naming state and federal grants, partnerships and "innovative financing" [S1][S3].

### Water supply, farms and rivers
- Says the board "forged a durable water-sharing agreement that keeps Skagit farms irrigated through summer droughts while protecting the instream flows our rivers and salmon depend on" [S1][S4].
- Says he has introduced a first-ever draft Water Priority Policy to the board. The policy would formally recognize agricultural water as the primary use for transfers of untreated water rights [S5].
- His campaign says "Climate change is already reshaping Skagit County's water reality," citing declining snowpack and longer summer droughts [S3].

### Data centers and battery storage
- At the county's July 14 hearing on its data center moratorium, Miller said: "I am personally opposed to the development of large-scale data centers in Skagit County, as they are fundamentally at odds with the county's commitment to protecting farmland and sustaining a strong agricultural economy" [S9].
- His campaign FAQ answers "No" on both data centers and battery energy storage systems. It says storage facilities "should never be permitted near residential areas, farmland, or waterways" [S5].

## Record

- Elected PUD Commissioner Position 1 in 2020 [S7].
- Board actions he cites as the board's record: navigating rising costs, the 2023–2027 Strategic Plan, and the water-sharing agreement for farm irrigation [S1][S4]. His FAQ also cites replacing aging water main, finishing the Judy Reservoir transmission line, and bringing fiber internet to Concrete, Lyman, Hamilton and Marblemount [S5].
- The PUD's water-rights transfer pilot, which lets the utility lease water rights to agricultural users from May through October, began in 2024 and runs through 2026. Before the pilot, transfers were allowed only during state-declared droughts [S10]. In January 2026 the board renewed the agreements for the 2026 irrigation season ($5,510 per participant for 300 acre-feet) and approved a $183,736 design contract for replacement backwash pumps at the Judy Reservoir treatment plant [S11]. These are board actions; the coverage does not record individual commissioners' votes [S11].
