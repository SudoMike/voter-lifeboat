## Background

- Danny Hagen (Prefers Independent Party) is the incumbent Skagit County Assessor. He was raised on Bow Hill and in La Conner and worked eight years in the Assessor's Office before being elected Assessor in 2022 [S1].
- In the 2022 general election he defeated Karie Storle (Prefers Republican Party), 54.76% to 45.11% [S8].
- He is a Professional Member of the International Association of Assessing Officers, President of the Washington State Association of County Assessors and 2nd Vice President of the Washington Association of County Officials [S1]. His campaign says he has completed more than 200 hours of continuing education and is a candidate for the IAAO Mass Appraisal Specialist designation [S3].
- He chairs the boards of Community Action of Skagit County and Chinook Enterprises, is a Rotarian and a Special Olympics volunteer, and graduated from Leadership Skagit in 2018 [S1].
- He filed for re-election on May 4, 2026, the first day of filing week, and is unopposed [S6][S1].
- The VoteWA online voters' guide carries the same statement and lists his campaign website as votedannyhagen.com [S2].

## Positions

### Property tax administration
- Says he is committed to "accurate valuations, consistent application of the law, and getting out into the community," citing more than 60 presentations to more than 2,000 residents [S1].
- Says the assessor's office "is not, and should not be, a partisan office" and that he runs as an Independent for that reason [S4].
- Describes Washington's budget-based property tax system, in which most taxing districts may raise total property-tax revenue by 1 percent a year plus new construction, as "generally the most consistent for taxing districts and for tax payers" [S4].

### Senior and disability property tax exemption
- His statement says ESSB 6162 (2026) raises the income threshold for the senior and disabled persons exemption from $48,000 to $78,000, adds a $7,500 (individual) or $15,000 (couple) standard deduction and widens access for disabled veterans; his office will take applications for 2027 this fall [S1].
- His campaign says he "led work on legislative improvements" to expand the program [S3].
- In February 2026 he told the Skagit Valley Herald that about 3,000 Skagit seniors were enrolled, that participants save about 40% to 80% on their bills, and that he expected enrollment to rise 50% to 100% with the higher threshold (the article put the 2027 threshold at $67,000 or $77,000, depending on the final bill) [S5].
