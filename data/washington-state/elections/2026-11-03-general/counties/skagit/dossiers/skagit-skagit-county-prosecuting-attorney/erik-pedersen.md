## Background

- Erik Pedersen (Prefers Democratic Party) has worked 32 years in the Skagit County Prosecuting Attorney's Office: deputy prosecutor 1994-1999, appellate and senior deputy 1999-2021, and chief civil deputy 2022-2026 [S1]. He was City of Aberdeen prosecutor from 1992 to 1994 [S1].
- Education: B.A. in business administration, University of Washington (1987); J.D., University of Puget Sound/Seattle University (1990) [S1].
- He is a past president of the Skagit County Bar Association [S1].
- He filed on May 4, 2026, as a candidate for the office [S4]. The incumbent, Rich Weyrich, was re-elected unopposed in 2022 [S6] and is not on the 2026 ballot [S1]. Pedersen is unopposed [S1].
- The VoteWA online voters' guide carries the same statement and lists no campaign website, only a campaign email address [S2].

## Positions

### Public safety and prosecution
- Says the office "strives to protect victims and the community by holding offenders responsible, especially violent offenders" [S1].
- Says the office balances victims' and community interests "with compassionate accountability, recognizing the effects of drug use and mental illness," guided by "truth, equity and justice" [S1].
- Says he will make decisions "based upon all the evidence" and use his experience and relationships "to lead for the future" [S1].

## Record

- Says he prosecuted criminal trials for 25 years and briefed hundreds of appellate cases, including multiple homicide cases; in the civil division he advised the county, helped develop efficiency policies and "defended the county in numerous lawsuits" [S1].
- In February 2026, as chief civil deputy, he told the Skagit Valley Herald that one group had filed 16 recall petitions against county officials, that the in-house staff time spent defending them was worth "over $100,000," and that 11 federal cases filed by recall petitioners against county officials and employees were pending. He said he believed the petitioners' motivations were political [S3].
