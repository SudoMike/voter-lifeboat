## Background

Alex Lathum, who prefers the Democratic Party, is the interim Skagit County Coroner [S4]. Coroner Hayley Thompson, first appointed in 2016 and elected as a Democrat, announced in April that she would resign before her term ended. Under state law the Skagit County Democratic Party then nominated three replacements [S6][S3]. On September 11, 2026 the Board of Commissioners voted unanimously to appoint Lathum for the rest of Thompson's term, which ends December 31. Her resignation took effect September 18. The other two nominees, Sam Irons and Jeremy Moselle, endorsed Lathum at their interviews [S3]. Thompson said she looked forward "to seeing Alex continue the work that we've done" [S3].

Lathum spent six years as lead microbiologist at the state Public Health Laboratory's COVID-19 and STI labs [S3]. His statement lists roles there as lead microbiologist, data consultant and management analyst, and earlier work as a laboratory technician at the Institute for Environmental Health [S1][S2]. He holds a B.S. in physics and astronomy (University of Washington), a B.A. in mathematics (University of Illinois) and an M.S. in mathematics (Emporia State University) [S1]. The pamphlet, written before his appointment, lists him as a current intern at the Skagit County Coroner's Office [S1]. At an October forum he said he started as a volunteer in the office "from the ground up," from "mopping floors" to taking part in death scene investigations, autopsies and budget meetings [S4]. He volunteers as a musician for local high school musicals [S1].

## Positions

### Running the office
- Pledges "independent, compassionate, and professional medicolegal death investigations." He says he will cooperate fully with investigative partners, respond to death scenes himself, and explain the investigation process and timeline to families [S1][S2].
- Says he will "actively track and analyze Skagit County death statistics" and publish the data to inform community safety decisions [S1].
- At the October forum he said he intends to follow standards set by the American Board of Medicolegal Death Investigators and the International Association of Coroners and Medical Examiners. "We want to do a thorough investigation, and that can't always be done quickly, but it's important that we do it correctly" [S4].
- Describes the office as a "tightly-run ship" with two full-time medicolegal death investigators and one part-time employee, and says more staff are needed [S4].
- In his appointment interview he said he wants to protect public health and give the deceased and their families "a trustworthy source of information" [S3].
