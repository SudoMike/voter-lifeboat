## What the office does

The coroner runs the county's medicolegal death investigations: it determines the cause and manner of sudden, violent or unexplained deaths, takes part in death scene investigations and autopsies, and notifies and informs families [S1][S3]. Under former Coroner Hayley Thompson (2016 to 2026), Skagit moved from hospital-based morgue and autopsy services to an independent facility. The office earned national accreditation from the International Association of Coroners and Medical Examiners by January 2024 and shifted to a mostly full-time professional staff [S2]. The office is partisan and serves a four-year term [S1].

## Race dynamics

Thompson resigned effective September 18, 2026. Commissioners appointed Democrat Alex Lathum as interim coroner from three Democratic Party nominees (alex-lathum.md S3). Lathum is a former lead microbiologist at the state Public Health Laboratory who volunteered and interned in the coroner's office (alex-lathum.md S1, S3, S4). Republican Derrill Fussell, a retired flower-bulb farm manager and technology company CEO, qualified for the ballot as a write-in with 1,197 primary votes. He is one of four "constitutional" write-in candidates on SkagitResidents.com [S3] (derrill-fussell.md S8). Both say the office is understaffed. Lathum describes two full-time investigators and one part-time employee [S3]. Fussell campaigns against planned cuts to coroner positions and on keeping accreditation (derrill-fussell.md S3, S5). Fussell says neither candidate has coroner experience and that character and administration matter most. Lathum points to his scientific background and work inside the office [S3]. Lathum reports no contributions under PDC mini reporting, and Fussell reports $1,400 raised (alex-lathum.md S7; derrill-fussell.md S10).
