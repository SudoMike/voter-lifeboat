## Background

Derrill Fussell, who prefers the Republican Party, did not file for the office. He reached the general election ballot as a write-in: he received 1,197 write-in votes in the August 4 primary, above the 1% threshold [S9][S8]. The Herald reports he and the other qualifying write-ins (for clerk and treasurer) each got about 5% of their races [S8]. His campaign says the write-in captured "nearly 7% of the vote" [S7]. The Herald describes him as one of four self-proclaimed "constitutional" candidates featured on SkagitResidents.com [S8].

He lives in Mount Vernon, where he and his wife raised three daughters [S1][S4]. He farmed in the Skagit Valley for twelve years and was general manager of what he describes as one of the world's largest flower bulb farms [S1][S4]. He was CEO of a technology company and holds a U.S. patent for a removable hard drive / data-security hardware [S1][S4][S9]. He says he held "the nation's highest civilian security clearance" and worked as a contractor at the White House West Wing and the Pacific Naval Command Center (NIWC Pacific) [S1][S4]. He has two semesters of college, most recently at Skagit Valley College [S4]. He has written ten children's books under the pen name Dr. Qooz, and his campaign says he has taught a U.S. Constitution class twice a month in Skagit County for six years [S1][S4]. He says his only son was killed in a single-car crash in Texas, and that the long wait during the autopsy process shapes his campaign [S1][S4].

The Herald reports that "in January" Fussell filed a petition to recall Prosecuting Attorney Rich Weyrich and Commissioners Ron Wesen, Peter Browning and Lisa Janicki [S9].

## Positions

### Running the office and its budget
- Says the coroner's office "succeeds on management, budgeting, and administrative oversight" and that his career prepared him for that [S1]. At the forum he said the coroner's role is primarily administrative [S9].
- Says the department is understaffed and he "will lobby for the full staffing this vital work demands" [S1]. His campaign says the county plans to cut two of roughly six coroner positions on January 1, 2027. It argues that cutting the office can cost the county money because state autopsy reimbursements depend on accreditation [S3][S7]. That description of the cuts is the campaign's own.
- Platform: "claim every reimbursement dollar" the county is entitled to under RCW 68.50.104, lock in multi-year autopsy contracts, pursue federal Coverdell forensic-science grants, and keep the office's IACME accreditation as a "permanent" baseline with public compliance reporting [S5].
- Promises timely investigations, turnaround time published as a metric, communication with every family "including our Swinomish, Upper Skagit, and Sauk-Suiattle neighbors," and open data on overdose and fentanyl deaths [S1][S5].
- Says he will complete every certification Washington requires [S1]. At the forum he said Lathum "has got ahead of me" on the state-mandated coroner education [S9].
- Says staff should rotate "so no one is required to serve 24/7, 365" [S4].

### Experience
- Argues that "neither candidate comes to this office with the lived experience of the work itself — that's why character and judgment matter most. The state provides the education. The voters provide the character" [S3][S4].
