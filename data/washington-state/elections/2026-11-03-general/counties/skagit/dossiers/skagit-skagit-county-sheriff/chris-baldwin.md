## Background

Chris Baldwin (prefers Independent Party) is chief of field operations at the Skagit County Sheriff's Office, where he has worked for 32 years [S1][S11]. His campaign says he joined the office in 1994, started in corrections and patrol, was promoted to sergeant in 2009 and to chief of field operations in 2019, and oversees the patrol bureau and all specialty assignments [S4]. He served 26 years on the regional SWAT team, rising to team leader. In 2023 he became the first commander of the North Cascades Regional SWAT Team, which combines resources from Skagit and Island counties [S4]. He also commands the office's Crisis Response Team and is a past chair of the Law and Justice Council [S1][S4]. He lists Skagit Valley College, the state corrections and basic law enforcement academies, and command-level tactical and supervisory courses as his education [S1]. He volunteers with people with special needs and disabilities and advocates for autism awareness [S1][S4]. He is not the incumbent sheriff. Sheriff Don McDermott is on his endorsement list [S6].

## Positions

### Public safety
- His statement promises "practical, common-sense leadership" and decisions "based on experience and integrity rather than politics" [S1].
- Listed priorities: strengthening public safety "while respecting the rights and dignity of every individual"; supporting and retaining well-trained deputies and staff; expanding partnerships with local agencies; addressing crime proactively; and "ensuring transparency and responsible use of tax dollars" [S1][S3].
- Says the office must "serve all individuals equally, regardless of their background, disability or circumstances" [S1].
- His campaign promotes the co-response model, in which mental health professionals ride with patrol deputies on crisis calls. The campaign says the model "frequently reduces uses of force" and that he will keep expanding it [S5].
- His campaign also cites added patrol deputies funded by grants, and the rebuilt inter-local drug task force that targets "mid-upper level drug trafficking organizations" [S5].

### Spending and accountability
- Lists "transparency and responsible use of tax dollars" among his priorities [S1].
- Declined the Auditor's offer to mail voters a supplemental page after his statement was left out of the printed primary pamphlet. He said, "I do not believe it is necessary to create additional expenses for taxpayers" [S7][S10].
- His campaign says the office has tracked every use of force, pursuit and canine use in professional-accountability software since 2012. It says the office was one of three agencies chosen to test data transfers to the state Use of Force Data Exchange [S5].

## Record

Everything below is as described by his campaign. It is his administrative record as a Sheriff's Office command officer, not a record in elected office.
- Oversaw a $3 million radio infrastructure project that the campaign says gives 99% radio coverage across the county [S5].
- Formed a Professional Standards and Accountability unit with a full-time lieutenant [S5].
- The campaign says that in 2026 the office was the third sheriff's office in the state to have its policies vetted by the Criminal Justice Training Commission. It says the office was the first to receive grant funding from that process, nearly $3 million (headed "$2.75 Million"), for more patrol deputies [S5].
- Helped stabilize the Skagit County Inter-local Drug Task Force after 2019 [S5].
- Secured grant funding for an officer wellness program and $30,000 from the Washington State Traffic Safety Commission for a traffic collision investigation team [S5].
- Primary: his campaign reports he received 94.06% of the vote in the August 4 primary [S8]. Cody Hart qualified for the general as a write-in with about 4% [S12].

### Voters' pamphlet omission (primary)
- In July 2026 the Auditor's Office said it had accidentally left Baldwin's statement out of the printed primary voters' pamphlet. At that time he was the only candidate on the ballot for sheriff [S10]. The office said Baldwin submitted on time. It corrected the online pamphlet and added a supplemental page, which the League of Women Voters offered to help distribute [S10].
- Baldwin asked the Auditor not to mail the extra page to every voter, to save the county money [S7][S10].
- His full biography and statement are printed on page 12 of the general-election local voters' pamphlet, with his phone, email and website and "No photo submitted" [S1].
