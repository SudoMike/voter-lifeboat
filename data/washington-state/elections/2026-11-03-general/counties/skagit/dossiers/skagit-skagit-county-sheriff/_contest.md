## What the office does
The sheriff runs the Skagit County Sheriff's Office for a four-year term [S1]. The office's work includes patrol of unincorporated areas, investigations and corrections operations, plus specialty units such as SWAT, the crisis co-response team, and the drug task force it supports (S6, as described by Baldwin's campaign). It is a partisan county office; both candidates state a party preference (S1, S2).

## Race dynamics
Neither candidate is the incumbent. Chris Baldwin (prefers Independent), the office's chief of field operations and a 32-year employee, filed in May and was the only candidate on the primary ballot (chris-baldwin.md S1, S11). His campaign reports 94.06% of the primary vote (chris-baldwin.md S8). Cody Hart (prefers Republican) did not file for the primary. He qualified for November with 1,018 write-in votes, about 4% (S4). He is one of four self-described "constitutional" candidates who reached the ballot that way, and he calls himself a "Constitutional Sheriff" and "change-in-leadership candidate" (S4; cody-hart.md S1). Hart is known locally for repeated recall petitions against county officials. Judges ruled his January 2026 petition insufficient and fined him (cody-hart.md S9, S10). Baldwin is recommended by both the Skagit County Democrats and the Skagit County Republican Party. The GOP list leaves out the Republican-preferring Hart (chris-baldwin.md S13, S14). Baldwin reports about $30k raised. Hart registered for PDC mini reporting and has no reports on file (chris-baldwin.md S9; cody-hart.md S7). The League of Women Voters of Skagit County held a forum with the sheriff candidates on October 5 (S5). The Skagit Valley Herald's nine-part general-election preview series began October 6 with the clerk and coroner races. No sheriff story had been published as of 2026-10-08.

### The voters' pamphlet question
- **The Herald's July 16 story is about the primary pamphlet, not the general one.** In July the Auditor's Office said it had "missed a page in the processing of the pamphlet," which left Baldwin's statement out of the mailed primary pamphlet. At the time he was running unopposed (S3). The office corrected the online version and printed a supplemental page. It did not mail that page, because Baldwin asked it not to spend the money; the League of Women Voters offered to hand it out (S3). The same release said the office had also left out the traditional disclaimer about party preference (S3).
- **The general-election pamphlet (PDF page 12) contains both candidates (S1):**
  - Baldwin: full biography, full statement, phone, email (info@ChrisBaldwinForSheriff.com), website (www.ChrisBaldwinForSheriff.com), "No photo submitted."
  - Hart: full biography, full statement, email (contact@codyhartinfo.com), website printed truncated as "www.CodyHart." (his own materials give CodyHart.org; cody-hart.md S6, S7), no phone, "No photo submitted."
  - Both are also listed on the all-offices page (PDF page 5) (S2).
- **The header.** On the other office pages, the extracted text begins with the office header; page 13, for example, opens "Skagit County Treasurer | 4-year term" (S2). On page 12 the text opens directly with Baldwin's biography. The header "Skagit County Sheriff | 4-year term" appears only after Baldwin's statement, just before his contact block (S1). This may be a layout or extraction artifact rather than a missing header. The page could not be rendered here to check visually. The text of both candidates' statements is complete on the page and matches the VoteWA voters' guide (race 186835).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal (experience):** the clearest divide. Baldwin offers a 32-year insider command record. Hart offers a self-described outsider "change-in-leadership" candidacy with no law enforcement career.
2. **Spending & accountability:** Hart's campaign centers on exposing "waste, fraud, and abuse" and public corruption. Baldwin lists "responsible use of tax dollars" and declined a supplemental pamphlet mailing on cost grounds. That is a weaker signal than Hart's.
3. **Public safety:** Baldwin's record mixes enforcement capacity (deputies, drug task force) with prevention (mental-health co-response). Hart offers a "law and order" line plus immigration-enforcement and gun-rights planks, with no program detail.
4. Taxes, housing and climate: no evidence for either candidate. Hart's parental-rights plank is recorded but not scored for a County office.
