## Background

Mark Hulst, who lists his party preference as Independent, is a Skagit County native who lives in Conway [S1][S5]. He is 63 [S5]. He served 12 years on the Mount Vernon City Council, 2012 through 2023, chairing its finance and public safety committees [S1][S3][S5]. He also served on the Mount Vernon Planning Commission and represented the city on the Skagit Transit Board, and he sits on the board of Volunteers of America Western Washington [S1][S3]. He owns Skagit Cellars and is executive director of the La Conner Chamber of Commerce [S5]. He chaired the Skagit County Republican Party in the late 1990s and early 2000s and later served as the party's state committeeman. The forum moderator said he made an in-kind contribution to the county Republican party in May 2026 [S5]. He said it has been "well over" 15 years since he attended an official Republican party function [S5]. In the August 4 primary he received 45.7% of the countywide vote to Jeremy Harrison-Smith's 54.1% [S5].

## Positions

### Elections
- Says the auditor's office should be defined by "integrity, professionalism, and service to the public - not politics." He says he will run vote-by-mail "accurately, consistently, and professionally - without unnecessary rhetoric or partisanship" [S1].
- Says he runs as an independent because the office "should rise above partisanship" [S2][S3]. At the September 8 forum he said "I am not going to be beholden to any political party when it comes to running the office" [S5].
- At the same forum he said there is no evidence of voter fraud in the county's elections and that the 2020 presidential election was not stolen. He said he does not support federal attempts to restrict mail-in voting, noting that the Legislature sets state election law. He said he would not obey federal directions to turn over voter registration lists, adding that the decision would rest with the secretary of state, and he said he would certify election results [S5].
- Agreed the auditor should be out in the community more, educating young people about local politics [S5].

### Office operations and spending
- Lists his priorities as administering elections "in full compliance with Washington State Law," "accurate, transparent, and accountable financial records," timely public records, staff support and efficient service [S2].
- Says his goal is to make the office run smoothly "and safeguard taxpayer resources," and lists improving operational efficiency and staff development as priorities [S1][S2].
- Said the auditor should audit each of the county's six vehicle-licensing sub-agencies regularly and give their employees support and training [S5].
- Proposes a property-title notification service, used in other counties, to alert owners to activity on their titles as a fraud protection [S5].
- No public statement found on county tax levels or the county's budget deficit.

## Scoring notes

- Experience: runs as "The Experienced Choice," citing 12 years on the city council, finance committee chair and business management [S2][S5]. He has not worked in an auditor's office. Harrison-Smith runs as a new generation of leadership.
- Partisanship is the race's main argument. Hulst runs as an independent, but he chaired the county Republican Party, and the party now recommends him [S5][S6]. Harrison-Smith runs openly as a Democrat [S5].
- Both gave the same answers on fraud, the 2020 election, mail voting and certification [S5].
