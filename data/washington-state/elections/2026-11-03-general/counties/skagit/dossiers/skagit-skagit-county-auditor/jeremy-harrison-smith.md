## Background

Jeremy Harrison-Smith, a Democrat, was born and raised in Skagit County and lives in Clear Lake with his wife and daughter [S1][S3]. He is the governor's Northwest Washington regional representative, a post his campaign says he has held for three years [S1][S3]. He was previously executive director of the Scleroderma Foundation Northwest Chapter, where he says he oversaw budgets, donor relations and work plans across Washington and Idaho, and he has worked as a preschool teacher [S1]. He lists Skagit County elections observing, English-Spanish interpreting and voter registration among his community work [S1]. He holds an MA in social justice in intercultural relations and a BA in sociology and international relations [S1]. His campaign says he has spent more than a decade supporting local candidates and expanding civic participation, particularly in the Latino community [S3]. He is 42 [S5]; no source found lists an elected office he has held. He is running for the seat of two-term Auditor Sandy Perkins, who endorses him [S1][S5]. In the August 4 primary he received 54.1% of the countywide vote to Mark Hulst's 45.7% [S5].

## Positions

### Elections
- Says he will "protect our accurate, secure, and accessible elections" and work with state partners to safeguard the county's vote-by-mail system [S1].
- His campaign says he will explore "tools like live streaming ballot processing" to build public trust [S3].
- At the September 8 Fidalgo Democrats forum he said he runs as a Democrat because the office is partisan under state law, and that the auditor's election work is nonpartisan. He also said "there is just one party nationally that is the biggest threat to the way we run our elections in Washington" [S5].
- At the same forum he said there is no evidence of voter fraud in the county's elections, that the 2020 presidential election was not stolen, that he does not support federal attempts to restrict mail-in voting, and that he would not obey federal directions to turn over voter registration lists. He said he would certify election results [S5].
- Wants the elections department to educate voters proactively, for example by visiting schools and encouraging young people to register [S5].

### Office operations and spending
- Says he will "modernize financial systems" and improve customer service for licensing and public records [S1][S2].
- Describes a future office that is fully staffed with bilingual employees and a joint front desk shared by the auditor, assessor and treasurer's offices [S5].
- On vehicle licensing sub-agencies, said shorter public hours at the Mount Vernon office will let licensing staff audit sub-agencies, and that staff should be cross-trained to cover shortages [S5].
- As a Spanish speaker, says he will advocate for expanded language access and outreach [S1].
- No public statement found on county tax levels or the county's budget deficit.

## Scoring notes

- Experience: first-time candidate pitching "a new generation of leadership" and "new ideas" [S2][S5]. He pairs that with state-level ties (the governor's office, the secretary of state) and the outgoing auditor's endorsement [S4][S5]. Hulst runs on 12 years of city council and business management experience.
- Both candidates gave the same answers on fraud, the 2020 election, federal limits on mail voting and certification [S5]. The difference is in emphasis: Harrison-Smith on language access, outreach and defending vote-by-mail against national threats; Hulst on nonpartisan administration and compliance.
- No position on taxes, housing, safety or climate. The office is administrative.
