## What the office does
The county auditor is the county's supervisor of elections and runs voter registration. The office is also the motor vehicle and vessel licensing agent, records public documents, and handles the county's payroll, expenditures and financial reporting (S2). The auditor oversees the county's vehicle-licensing sub-agencies (S3). It is a four-year, partisan office (S1, S3). Because of the county's budget shortfall, the office is closed on several days in 2026 (S2).

## Race dynamics
Two-term Auditor Sandy Perkins is not running. She endorses Democrat Jeremy Harrison-Smith, the governor's Northwest Washington representative (S3; jeremy-harrison-smith.md S1). Mark Hulst, a former 12-year Mount Vernon city councilmember, runs as an independent (S1, S3). Harrison-Smith led the primary, 54.1% to 45.7% (S3). Partisanship is the main argument in the race. At the September forum, hosted by the Fidalgo Democrats, the moderator noted that Hulst chaired the county Republican Party in the late 1990s and early 2000s. Hulst said he will not be beholden to any party (S3). The county Republican Party now recommends him (mark-hulst.md S6). Harrison-Smith runs as a Democrat and says one national party is the biggest threat to Washington's elections (S3). Both candidates said there is no evidence of local voter fraud, the 2020 election was not stolen, they oppose federal limits on mail voting and they would certify results (S3). The League of Women Voters forum for this race is October 12, after this research date. The Herald's preview story for the race had not run as of 2026-10-08. Harrison-Smith reports about twice Hulst's fundraising ($47.1k to $23.1k), and Hulst has also lent his campaign $30,000 (jeremy-harrison-smith.md S7; mark-hulst.md S7).
