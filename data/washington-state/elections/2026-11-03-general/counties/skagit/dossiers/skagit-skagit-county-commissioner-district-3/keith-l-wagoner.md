## Background

Keith Wagoner, who prefers the Republican Party, is the state senator for the 39th Legislative District (2018 to present) and the Senate Minority Whip. He was Sedro-Woolley mayor from 2016 to 2018 and served on the Sedro-Woolley City Council from 2010 to 2015 [S1][S2]. He served 23 years in the U.S. Marine Corps and Navy as a helicopter pilot and retired as a commander. He holds a B.S. in physical oceanography from the U.S. Naval Academy and an M.S. in global leadership from the University of San Diego [S1]. His campaign says he grew up on a goat farm in Alger and graduated from Burlington-Edison High School in 1979 [S3]. He lists the Fraternal Order of Police's 2020 Legislator of the Year award [S1]. At the September forum he said he was Sedro-Woolley's first Hispanic mayor [S12]. He is 65 [S12].

He registered his commissioner campaign with the PDC in June 2025, after Commissioner Lisa Janicki announced her retirement. He said he "was never going to run against her" [S8][S7]. If elected, he says he will keep his Senate seat through the end of that term in January 2029 [S8][S9][S12]. In the August 4 primary, where only District 3 voted, he led with 45% to incumbent Joe Burns's 40.7%; independent Connie Krier had 14.3% [S12][S11]. All county voters decide the general election [S11].

## Positions

### Taxes and county budget
- Says he understands "the need to reduce regulatory and tax burdens at all levels of government" [S1][S2].
- Told the Herald the biggest issue for the next commissioner is a severe county budget shortfall. He would work with the other commissioners to "reassess and reduce spending" by scouring the budget to "determine which expenditures are essential, which are valuable and which are just nice-to-have" [S9].
- At the September 8 Anacortes forum he described the county's choice as cutting services, raising taxes, or both to close a $10 million deficit. He cited his anti-tax record: "I'm the guy who's going to work for solutions that do not involve taking money out of our pockets" [S12].
- His platform says he "led in the fight to defeat a proposed 300% increase in property taxing authority to local government." It says his liquor- and cannabis-revenue bills for local governments "would eliminate any need for increasing your property taxes" [S4]. (The bill pages are under Record.)
- Said he wants behavioral health facilities fully funded [S12].

### Housing and land use
- Wants to remove "regulatory steps that keep people from building here and investing here" [S10]. Says the county permitting department should be more efficient for housing, because "there are people who can't get their permits for over a year" [S12].
- Cited his legislative work to reduce taxes on multi-family housing [S10].
- Said he does not support fully contained communities [S12].

### Public safety
- His platform says he has "consistently stood with law enforcement and first responders." It says he was prime sponsor of legislation creating a statewide co-responder training curriculum [S4].
- Supports Flock Safety license-plate cameras "with some regulation," citing kidnapping and murder cases: "It's a tool that can be misused, but there's ways to use it correctly" [S12].

### Agriculture, energy and data centers
- Campaign priorities include reducing regulatory burdens on farming and small businesses and preserving "rural and small city culture" [S3][S4].
- At the July forum he cited his opposition to the state Lorraine Loomis Act and an agricultural overtime bill. He also said he opposed the Goldeneye facility [S10].
- Said the county's clean energy advisory group should include people with policy and technical expertise, and decisions should not be made "based on emotions" [S10].
- On data centers, he applauded the commissioners' moratorium "to investigate and learn the facts." He pointed to Quincy, where he said a data center reduced property taxes. "If we can get it right — a spent gravel pit or a quarry that's not being used — maybe it's a good thing ... and it would drive our taxes down" [S12].

### County government and the Guemes ferry
- Would support expanding the Board of Commissioners to five members. He warned that a charter review commission "can do anything they want" [S12].
- On Guemes Island ferry fares, he said the county could ask the state for help and that holding both offices could help with that. He called the state budget "an absolute disaster" in recent years [S12].
- Defends holding the Senate and commissioner seats at once. He cites legislators who also serve on county councils, says he usually attends legislative obligations remotely, and calls the pairing "a great synergistic match" [S9][S12]. He called questions about drawing both salaries "kind of insulting" [S12].

### Immigration and other forum questions
- Said ICE has the legal right to deport "illegal aliens" and people who have committed crimes. He also wants immigration reform for farm workers: "They're fearful sometimes, and we need them up here" [S12].
- Said the 2020 presidential election was not stolen [S12].
- Asked about the CDC's new childhood vaccination schedule, he said he did not know because he is not familiar with the new recommendations [S12].

## Record

- **Senate tax votes (Tier 1 roll calls):**
  - ESSB 6346 (2026, "Establishing a tax on millionaires," chapter 238, Laws of 2026) [S14]: voted Nay on third reading and final passage on 2026-02-16 (passed 27-22). Voted Nay on concurrence and final passage as amended by the House on 2026-03-11 (passed 27-21). On 2026-02-16 he also voted Yea on three floor amendments offered by Sens. Warnick, MacEwen and Gildon, all of which failed [S13].
  - ESSB 5813 (2025, a more progressive rate structure for the capital gains and estate taxes) [S16]: voted Nay on final passage on 2025-04-19 (27-21) and on final passage as amended by the House on 2025-04-24 (27-21) [S15].
  - ESSB 5814 (2025, excise taxes on select services) [S18]: voted Nay on final passage on 2025-04-19 (27-22) and as amended by the House on 2025-04-24 (26-22) [S17].
- **Sponsored bills:** prime sponsor of SB 5568 (2023-24, restoring liquor sales revenue distributions to local governments) [S19] and SB 5404 (2023-24, increasing cannabis revenue distributions to local governments) [S20]. In January 2024 the Senate Labor & Commerce Committee sent both bills to Ways & Means without recommendation. Neither advanced further [S19][S20]. His platform says the majority "would-not-hear-it in committee" [S4]. The bill histories show a committee executive action in 2024 [S19][S20].
- As senator, he cites state funding he secured for east Skagit projects, including the Helping Hands Solution Center, the SWIFT Center, the Skagit YMCA and Denny Engberg Memorial Field. He also cites work to create the Sedro-Woolley Library [S10]. His pamphlet statement says no senator in the district's history "has brought more of your hard-earned tax dollars back to east Skagit County" [S1]. That is his own claim.

## Scoring notes

- Taxes: the record goes beyond the ESSB 6346 vote. He voted against two 2025 tax increases (ESSB 5813, ESSB 5814), sponsored local revenue-sharing bills framed as property-tax relief, and stated at forums that he opposes county tax increases.
- Spending: repeated statements favor reducing county spending to close the deficit. He makes an exception for behavioral health facilities.
- Experience: long elected tenure (council, mayor, state senator since 2018, Minority Whip) and a 23-year military career. He plans to hold both offices.
- Safety: supports law enforcement and Flock cameras with regulation, and also sponsored co-responder training. Burns opposes Flock cameras.
- Housing: permitting speed and deregulation. Burns also backs permit streamlining, so the gap on housing is narrow.
