## What the office does

The three-member Board of County Commissioners is the county's legislative body. It must adopt a balanced budget each year, which for 2026 meant about $83 million in general fund expenses [S3]. The board sets county land-use and permitting policy, runs county services such as the Guemes Island ferry, and fills vacancies in county offices (it appointed the interim coroner in September 2026) (joe-burns.md S12, S15; ../skagit-skagit-county-coroner/alex-lathum.md S3). District 3 covers northeast Skagit County, including Burlington, Sedro-Woolley and Concrete [S3]. Candidates are nominated by District 3 voters in the primary and elected by the whole county in the general [S2][S4]. The ballot lists the seat as "short/full term" [S1]: the winner fills the rest of Lisa Janicki's term, which ends December 31, 2026, and then a new full term (joe-burns.md S6). Commissioners are paid about $155,000 a year [S3].

## Race dynamics

Democrat Joe Burns, 42, a former Sedro-Woolley council member and auto shop teacher, was appointed in January 2026 to finish Janicki's term [S5][S3]. Republican state Sen. Keith Wagoner, 65, a former Sedro-Woolley mayor and the Senate Minority Whip, led the August primary 45% to 40.7%, with independent Connie Krier at 14.3% [S3]. Both live in Sedro-Woolley [S3]. The county faces a general fund deficit of about $10 million going into 2027 [S3]. Wagoner runs on his anti-tax record and on cutting spending ("solutions that do not involve taking money out of our pockets"). Burns says preserving services like senior centers and Meals on Wheels without "extreme cuts" will take creativity (keith-l-wagoner.md S9, S12; joe-burns.md S11). Wagoner would keep his Senate seat through January 2029 and hold both offices. Burns's campaign stresses that he quit his other jobs to serve full time (keith-l-wagoner.md S8; joe-burns.md S3, S7).

On data centers, Burns supported the county's moratorium and opposes large facilities on water and floodplain grounds. Wagoner applauded the moratorium but said a data center on a spent gravel pit "would drive our taxes down" [S3]. On Flock license-plate cameras, Burns now opposes them and Wagoner supports them with regulation [S3]. Burns has the Skagit County Democrats, Rep. Rick Larsen and most countywide officials. Wagoner has the Skagit County Republican Party, the Sedro-Woolley mayor and 39th District Republican legislators (joe-burns.md S4; keith-l-wagoner.md S5, S6). Burns has raised more ($48.3k to $31.5k) (joe-burns.md S5; keith-l-wagoner.md S7).

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Taxes:** Wagoner has a Tier 1 record of voting against state tax increases (ESSB 6346, ESSB 5813, ESSB 5814), sponsored local revenue-sharing bills, and says he opposes county tax increases. Burns has no stated tax position beyond "protecting taxpayer dollars."
2. **Spending:** Wagoner would scour the budget and reduce spending. Burns would preserve discretionary services and warns against deep cuts.
3. **Experience:** a long legislative and executive record (Wagoner) against a nine-month appointed incumbent who presents himself as a new perspective (Burns).
4. **Safety:** Wagoner emphasizes law enforcement support and Flock cameras. Burns emphasizes behavioral health and North Star, and opposes Flock cameras.
5. **Housing:** both favor faster permitting. Burns adds workforce and first-time-buyer housing, and Wagoner adds deregulation. The difference is small.
6. Data centers, the Guemes ferry and dual office-holding separate the candidates, but they map to no scored County axis (tech and local-control do not apply to County contests). Climate evidence is thin for both. Neither candidate has stated a position on the 2026 initiatives or on parents-and-schools or gender topics.
