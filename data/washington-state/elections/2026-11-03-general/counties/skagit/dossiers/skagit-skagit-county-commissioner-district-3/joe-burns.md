## Background

Joe Burns, who prefers the Democratic Party, is the appointed incumbent Skagit County Commissioner for District 3 [S1][S2]. On January 5, 2026, Commissioners Ron Wesen and Peter Browning chose him from three Democratic nominees to finish Lisa Janicki's term, which runs through the end of 2026. Skagit County Democratic precinct committee officers had ranked him second of the three [S6]. Before the appointment he served four years on the Sedro-Woolley City Council and taught auto shop at Sedro-Woolley High School [S6]. He spent about two decades in the automotive industry, including nine years as shop foreman at Rallye Auto Sales. He studied gas and diesel automotive technology at the College of Southern Idaho and holds the AWC Certificate of Municipal Leadership [S1][S8]. He is a Lions Club member and former president, the Loggerodeo parade foreman, and former board chair of the Helping Hands Food Bank [S1]. He stepped down from the city council and left teaching to serve as commissioner full time [S7][S8]. He is 42 [S11].

In the August 4 primary, where only District 3 voted, he received 40.7% to state Sen. Keith Wagoner's 45% [S11]. All county voters decide the general election [S10].

## Positions

### Affordability and housing
- His statement centers on whether families "will be able to afford to live, raise a family, and build a future in Skagit County 20 years from now." It pledges "protecting taxpayer dollars, planning responsibly for growth, and focusing on solutions to affordability" [S1][S2].
- Told the Herald he would address housing costs by "streamlining permitting so housing projects don't stall in red tape, looking at zoning that allows more housing types where it makes sense," and making sure growth areas have roads, water and infrastructure [S8]. At the July forum he said he would work to get permits done more quickly [S9].
- His campaign says he "pushed back on blanket apartment mandates from the state" while on the city council. It says he is working with cities on workforce and middle-income housing, aligning infrastructure with housing growth, and focusing on "homeownership and pathways for first time buyers" [S3].

### County budget and services
- Asked about managing an $83 million general fund, he cited his council service and his cost control as an auto shop foreman. He said the county needs "new perspective and new creativity" heading into a 2027 deficit to avoid "extreme cuts that make the government not functional anymore" [S11].
- Said he would work to preserve funding for senior centers and Meals on Wheels, "even if doing so isn't constitutionally required" [S11].
- At the July forum he said he has worked on "figuring out which are the most necessary county services during a deficit" [S9]. He told the Herald he has supported more transparency in county finances [S8].
- His campaign says he would "invest in the roads, broadband, and infrastructure that rural businesses depend on" [S3].

### Public safety and behavioral health
- His campaign says he is strengthening mental and behavioral health services and expanding substance use prevention and overdose response [S3]. He named continuing the North Star homelessness and behavioral health initiative as a priority when appointed [S6].
- As a Sedro-Woolley council member he voted to allow Flock Safety license-plate cameras. He says he has since lost faith in the company over a "backdoor" in its system: "I won't approve one if it comes up before me" [S11].
- Says he has pushed for improved emergency communications and response [S8].

### Land, water, agriculture and data centers
- Campaign priorities include salmon restoration (including Hansen Creek), flood control on the Skagit River, and preserving farmland through the Farmland Legacy program [S3][S9].
- At the July forum he said he would push for more water rights so farmers need not rely on the Skagit PUD for irrigation water. He also wants the county property map to show whether a property is in the 100-year floodplain [S9].
- Supported the county's six-month data center moratorium [S12]. Said his main concerns are water, power and land use [S13]. In August he said data centers "aren't a good idea for Skagit County": "We don't have enough water to allocate to our farmers, according to the state, so why should we be allocating large (amounts) of water to a data center?" [S14]. At the September forum he said he could support small data centers of about 2,000 square feet but not "massive mega centers" [S11].

### Immigration and other forum questions
- Said he does not agree with the way ICE has operated in the county. He argued that "a supermajority" of immigrants pay taxes and should get "a fair shot at a better life" [S11].
- Said he does not support fully contained communities and that the 2020 presidential election was not stolen [S11].
- Said he does not support the CDC's new childhood vaccination schedule [S11].
- On expanding the Board of Commissioners to five or seven members, he said he would need more research [S11].

### Campaign and office
- Says the campaign "is supported by everyday people, not a long list of corporate donors" [S7]. His campaign stresses that the commissioner job is "not a part time gig," which contrasts with Wagoner's plan to keep his Senate seat [S3].

## Record

As a commissioner since January 2026 (votes are board actions he took part in):
- **Data center moratorium:** the board adopted a six-month moratorium on new data center permit applications in unincorporated Skagit County on June 1, 2026, and Burns supported it [S12]. The board held the required public hearing on July 14 [S13].
- **Guemes Island ferry fares:** in January a judge ruled that the 2025 fares, up an average of 30%, were too high because projected capital costs had been counted as operating costs. In March the board adopted a resolution setting an end-2026 deadline to convert non-expiring paper tickets, and the judge later found it inconsistent with her ruling [S16]. On September 14, 2026 the board, including Burns, voted unanimously to uphold the 2025 fares, stating that projected and actual operating costs were consistent. The petitioners said the resolution did not meet the court's order, and the county has appealed the July 31 order [S15]. A 14% average fare increase is proposed for 2027 [S15]. At the September forum Burns suggested changes to multi-ride passes and said any changes should be made cautiously [S11].
- **Northern State Recreation Area ball fields:** said he could not see the county committing money to Sedro-Woolley's proposal to move its baseball fields there: "financially, I don't see how we can commit anything to it" [S17].
- **Budget and North Star:** at the August State of the County luncheon he presented 2027 budget priorities: core services, healthy reserves, and full cost reimbursement for grant-funded positions, against a general fund deficit of about $10 million. He also reported $2.3 million in opioid settlement funds gathered through North Star for the STAR Center and recovery housing, and a $250,000 state allocation toward bridge housing [S14].
- His pamphlet statement says he has "helped secure grant funding, supported local agriculture, and strengthened flood protection" [S1]. That is his own claim.

- Spending: favors preserving services (senior centers, Meals on Wheels) and avoiding deep cuts, while also stating budget-control themes. Wagoner calls for reducing spending.
- Safety: emphasizes behavioral health, overdose response and the North Star initiative, and now opposes Flock cameras.
- Housing: supports permit streamlining and more housing types, alongside workforce and homeownership programs. On housing he is close to Wagoner.
- Taxes: no explicit statement on county tax increases or levy votes found, beyond "protecting taxpayer dollars."
