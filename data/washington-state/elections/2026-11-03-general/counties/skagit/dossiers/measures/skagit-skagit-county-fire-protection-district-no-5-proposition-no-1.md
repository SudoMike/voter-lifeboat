## What it does

Proposition 1 is a multi-year levy lid lift for Skagit County Fire Protection District No. 5's regular property tax levy [S1][S3]:
- It sets the levy at $0.78 per $1,000 of assessed value in 2026, for collection in 2027 [S1].
- For each of the next five years it allows a limit factor of up to 103%, "but not to exceed the $1.50 rate" [S1].
- The maximum allowable levy in 2031, collected in 2032, becomes the base for later levy limits under chapter 84.55 RCW [S1].
- Passage needs a simple majority [S4].

The explanatory statement says the district "seeks voter approval to restore its fire levy to $0.78." It adds that without voter approval fire districts may raise annual revenue only 1% over the prior year, and that approval will let the district "continue providing fire and emergency medical services at existing levels" [S1]. It calls the levy "the primary source of funding for emergency services provided by the District" [S1].

**The district.**
- It serves Allen, Bow, Edison, Samish Island, Chuckanut Drive and nearby areas [S1].
- The fact sheet describes the area as the rural and agricultural land generally north of Josh Wilson and Pulver Roads, west of I-5 and Hobson Road, south of the Whatcom County line, and north of Biscuit Ridge to Samish Island [S4].
- It is staffed entirely by volunteers [S1][S4].
- It has three elected commissioners: Kirk Damon, Tom Perry and Dave Lohman [S6].

**Resolution No. 2026-1** [S3]:
- The board adopted it on April 15, 2026; Commissioners Kirk Damon and David Lohman signed it.
- Its recitals say the district "will not be able to maintain its present service levels to its citizens without the approval of a six (6)-year levy lid lift."
- They also say rising demands and costs will require spending on "improved capital facilities, additional staffing, apparatus maintenance and operations" beyond what the current rate can fund.
- It states that the new money "shall not supplant existing funds."

## Cost

- **Rate.** The current regular levy rate is about $0.3363 per $1,000 [S3][S4]. The measure sets it at $0.78, an increase of about $0.44, or about 2.3 times the current rate (computed for this dossier from [S1] and [S4]).
- **Per home, per the district** [S4]:
  - At $0.78 the total district levy would be about $234 a year on a $300,000 home and $390 on a $500,000 home ($6.50 a month per $100,000 of assessed value).
  - The net increase over the current rate would be about $133 a year on a $300,000 home and $222 on a $500,000 home.
- **District revenue.** The district's 2026 assessed value is about $1.22 billion, and it collects $410,498 [S4]. At $0.78 on that value the levy would raise about $950,000 in 2027 (computed for this dossier from [S4]).
- **Later years.** Revenue may grow up to 3% a year for five years, capped at $1.50 per $1,000 [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This raises an existing levy rather than creating a new tax. Its effect is to more than double the regular levy rate [S1][S4]. The ballot title uses "restore," but no source gives a year when the district last levied $0.78. Because the 2031 levy becomes the permanent base for later limits, the increase continues after the six years [S1][S3].

**Why now, per the district** [S4]:
- Since 2000, district assessed value grew from $218.5 million to $1.22 billion, while tax revenue grew from $208,253 to $410,498.
- Because of the 1% limit, "revenue growth has not kept pace with rising costs," and inflation-adjusted purchasing power "has declined since 2000."
- The district lists its needs as fire apparatus replacement (aging engines, tankers and support vehicles), station and facility maintenance, and rising fuel, maintenance and communications costs.
- It also says failing to replace aging apparatus could lower its fire protection rating, "which directly raises insurance premiums."

**Rating.** The Washington Survey and Rating Bureau gave the district a 6.34 rating on January 1, 2026, which rounds to a protection class of 7 on a 1 (most protection) to 10 scale [S5].

**Comparison with other districts.** The fact sheet compares the district's rate with six other Skagit County fire districts [S4]:

## Arguments for

The statement for, from Citizens for District 5 Fire Levy (spokesperson Andrea Leebron-Clay; members Connor McGreevy and Tom Pasma) [S1][S2]:
- Services are "carried out entirely through the efforts of well trained and properly equipped local volunteers."
- "Fire engines have a limited service life, and the cost to maintain and eventually replace them continues to climb," and inflation has raised equipment, communications, fuel and operating costs.
- Because of the 1% limit, "funding has not kept pace with the rising costs associated with providing services."
- The levy "safeguards the area's fire protection classification rating and helps homeowners avoid costly spikes in insurance premiums."
- "To maintain a local fire department and restore essential purchasing power to the district, vote Yes."

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed: "No person in the jurisdiction contacted the Auditor's Office to provide a statement. There are no statements against the ballot measure" [S1]. No organized opposition was found; the PDC query returned no committee opposing the measure [S8].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote raises the district's regular levy rate from about $0.3363 to $0.78 per $1,000 (more than double), allows 3% annual revenue growth for five years, and makes the 2031 levy the permanent base [S1][S3][S4]. The net cost is about $222 a year on a $500,000 home [S4]. This matches the Spokane Fire District 9 precedent for a lid lift that more than doubles a regular levy: +2.
- `spending`: the explanatory statement frames the money as keeping service "at existing levels," mainly by replacing aging apparatus [S1][S4]. The resolution's recital mentions "additional staffing" and "improved capital facilities" [S3], but the district's voter materials describe no specific expansion. No mapping.
