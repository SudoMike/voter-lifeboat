## What it does

Proposition 1 authorizes La Conner School District to levy property taxes for four years, 2027 through 2030, "to undertake modernization of existing buildings and the replacement and upgrade of technology systems" [S1]. The money "may be used only to support the construction, modernization, remodeling to school facilities, technology improvements and training" [S1]. The district calls it a capital levy [S1].

**Schedule** [S1][S3]:

| Collection year | Approximate rate per $1,000 | Levy amount |
|---|---|---|
| 2027 | $0.307 | $350,000 |
| 2028 | $0.308 | $365,000 |
| 2029 | $0.308 | $380,000 |
| 2030 | $0.308 | $395,000 |

The total is $1,490,000 [S1].

**Resolution No. 26-14** [S3]:
- The board adopted it on July 27, 2026 [S3][S4].
- It says technology work "shall include but not be limited to enhancing infrastructure, acquiring hardware, licensing software, implementing online applications and training, and providing for staffing related to the installation of the foregoing," plus ongoing subscription and license fees and training.
- If projects become impractical, the district may move the money to "other capital purposes," but only for school facilities or its technology plan.
- The resolution's recitals say both "six years" and "four years"; the levy schedule and ballot title cover four collection years, 2027-2030 [S1][S3].

**What the district plans to buy, per board discussions.**
- The levy would "upgrade student and staff computers and technological devices, and address repairs to facilities on campus" [S4].
- On Sept. 21 the board discussed replacing aging computers and iPads and improving classroom audio-visual systems [S5]. Board members said it is more efficient to upgrade technology before it stops working, and that past gaps forced sudden replacements [S5].
- Collection would begin in April 2027 [S5].

## Cost

- About $0.31 per $1,000 of assessed value each year [S1].
- The Herald reported the levy at 30 cents per $1,000, or about $150 a year on a $500,000 property [S4]. At the pamphlet's $0.307 rate, a $500,000 property would pay about $154 in 2027 (computed for this dossier from [S1]).
- The pamphlet notes that some homeowners may qualify for exemptions through the Skagit County Assessor [S1].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

The pamphlet and resolution do not describe this as a replacement levy, and no expiring La Conner capital or technology levy is named in any source checked [S1][S3][S4].

- Voters last saw a La Conner technology levy in February 2016: "Replacement Technology Capital Projects Levy" (Proposition 2) failed with 42.44% yes [S7].
- This research checked the Secretary of State's Skagit County results for most February, April, August and November elections from 2016 through 2026 and found no later La Conner technology or capital levy.
- In September 2026, the Herald reported that a computer replacement for teachers "will use the last of the technology levy funding" [S6]. The sources do not say what that money comes from.

**District finances.**
- The 2026-27 budget is $13.6 million, $1.3 million more than the prior year [S4].
- The district projected enrollment falling from 457 to 442 [S4]; in September it reported 449 full-time-equivalent students against a budget of 441.9 [S6].
- Three retiring staff were not replaced, and the board is working toward a reserve equal to 10% of the budget [S4].
- Board President Susie Deyo said she and Superintendent David Cram "have been conservative on increasing any taxes in the community, but that the levy is necessary," and that she hopes the Legislature will increase funding [S4].

## Arguments for

No statement for was filed: "No person in the jurisdiction contacted the Auditor's Office to provide a statement. There are no statements for the ballot measure" [S1]. The district's case comes from board discussions [S4][S5]:
- Deyo called the levy necessary [S4].
- Board members described upgrading technology before it fails as more efficient [S5].
- Board member John Agen said: "We don't get funded (enough) by the state" [S4].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed: "There are no statements against the ballot measure" [S1]. No organized opposition was found [S4][S5][S6].

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote adds a four-year capital levy of about $0.31 per $1,000 ($1.49 million total) [S1]. No expiring capital or technology levy is named, so it is a new line on current bills, but a modest and temporary one [S1][S7]. The King precedent for a modest technology levy restored after a gap (Highline Prop. 2) is +1.
- `spending`: the money replaces aging computers and devices and repairs existing buildings [S1][S4][S5]; that is upkeep rather than program expansion, so no mapping.
- `parental-rights`: the levy funds facilities and technology. No source ties it to curricula, notification, record access or opt-outs [S1][S3], so no mapping.
