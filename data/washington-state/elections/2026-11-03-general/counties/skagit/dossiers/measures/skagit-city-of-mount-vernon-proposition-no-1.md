## What it does

Proposition 1 asks Mount Vernon voters to renew the Mount Vernon Transportation Benefit District's sales and use tax of two-tenths of one percent (0.2%) "for a period of ten years" under RCW 82.14.0455, to pay for transportation improvements in the city identified in Resolution No. 1097 [S1][S2]. The City of Mount Vernon has assumed the role of the transportation benefit district [S1]. A July 16, 2026 letter in the city's filing says Resolution 1097 provides for renewal of the tax under RCW 36.73.040(3)(a), RCW 36.73.065(1) and RCW 82.14.0455, and authorizes the ballot title as printed [S3].

- The city created the district by Ordinance No. 3679 on May 25, 2016, and voters approved the 0.2% tax for 10 years on November 8, 2016 [S1]. That 2016 measure passed with 68.19% yes (8,202 to 3,826) [S8].
- Unless renewed, collection ends in April 2027 [S1]. The city's page says the current tax "will expire at the end of March 2027" [S4].
- If approved, the tax continues "at the same rate for an additional 10-year period" and "can be used for no other purpose" than transportation improvements [S1].
- The explanatory statement lists street repair, preservation and maintenance, capital improvements for roads, and bike and pedestrian projects from the city's Capital Improvement Program and the Transportation Element of its Comprehensive Plan [S1].
- The city council approves how the money is used, through a six-year Transportation Improvement Plan updated each spring [S4].
- The council voted on May 13, 2026 (reported as "Wednesday" in a May 14 story) to send the renewal to the November ballot [S5]. The city's cover sheet lists the election type as a voter-approved sales tax [S3].

## Cost

- The rate stays at 0.2%, or 20 cents on every $100 spent in Mount Vernon [S1][S4].
- The city estimates the tax raises $2,300,000 a year [S1].
- The Department of Revenue lists Mount Vernon's combined sales tax rate at 9.0% (local 2.5% plus state 6.5%) [S7]. That rate already includes this 0.2%, so renewal leaves it unchanged; if the tax lapsed and no other rate changed, the combined rate would be 8.8% (computed for this dossier from [S1] and [S7]).

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This is a renewal at the same rate, not a new tax. The city's FAQ says: "No, this is the same tax voters approved in 2016. The City is asking our community to keep this funding at the same level" [S4]. State law requires the renewal vote every 10 years [S4][S5].

**What the tax has paid for, per the city.**
- City Engineer Frank Reinart told the council the city has used about $11.8 million in district money on projects since 2017, including estimates for the rest of 2026 [S6].
- Reinart said the money helped on about 13 miles of street pavement, about one mile of sidewalks, 436 curb ramps (about 10% of the city's ramps) and five major intersection improvements [S6]. The city page gives the same 436 ramps and five intersections and "over 13 miles of roads and sidewalks" [S4].
- The city often combines district money with state and federal grants [S4][S6]. Projects the city lists include the College Way interchange widening under I-5, the Hoag Road and LaVenture signal, the Anderson Road and Cedardale roundabout, and design work for 15th Street, 30th Street and Riverside Drive [S4].
- The city says without renewal it "would lose a major source of funding for street maintenance and safety upgrades," and that general-fund money also pays for police, fire, parks and the library [S4].

**Figures that differ between sources.** The statement for says "Matched grants have turned $12 million dollars into nearly $27 million dollars in state and federal grants" and that the city overlaid "about 14 miles" of streets [S1]. The city engineer's figures are $11.8 million and about 13 miles [S6]. No source checked here documents the $27 million grant total.

## Arguments for

The statement for, from the Mount Vernon Transportation Benefit District Pro Committee (spokesperson LuAnne Gordan Burkhart; members Gary Molenaar and Suzanne Butler) [S1][S2]:
- The tax "pays for transportation improvements, and only transportation improvements" [S1].
- Because it is a sales tax, "thousands of tourists and visitors to our city contribute to it" [S1].
- Voters approved it ten years ago and "the city is simply asking us to continue to support it, at the same rate" [S1].
- It cites the College Way/I-5 widening, the Hoag Road/LaVenture signal, 18th Street resurfacing, Freeway Drive work, 436 crosswalks and curbs, and the Fir/30th Street four-way stop [S1].

Mayor Peter Donovan said he favors renewal: "It's nice to know that a portion of their purchases is going to address the wear and tear that they've caused on our streets, rather than have it be carried on the backs of the residents" [S5].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed: "No person in the jurisdiction contacted the Auditor's Office to provide a statement" [S1]. As of July 23, the city had received no requests to serve on the "against" committee [S6].

Councilmember Andrew Vander Stoep voted to put the measure on the ballot but said he would not support the tax: "Sales tax is generally considered a pretty regressive tax, but I think it's fair to have the voters decide" [S5].

## Endorsements and campaign

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote renews an existing 0.2% sales tax at the same rate for ten more years (about $2.3 million a year); the combined rate stays at 9.0% [S1][S4][S7]. A same-rate renewal: +1, as in the Spokane and King renewal precedents.
- `spending`: the money continues street maintenance and transportation capital work the city already funds this way [S1][S4]; no expansion beyond the current program is described, so no mapping.
- `local-control`: the city council already governs the district and keeps doing so [S1][S4]; no mapping.
