## What it does

Proposition 1 asks whether the City of Sedro-Woolley should "be annexed to and become a part of the Central Skagit Rural Partial-County Library District" [S1]. The library district covers the rural area within the Sedro-Woolley School District but not the city [S1]. The library says its legal boundaries also exclude the towns of Lyman and Hamilton [S5].

**How library service works now.**
- City residents get library service through an agreement between the city and the library district, "with fees tied to the Central Skagit Library levy rate" [S1].
- The library says three interlocal agreements signed in 2017 govern the arrangement, and that the city's annual payment is set by the district's levy rate so city residents and district residents "pay the same amount" [S5].
- Those agreements "do not expire until 2039" [S5].
- City residents cannot vote on library district ballot measures [S1][S5].

**What changes if it passes.**
- The city would become part of the district on January 1, 2027, and residents could vote on library district ballot measures from that date [S1][S5].
- Because taxing district boundaries are set each August 1, the district could first levy property tax inside the city in 2028; the city keeps paying for library service through 2027 [S1][S4].
- In 2028 the city would stop its annual library services payment, as set out in a pre-annexation agreement [S5].

**How it reached the ballot.**
- The council chose annexation from five options and approved putting it on the ballot on May 6, 2026 (reported as "Wednesday" in a May 8 story) [S6].
- On July 22, 2026 the council passed Ordinance 2124-26 declaring its intent to join the district, and Resolution No. 1191-26 calling the election [S3][S7]. The library board of trustees adopted a concurrence resolution on July 23 [S5].
- The city's cover sheet lists the measure type as annexation [S3].

## Cost

- The city estimates a 2028 library district levy rate of $0.2320 per $1,000 of assessed value, collecting about $517,255 from property in the city [S1].
- The explanatory statement says the city "shall reduce its budgeted property tax levy by up to $474,115 in 2028 to offset most of the new library district property tax" [S1].
- The city's estimate for a home assessed at $519,450: the library would collect $126.60 in 2028, city property tax would fall by about $115.57, "a net increase of $11 per year" [S4]. The pamphlet gives the same $11 figure [S1].
- The library's 2026 levy rate is 24 cents per $1,000, which the library calls "less than half the maximum legal amount" [S5].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

Annexation replaces the city's contract payment, which comes out of city property taxes, with a direct library district property tax on city property [S1][S4][S7]. The library says the switch "does not make any difference to library funding": it receives the same amount either way [S5].

**The levy lid lift question.** The sources describe the link to a possible future library levy increase differently:
- The Herald reported in May that "the reason for the annexation vote is that the library district is considering asking district voters to approve a property tax increase" [S6]. Finance Director/City Clerk Kelly Kohnken said the district had told the city it is considering a levy lid lift, which "will impact the city's payment to the library district as our payment is based on the library district's levy rate" [S6].
- The Herald reported in August that the library district is considering asking voters in 2027 or 2028 for a levy rate increase [S7].
- The city says that if annexation fails and the district later passes a levy lid lift, the city would have to pay a proportionate increase "with no corresponding city revenue," which "will require the city to decrease other core services" [S4]. City Administrator Charlie Bush made the same point to the Herald [S7].
- The library's FAQ answers "Does this have something to do with a levy lid lift?" with "No, it does not," says no decisions have been made, and says annexation "does not affect the Library District's levy rate now or in the future." It adds that city residents could vote on any future lid lift only if annexation has passed [S5].

**Background.** The library district was approved by voters in 2012 [S5]. In 2017 it took over operation of the city's public library [S5]. A new library building opened in 2021, mostly paid for by $6 million in city-issued bonds that the district repays each year until 2039 [S5].

## Arguments for

The statement for (committee spokesperson Millissa Macomber) [S1][S2]:
- "It simplifies how we fund library service."
- "We are already paying for the library - this allows us to pay the District directly," and the city council "has committed to lowering City property taxes when we approve this ballot measure."
- "Voting yes gives us a say on future District measures - such as bonds or levies. Without annexation, we have no say."
- It lists library programs and services and the State Street building.

Councilmember Allan Henderson said annexation would give the city's "13,000 residents" a vote on any library lid lift and called it "softer on the taxpayers than option one. I never like to see taxes go up, ever" [S6]. Bush said the cost is "as low as we can make it" for the average homeowner [S7].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed: "No person in the jurisdiction contacted the Auditor's Office to provide a statement" [S1]. The city had sought up to three members for a "Con" committee [S4]. No organized opposition was found in the news coverage [S6][S7].

## Endorsements and campaign

## Lean notes

- `taxes`: a yes vote puts a new library district property tax (about $0.232 per $1,000, about $517,255 in 2028) on city property. The city says it will cut its own levy by "up to" $474,115 to offset most of it, for a net increase of about $11 a year on a $519,450 home [S1][S4]. The offset is the city's stated commitment for 2028, not a permanent rate [S1]. The net increase is small, comparable to other annexations into an existing taxing district: +1.
- `local-control`: a yes vote moves library funding from a city-negotiated contract into a separate district that sets its own levy, and in exchange gives city residents a vote in that district [S1][S4][S5]. This formalizes an existing service relationship, like the King County Fire District 43 annexation precedent: +1.
- `spending`: the library says its funding is the same either way [S5]; no mapping.
