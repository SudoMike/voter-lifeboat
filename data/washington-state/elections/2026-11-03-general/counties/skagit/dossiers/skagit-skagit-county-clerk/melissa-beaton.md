## Background

Melissa Beaton, a Democrat, is the incumbent Skagit County Clerk. Her pamphlet statement and campaign site say she was elected in 2019, and the Herald reports she has served as clerk since 2019 [S1][S2][S4]. She grew up in Skagit County and began working for local law firms in high school, later as a legal secretary [S1][S2]. She earned an associate's degree from Skagit Valley College [S2]. She spent about two decades at Skagit County Superior Court, rising from receptionist (the Herald says "services assistant") to Superior Court manager [S1][S2][S4]. She is a Certified Court Manager through the National Center for State Courts [S1][S2]. She is a past president of the Washington State Association of County Clerks, and her campaign says she was named Washington State Clerk of the Year in 2024 [S1][S2]. Her campaign says she has served on the state Board of Judicial Education, the Court Management Council, the Public Trust & Confidence Committee and, by appointment of then-Chief Justice Debra Stephens, the Gender & Justice Commission [S2]. She also serves on the Mount Vernon City Council (her site says she was elected in 2018 and is serving her final term) and the Skagit County Law & Justice Council [S1][S2]. The Herald describes her as having 41 years of legal experience [S4].

## Positions

- Says the clerk's office "operates within the executive branch, independent of the judiciary, serving as the administrative and financial office of Superior Court" [S1].
- Says she manages a $3 million budget and 19 staff and has "worked to modernize services, implement e filing, and build a strong, effective team" [S1].
- Says the office should help people "navigate a complex system with dignity, compassion, and respect" and that the county "deserves a Clerk invested in the community and working every day to make the justice system more accessible" [S1][S2].
- At the October 5 League of Women Voters forum she said a clerk must understand constitutional requirements, statutes, rules of procedure and administrative processes: "You must have a deep understanding of why we operate as we do" [S4].
- Asked about cybersecurity, she said that work is typically done by the county's information services department, not the clerk's office. She said the case management team handles public, restricted and sealed documents and releases them only with proper documentation [S4].
- Named the county budget deficit and staff losses as her main concern: "We lost a full-time staff member last year, we are expected to lose another one to potentially two (full-time equivalents) for 2027" [S4].
- No public statement found on county tax levels.

## Record

- County Clerk since 2019. She says she implemented e-filing and modernized services in that time [S1][S4].
- About two decades in Superior Court administration before that, ending as Superior Court manager [S1][S2].
