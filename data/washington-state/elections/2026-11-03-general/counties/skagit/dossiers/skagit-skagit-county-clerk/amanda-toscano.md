## Background

Amanda Toscano, who lists her party preference as Independent, is a lifelong Skagit County resident and real estate agent. She grew up in a rural working-class family and graduated from Concrete High School [S1][S4]. She earned a degree in Vancouver, Washington, and worked in corporate customer service and management [S1]. She told the October 5 League of Women Voters forum that her education is in travel and hospitality, and that she has corporate management experience and 10 years in real estate [S2]. Her slate page lists her brokerage as C21 North Homes Realty in Burlington [S4]. Asked about working in the court, she said, "Obviously, I've never been there" [S2]; no source found lists a public office she has held. She filed as a write-in candidate for the August 4 primary, when Clerk Melissa Beaton was the only name on the ballot, and qualified for the general with 1,195 write-in votes, about 5% [S2][S3]. She said she entered the race "to give voters a choice," adding: "I feel like I already won because I did make it on the ballot" [S2]. She is one of four write-in candidates promoted together on SkagitResidents.com as "constitutional candidates." The Herald reports the four call themselves "constitutional" candidates [S3][S4].

## Positions

- Calls the clerk "the guardian of the Superior Court record" and says she will be a "citizen representative to the courts" who will "listen to anyone who wants to be heard" so procedures are "clear, consistent, and customer friendly at the counter and online" [S1].
- Says she will "prioritize efficiency, accuracy, and accessibility" and "be a careful steward of public resources, prioritizing needs over wants," while working "to improve transparency and reduce barriers to justice" [S1].
- Asked about cybersecurity at the forum, she listed computer programs she knows and said: "I know for most of the state, things are antiquated and easy to get into, so you've got to check your emails and make sure you're not getting phished" [S2].
- On the job's challenges she said: "Obviously, I've never been there, but I would think that being at court is probably not exactly a happy occasion." She said treating everyone with equal respect is important [S2].
- Before the primary she joined the other three write-in candidates in demanding that the county correct a primary voters' pamphlet that labeled the clerk, coroner and treasurer races "Unopposed" [S5].
