## What the office does
The county clerk is an independently elected executive-branch officer who serves as clerk and administrative and financial officer of Superior Court (S1, S2). The clerk receives, processes and permanently keeps all Superior Court filings and exhibits. The office collects fees, fines, victim restitution and trust funds, issues writs, warrants and letters testamentary, and keeps the minutes of every court session (S2). The office has a $3 million budget and 19 staff (S1). It is a four-year, partisan office.

## Race dynamics
Democrat Melissa Beaton has been clerk since 2019, after about two decades in Superior Court administration (S3; melissa-beaton.md S1). Her endorsers include officials from both parties and the Skagit County Democrats (melissa-beaton.md S3, S5). Independent Amanda Toscano, a real estate agent, filed as a declared write-in candidate after Beaton was the only name on the primary ballot. She qualified for the general with 1,195 write-in votes (about 5%) (S3, S4). She is one of four write-in candidates (sheriff, coroner, treasurer, clerk) promoted as a "constitutional" slate on SkagitResidents.com (S4, S5). At the October 5 League of Women Voters forum, Beaton stressed legal expertise and her concern about budget-driven staff cuts. Toscano stressed customer service and document handling, and said she ran to give voters a choice (S3). The Democrats endorse Beaton (melissa-beaton.md S5). The county Republican Party's general-election list makes no endorsement or recommendation in this race (S6). Beaton has raised about $8.4k, Toscano about $1.3k (melissa-beaton.md S6; amanda-toscano.md S6).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** an incumbent with decades of court administration against a first-time candidate who says she has "never been there." This is the clearest axis.
2. **Spending:** Toscano's pamphlet pledge to prioritize "needs over wants" is one general restraint statement. Beaton describes her office's staff losses under budget cuts but takes no position on county spending levels. Neither candidate has evidence on taxes, housing, safety or climate.
