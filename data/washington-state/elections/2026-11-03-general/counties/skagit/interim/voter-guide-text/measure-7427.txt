## ArgumentFor
Library annexation makes sense for the residents of Sedro-Woolley. It simplifies how we fund library service. Right now the City of Sedro-Woolley pays the Central Skagit Library District so we can use the library at no charge. Annexation means we will be a permanent part of the District and that can’t be changed in the future unless voted on by us.
We are already paying for the library - this allows us to pay the District directly. And the Sedro-Woolley City Council has committed to lowering City property taxes when we approve this ballot measure. Voting yes gives us a say on future District measures - such as bonds or levies. Without annexation, we have no say.
Our library is doing great things for everyone in our community - programming for all ages, resources for job seekers, computer access, low-cost printing, notary services, meeting spaces for large and small groups, board games, mystery night boxes and of course books and movies! The beautiful space on State Street creates a welcome environment for all of us as well as visitors to the area. Annexation allows us to contribute directly to all the resources the library offers.
Join me in voting yes.

## ExplanatoryStatement
If approved, City residents would remain eligible for library services and, beginning January 1, 2027, would be eligible to vote on library district ballot propositions. Because taxing district boundaries for the following tax year are set on August 1, the library district could begin levying property taxes within the city limits in 2028. The City would continue paying for library services through 2027. The City estimates that in 2028 the library district levy rate would be $0.2320 per $1,000 of assessed value and that the district would collect approximately $517,255 from property within the City. Therefore, the City shall reduce its budgeted property tax levy by up to $474,115 in 2028 to offset most of the new library district property tax. Based on current estimates, a home with an assessed value of $519,450 would pay approximately $11.00 more in property tax in 2028. If the proposition is rejected, the City would remain outside the library district and library services would continue to be governed by the interlocal agreement, unless changed by the City and the district. If rejected, City residents would be ineligible to vote on library district ballot propositions, including library district property tax propositions (levy lid lifts).
