Write-ins Want to vote for a write-in candidate? In each contest on your ballot, below the candidate names, there is a dashed line with a box to the left of it. Mark the box and print name of your write-in candidate choice on the line. Why don't the names of write-in candidates show in the results reports? Under Washington law, results for individual declared write-in candidates are tabluated only when there are enough write-in votes to impact the outcome of a race. Until then, all of the write-ins are reported as a single total. Write-in votes for persons who have not filed as a write-in candidate are not individually tabulated. 9 Your Signature Matters Your signature is needed to make sure your ballot gets counted. Your ballot will only be counted if your signature is verified. If you registered to vote online or through Department of Licensing, the signature from your driver’s license, permit, or state ID will be on your voter registration record. The signature on your return envelope is compared to the signature on your voter registration record. Trained election officials are responsible for verifying each and every signature. If your signature is missing or does not match your voter registration record, the county elections office will contact you by mail before results are certified. They may call, text, or email if you gave them this information. Your ballot will only be counted if your signature is verified. You can track and check your ballot status at VoteWA.gov.

FAQs What is a ballot "Measure"? • Ballot measures are anything on a ballot that is not a race between candidates. Measures include initiatives, referenda, constitutional, levies, district formations, etc. • The city or town attorney, or the county prosecuting attorney is responsible for preparing the concise description. • Most levies require tax collection within 12 months of receiving voter approval. General obligation bonds do not have a requirement for collection within 12 months of receiving voter approval. What is an explanatory statement? • An explanatory statement is prepared by the district's attorney; it outlines the effect the ballot measures would have if it is added by the voters. How are committees in favor of or in opposition to a measure formed? • Districts that choose to participate in the local voters' pamphlet are responsible for appointing committee members who agree to write the statements in a way to persuade voters to vote for or against a measure. The Auditor's office does not check statements for factual or grammatical accuracy. *Do not vote on pamphlet

Dixie School Distirct No. 101 | Proposition No. 1 EXPLANATORY STATEMENT PROPOSITION NO. 1 - REPLACEMENT CAPTIAL LEVY FOR HEALTH, SAFETY AND ENERGY EFFICIENCY IMPROVEMENTS Passage of Proposition No. 1 would allow the Dixie School District to replace an existing capital levy that will expire at the end of calendar year 2026. The replacement levy will be used to support the repairing, modernization, and remodeling of school facilities by continuing to fund health, safety and energy efficiency improvements at Dixie School. Contact Us! 509-524-2530 elections@wwcowa.gov WHAT IS A SIGNATURE CHALLENGE? In our vote-by-mail system, we check a voter’s identity by matching the signature on their ballot to the signatures in their voter registration record. A voter’s signature is a unique way to confirm their identity. If a voter forgets to sign their ballot, or if trained staff find a mismatch between the ballot signature and the one on record, the ballot is held, or “challenged,” until the voter can resolve the issue. The ballot stays unopened and is securely stored.
