## Background

Richard Schram has worked at the Walla Walla County Sheriff's Office for more than 23 years, rising from corrections deputy to patrol deputy, patrol sergeant and chief deputy; he has served in command for more than eight years, appointed chief deputy under two sheriffs (S1, S2, S5). He oversees deputies and detectives, operations, hiring, training and policy, works on the office's budget and grants, and is the SWAT commander (S2, S5). He has a B.S. in criminal justice and graduated from the FBI National Academy (Session 287) (S1, S5). He chairs the SEATech criminal justice advisory committee and serves on Walla Walla Community College's (S1). He is 44, lives in Burbank and prefers the Republican Party (S1, S5).

In the certified August 4 primary he received 8,778 votes (61.93%) (S9).

## Positions

### Public safety and community policing
- Priorities: "The safety of our loved ones and neighbors. The prosperity and security of our businesses. The protection of our individual freedoms." He says he will "uphold our laws with integrity, strengthen community partnerships, and prioritize recruiting and retaining" staff (S1).
- Wants deputies who are "proactive, visible, and engaged," with routine foot patrols so residents know deputies by name (S3). At the Waitsburg forum: "We need to pull over, get out of the car, shake hands and find out what is going on in your neighborhood" (S7).
- Says keeping the county safe encourages business growth, which funds competitive contracts (S5).

### Budget and staffing
- Calls the constrained county budget the office's critical issue: both unions are out of contract, and without competitive wages the office faces turnover; "current county revenue cannot bridge this gap alone. The long-term solution requires economic growth" (S5).
- Promises "Disciplined Budgeting": "every request is based on real operational needs and every dollar spent is clearly justified" (S3).

### Immigration enforcement
- "Our federal partners know we will not plan or carry out missions to apprehend people for ICE detainers"; state law "prohibits us from enforcing civil immigration detainers" (S5). The office would help ICE only if agents were under attack: "we will go and stop violence. But that is it" (S6).
- Rejects the idea that sheriffs choose which laws to enforce (S6).

### Policy
- Has been the office's primary policy writer for eight years; will keep policies compliant with state and federal law and evaluate Attorney General model policies that go beyond what the law requires, adopting provisions that are "lawful, practical, and appropriate" for the county (S3).
