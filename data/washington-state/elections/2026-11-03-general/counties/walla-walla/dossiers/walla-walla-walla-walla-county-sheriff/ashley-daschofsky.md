## Background

Ashley Daschofsky was a Marine Corps military police officer from 2005 to 2010, a College Place police officer from 2012 to 2016, and has been a Walla Walla County deputy sheriff since 2016 (S1, S2). His experience includes 911 dispatching, collision investigation, field training and K9 handling (2017-2025) (S1, S2). The Washington Traffic Safety Commission named him its DUI enforcement officer of the year six times (2016 and 2021-2025), and he received a 2026 lifesaving award for an off-duty rescue from a vehicle fire (S1, S2). He completed the FBI-LEEDA supervisor, command and executive leadership institutes (S1, S2). He is 42, a Walla Walla High School graduate, lives in College Place, has not held elected office and prefers the Republican Party (S1, S3).

In the certified August 4 primary he received 5,235 votes (36.93%) (S8).

The Union-Bulletin reported on August 21 that he is on the Prosecuting Attorney's Potential Impeachment Disclosure list. According to a letter from Prosecuting Attorney Gabe Acosta, a Sheriff's Office internal investigator found that his statements to another deputy about the results of an internal investigation were "false, malicious or disparaging" and motivated by "a longstanding disgruntlement over having been passed over for promotion." He was added after a review completed March 26, 2025, but was not notified until a July 2026 letter. Daschofsky says his statements were accurate, and the listing does not involve a use-of-force incident or an investigation he conducted (S4).

## Positions

### Leadership and staffing
- "Over the last eight years the Sheriff's Office has continued to lose employees as fast as we have hired them." He blames the current command staff, saying "it's time for a change at the command staff level" (S3).
- Would "significantly" reduce command-level positions and repurpose those salaries to victim advocates and code enforcement, which the office does not now provide (S1, S3). His pamphlet statement: "I will slash bloated administrative positions and redirect every dollar to real priorities" (S1).
- Will lead "from the front, on the street," not "behind a desk in an office" (S1, S3).

### Public safety
- Priorities: community first, leadership, transparency (public reporting on crime trends, response times and use of force), accountability, and "proactive policing": "intelligence-led enforcement" and visible presence, "while treating everyone fairly and without bias" (S2).
- Pamphlet: "keeping families safe and holding criminals accountable" (S1).

### Immigration enforcement
- Help to ICE would be "limited to public safety and stopping incidents of violence only." ICE agents in the county "will be held to the same standards I will hold my deputies to" (S3).
- Says crime victims fear reporting because of immigration enforcement: "It breaks my heart." He said he has chosen a Hispanic undersheriff who would reach out to those communities (S5).
- Rejects the idea that sheriffs choose which laws to enforce (S5).
