## What the office does

The sheriff runs the Walla Walla County Sheriff's Office, the county's law enforcement agency, and is the county's highest-paid elected official: $156,563 in 2027 (S3). Both candidates describe the job as executive leadership of deputies, detectives, the budget and policy (S1, S3).

## Race dynamics

Sheriff Mark Crider, first elected in 2018, did not file for reelection, so the seat is open (S4). Both finalists work for him and both prefer the Republican Party (S1, S4). Chief Deputy Richard Schram has 23 years at the office and is endorsed by Crider, the undersheriff and the Commissioned Deputies Association. Deputy Ashley Daschofsky, a Marine veteran and K9 handler with the office since 2016, says the command staff has failed to stop turnover (S1, S3). In the certified August primary, with both guaranteed to advance, Schram received 8,778 votes (61.93%) and Daschofsky 5,235 (36.93%) (S2).

In August the Union-Bulletin reported that the Prosecuting Attorney placed Daschofsky on its Potential Impeachment Disclosure ("Brady") list in March 2025, after an internal investigation found his statements to a co-worker about another internal investigation "false, malicious or disparaging." He says what he said was accurate and that he was never notified at the time (S5).

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Experience/continuity:** Schram is the command-staff insider who promises to build on the office's culture. Daschofsky says "what we are doing is not working, and it's time for a change at the command staff level" (S3).
2. **Spending/staffing:** Schram stresses disciplined, justified budget requests and competitive wages in a tight county budget. Daschofsky would cut command-level positions and move those salaries to victim advocates and code enforcement (S1, S3).
3. **Safety:** both are enforcement-oriented and community-policing focused. Both say the office will not enforce civil immigration law beyond stopping violence, and both reject the idea that sheriffs choose which laws to enforce (S3, S6).
4. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives: none found for either candidate.
