## Background

Nicholas Holce was a Walla Walla County deputy prosecuting attorney from 2013 to 2020. He has been a criminal defense attorney since 2020 and an appellate criminal defense attorney since 2025, and has served as a judge pro tempore in superior and district court (S1, S2). He is secretary/treasurer of the Washington State Bar Association's Criminal Law Section (on its committee since 2020) and was an associate editor of the Gonzaga Law Review (S2). He earned an AA from Walla Walla Community College (2007), a BA in English from Whitman College (2009) and a JD from Gonzaga University School of Law (2013) (S2). He serves on the board of the Little Theatre of Walla Walla and was board president of Walla Walla Community Hospice (2017) (S2).

He ran for this seat in 2022 and lost to Jared Hawkins (about 62% to 37% on election night); the Union-Bulletin then described him as a public defender and former deputy prosecutor (S3). He filed again in 2026 for the seat Hawkins is leaving (S6).

## Judicial approach (from own statements)

- Says his work as both prosecutor and court-appointed defense attorney qualifies him. He has argued search, seizure and evidence motions in district court, superior court and the Court of Appeals; tried DUI, reckless driving, poaching and property cases in district court; and represented clients on both sides of protection orders (S2).
- Notes the district judge reviews and authorizes search warrants. As a prosecutor he defended warrants against challenges and helped review them before submission (S2).
- At the Waitsburg forum he said district court is designed to help people "that are in the community now and will be in the community tomorrow," and that a court "more engaged with all the different pathways of employment opportunities, housing opportunities" would improve justice. He recalled regular meetings of the court with prosecutors, jail staff and treatment workers (S5).
- At the AAUW forum he supported more treatment options for non-felony offenders and declined to give a personal opinion on a local issue, saying the judge's job is to apply the law (S4).
- In 2022 he said bringing treatment courts to district court "is something that motivated me to run" (S3).
