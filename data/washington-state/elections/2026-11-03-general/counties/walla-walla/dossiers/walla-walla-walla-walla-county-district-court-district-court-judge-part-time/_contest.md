## What the office does

Walla Walla County District Court is the lower court for the county and the cities of Walla Walla and College Place. It hears misdemeanors, traffic infractions, small claims, protection orders and civil disputes that do not involve real estate (S3). It has two judges: a full-time presiding judge and a part-time judge who carries a smaller caseload and fills in when the full-time judge is unavailable (S3, S4). The part-time judge may keep practicing law (S3). The court runs a Community Court drug-court program that lets defendants defer punishment for treatment (S3).

## Race dynamics

The seat is open. Full-time Judge Kristian Hedine is retiring, and part-time Judge Jared Hawkins is running unopposed for the full-time seat (S2, S3). Two local attorneys filed for the part-time seat, so there was no primary (S2). Nicholas Holce is a former Walla Walla County deputy prosecutor and, since 2020, a criminal defense attorney; he lost the 2022 race for this seat to Hawkins (S1, S4). Janelle Carman-Wagner, a former Assistant Attorney General, has run a general practice in Walla Walla since 2005 and has sat as a superior court judge pro tempore since 2015 (record in her dossier). The Union-Bulletin reported that the forum questions "did not bring out differences in the candidates" (S5).

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Safety (treatment vs accountability):** both support more treatment options for non-felony offenders (S5). Carman-Wagner stresses holding people accountable and sending them to reliable treatment instead of "superficial" programs. Holce stresses a court engaged with employment, housing and treatment pathways for people "in the community now" (record in the candidate dossiers).
2. **Experience/formation:** Holce: prosecution, then defense and appeals, and judge pro tempore service in district and superior court. Carman-Wagner: civil, family, administrative and criminal practice and more than 20 superior court pro tem appointments. Neither has been elected to the bench.
