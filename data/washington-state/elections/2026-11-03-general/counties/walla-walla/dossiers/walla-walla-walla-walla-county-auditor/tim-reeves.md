## Background

Tim Reeves is a retired economist, corporate facilitator and finance consultant. He says he has "grown teams at companies including Boeing and Microsoft" and managed the books and payroll for his wife's small businesses (S1, S2). He holds a BA and MA in economics and an MBA in finance (S1). He serves on the Walla Walla Parks, Recreation and Urban Forestry Advisory Board and the board of the Walla Walla Arts Collaborative, and took part in the Civics Academy and Police Citizens Academy (S1, S3). He is 74, has not held elected office, and prefers the Democratic Party (S1, S3).

In the certified August 4 primary he placed second with 5,727 votes (36.86%) (S8).

## Positions

### Office management and reporting
- "Something is broken in the Auditor's office, and it's not the skilled and dedicated staff." He cites commissioners' frustration over "the lack of timely, accurate, detailed reporting of the county's expenses at a time when costs are exceeding revenues" (S1, S2).
- Says the most recent annual report on the auditor's website is dated 2020 and that delays in moving to Workday, the county's new financial software, risk the old license expiring before the new system is tested, "a $4 million failure" (S3).
- Proposes reporting actual-to-budget performance through the year, a public "dashboard" of key performance indicators and quarterly "State of the County" updates (S4). At the Waitsburg forum he said financial information should be disclosed within 10 days, "just like it's done in the corporate world" (S6).
- Wants the office to move "from transactional record keeping and reporting to one of strategic adviser," taking a multiyear view and helping departments prioritize spending (S4).
- Priorities: build trust, promote customer service, timely reporting, "embrace technology to streamline processes and reduce costs," and protect elections (S2).

### Elections
- Said at the AAUW forum: "A request for voter information that's not authorized by the Secretary of State's office must be refused and reported," adding that in some places local officials "have succumbed to pressure to share data or interfere with the voting process. We cannot let that happen here" (S5).
- Says the auditor has no plan for a possible disruption to mail-in voting (S3).
