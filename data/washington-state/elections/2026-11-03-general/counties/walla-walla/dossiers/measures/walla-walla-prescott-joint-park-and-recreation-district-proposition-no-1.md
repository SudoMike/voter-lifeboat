## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would authorize the Prescott Joint Park and Recreation District to levy $175,000 of excess property taxes for its maintenance and operation expenses during 2027, as provided in the board's Resolution No. 7-14-2026 B [S1][S2][S3]. It is a one-year operations and maintenance levy under RCW 84.52.052, in addition to regular property taxes. The explanatory statement calls it "the primary source of funding used by the Prescott Joint Park and Recreation District to operate and maintain its facilities" [S1].

- **The district.** It covers parts of Walla Walla and Columbia counties [S2][S4] and has operated for 67 years [S6]. Its main facility is the Prescott community pool at 303 W. Second St., which the Union-Bulletin describes as the only public pool of its kind between Dayton and Burbank; it recorded 2,529 swim visits in the 2024 season [S6].
- **History.** The district asks voters for this levy every year [S4]. In 2024 voters approved $145,000 (about $0.31 per $1,000) [S4]. The November 2025 request, the same $175,000 at $0.35, received 122 yes and 83 no (59.51%) in Walla Walla County's certified results, short of the 60% it needed, and failed [S5]. The 2026 argument for describes a summer without the pool: "families had to drive to other towns for swim lessons" [S1].

## Cost

- $175,000 collected in 2027, approximately $0.35 per $1,000 of assessed value [S1][S3]. At that rate the Union-Bulletin estimated the 2025 version at about $70 a year on a $200,000 home [S4].
- One year only; a no vote means no additional levy [S1].
- An excess levy of this kind needs 60% approval [S4][S5].

## Arguments for

Written by committee members Hailey Hudson and Debby Phillips: the pool is where "kids learn to swim, families make memories, teenagers find summer jobs, and neighbors gather"; "Voting YES ... is about making sure our kids don't have to experience another summer without these opportunities"; "Once a community pool is gone, it's incredibly difficult to bring it back" [S1].

## Arguments against

None submitted; no committee against was named [S1][S2].

The district has sought the levy annually, but the 2025 levy failed, so no excess levy is being collected for 2026 and approval restarts the tax. The amount equals the failed 2025 request and exceeds the last approved one ($145,000). Direction +2, as for other new or restored levies [S1][S4][S5].
