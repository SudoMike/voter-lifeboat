## What the office does

The three commissioners are the county's policymakers and executives: they direct long-range planning, make budget decisions, set land-use and environmental policy, manage county roads, oversee public health, set property tax levies, and hire and supervise department managers (S4). The 2027 salary is about $122,315 and the term runs through 2030 (S4).

## Race dynamics

Commissioners are nominated by district but elected countywide in the general (S4, S6). In the August primary only District 3 voters (College Place to Burbank) took part; the 2022 District 3 general was on all 62 county precincts (S2, S3). Incumbent Gunner Fulmer led the primary with 2,339 votes (44.85%); Troy Woody had 1,565 (30.01%) and independent Rikki Wescott 1,296 (24.85%) (S2). Both finalists prefer the Republican Party (S1).

The county has a structural budget gap: a hiring freeze and department cuts in the last two budget cycles have not closed it (S6). The commissioners, Fulmer among them, have declined to consider a moratorium on Amazon's planned data center campus at the Port of Walla Walla's Wallula Gap Business Park, which some residents oppose (S5).

## Differentiating issue axes

1. **Reform:** Woody's central plank is a charter-county vote to replace the three full-time commissioners with five part-time ones, hire a county administrator and make five elected department heads appointed. He also pledges to serve at most two terms. Fulmer stresses relationships and working through the Washington State Association of Counties, which he will lead as president in 2027 if reelected (S1, S4).
2. **Experience:** Fulmer is a first-term incumbent with nearly 30 years as a firefighter-paramedic and police officer. Woody, a business owner and former school board member, runs as "not a career politician" (S1, S4).
3. **Taxes and spending:** both reject tax increases as the fix for the deficit. Fulmer says he voted against seeking relief from the 1% property tax cap and wants the state to pay more of indigent defense. Woody calls the deficit "a structural problem, not a revenue problem" and proposes a zero-based budget review (S4, S6).
4. **Permitting/housing:** both want faster permitting. Woody would expand permit-office hours and drop permits such as those for replacing windows; Fulmer cites new permitting software and state barriers to building (S4, S6).
5. Positions on the 2026 statewide initiatives: none found for either candidate.
