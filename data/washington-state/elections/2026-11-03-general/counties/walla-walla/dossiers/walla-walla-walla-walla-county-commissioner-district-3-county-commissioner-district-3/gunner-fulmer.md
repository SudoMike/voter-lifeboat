## Background

Gunner Fulmer has been the District 3 commissioner since January 2023 and chaired the board in 2025 (S1). He chairs the Valley Transit board and is vice chair of the Benton-Franklin-Walla Walla County Good Roads board (S1). County commissioners statewide chose him for a four-year leadership track at the Washington State Association of Counties; if reelected he will be sworn in as its president in January 2027 (S1, S2). A 1991 Walla Walla High School graduate and Navy veteran, he worked at the Walla Walla Regional Airport fire and security department, was a City of Walla Walla firefighter/paramedic from 1996 to 2008, and was a Walla Walla police officer from 2008 until retiring as administrative sergeant at the end of 2022 (S1). He lives in Touchet (S2) and prefers the Republican Party (S1).

In the August 4 primary, voted in District 3 only, he placed first with 2,339 votes (44.85%) (S9).

## Positions

### Budget and taxes
- "I do not believe simply raising taxes is the answer." He wants to understand the causes of the budget shortfall and "pursue long-term solutions rather than relying solely on increased taxes" (S2).
- "When counties recently voted on seeking relief from the property tax cap, I voted 'No' because I do not believe higher taxes are the solution" (S3).
- On primary night he said he plans to keep working on the budget "without raising taxes for citizens" (S7).
- Wants the state to take on more of the cost of enforcing state laws, especially indigent defense, which he calls the top issue facing residents; he says the state pays less than 5% of it (S3). At the Waitsburg forum he said counties are "taking the fight to the legislators" and that "counties are going broke across the state" (S6).

### Growth, permitting and the data center
- Will keep improving the planning and permitting department, building on new permitting software, and will work with legislators on state barriers "that make it difficult and expensive for individuals and businesses to build and operate in Washington" (S2).
- Opposed a moratorium on data centers before the State Environmental Policy Act review (S3). At the September 14 meeting he and the other two commissioners declined to consider a one-year moratorium on Amazon's planned campus. He said the county cannot "treat one business differently than another," that the site is designed for such a project, and asked, "Pause a project … for what?" (S4). At the AAUW forum he said Amazon had not been forthcoming enough and that he was "eyes-wide-open on this project" (S5).

### Governing approach
- Stresses relationships and collaboration: "I think that's what got me here to begin with, the relationships that I've built" (S5). Pledges to strengthen partnerships with the cities of Walla Walla and College Place and the Port of Walla Walla (S1, S2).
