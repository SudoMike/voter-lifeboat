## Background

Troy Woody owns Hot Solar Solutions and HSS Electric and is the Mr. Electric franchisee in Kennewick; he started his business about 12 years ago (S1, S3). He was raised on a dryland wheat farm, graduated from Connell High School, spent 20 years in hotel management, mostly for Red Lion Hotels, and then managed Franklin County's TRAC facility for 10 years (S3). He served two terms on the Columbia (Burbank) School Board (S1, S3). He is a Rotary member, a judo coach and a 25-year county resident with seven children (S1, S3). He lives in Burbank and prefers the Republican Party (S1). (The Union-Bulletin gave his age as 51 in July and 61 in October.) (S3, S4)

In the August 4 primary, voted in District 3 only, he placed second with 1,565 votes (30.01%) (S8).

## Positions

### Charter reform and term limits
- His top priority is putting charter-county reform to a public vote: five part-time commissioners in place of three full-time ones, a hired county administrator, and appointed professionals in place of five elected "technical" offices. He estimates savings of about $200,000-$225,000 a year in salaries (S1, S2, S3).
- "Success for me is that if I'm elected, I take a pay cut" (S5). Says that if the reform passes his pay would fall from $120,000 to $30,000-$35,000 (S3).
- Commits to a maximum of two terms, "hopefully only one" (S1, S2). "I am not a politician" (S1).
- His site posts letters showing the Walla Walla County Republican Central Committee declined to endorse him; it quotes the chair's response that his charter-county platform "does not align with the traditional Republican platform" (S2).

### Budget and taxes
- "The county budget has grown 152% since 2008 — nearly twice the cumulative inflation rate — and we still can't balance it. That's a structural problem, not a revenue problem." Proposes a zero-based budget review of every department and scrutiny of every contract (S4).
- Wants counties and cities to form a coalition to sue the state over unfunded mandates under RCW 43.135.060 (S4, S6).

### Permits and regulation
- Would extend the county permit office's 10 a.m.-3 p.m. hours, expand online permits and drop permits "that no longer serve a clear purpose — like requiring a permit to change windows in your own home" (S3, S4). Would "hold the line on any new regulations that don't have a clear public safety justification" (S4).
- "Economic growth starts with getting government out of the way"; would protect property rights and agricultural land use (S3).

### Data center
- Does not support a blanket moratorium, but would require data centers "to fully demonstrate their infrastructure impact before approval — water, power, roads and emergency services" (S4). At the AAUW forum he said he supported the Amazon project as a benefit to the county (S5) and called it "a fairly low-impact massive influx of revenue" (S6).

## Endorsements

None found from organizations; the county Republican Central Committee declined to endorse him (S2). He was his own largest contributor ($7,965) as of July 24 and led the race in fundraising ($11,990 raised as of Sept. 29) (S3, S7).

- Experience: business owner and former school board member running explicitly as an outsider against "career politicians".
