## BallotTitle
Timberland Regional Library District

Proposition No. 1

Regular Property Tax Levy Lid Lift for Library Services, Operations and Maintenance

The Timberland Regional Library District's Board of Trustees adopted Resolution No. 26-002 concerning a proposed increase of the District's regular levy rate.

If approved, this proposition would restore the District's regular property tax levy rate for library services, operations and maintenance from $0.22 to $0.35 per $1,000 of assessed valuation for both 2027 and 2028, subject to applicable limitations.  The resulting 2028 levy dollar amount would be used for the purpose of computing subsequent levy limitations under chapter 84.55 RCW.

Should this proposition be approved?

## ExplanatoryStatement
State law allows Timberland Regional Library District (the “Library”) to annually collect up to $0.50 per $1,000 of assessed property value to fund Library services, operations, maintenance and improvements. Due to tax limitation laws, the Library has collected at a lower rate for many years. The Library last requested an increase in 2009, but that resolution was not adopted.
Approval of Proposition 1 would authorize the Library to reset its regular property tax levy from $0.228924 to $0.35 per $1,000 of assessed value in 2027 and 2028. For example, the estimated increase for a home valued at $334,000 is about $40.44 annually, or $3.37 monthly. The resulting 2028 levy amount would be used to calculate all applicable tax limitations thereafter.
If approved by voters, Proposition 1 would provide funding necessary to maintain Library services, staffing, collections, and open hours. If not approved, further reductions in staffing, books and materials, hours, online services, bookmobile services and programming will begin in 2027, and maintenance and improvements of Library facilities could also be delayed.

## FiscalStatement


## ArgumentFor
Timberland Regional Library (TRL) has 29 library branches in five counties, plus the traveling Anywhere Library that visits places like community centers, churches and assisted living facilities. These branches operate as a community crossroads of knowledge and fun. Great children’s sections keep kids learning year-round. Teen Spaces provide safe places for studying, crafting and making friends. Free branch programs support job-hunters and retirees. Patrons can check out books, movies, games, nature backpacks, assistive devices and more.
However, TRL is now at a crossroads of a different kind: one of funding and costs. About 96% of its revenue comes from a property tax levy that hasn’t increased in 25 years. The value of that income has dwindled, nibbled away by higher prices for materials, maintenance, salaries, even electricity. State law caps the amount libraries can collect on increasing property values at 1% annually until voters lift the lid.

This measure would increase TRL’s levy from $0.22 to $0.35 per $1,000 of property value. This means taxes on a home valued at $333,733 would increase by about $3.58 per month or $43 per year. After a quarter-century, it’s a long overdue cost-of-living raise.

With your help, TRL can restore hours and staffing. It can expand local book, ebook, music and movie collections, and add community programs that are the heart of any thriving local library. Please vote yes to lift the levy lid and bring library funding closer to today’s reality.

## RebuttalFor
Libraries matter, but raising property taxes without accountability is wrong. I’m urging a “No” vote because taxpayers should not shoulder higher property taxes without transparency and accountability. Many services provided by libraries are delivered through nonprofits and community organizations more efficiently and effectively.  Responsible budgeting and oversight are necessary to ensure taxpayer dollars benefit the community without waste, mismanagement, or unnecessary duplication of services and resources. Vote “No”.

## ArgumentAgainst
A "No" vote on the Timberland Regional Library levy lid lift is a vote for fiscal responsibility, taxpayer accountability, and careful budgeting. Property owners throughout Washington have experienced rising tax bills due to increasing property values and voter-approved levies for schools, fire districts, parks, and other local governments. At a time when many families, seniors on fixed incomes, and small businesses are struggling with higher costs for housing, groceries, insurance, and utilities, another property tax increase deserves scrutiny.
Before asking taxpayers for additional revenue, the library should demonstrate that it has fully explored opportunities to reduce costs, improve operational efficiency, prioritize essential services, seek grants and private partnerships, and better manage existing resources. This is about priorities, and a property tax could put future investments in schools and emergency services at risk.
Libraries provide important educational, cultural, and community services, but supporting libraries does not require supporting every request for higher taxes. Taxpayers have a reasonable expectation that existing dollars will be spent efficiently before additional burdens are placed on homeowners and businesses. A "No" vote encourages the library to focus on its core mission, evaluate programs and staffing levels, and identify long-term needs.
Voting "No" is not a vote against libraries or literacy. It is a vote for responsible financial stewardship and accountability. Everyone's budget is tight, voters must prioritize investments, and the library should first demonstrate prudent management of existing resources before asking voters to approve higher property taxes, especially when households are already facing significant financial pressures.
