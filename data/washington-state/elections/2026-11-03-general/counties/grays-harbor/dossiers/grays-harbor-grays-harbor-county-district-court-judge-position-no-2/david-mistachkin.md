## Background

David Mistachkin has been a licensed Washington attorney since November 2003 [S3]. He spent about 15 years at the Aberdeen firm Ingram, Zelasko & Goodwin, mostly in criminal defense and family law, and became a partner in January 2014 [S3][S4]. The Daily World's 2016 profile described about 11 years there as a criminal defense attorney, with civil, family and personal-injury cases on the side [S4]. He has served as a court commissioner for the Grays Harbor and Pacific County superior courts and as a pro tem judge in nearly every municipal court in the county [S1][S3]. His voters'-guide statement lists a J.D. and B.A. from Willamette University, both dated 2000 [S1]. The Daily World has reported, and he told the paper, that he graduated from law school in 2003 [S3][S4][S6]. He has served on the board of Habitat for Humanity of Grays Harbor, including as president, coaches youth soccer and sits on the Montesano Youth Soccer Club board [S1][S3]. The supplemental county pamphlet carries the same statement as the VoteWA guide [S2]. His VoteWA entry lists a Facebook page as its website [S1]; social media is not used as a source here.

## Positions

- **Judicial approach.** Says courts "should apply the law fairly, protect the public trust, and treat every person with dignity and respect" [S1]. Says judges should take time to explain decisions so participants understand the process and the outcome [S3].
- **Top priority: confidence in the court.** Says his "absolute top priority" is a higher level of public confidence in the court system, and hopes to "restore what was once a sterling reputation for the court system in our county" [S3].
- **Court staff and justice partners.** Lists improving staff morale as his second priority. Also wants better relationships with law enforcement, the prosecutor's office, the public defense office and the county commissioners, through "a collaborative approach to conflict resolution" [S3]. Says he wants to return to "the days when staff morale was high, and attorneys liked practicing law here rather than dreading it" and to "restore common sense local court rules that promote access to justice" [S3].
- **Sentencing and repeat offenses (2016, as a sitting District Court judge).** Said deferred prosecution and mitigation can help keep people from reoffending, and that within statutory limits he tries "to fashion sentences that will be most effective in preventing people from being repeat offenders," including addressing "whatever the underlying issue may be" [S4].

## Record

- **District Court judge, 2015-2016.** Appointed by the county commissioners and took the bench Jan. 2, 2015 [S1][S4]. Lost the November 2016 election for District Court #1 to Kyle Imler, 10,615 votes (45.85%) to 12,539 (54.15%) [S5].
- **Superior Court judge, 2018-2024/2025.** Defeated appointed Judge Ray Kahler for Superior Court Position 3 in November 2018 with 12,398 votes (51.17%) in the reported count [S6]. Re-elected unopposed in 2020 [S7]. Lost the November 2024 election to Steve Jackson, 14,734 votes (47.45%) to 16,119 (51.91%) [S8][S9]. His statement gives his Superior Court service as 2018-2025; his Q&A answer gives 2018-2024 [S1][S3].
- Says that as a judge he presided over criminal, family law and therapeutic court matters, and that he has handled jury trials from misdemeanors to murder as lawyer and judge [S1][S3].
- **Federal lawsuit.** In October 2025 he sued Grays Harbor County and three sheriff's deputies in U.S. District Court. He alleges they arrested him for DUI without probable cause on Feb. 12, 2024, although a breath test showed 0.017 blood alcohol, below the 0.08 legal limit [S9]. He was then still a Superior Court judge [S1][S8]. The Daily World report does not give the outcome of the arrest or the lawsuit [S9].
- No published rulings or appellate reversals were reviewed.
