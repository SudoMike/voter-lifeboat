## What the office does

District court has jurisdiction, alongside superior court, over misdemeanors and gross misdemeanors committed in the county, with sentences capped at a $5,000 fine and one year in jail unless a statute provides otherwise. It also hears traffic infractions, holds preliminary hearings, approves bail and arraigns defendants on warrants from other limited-jurisdiction courts [S10]. Grays Harbor County District Court sits in two locations, District Court 1 in Montesano and District Court 2 in Aberdeen, and also handles general civil, name-change and small-claims cases, protection orders and probation [S9]. The seat is countywide and carries a four-year term [S1].

## Race dynamics

- **Candidates.** Incumbent Megan Valentine was appointed in 2021 and elected unopposed in 2022 [S1][S2][S5]. David Mistachkin is a former Grays Harbor District Court judge (2015-2016) and Superior Court judge (2018 to 2024/2025) [S1][S3].
- **How the challenger left the bench.** Mistachkin lost his 2024 re-election bid for Superior Court Position 3 to Steve Jackson, 16,119 votes (51.91%) to 14,734 (47.45%) [S4][S6].
- **No primary.** With only two candidates filed, the race skipped the August primary and went straight to the general [S13].
- **Court operations are the main dispute.** Valentine served three and a half years as presiding judge, overseeing District Court operations [S3]. During that time the court had public disputes with the county clerk over jury administration (February 2025) and with the county commissioners over staffing, which led the court to cancel three days of criminal dockets in July 2025 [S7][S8]. In September 2026 the court cut phone and counter hours, saying it had about 69% of its approved staffing [S9]. Mistachkin's stated priorities are restoring confidence in the court, improving staff morale and repairing the court's relationships with law enforcement, the prosecutor, public defense and the commissioners [S3]. Valentine runs on programs she started: the DUI Therapeutic Court, pretrial monitoring, the Blake refund event and a new case-management system [S1][S3][S14].
- **Money.** As of the latest PDC summaries, Valentine reported $19,385 raised and $14,738 spent [S11]; Mistachkin reported $4,625 raised and $4,062 spent [S12].
- **Bar ratings.** No bar-association rating for this race was found.

## What genuinely differentiates the candidates

- **Experience (`experience`).** Both have judicial records. Valentine is the sitting judge with five years on the District Court bench and three and a half as presiding judge [S1][S3]. Mistachkin has about eight years on the bench in two courts, ending with his 2024 defeat, plus service as a court commissioner and pro tem judge [S1][S3][S4].
- **Professional formation and diversion posture (`safety`).** Valentine practiced as a prosecutor and private attorney and represented foster youth; as judge she founded the DUI Therapeutic Court, which pairs treatment with intensive supervision, and added pretrial monitoring to probation services [S1][S3][S14]. Mistachkin spent most of his practice as a criminal defense and family law attorney and says he presided over therapeutic court matters as a judge [S1][S3].
- **Judicial approach (`judicial`).** Neither statement is specific enough to place a candidate on the as-written vs. pragmatic axis. Valentine says a judge "should not substitute their judgment for the judgment of the elected prosecutor or jurors" [S3]; Mistachkin says courts should "apply the law fairly" [S1].

## Not differentiating

- Both pledge to treat everyone in court with dignity and respect [S1][S3].
- Both have taught or volunteered with youth programs [S1][S3].
