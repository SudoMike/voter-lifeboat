## Background

Megan Valentine is the incumbent judge in District Court Position 2. She was appointed in 2021 and elected in 2022, and says she is the county's first female District Court judge [S1]. She reports more than 20 years of legal practice in Grays Harbor County as a private attorney, a prosecutor and an advocate for children and families, covering criminal, family and civil matters [S1][S3]. She told The Daily World her career has also included running a business and representing foster youth [S3]. She holds a law degree from Cornell, an MBA from Eastern Washington University and a B.A. from the University of Washington [S1]. Her community service includes friends of the library and teaching youth about law [S1], including through Judges in the Classroom [S3]. The supplemental county pamphlet carries the same statement as the VoteWA guide [S2].

## Positions

- **Judicial approach.** Says a judge must "listen carefully, remain fair and impartial, and make decisions based on the law and the facts" [S3]. Says a judge "should not substitute their judgment for the judgment of the elected prosecutor or jurors and must respect the decisions of others" [S3]. Her statement opens with "Our state and federal constitutions rely on the courts to safeguard your rights and liberties" [S1].
- **Public safety and treatment courts.** Says she will prioritize public safety "by using proven resources to reduce repeat offenses," citing the DUI Therapeutic Court, whose purpose she describes as breaking "the cycle of impaired driving" [S3]. Describes the therapeutic court and pretrial monitoring as reforms that address offenses "through accountability and treatment" and are steps toward "preventing crime and saving taxpayer dollars" [S1].
- **Access to the courts.** Lists access as a priority and points to the new electronic case-management system, coordination with other courts on rescheduling, and more accessible parking [S3].
- **Use of public money.** Says she will draw on her business background to use public resources responsibly, and that she has obtained state reimbursement for court programs through the District and Municipal Court Judges' Association [S3]. Says she secured nearly $1 million in state funding for local programs over five years [S1].

## Record

- Appointed in 2021; elected unopposed in November 2022 with 18,078 votes (97.61%) [S1][S4].
- Presiding judge of District Court for three and a half years, overseeing its operations [S3].
- **DUI Therapeutic Court.** Says she founded it [S1][S3]. It took its first participants in May 2025 and provides intensive supervision, treatment and accountability for people charged with DUI. Participants get a shorter probation term and reduced jail time, fines and fees. It is funded through reimbursement from the state Administrative Office of the Courts [S5].
- **Pretrial monitoring.** Says she expanded District Court probation services to include pretrial monitoring [S1].
- **Blake refunds.** As presiding judge she led a Sept. 30, 2025 Blake Refund Day. The court found about 2,500 District Court cases eligible for refunds of legal financial obligations, nearly $550,000 in all, after the 2021 State v. Blake ruling struck down the drug-possession statute [S6][S7]. She said it is "the court's responsibility, and privilege, to help restore fairness" to people affected [S6].
- **Online services.** In March 2025 the court launched online protection-order petitions and email requests for mitigation or contested hearings [S12].
- **Jury dispute with the county clerk.** In February 2025 she presented the county commissioners with a plan, costing roughly $140,000, for District Court to take over its own jury administration after County Clerk Kym Foster said her office would stop providing it. She left the meeting during Foster's remarks [S8]. In March the clerk agreed to keep the work, with District Court reimbursing the costs [S9].
- **Staffing dispute with the commissioners.** In July 2025 the court cancelled three days of criminal dockets, citing the commissioners' refusal to put its request to fill a vacant criminal deputy administrator position on their agenda. County officials said District Court had earlier eliminated its court administrator position [S10]. Valentine said delaying the hire "puts the fairness and safety of the system at risk" [S10].
- In September 2026 the court cut phone and counter hours, citing about 69% of approved staffing and a new state-required case-management system [S11].
- No published rulings or appellate reversals were reviewed.

## Scoring notes

- Sitting judge and presiding judge, running on a five-year record of court programs and administration [S1][S3].
- Formation: prosecutor, private practice and foster-youth advocacy. Founded a treatment-based DUI court and added pretrial monitoring, both framed as accountability plus treatment [S1][S3][S5].
- The court's public disputes with the clerk and commissioners over juries and staffing took place while she was presiding judge [S3][S8][S10].
- Her judicial-approach statements (deference to prosecutors and juries; courts as guardians of rights) do not clearly place her on the as-written vs. pragmatic axis [S1][S3].
