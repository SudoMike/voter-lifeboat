## What the office does
The three-member board of county commissioners is the county's legislative authority. It provides courthouses, jails and other public buildings, manages county roads, fixes county taxes, manages county property, funds and business, and adopts ordinances and regulations not in conflict with state law [S3]. It is a partisan county office with a four-year term [S1].

**How the seat is elected:** under RCW 36.32.040 only the voters of a commissioner district nominate candidates for that district's seat in the primary [S2]. The top two then face every voter in the county in the general election; the voters' guide labels this contest "Countywide Commissioner #3" [S1][S8]. The Aug. 4 primary was District 3 only; in November about 20,400 more votes are expected from Districts 1 and 2 [S8].

## Race dynamics
District 3 Commissioner Vickie Raines decided not to seek reelection and then resigned effective March 31, 2026. She had held the seat since winning the 2014 election and was re-elected twice [S4][S9]. Five people applied for the vacancy, including both general-election candidates and Gordon Mitchell [S4]. On May 5 the two remaining commissioners, Republicans Rick Hole and Georgia Miller, appointed James J. "J.R." Streifel Jr. (R) [S5][S8]. He took office May 16 and holds the seat now, until the 2026 general election is certified (no later than Dec. 3) [S5].

Three candidates filed: Streifel (R), Stan Sturgeon (no party preference) and Gordon E. Mitchell (R) [S6]. In the District 3 primary Sturgeon led with 3,598 votes (53.73%) to Streifel's 2,099 (31.34%), and Mitchell was eliminated [S7]. Sturgeon, a former North Beach School Board member and Ocean City Drainage District chair, is endorsed by Raines. Streifel is a former Aberdeen fire captain and former Cosmopolis School Board member [S1][S8].

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Experience:** a five-month appointed incumbent against a challenger with 12 years on a school board and a seat on a newly formed drainage district board. Neither has been elected to county office.
2. **Safety:** Streifel stresses support for law enforcement and addressing homelessness "with compassion while maintaining accountability." Sturgeon stresses community policing, the Resident Officer Program and crime-prevention programs (see candidate dossiers).
3. **Housing:** Sturgeon names affordable housing as a top priority and would keep supporting the Coastal Community Action Program and the county housing authority. Streifel's materials do not address housing beyond homelessness.
4. **Spending:** both cite fiscal responsibility. Sturgeon pairs a balanced budget with continued infrastructure investment; Streifel cites "responsible budgeting" and his fire-department budget oversight.
5. **Taxes, climate, reform:** no clear evidence in this race.
