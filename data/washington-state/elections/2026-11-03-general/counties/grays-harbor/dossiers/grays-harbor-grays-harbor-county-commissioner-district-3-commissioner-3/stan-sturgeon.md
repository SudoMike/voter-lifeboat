## Background

Stan Sturgeon states no party preference. His statement says he filed as a nonpartisan "so I can put the needs of the citizens of Grays Harbor before anything else" [S1]. His campaign committee is based in Ocean Shores [S2]. He graduated from North Beach High School in Moclips and has lived in Grays Harbor for 50 years. He and his wife have five children and twelve grandchildren [S1][S3]. He is a local business owner and the North Beach School District's maintenance and facilities supervisor [S1][S9]. A supporter's letter describes him as a former home builder [S5].

He served 12 years on the North Beach School District board, nine as president [S1]. In February 2026 he was sworn in as one of three appointed commissioners of the newly formed Ocean City Drainage District, and the board elected him chair [S7]. The district was formed by a vote of property owners (235 to 57) to work on flooding along State Route 109 [S7]. He has volunteered as a youth coach and on Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife projects. He has served 27 years on the Twin Harbors Chapter committee of the Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation, the last 16 as chair [S1][S3].

He was one of five applicants for the District 3 vacancy in April 2026 [S11]. He led the District 3 primary with 3,598 votes (53.73%) to appointed incumbent JR Streifel's 2,099 (31.34%) [S8]. He spoke at the Grays Harbor Young Professionals candidate forum at Tinderbox Coffee Roasters in September [S10].

## Positions

### Budget and spending
- Lists fiscal responsibility, family-wage jobs and affordable housing as his three priority areas [S1].
- Says he is "committed to a strong budget" and "will work towards a balanced budget." He adds that "the county needs to continue to invest in infrastructure as long [as] we ensure fairness for all citizens," calling infrastructure important for housing [S2].
- After the primary he picked up a copy of the county budget to study [S4].

### Housing
- Would "work on affordable housing initiatives by continuing to support the Coastal Community Action Program and the Housing Authority of Grays Harbor County" [S2].
- Endorsed by The Affordable Housing Council of Olympia Master Builders. The council says it looks for candidates who favor "a regulatory environment that allows our communities to grow and prosper" [S5].

### Public safety
- Supports "strengthening community policing and supporting the Resident Officer Program." He calls "investing in programs to reduce crime rates and build safer neighborhoods" a priority [S2].

### Jobs and economy
- Would support local small businesses and partner with other agencies on job creation [S2]. He says he "firmly believe[s] in family wage job growth," and he toured the Port of Grays Harbor in August [S4].

### Health care
- Advocates "healthcare coverage and mental health services within our county," citing an aging population [S2]. His statement says health care will require county, community and business partnerships [S1]. He pointed to the new Ocean Shores clinic, a hospital-city partnership, as the kind of partnership the county needs [S4].

### Environment and resources
- Says the county can protect forests, waterways and coastlines while families make a living, "by supporting responsible resource management, local jobs, and common sense." He says public lands "should remain open to the Public whenever possible" [S2].

### Gender, LGBTQ+ and reproductive policy
- No public statements found as of 2026-10-08.

### Parents and schools
- No public statements found on parental notification, record access or curricula opt-outs as of 2026-10-08.
- Served 12 years on the North Beach School District board [S1]. He says he supports career and technical education programs at local high schools and at Grays Harbor College [S4].
