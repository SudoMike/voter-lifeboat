## Background

James J. "JR" Streifel Jr. prefers the Republican Party [S1][S6]. His campaign committee is registered in Aberdeen [S3]. He is an Aberdeen High School graduate and says his family has lived in the community for most of his life, over 60 years [S1]. He served 25 years in the Aberdeen Fire Department, 15 of them as captain. For 12 years he was its public education officer, and he was also training officer with budget-oversight duties [S1][S2]. He has also worked as a Department of Transportation maintenance technician, an electrician at Hoquiam Plywood, a field representative for Oval Strapping Northwest and an Aberdeen/Hoquiam school bus driver [S1]. His website says he also has a small-business background [S2].

His listed elected experience is the Cosmopolis School Board and high school class senator [S1][S6]. His community service includes the Grays Harbor County Human Services Advisory Board, volunteering at Stafford Creek Correctional Center, YMCA camp counseling, Sunday school teaching, his church's board of trustees and council, and The Lord's Pantry food bank [S1]. He describes himself as "a dedicated Christian, and a leader in his church" [S2].

In 2024 he ran for state representative, Legislative District 24, Position 1, and placed fifth of five in the August primary with 7,896 votes (13.86%) [S4][S5]. He was one of five applicants for the District 3 commissioner vacancy left by Vickie Raines [S7]. Commissioners Rick Hole and Georgia Miller appointed him on May 5, 2026, and he took office May 16. His appointment runs until the 2026 general election is certified [S6][S13]. He placed second in the District 3 primary with 2,099 votes (31.34%) to Stan Sturgeon's 3,598 (53.73%) [S8]. He took part in a Republican candidate forum in July and the Grays Harbor Young Professionals forum in September [S9][S12].

## Positions

### Public safety
- Calls public safety "one of my highest priorities." He promises "dependable emergency services, and leadership that supports law enforcement, first responders, and public employees" [S1].
- Says the county "must address homelessness with compassion while maintaining accountability and protecting our neighborhoods" [S1].

### Budget and economy
- Cites "practical solutions, responsible budgeting, and strong leadership" learned as a firefighter, business owner and community leader [S1]. His website says his fire-department budget oversight ensured "taxpayer dollars were used efficiently and effectively" [S2].
- Commits to "economic development that creates good-paying jobs" and encourages local investment [S1][S6]. He will "support responsible growth" and work to improve county services [S1].
