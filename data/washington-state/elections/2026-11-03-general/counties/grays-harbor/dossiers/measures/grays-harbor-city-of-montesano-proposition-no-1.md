## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would raise the City of Montesano's regular property tax levy "to a total authorized rate of not to exceed $3.22 per $1,000 of assessed valuation for collection beginning in 2027" [S1]. For up to six years, later levies could grow by "an annual 5% limit factor" instead of the usual 1% [S1]. The ballot title says the money "would fund current service levels in the General Fund and Public and Safety Emergency Services" [S1]. A lid lift needs a simple majority [S12].

- **How it got on the ballot.** Fire Chief Dave Busz presented the plan on July 14, 2026, and the council held the first reading of Ordinance No. 1679 [S4]. On July 28 the council adopted Ordinance No. 1679 by a 6-0 roll call (Skaramuca, McLean, Cope, Jester, Caryl and Thomas; Roth presiding as mayor pro tem) [S5][S7].
- **The draft was different.** The ordinance in the July 14 packet would have authorized up to $3.60 per $1,000 with a 106% limit factor in the ordinance body, and its draft ballot title cited "an estimated increase of $.40 per $1,000 over the 2018 levy" [S3]. The ballot title certified for November says $3.22 and 5% [S1]. The adopted ordinance is not posted on the city's ordinance page (the list ends at No. 1663), so this research did not see the final text.
- **What the city says it pays for.** The fire department's Proposition 1 page says the city's costs for labor, goods and services rose more than 36% in five years, and departments cut programs and deferred maintenance to balance budgets [S2]. It says the levy would let the fire department:
  - replace essential equipment that "is scheduled to expire in 2028";
  - add two full-time firefighter/EMTs, one in late 2027 and one in mid-2028;
  - staff a two-person fire/EMS crew around the clock [S2].
  It lists other city needs: City Hall painting and windows (about $500,000), fire station HVAC (about $50,000), City Hall HVAC (about $70,000) and police evidence room updates [S2]. Adding firefighters goes beyond the ballot title's "current service levels" wording [S1][S2].

## Cost

- **Current rate.** Montesano's regular ("Basic") levy for 2026 taxes was $1.7038417 per $1,000 on $635,112,199 of assessed value, raising $1,082,131 [S9]. The fire department page puts it at "approximately $1.70" [S2].
- **What yes changes.** The 2027 rate would be up to $3.22, an increase of about $1.52 per $1,000 (computed for this dossier from [S1] and [S9]):
  - about $468 a year (about $39 a month) on a $309,000 home, the example the city uses [S2];
  - about $455 a year on a $300,000 home.
  The city's own worked example for a $309,000 home is an image on its page that this research could not read as text [S2].
- **Revenue.** At $3.22 on the 2026 assessed value, the levy would raise about $2.05 million, roughly $960,000 more than in 2026 (computed for this dossier from [S9]; 2027 values will differ).
- **2028 to 2032.** The levy amount can grow up to 5% a year [S1]. The fire department page says the rate "would not exceed $3.22 per $1,000" [S2].
- **Other city property taxes.** Voters approved a $429,000, five-year bond for a new ambulance in February 2025 [S11]. Its pamphlet statement estimated about $42.63 a year on a $300,000 home [S11]. The assessor lists it among levies added for 2026 [S9]. The fire department page says the new ambulance is being built and is expected in 2027 [S2].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

The city calls this a renewal. The fire department page says it "renews the 2018 Levy, for which voters approved a rate of $3.22," that the rate has since fallen to about $1.70, and that "The 2018 Levy expires 12/31/2026" [S2]. The ballot title also calls it "a renewal property tax levy" [S1]. Legally it is a lid lift under RCW 84.55.050 that raises the regular levy above the 1% growth limit [S3][S12].

- Secretary of State results show a City of Montesano Proposition No. 1, "Levy for Fire Safety and Emergency Services Purposes," passed on Feb. 14, 2017 with 58.73% (582 yes, 409 no) [S10]. The city dates the levy to 2018 [S2]. This research did not confirm that the 2017 measure is the levy the city means, though a 2017 vote for 2018 collection would fit.
- The fire department page does not say what the city would cut if the measure fails [S2].

## Arguments for

No statement for was filed in the voters' guide, and no committee is listed [S1]. The city's case comes from its fire department page and council meetings:
- Costs rose more than 36% in five years and departments have deferred maintenance [S2].
- "Having two responders available improves the Department's ability to provide adequate patient care, respond to emergencies, and safely perform tasks that can be difficult or unsafe for a single responder" [S2].
- Levy money would let the department plan for apparatus replacement "rather than relying solely on individual bond measures or financing" [S2].
- At a GGHI business luncheon reported Sept. 30, Mayor Tyler Trimble called fire and EMS "a tremendous expense" and criticized low reimbursement rates [S8].
- Councilman Caryl said he was "excited to support the passing of the proposed Levy Lid Lift" [S4].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed [S1]. No organized opposition was found. Council members acknowledged the cost:
- Councilman Roth said "the conversation of taxes can be very difficult" [S4].
- On July 28, Caryl called it "a serious issue" and said "it is going to be tough for people to make a decision," and Councilwoman McLean said "It will definitely be a hard decision for the voters to make" [S5].
- At the Sept. 8 meeting, resident Tony Broten asked "who is leading the Levy Lid Lift and how that information is getting out to the citizens" [S6].

## Supporters and opponents (PDC committees)

The city frames it as restoring a 2018 voter-approved rate [S2], but the increase over today's bill is large. Direction +2.
