## What it does

Proposition No. 1 asks whether Fire District 1 may "impose a PERMANENT regular emergency medical services property tax levy of fifty cents or less per thousand dollars of assessed valuation" [S1]. The board adopted Resolution 2026-006 [S1]. The voters' guide record has only the ballot title: no explanatory statement and no statements for or against [S1].

- **The district.** Grays Harbor Fire District 1 serves about 3,000 residents over 97 square miles around Oakville [S7]. Chief Carlton Rhoades said it answers about 500 calls a year, roughly 82% of them medical, with transports averaging about 25 a month [S7]. In late 2025 Rhoades said the district has one full-time employee and relies on volunteers [S6].
- **Rules for a permanent EMS levy.** Under RCW 84.52.069 [S11]:
  - Money may be used only for emergency medical care and services, including personnel, training, equipment, supplies, vehicles and structures.
  - A permanent levy needs a 60% yes vote. If turnout on the measure is no more than 40% of the district's voters in the last general election, the yes votes must also equal 60% of that 40% figure.
  - The district must keep separate accounting of what it spends from the levy, update the statement at least every two years and give it to the public free on request.
  - The resolution must provide a referendum: a petition signed by 15% of registered voters, filed at any time, sends the levy back to the ballot.
  - The levy is outside the $5.90 per $1,000 aggregate limit in RCW 84.52.043.
- **Only one of two planned levies is on the ballot.** The board workshop on June 17 listed "Levy Lid Lift" for discussion [S3]. On July 8 Commissioner Devin Harris "motioned to move forward with both levies," and the motion carried [S4]. A July 24 special meeting agenda listed Resolutions 2026-005 and 2026-006 [S5]. The district's levy page refers to an "Upcoming November 2026 Fire Levy Lid Lift & EMS Levy" [S2]. The Grays Harbor voters' guide lists only this EMS measure for Fire District 1 [S1]. This research did not find what became of Resolution 2026-005.

## Cost

- **Rate.** Up to $0.50 per $1,000 of assessed value, with no end date [S1]. That is up to $150 a year on a $300,000 home (computed for this dossier from [S1]).
- **New tax.** For 2026 taxes, the assessor lists only an expense (regular) levy for Fire District 1: $0.8564621 per $1,000 on $313,212,928 of assessed value, raising $268,255 [S9]. Fire District 2, South Beach RFA and other districts have separate EMS lines; Fire District 1 has none [S9]. A yes vote adds a new tax rather than renewing one.
- **Revenue.** At $0.50 on the 2026 assessed value, about $157,000 a year, more than half again the district's current $268,255 levy (computed for this dossier from [S9]; the EMS assessed value and future values will differ).
- The district has not published a per-home estimate or a budget for the levy money as of 2026-10-08 [S1][S2].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

Nothing; the district has no EMS levy now [S9]. In November 2019 voters approved a Fire District 1 "Property Tax Levy for Fire Protection and Emergency Medical Services" with 73.84% (429 yes, 152 no) [S10]. This research did not find that measure's terms or rate.

**Why the district says it needs money.**
- The district's page says that as property values rise, its levy rates fall while costs for trucks, ambulances, protective gear, volunteer training and medical calls do not [S2]. "For us, this isn't simply about the tax rate. It's about maintaining the resources needed to answer the next 911 call" [S2].
- Until mid-2025 the district contracted with a Lewis County ambulance consortium for transports. Rhoades said the per-transport cost rose from $1,200 to $2,500 and a new contract starting June 1, 2025 would have charged $4,600 [S6].
- The district started its own basic life support transports on June 1, 2025, using a used ambulance bought for $60,000 with a loan from a local lender and a second used ambulance for $38,000 [S6]. It charges residents $1,200 plus $28 a mile [S6]. The Confederated Tribes of the Chehalis Reservation donated a cardiac monitor [S6].
- Rhoades said transports that once took up to 90 minutes to reach a hospital now get there in about half an hour [S7].

- `spending`: the district frames the money as sustaining volunteer EMS and in-house ambulance service [S2][S6]; no audit dispute in the record. Not mapped.
