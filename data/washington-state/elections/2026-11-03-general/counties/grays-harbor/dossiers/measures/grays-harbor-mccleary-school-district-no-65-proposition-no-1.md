## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would let McCleary School District issue $12,800,000 of general obligation bonds maturing within 21 years, repaid by annual excess property taxes [S1]. The board adopted Resolution No. 2026-08 [S1]. The money would pay for work at McCleary School [S1]:
- safety and security: a single-point entry security vestibule, door locks, keycard access and the fire alarm system;
- facilities: building exteriors, HVAC, drainage systems, parking areas and access routes;
- playgrounds modernized for safety and accessibility.

The district's page also lists roof repairs [S2].

- **The district.** McCleary serves 332 students in pre-K through 8th grade; its high school students attend neighboring districts, which McCleary pays [S5]. The district reaches into Mason County, and the explanatory statement lists both county assessors for exemptions [S1].
- **Vote threshold.** Bonds need a 60% supermajority [S5][S6]. State law also requires turnout of at least 40% of the voters who voted in the last general election [S9].
- **Why now.** The district says its aging roof, HVAC and drainage systems have caused water damage, and it has urgent plumbing, fire alarm and security needs [S2]. Superintendent Susan Zetty described a near-flood on Dec. 17, 2025, when a power outage stopped a sump pump and a custodian bailed water overnight [S5]. The Daily World reported the HVAC system is 20 years old and getting expensive to maintain [S5]. A summer 2025 community survey ranked safety and security as a high priority, and the district sought more feedback in summer 2026 [S2][S5].

## Cost

- **Rate.** The district estimates $1.42 per $1,000 of assessed value [S2]:
  - $426 a year ($35.50 a month) on a $300,000 home;
  - $639 a year on a $450,000 home;
  - $852 a year on a $600,000 home.
- **Total.** The district says the bond "would raise $12.8 million over a 21-year period" [S2].
- **Existing bond levy.** For 2026 taxes McCleary levied $1.1868891 per $1,000 for an existing bond, raising $640,277 [S8]. The district's four-year budget plan shows debt service property taxes of $627,117 in 2025-26 and $613,248 in 2026-27, then none in 2027-28 or 2028-29, with the last bond principal payment in 2026-27 [S7]. Read together, the current bond tax appears to end after 2026 collections (inference from [S7] and [S8]).
- **What yes changes, compared with no.** If the current bond tax ends as the budget plan shows, a no vote leaves no McCleary bond tax from 2027, and a yes vote adds about $1.42 per $1,000 for up to 21 years. Compared with the 2026 bill, the new rate is about $0.23 per $1,000 higher than the expiring bond, about $70 a year on a $300,000 home (computed for this dossier from [S2] and [S8]).
- **Operating levy.** Voters renewed a separate two-year educational programs and operations levy in February 2026 with 50.45% in Grays Harbor [S6]; its 2026 rate was $2.3465245 [S8]. This bond does not affect it.

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

**The February 2026 bond failed.** In February the district asked for $19,300,000 in bonds maturing within 20 years under Resolution No. 2026-02 [S4]. That version also included an auxiliary gymnasium for student and community use and educational technology upgrades [S4], at an anticipated rate of $2.22 per $1,000 [S5]. It got 43.45% in Grays Harbor (335 yes, 436 no) and 3 of 8 votes in Mason County, short of 60% [S6].

**The November version.** It is $6.5 million smaller, runs 21 years instead of 20, and drops the auxiliary gym and the technology line from the ballot title (computed for this dossier from [S1] and [S4]). The district's FAQ still says the bond would help fund "student technology tools" [S2], which this research could not reconcile with the November ballot title.

**History and grants.** Business Manager Tiki Willey said the last major remodel was 20 years ago, and the downstairs and gym were expanded three years ago [S5]. The district applied for a state Small Schools Modernization grant and says state funds "don't cover repair, replacement, and building costs" [S2][S5]. It is applying for grants for a field ramp, painting and gutters, access control and the fire alarm [S2].

## Arguments for

No statement for was filed in the voters' guide, and no committee is listed [S1]. The district's case:
- "Bonds are for building, levies are for learning" [S2].
- Priorities came from community surveys in 2025 and 2026, and most projects would also serve community use of the building [S2].
- On the February measure, Zetty said: "We feel a very big responsibility to be really fiscally responsible for people's funds. I don't ever want to forget we're dealing with real people and money" [S5].
- The district is holding information sessions on Oct. 8, Oct. 26 (Zoom) and Oct. 30 [S3].

## Arguments against

No statement against was filed, and no committee is listed [S1]. No organized opposition was found in news coverage. The February vote, at 43.45% yes in Grays Harbor, is the most recent measure of local support [S6].
