## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would "restore the District's regular property tax levy rate for library services, operations and maintenance from $0.22 to $0.35 per $1,000 of assessed valuation for both 2027 and 2028, subject to applicable limitations" [S1]. The 2028 levy amount becomes the base for later levy limits under chapter 84.55 RCW [S1]. The Board of Trustees adopted Resolution No. 26-002 [S1]. The trustees voted unanimously on July 22, 2026 to put it on the ballot [S3][S5].

- **The district.** Timberland Regional Library (TRL) runs 29 libraries in Grays Harbor, Lewis, Mason, Pacific and Thurston counties [S1][S2][S6]. TRL scheduled Grays Harbor information sessions at its Elma, Montesano and Hoquiam libraries [S5]. Its Grays Harbor trustee is Dustin Loup [S3][S7].
- **Who votes in Grays Harbor.** The library district does not cover all of Grays Harbor County. In the assessor's 2026 levy book, Timberland's Grays Harbor assessed value ($11,239,680,897) equals the county total ($14,056,936,383) minus the City of Ocean Shores ($2,817,255,486), so Ocean Shores property is outside the district (computed for this dossier from [S8]).
- **Vote threshold.** The combined vote of all five counties decides it. It passes with more than 50% yes and does not need to pass in each county [S2][S4]. RCW 84.55.050 requires a simple majority for a lid lift [S9].
- **What the money would pay for.** The explanatory statement says approval "would provide funding necessary to maintain Library services, staffing, collections, and open hours" [S1]. TRL says it would provide more open, staffed hours, larger collections with shorter waits, continued public computers, wifi and printing, and improved meeting and study spaces [S2].
- **If it fails.** The explanatory statement says reductions in staffing, materials, hours, online services, bookmobile service and programming "will begin in 2027" and facility maintenance could be delayed [S1]. TRL lists fewer staffed hours, less Anywhere Library mobile service, smaller collections and card-access-only unstaffed hours at branches with Expanded Access [S2]. Board President Brian Mittge said "some of those small libraries, might be unstaffed. So keycard access only" [S4].

## Cost

- **Rate.** From about $0.22 per $1,000 (the explanatory statement gives $0.228924) to $0.35 per $1,000 in 2027 and 2028 [S1]. State law allows up to $0.50 [S1][S2]. The rate charged in Grays Harbor for 2026 taxes was $0.2244042 [S8].
- **Per-home estimates in Grays Harbor.**
  - TRL: $34 a year ($2.83 a month) on the 2025 Grays Harbor median home value of $263,570 [S2].
  - Explanatory statement: about $40.44 a year ($3.37 a month) on a $334,000 home [S1].
  - Statement for: about $43 a year ($3.58 a month) on a $333,733 home [S1]. TRL's levy page gives the same monthly figure for Mason County's $333,773 median, not Grays Harbor's [S2].
- **Revenue.** About $14 million more districtwide in 2027 than without a lid lift [S2][S4]. In Grays Harbor, the 2026 TRL levy raised $2,522,232 [S8]. At $0.35 on the same assessed value it would raise about $3.93 million, roughly $1.4 million more (computed for this dossier from [S8]; 2027 values will differ).
- **After 2028.** The Sept. 8 Daily World story says the levy would then grow at most 1% a year [S5]. RCW 84.55.050 lets a ballot measure set the final year's levy as the base for later limits [S9].
- **Other districts.** TRL says no other taxing district would lose money through prorationing, because all five counties have room under the $5.90 per $1,000 cap [S2].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This raises the rate of TRL's existing regular levy; it is not a new tax [S2][S3]. The levy is about 96% of TRL's budget [S1][S2]. TRL says the 1% annual limit on levy growth has lowered its rate from the $0.50 maximum to $0.22 [S2][S6].

**Levy history.** TRL says the last voter-approved lid lift was in 1987 [S2][S3]. The explanatory statement says TRL last asked for an increase in 2009 and that "resolution was not adopted" [S1], and The Daily World describes the 2009 vote as "narrowly defeated" [S3][S5]. TRL and the former trustees say it has not had a full levy rate since 2001, 25 years ago [S2][S6]. This research did not reconcile the 1987 and 2001 dates.

**The 2026 budget crisis.**
- TRL found a $3.8 million deficit early in 2026 [S4][S7]. Former Executive Director Cheryl Heywood announced it in March with a proposal to cut 20% of staff, and resigned after pushback [S4].
- Loup told the McCleary Friends of the Library in May that the first plan included 61 layoffs and would have moved several branches, including McCleary, to Expanded Access Hours only; those decisions were partly rolled back [S7]. The Daily World reported the McCleary layoffs were rescinded and its hours were not cut [S7].
- Mittge said TRL cut $3.69 million, including $2.23 million from new books, programs, travel and training and $1.16 million through voluntary and involuntary layoffs [S4]. He called the cuts "not a particularly sustainable response for years ahead" [S4].
- TRL says staffing fell from about 251 FTE in 2016 to 182 FTE as of August 2026, and that a June 2026 administrative restructure, salary revisions and furloughs are projected to save over $222,700 in the second half of the year [S2].
- New policies require multi-year forecasts and a fund balance of at least 30%, and TRL now posts monthly financial reports [S4].

## Arguments for

The statement for in the Grays Harbor record lists no author or committee [S1]. It says:
- About 96% of TRL's revenue comes from a levy that "hasn't increased in 25 years," and rising prices have eroded it [S1].
- State law caps growth at 1% a year "until voters lift the lid" [S1].
- Approval would let TRL "restore hours and staffing" and expand collections and programs. It calls the increase "a long overdue cost-of-living raise" [S1].
- Its rebuttal says libraries "have endured a 25-year financial drought" and serve families, students, job-seekers and seniors [S1].

Other supporters:
- Loup said: "We need to allow our communities to make this decision. If we don't put it on the ballot, they don't get to make that decision" [S3].
- Four former TRL trustees, including former Grays Harbor trustee Corby Varness, wrote that rural residents could lose access and several branches could go unstaffed if it fails. They said they were concerned about TRL's direction in the past year but are "pleased with the changes" since [S6].

## Arguments against

The statement against in the Grays Harbor record lists no author or committee [S1]. It says:
- A no vote "is a vote for fiscal responsibility, taxpayer accountability, and careful budgeting" while property owners face rising bills from many levies [S1].
- The library should first show it has cut costs, improved efficiency, sought grants and partnerships and managed existing resources better [S1].
- A property tax increase "could put future investments in schools and emergency services at risk" [S1].
- Its rebuttal says many library services are "delivered through nonprofits and community organizations more efficiently and effectively," and that taxes should not rise "without transparency and accountability" [S1].
- It says: "Voting 'No' is not a vote against libraries or literacy" [S1].
