## What it does

Proposition No. 1 would set Fire District 2's regular property tax levy at $1.50 per $1,000 of assessed value "in 2026 and 2027 for collection each subsequent year" [S1]. The 2027 levy amount becomes the base for later levy limits under chapter 84.55 RCW [S1]. The board adopted Resolution No. 26-02 [S1]. A lid lift needs a simple majority [S11].

- **What the money is for.** The ballot title says the funds "will maintain and improve fire protection, and life safety services, replace apparatus and equipment, and provide for firefighter safety" [S1]. The district's page lists 24-hour career firefighter/paramedic staffing, volunteer recruitment and the volunteer stations in Brady and Wynoochee, ambulance and engine replacement and maintenance, EMS supplies, protective gear, training, communications and reserves [S2].
- **The district.** Fire District 2 is headquartered on Olympic Highway outside Aberdeen, with stations in Brady and the Wynoochee Valley [S9]. The district says it covers more than 300 square miles [S2]. In January 2026 it added a career battalion chief position and three captains [S9].
- **How the district describes it.** Its page calls the measure "a two-year permanent Fire Levy Lid Lift": the rate is reset for 2027 and 2028 collections, and the 2028 amount then sets the base for later years "without requiring repeated levy restorations" [S2]. After that the levy grows under the normal limits, so the rate can drift down again (from [S1], [S2] and [S11]).

## Cost

- **Rate change.** The explanatory statement says the levy "has dropped to approximately $1.40 per thousand in 2026" [S1]. The assessor's figure for 2026 taxes is $1.3997717 on $1,115,419,411 of assessed value, raising $1,561,333 [S10]. A yes vote raises it by about $0.10 per $1,000, about $30 a year on a $300,000 home (computed for this dossier from [S1] and [S10]).
- **Revenue.** About $112,000 more a year at 2026 values (computed for this dossier from [S10]).
- **The district's other levies.** For 2026 taxes Fire District 2 also levied $0.4847334 per $1,000 for EMS and $0.2793285 for a $3.25 million facilities bond running 2016 to 2036 [S10]. If the measure passes, the district's combined rate would be about $2.26 per $1,000 (computed for this dossier from [S10]).
- The district has not published its own per-home estimate as of 2026-10-08 [S1][S2].

## Fiscal mechanics / What it replaces

This is the district's third vote since 2020 to set its regular levy at $1.50:
- **November 2020.** Voters approved a regular levy of up to $1.50 per $1,000 for 2021 collection [S4], 57.73% to 42.27% (2,251 to 1,648) [S6].
- **November 2023.** The rate had fallen to about $1.11, and the district asked to restore $1.50 for 2024 collection [S3]. It passed 62.45% to 37.55% (1,001 to 602) [S7].
- **November 2026.** The rate is about $1.40, and the district asks again for $1.50 [S1].

The 1% annual limit on levy growth lowers the rate as assessed values rise, which the explanatory statement cites [S1]. Without voter approval each reset wears off over time (from [S1], [S3] and [S11]).

**EMS levy.** Separately, voters renewed the district's six-year EMS levy of up to $0.50 in August 2024, collected from 2025 [S5], with 70.31% (1,648 to 696) [S8]. Voters had approved six-year EMS levies in 2006, 2012 and 2018 [S5]. This measure does not change the EMS levy.

## Arguments for

The statement for is signed by Scotty Stead (chairman) and Sharilyn Everitt (committee member) [S1]. It says:
- Residents count on the district in medical emergencies, house fires, vehicle accidents and wildfires, when minutes matter [S1].
- Over the past three years the district replaced outdated engines, improved emergency medical and firefighter safety equipment, renovated the Brady fire station and strengthened emergency response [S1].
- "A strong fire district protects lives, homes, and businesses while contributing to a safer, more resilient community" [S1].

The district's case: the board "has determined that this proposition is necessary to maintain an effective level of services, firefighter staffing, equipment, and facilities in light of rising costs, rising demands, and the continued growth of our community" [S1]. Fire Chief John McNutt is taking questions [S2].

## Arguments against
