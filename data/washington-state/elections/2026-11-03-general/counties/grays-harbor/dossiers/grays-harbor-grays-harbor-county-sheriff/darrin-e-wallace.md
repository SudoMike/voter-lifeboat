## Background

Darrin Wallace is the incumbent Grays Harbor County sheriff and states no party preference [S1][S4]. He won the office in November 2022 with 73.94% of the vote over Michael Catlett [S7]. Before that he spent 20 years in the U.S. Coast Guard and retired as a chief warrant officer. His last two assignments were executive petty officer of Station Golden Gate and commanding officer of Station Grays Harbor in Westport [S1][S4]. He has been with the sheriff's office for 20 years. He worked up through deputy (including North Beach resident deputy), detective, patrol sergeant, detective sergeant, supervisor of the Region 3 Critical Incident Investigation Team and chief of investigations [S1][S2][S4]. He says he now supervises about 100 employees and manages a budget of about $20 million [S2][S4]. His son serves as an Aberdeen police officer [S2].

He led the certified August primary with 10,022 votes (59.94%) to Ed Welter's 6,626 (39.63%) [S6].

## Positions

### Public safety and enforcement
- Says that over the past four years he has "proven to be tough on crime." He says he will "continue to aggressively investigate crimes and arrest individuals responsible," including violent crimes, crimes against children, theft and drug dealing [S1].
- Calls a "safe, secure, and trusting community" his top priority, to be pursued through consistent presence, partnerships with local agencies and "proactive efforts to address concerns before they become problems" [S2][S4].
- Will keep funding a sheriff's office position on the Grays Harbor Drug Task Force and supporting its work with local, state and federal agencies [S2][S4].
- Cites falling numbers during his term. In the Daily World Q&A he gave burglary down 39%, theft 32%, vehicle theft 45%, domestic-violence assaults 28%, assaults 45% and shootings 50% [S4]. His website gives figures from 2023-2025 sheriff's office call data: burglary down 38%, theft 32%, vehicle theft 35%, domestic violence 27%, assault 34% and shootings 50% [S2].

### Jail and criminal justice center
- Calls the new criminal justice center his "#1 priority." He describes the jail as over 45 years old and "in a constant state of disrepair," and calls its condition a liability for corrections officers and inmates [S1][S4].
- Says a new facility would expand "mental health and substance use rehabilitation services" and put sheriff's office staff, emergency management and the jail in one location [S4].
- Plans to get the jail accredited through the Washington Association of Sheriffs and Police Chiefs, which he says would reduce lawsuit exposure and insurance costs [S1].

### Budget and resources
- Says he has stayed under budget every year of his term [S4].
- Says modular Steel Cell units could cut the jail's estimated cost from about $100 million to about $50 million. He says the county budget is "very constrained" and that funding could come from a grant, a bond or a public-private partnership [S4].

### Recruitment
- Proposes a sheriff's cadet program to introduce young people to law enforcement and prepare them for jobs with the office [S1][S2].

### Firearms
- Says he supports the Second Amendment and that the right to keep and bear arms "shall not be infringed upon" [S1].

## Record

- Grays Harbor County Sheriff since early 2023, after winning the 2022 general election [S5][S7]. Shortly after taking office he called a new jail his "highest priority" [S5].
- Succession: at a Republican-sponsored forum on July 26, 2026, he said he planned to retire once the criminal justice center was under contract and that Chief of Special Services Jeremy Holmes would be "the next sheriff." The next day he posted on Facebook that he intends "to serve the remaining four years of this next term" [S5].
- The county bought a 15-acre site next to Beacon Elementary School in Montesano in 2023 with a possible jail in mind. The commissioners have said they are waiting for the sheriff's office to launch public outreach in Montesano before going further [S5].

## Endorsements

His campaign lists endorsements from 15 Washington county sheriffs (including those of Pacific, Thurston, Lewis, Mason, Clark and Snohomish counties). It also lists retired Grays Harbor Sheriff Rick Scott, current undersheriff Kevin Schrader and other members of his command staff, former county commissioners Vickie Raines and Dan Wood, and Central Grays Harbor Professional Firefighters IAFF Local 315 [S2].
