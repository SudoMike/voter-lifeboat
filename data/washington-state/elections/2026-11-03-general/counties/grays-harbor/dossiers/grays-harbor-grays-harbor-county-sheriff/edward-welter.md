## Background

Edward "Ed" Welter is a detective with the Grays Harbor County Sheriff's Office and the mayor of Westport. He states no party preference [S1][S8][S12]. He says his mayoral term runs 2024-2027. He says the Municipal Research and Services Center and the law firm Ingram, Zelasko & Goodwin both told him running for sheriff while mayor is lawful [S2][S6]. He has a BA in economics from Pacific Lutheran University and an associate degree from TCC, and is a certified peace officer [S1][S3]. Before law enforcement he worked as a financial analyst at telecom companies, where he says he managed a $15 million operating budget [S1][S3]. He joined the Coast Guard in 2010 and was stationed in Westport from 2013. He then became a Washington Fish and Wildlife officer in Grays Harbor and moved to the sheriff's office in 2018 [S3]. In the sheriff's office he has been South Beach resident deputy, narcotics K9 handler, fire investigator, Critical Response Unit (SWAT) member and defensive tactics/OC instructor, and is now a detective. He cites about 12 years in law enforcement [S1][S3][S6].

Public Hospital District #2 appointed him to its Position 2 seat in October 2025 for the rest of that year. He was also on the November 2025 ballot for the full term [S10]. He placed second in the certified August primary with 6,626 votes (39.63%) to Wallace's 10,022 (59.94%) [S9].

## Positions

### Public safety and enforcement
- Says "the public deserves to have our laws enforced" and that the sheriff must make sure all laws "the public has voted into existence are enforced and any violations of those laws are investigated" [S4][S8].
- Rejects the attitude that "it's just a property crime" and says all crimes should be "investigated to their fullest" and the best possible case sent to the prosecutor [S4].
- On opioids and street drugs, calls for "proactive enforcement" against habitual drug use, drug dealing and trafficking in stolen property, and close work with prosecutors. He adds that "accountability does not always mean prison." He has seen therapeutic-court successes, but says a defendant has an incentive to finish that process only if the criminal case carries enough consequences [S6].
- Wants county nuisance ordinances (junk vehicles, septic) written so they can be enforced, and wants them enforced proactively with code enforcement [S6].
- On automatic license plate readers, says capturing plate data is not a Fourth Amendment violation. He supports SB 6002 (2026), which exempts the data from public records requests and sets retention, use and audit rules [S6].
- Describes being a "constitutional sheriff" as enforcing the laws on citizens' behalf while protecting them "from unlawful government intrusion as well as from the intrusion of criminals" [S6].
- Envisions "proactive community policing," uniform training and more staff [S8].

### Staffing and budget
- Says sheriff's office staffing has not changed much since 2000 (35 contracted then, 34 now) while the unincorporated population grew by almost 30%. By his figures that is a drop from 1.5 to 1 officer per 1,000 residents, against a national average of 2.5 [S4][S8].
- Says that "while the current state of the county budget needs to be considered, so does the future," and that he will work with the commissioners on service levels [S4][S8].
- On the portable office building and "antiquated jail," says he will work with the commissioners to find "fiscally responsible ways to improve the working conditions for our deputies" [S4][S8].
- Says he will work with state legislators to secure available funding [S1].

### Engagement and representation
- Pledges to attend block watch and city council meetings across the county and to seek a recurring spot on the commissioners' agenda for sheriff's office updates [S4].
- Says the southwest coast "goes greatly unrepresented at the state level" and that he will work with legislators and lobbying groups on funding and policy [S4][S8].
- Would stand on-call administrative duty shifts and keep an open-door policy inside the office [S4].

### Deputy training and career paths
- Proposes a standard advanced-training plan for every deputy's first five years and clearer career paths beyond patrol and investigations [S4].

## Record

- As mayor of Westport, lists retaining FEMA grant funding for a $14 million tsunami tower, $250,000 in direct state spending for city infrastructure, and land leases of $1.4 million and $300,000 [S2][S6].
- At the October 3, 2026 groundbreaking of the Westport tsunami vertical evacuation structure, he said the city "worked very, very hard to retain" the federal BRIC funding after being told in April 2025 that it was no longer available. The project costs about $15.3 million, $13.7 million of it from BRIC [S11].
- Says he has won convictions, working with the Grays Harbor prosecutor's office, in cases involving burglary, vehicle theft, child sexual assault, assault and homicide by delivery of fentanyl [S6].
