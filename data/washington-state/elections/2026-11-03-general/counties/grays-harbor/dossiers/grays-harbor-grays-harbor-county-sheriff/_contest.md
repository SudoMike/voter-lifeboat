## What the office does
The sheriff runs the county's law enforcement and its jail. Wallace says the job covers about 100 employees in the criminal, corrections, administrative, emergency management, court security and corrections medical divisions, with a budget of about $20 million (S2). It is a county office with a four-year term (S1).

## Race dynamics
Incumbent Darrin Wallace is seeking a second term. He won the office in 2022 with 73.94% of the vote over Michael Catlett (S5). His challenger, Ed Welter, is a detective in Wallace's own office and the sitting mayor of Westport. Neither candidate states a party preference (S1, S2). Wallace led the certified August primary 10,022 votes (59.94%) to 6,626 (39.63%) (S4).

The new jail is Wallace's central issue. He calls a new criminal justice center his top priority and says modular Steel Cell units could cut its estimated cost from about $100 million to about $50 million. He says he would fund it through a grant, a bond or a public-private partnership (S1, S2). In 2023 the county bought a site next to Beacon Elementary School in Montesano, and the commissioners have said they are waiting for the sheriff's office to start public outreach there (S3). At a July Republican-sponsored forum, Wallace said he planned to retire once the jail was under contract and to mentor Chief Jeremy Holmes as his successor. The next day he posted that he intends to serve the full four-year term (S3).

Welter's campaign is about engagement and staffing. He says the deputy count has barely changed since 2000 while the unincorporated population grew by almost 30% (S2, S6). He pledges to attend public meetings and to press the county's case with state legislators (S1, S2).

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** a first-term sheriff with 20 years in the office, against a detective and small-city mayor who says he brings "a new perspective."
2. **Spending:** Wallace pairs a roughly $50 million jail project with a claim that he has stayed under budget every year. Welter calls for more deputies and better facilities while acknowledging budget limits.
3. **Safety:** both use enforcement framing. Wallace stresses "tough on crime" and the Drug Task Force. Welter says all crimes, property crime included, should be fully investigated, and he backs therapeutic courts as part of accountability. The difference is small.
4. **Taxes, housing, climate, reform:** no evidence in this race.
