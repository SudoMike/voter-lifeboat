## Background

Daniel Crawford prefers the Republican Party and is the Grays Harbor County chief criminal deputy prosecutor [S1][S6]. He still held that post under interim Prosecutor Jason Walker in June 2026 [S9][S10]. He launched his campaign before Walker's February 2026 appointment [S6]. He has no prior elected experience [S1].

He calls himself a U.S. Marine Corps veteran and a career prosecutor with more than 15 years of public service and more than 100 jury trials [S1][S3]. Before Grays Harbor he was chief criminal deputy prosecutor in Pacific County, where he says he performed the elected prosecutor's duties for a substantial period during a leave of absence. He was earlier a deputy prosecutor in Grant, Mason and Adams counties [S3]. He graduated from South Kitsap High School in 1990, holds a B.A. from the University of Southern California and a J.D. from Syracuse University College of Law, and has been licensed in Washington since 2011 [S1][S3]. He describes himself as a homeowner, business owner and CrossFit coach, and says his business offers free physical training to local recovery programs [S1].

He trailed Walker by 38 votes in the certified August primary, 8,823 (49.84%) to 8,861 (50.05%) [S11].

## Positions

### Public safety and prosecution
- Says he is running "to put public safety first and restore accountability in the justice system." He cites "rising drug crimes, fentanyl distribution, property crime, and repeat offenders who are too often released only to offend again" [S2].
- Will "aggressively pursue violent offenders, those who prey on children, drug traffickers, and domestic abusers." He describes the job as "enforce the law, support law enforcement, and protect law-abiding citizens" [S2].
- Described the culture he wants in the office in October 2025: "we're going to be hard on crime, we're going to get the worst of the worst people out of our community, and the people we can help, we're going to do our best to put them in our therapeutic courts and help them" [S8].
- Says "accountability, structure, compassion, and support must work together." He pledges to treat people "fairly and respectfully" and to give victims a voice [S1].
- Says his approach rests on "transparency, accountability, and a victim focused prosecution," and that an office "must be both firm, fair and transparent" [S3].

### Office staffing and pay
- In October 2025, as chief criminal deputy under Prosecutor Norma Tillotson, he asked the commissioners to approve promotions and raises for three prosecutors, about $89,376 a year in total. He cited heavy caseloads and major felony work beyond their experience [S7].
- Said losing those prosecutors would be "catastrophic for being able to prosecute crime," and that three more criminal prosecutors in vacant positions would ease the workload [S8].
- When Commissioner Georgia Miller attributed retention problems to leadership and workplace culture, he kept the focus on pay and merit. He said his attorneys "see a change of culture at least as far as the criminal division is concerned" [S7].

## Record

- County Administrator Sam Kim said that by October 2025 the number of trials in the criminal division had "tripled or quadrupled due to (Crawford) pushing for more trials and Public Defense being less willing to accept plea deals" [S8].
- In May 2026 he and a deputy prosecutor won a jury conviction of Michael Hogle for first-degree child rape and child molestation. He asked for a 60-year sentence, and the court imposed 45 years to life [S9].
- At the June 2026 sentencing of Steven Shay Smith for murder, he said, "It's disappointing that this is the maximum," and "If he gets out, he will do this again" [S10].
