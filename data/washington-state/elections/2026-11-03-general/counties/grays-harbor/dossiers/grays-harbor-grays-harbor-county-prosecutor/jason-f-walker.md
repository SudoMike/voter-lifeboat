## Background

Jason F. Walker prefers the Democratic Party and is the appointed interim Grays Harbor County prosecuting attorney [S1][S6]. The Board of County Commissioners appointed him effective March 17, 2026, to finish the term of Norma Tillotson, who resigned at the end of 2025. He was chosen from three nominees put forward by the Grays Harbor Democrats [S6][S7]. He has no prior elected experience [S1].

He was a Grays Harbor deputy prosecutor from 2011 to 2015 and chief criminal deputy from 2015 to 2022. From 2022 to 2026 he was a staff attorney at the Washington Association of Prosecuting Attorneys (WAPA) [S1]. (The Daily World dates his chief criminal deputy tenure from December 2011 to December 2022 [S6].) He says he has tried more than 85 cases to a jury and defended more than 60 convictions on appeal [S1][S2]. He started community college at 30 and holds an A.A. from Portland Community College, a B.S. in political science from Portland State University and a J.D. from Lewis & Clark Law School [S1][S3]. He has judged YMCA mock trial competitions since 2015 and coached the University of Washington law school mock trial team in 2026 [S1].

He led the certified August primary by 38 votes, 8,861 (50.05%) to Daniel Crawford's 8,823 (49.84%) [S8].

## Positions

### Public safety and prosecution
- Will "prioritize prosecution of repeat criminals, violent and sex offenders, and those who threaten the community" and focus resources on the cases with "the greatest impact on public safety" [S4].
- Calls himself "an early champion of diversion and therapeutic courts" that can "stop the revolving door for those who can be reformed." He says Grays Harbor's therapeutic courts should "remain focused on accountability—with real consequences for failure" [S4].
- Says he will ensure "serious criminals are held accountable" [S1].
- Pledges to treat law enforcement as partners, give clear legal guidance during investigations and "ensure the rights of all are respected" [S4].

### Victims
- Promises a "trauma-informed, victim-centered approach," with victims informed of their rights and connected to resources [S2][S4]. He says he became a prosecutor after seeing domestic violence affect someone close to him in college [S2][S3].

### Office stability and management
- Says turnover rose "dramatically" while he was away from the office, and that with "too little training and the loss of institutional knowledge, provable charges were reduced or dismissed" [S1]. His website cites "nearly 300% turnover" in the past few years [S4].
- Pledges to rebuild a stable team, set consistent office policies, bring WAPA training methods to the county, use data to track performance and outcomes, and work with the courts to cut delays [S4].

## Record

- As chief criminal deputy, says he convicted the woman who fatally stabbed an Aberdeen hotel owner and the man responsible for a 2003 McCleary cold-case rape, which was solved through forensic genetic genealogy [S1][S3].
- At WAPA, says he created the first statewide basic training "boot camp" for new prosecutors, trained more than 150 of them, and worked on recruiting and retention in rural counties [S1][S2][S3].
- As interim prosecutor, wrote to the Washington Supreme Court on April 24, 2026 in "strong opposition" to proposed amendments to CrR/CrRLJ 2.2 and 3.2. He argued that capping bail at $200 for most misdemeanors, with 10% posted, would recreate bail forfeiture as an informal disposition. He said it would give defendants a "perverse" incentive to forfeit $20 and not return to court [S9].
