## What the office does
The prosecuting attorney prosecutes criminal cases for the State of Washington in Grays Harbor County and represents the county in civil matters (S3). It is a partisan office with a four-year term (S1). At $237,460 a year it is the county's highest-paid elected office (S2).

## Race dynamics
Prosecutor Norma Tillotson (D) resigned at the end of 2025 with a year left in her term. The county commissioners appointed Jason Walker (D) as interim prosecutor, effective March 17, 2026, from three nominees put forward by the Grays Harbor Democrats (S2, S3). Walker was the office's chief criminal deputy from December 2011 to December 2022, then spent 2022-2026 as a staff attorney for the Washington Association of Prosecuting Attorneys (S1, S2). His challenger, Daniel Crawford (R), is the office's current chief criminal deputy prosecutor and still held that post under Walker in June 2026 (S2, S6). Walker edged Crawford in the certified August primary 8,861 (50.05%) to 8,823 (49.84%), a 38-vote margin and the closest race in the county (S4).

Both candidates run on holding offenders accountable, supporting victims and working with law enforcement (S7, S8). The live dispute is the office's instability. In October 2025, before Walker returned, Commissioner Georgia Miller told Crawford the office had "a leadership issue and a culture issue." She cited two internal investigations, and she said retention had worsened after a 2023 pay increase. Crawford argued for raises to keep his felony prosecutors (S5). Walker says turnover rose "dramatically" while he was away and that charges were reduced or dismissed because of thin training (S1). His website puts turnover at "nearly 300%" (S7).

The Daily World's October 2026 written candidate Q&A series did not include this race as of 2026-10-08.

## Differentiating issue axes
1. **Record vs. renewal:** an appointed incumbent who returned from a statewide prosecutors' association post, against the office's current chief criminal deputy running as a challenger. Both cite deep trial records (85+ and 100+ jury trials).
2. **Safety:** both use accountability framing. Crawford's materials stress aggressive prosecution and repeat offenders "too often released." Walker calls himself "an early champion of diversion and therapeutic courts" with "real consequences for failure." He also opposed proposed court-rule amendments that would cap misdemeanor bail. Both endorse therapeutic courts for some defendants.
3. **Spending:** Crawford argued before the commissioners for raises and more prosecutor positions. Walker stresses training, retention and efficiency, without a specific budget ask.
4. **Taxes, housing, climate, reform:** no evidence in this race.
