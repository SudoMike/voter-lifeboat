## Background

Brielle S Joyce prefers the Democratic Party and filed for auditor during the May 2026 filing week [S1][S8]. She moved to Grays Harbor during the COVID-19 pandemic in search of her first home [S2][S5]. She has managed for Verizon and now works full time at a local bank, and she also runs an online business [S1][S5]. She lists "college and trade school" as education [S1]. She has not held elected office [S1].

She serves on her church's Fellowship Committee [S1]. She is a lead fundraising volunteer for Family Promise, a local homeless-shelter nonprofit, and says she is dedicated to alleviating food scarcity [S2][S5]. She placed second in the August primary with 7,885 votes (44.45%) to Charlee Paull's 9,835 (55.44%) [S6]. She spoke at the Grays Harbor Young Professionals candidate forum at Tinderbox Coffee Roasters in September [S7].

## Positions

### Auditor's office operations
- Lists her priorities as "clean and timely audits," restoring licensing, expanding training, strengthening election services and more community outreach [S4].
- Says her top priority is an office "that runs smoothly today and grows stronger every year." She would make sure elections and licensing "have the dedicated staff they need" [S2][S5].
- Proposes ongoing continuing education and cross-training so staff can cover across departments. She wants every part of the office to have "sufficient back up" [S2][S4].
- Would build partnerships with Grays Harbor College for internships to create "a pipeline of qualified candidates" for the office [S4].
- Her website lists timely annual budget reporting, integrity in accounts payable and receivable, timely recording, easy public access to records, and online services for licensing, records and elections [S2].

### Elections
- Promises "continuing education, security compliance, accurate voter rolls, transparent ballot processing, and clear public reporting" [S2]. Her statement pledges to "protect the sanctity of voting" [S1].

### Money in politics
- Her statement says: "It's time to get money out of politics and work together neighbor with neighbor" [S1]. She describes a "grassroots, community-funded campaign" [S5].
