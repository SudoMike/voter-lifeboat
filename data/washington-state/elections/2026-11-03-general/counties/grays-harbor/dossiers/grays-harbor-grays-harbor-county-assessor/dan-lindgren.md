## Background

Dan Lindgren prefers the Republican Party. He has been Grays Harbor County Assessor since 2015 [S1][S2]. He started in the Assessor's Office as an appraiser in March 2007 and says he completed more than 14,000 property appraisals before becoming assessor [S1][S2]. He has been a Washington State accredited real property appraiser since 2007 and reports 25 years of construction and property-management experience [S1]. He graduated from Hoquiam High School and holds an associate degree in business administration and economics from Grays Harbor College [S1][S2].

In 2025 the state's county assessors elected him unanimously to the executive board of the Washington Association of County Officials. He has chaired the Washington State Association of County Assessors' technology committee for 11 years and sits on its legislative committee [S1][S2]. He has served on the Aberdeen Planning Commission for 13-14 years [S1][S2] and is on the executive board of the West Puget Sound Chapter of the International Association of Assessing Officers [S2].

He won earlier terms as a Democrat. In 2022 he sought a third consecutive term as a Democrat against Republican Rick Hole and won the general election 15,380 votes (52.67%) to 13,786 (47.21%) [S4][S5]. When he announced his 2026 re-election bid, he said he was changing his party affiliation to Republican because his values "now align more closely with the Republican Party." He said the change was not based on "any disagreement with local Democratic leaders or members" [S2].

He led the certified August 2026 primary with 9,629 votes (55.23%) to Josh Nuxoll's 7,775 (44.60%) [S6]. He spoke at a Sept. 16 candidate forum hosted by Grays Harbor Young Professionals in Aberdeen [S7].

## Positions

### Property assessment and office administration
- Says he has focused on "fair and accurate property assessments, excellent customer service, transparency, and responsible management of taxpayer resources" [S1].
- Says the assessor's office is "a professional, non-partisan position." He says its job "is to apply the law fairly and consistently to everyone" and that this "does not change based on party affiliation" [S2].
- The office's website, under his name, says that "as the Assessor I do not determine or raise your taxes." It says his job is to value property as state law requires and to calculate the levies set by taxing districts and voters [S3].

### Taxes and government
- "I believe in open government, strong property rights, and responsible taxation. I do not support unnecessary tax increases and believe government should operate efficiently and responsibly on behalf of taxpayers" [S1].
- According to his campaign press release, on the state assessors' association legislative committee he has helped shape property-tax policy, "including fighting bills that would increase property taxes" [S2].
