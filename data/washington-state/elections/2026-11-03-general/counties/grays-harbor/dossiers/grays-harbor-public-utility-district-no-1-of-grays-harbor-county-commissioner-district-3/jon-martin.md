## Background

- Jon Martin of Ocean Shores is the incumbent PUD commissioner for District 3 and is running unopposed. He submitted no voters'-guide statement [S1][S5][S10].
- In July 2021 the PUD board appointed him, from six applicants, to fill the seat left by Russ Skolrood's retirement [S2][S3]. At the time he was a local businessman, Ocean Shores City Council president and incoming board chairman of Greater Grays Harbor, Inc. [S2].
- He was elected unopposed in November 2022 for the rest of the term, with 18,150 votes (97.46%) [S2][S4].
- The Daily World has described him as a former Ocean Shores mayor [S9]. He has served as PUD board president [S6][S7].

## Positions

### Rates and budgets

- As board president, Martin said of the 3% rate increase that took effect in May 2025 that "passing a rate increase is never an easy decision." He said the PUD had absorbed costs to avoid increases for three years, but rising power, construction and maintenance costs left it "no choice" [S6].
- The board adopted a $124 million 2026 budget with no rate increase, using reserves to absorb higher Bonneville Power Administration costs. Martin said not raising energy prices "is one of the true pleasures of this job" [S7].

### Energy supply

- In an October 2026 op-ed he wrote that about 10% of the PUD's power comes from nuclear generation through Bonneville. He described small modular reactors as a possible source of predictable costs and reliable power, while noting that their cost, build time and delivery to utilities remain open questions [S8].
- He wrote that the PUD's approach is to seek "reliable power at the lowest practical cost" [S8].
