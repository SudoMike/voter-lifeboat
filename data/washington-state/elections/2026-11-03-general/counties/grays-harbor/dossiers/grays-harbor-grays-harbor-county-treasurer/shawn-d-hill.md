## Background

Shawn D. Hill prefers the Democratic Party and is the appointed incumbent Grays Harbor County Treasurer. The county's treasurer page lists him in the office [S1][S7]. Treasurer Ken Albert retired on June 30, 2025. The Grays Harbor Democrats nominated three people under the state rule that an appointee must share the departing official's party. The Board of County Commissioners chose Hill in July 2025 by a 2-1 vote, with Commissioner Vickie Raines voting no [S3][S5]. This is his first time on the ballot for the office [S1][S3].

Albert hired him as chief deputy treasurer in 2016, and he held that job for nearly nine years [S1][S5]. Before that he was a fiscal analyst at the state Department of Ecology. At Grays Harbor Paper he was elected treasurer of Association of Western Pulp and Paper Workers Local 913 [S1][S5]. He earned an associate degree from Grays Harbor College in 1997, where he later taught Microsoft applications, and a bachelor's in accounting from Western Governors University in 2013 [S1][S3][S5]. He is a lifelong Grays Harbor resident and has directed a church choir for more than 30 years [S1].

In October 2024 the county commissioners gave him and two other staff members a Leadership and Service Award for a clean state audit report [S3]. He says he was the only chief deputy in the state to complete a new State Auditor's Office banking and revenue schedule correctly [S1][S4].

He led the certified August 2026 primary with 9,442 votes (53.37%) to Wes Cormier's 8,231 (46.53%) [S6]. He spoke at a Sept. 16 candidate forum hosted by Grays Harbor Young Professionals in Aberdeen [S8].

## Positions

### Office administration and trust
- Calls trust his top priority: trust with departments and districts, and taxpayers' trust that "their property is being assessed correctly" and that the office can help them through financial problems [S1][S5].
- Says the office "must be executed in a non-partisan manner" [S1][S4].
- Wants to let taxpayers without an escrow account make monthly payments and to help people with electronic payments [S5].
- Wants to keep the current office staff and culture, "where all are welcome to bring forth ideas for process improvement" [S5].
- Wants continued emphasis on security against scammers' "hi-tech tools and techniques," working with partner banks and county IT [S5].
- When appointed, said he wanted "to work with the treasurers around the state to help clarify how they would like to enter bills for changing things" [S3].

### Fiscal stewardship
- "It is my responsibility to be a careful steward of the public's money... We need to make sure today's decisions do not create unnecessary financial burdens for future generations" [S5].
- Wants to leave the county "in a stronger financial position," which he says "will require healthy reserves, responsible debt management, and the financial flexibility to respond when challenges arise" [S5].

## Record

- Chief deputy treasurer, 2016 to July 2025. Treasurer since July 2025 [S3][S5].
- Says the office under his watch adopted software aligned with the Assessor's Office, in-house credit-card processing, electronic excise filing and a property-tax lockbox. He says the lockbox cut tax processing "from weeks to as quickly as a single day" [S2][S4].
- Prepares the financial reports for the annual state audit and says the office has been recognized for clean audits [S1][S5].
