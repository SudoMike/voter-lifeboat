## Background

Wes Cormier prefers the Republican Party and lives in rural Elma with his wife and four children [S1][S2]. He graduated from Hoquiam High School and attended Grays Harbor College and Portland Community College. A 2016 profile said he does not hold a degree [S3][S5][S6]. He was a Grays Harbor County juvenile officer (2003-2005) and then a senior real estate appraiser in the Grays Harbor County Assessor's Office (2005-2012) [S4][S8].

He was elected Grays Harbor County District 1 commissioner in 2012 and re-elected in 2016. Preliminary 2016 results had him at 54.99% to Democrat Jamie Nichols' 44.85% [S6][S7][S8]. He served two terms, eight years, and says he kept a pledge to serve no more than two [S1][S3]. In 2020 he ran for state Senate in the 19th Legislative District and finished third in the primary [S4]. He served on more than a dozen boards, including the county Finance Committee, Board of Health, Grays Harbor Transit Authority, Olympic Region Clean Air Agency, Chehalis Basin Flood Authority and Canvass Board, and chaired the county tourism board (LTAC) for eight years [S3][S5].

He announced his treasurer campaign in March 2026 [S4]. He placed second in the certified August 2026 primary with 8,231 votes (46.53%) to Shawn Hill's 9,442 (53.37%) [S9]. He attended a Sept. 16 candidate forum hosted by Grays Harbor Young Professionals in Aberdeen [S10].

## Positions

### Office administration
- Lists his top priorities as upholding state law, "top customer service," and being "an accessible and transparent county treasurer" [S5].
- Wants a "very streamlined Treasurer's Department" operating "as cost effective as possible," and says he would keep evaluating how the department serves the public [S5].
- Says the job requires "careful stewardship of public money, smart internal controls, efficient operations" and broad financial analysis [S4].
- Describes himself as a commissioner who was "a pragmatic decision maker with a focus on working families, local businesses, and the community" and promises "accountability, integrity, and protecting the interests of Grays Harbor residents" [S1][S12].
- Cites his appraisal background as giving him knowledge of "our complex levy system" [S1][S4][S5].

### Budgets and spending
- Says he helped manage more than $100 million in public funds as commissioner, reviewing and approving budgets and department funding requests [S5].
- In 2016, as commissioner, he voted against filling positions not already in the budget during the year. He argued that "every budget is negotiable... There is nothing that's off limits" [S6].
- In his 2020 Senate questionnaire he said "the state must cut spending" and proposed a priority-based budget in which agencies "justify their FTE's, projects, goals and expenditures" [S8].
- Says he "always fought for balanced budgets and fiscal responsibility" [S3].

### Taxes
- "I NEVER voted to raise your property taxes, but to reduce them" [S3].
- Says he led the successful repeal of the county admission tax [S3].
- In 2020, on closing a state budget gap: "A tax would be a slap in the face to hard working Washingtonians." Asked which sectors should bear any increase, he answered, "A tax on politicians" [S8].

### Regulation and permitting
- Says he proposed and passed an ordinance letting property owners build accessory buildings up to 800 square feet without an inspection, permit or fee, and an expedited permit process for agricultural outbuildings up to 1,500 square feet [S3][S6].
- Says he voted against using eminent domain to take private property for a flood-control project [S3].
- Says he sent the state a bill for the cost of unfunded mandates [S3].

### Government process and campaign finance
- Says he created a county hiring committee "to squash nepotism and favoritism," a post-meeting media Q&A session, evening meetings and annual town halls in every commissioner district [S3][S6].
- Kept his pledge to serve no more than two terms as commissioner [S1][S3].
- In his 2020 Senate questionnaire: "I do not accept endorsements or give them. I do not accept money from PAC's, special interest or lobbyists" [S8]. No 2026 statement on this was found.

## Record

- District 1 County Commissioner for two terms (eight years, starting after his 2012 election) [S1][S8].
- His campaign lists these accomplishments as commissioner: repeal of the county admission tax, waiving Olympic Region Clean Air Agency fees for property owners in flood-disaster counties, a variance that kept the Montesano senior meals program at City Hall, a vote to keep the public hospital district's boundaries small, and a vote against a 20-year garbage and recycling contract [S3][S6].
